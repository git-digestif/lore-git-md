This thread is about adding scope hints to Git’s advice messages—telling users whether to use `--global` or `--local` when disabling an advice with `git config`. Vsevolod Myalitsin’s v4 patch series proposes a boolean `is_global_hint` to simplify the design, replacing an earlier enum-based approach that caused build failures. The series has three patches: a refactor of `vadvise()`, the scope-hint mechanism itself, and marking `defaultBranchName` as global.

The discussion has converged on whether the hint should default to `--global` for all advice settings or retain per-setting flexibility. Jeff King (Peff) argues that advice messages are about silencing chatter for a *user*, not a repository, so `--global` is always appropriate. Junio initially countered that some users might want per-project settings, but in his latest reply he suggests an even simpler approach: always recommend `--global` in the hint, eliminating the need for `is_global_hint` entirely. This would further simplify the patch but may conflict with Peff’s view that some settings (like `defaultBranchName`) are inherently global. The series remains under review, with no clear maintainer decision yet. A tangential documentation patch about `<file-option>` in `git-config.txt` may be resolved separately.
