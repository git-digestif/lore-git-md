This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason responds to Junio C Hamano’s concern about `clang-format`’s inability to replicate Git’s nuanced line-wrapping conventions, which prioritize human judgment over rigid column limits.

Ævar acknowledges the limitation—no formatter can fully replicate Git’s flexible line-breaking rules—but argues that punting on the `ColumnLimit` rule is a pragmatic way to make progress on the broader `clang-format` proposal. He frames the RFC as a low-stakes exploration rather than a push for adoption, noting that he initially investigated `clang-format` out of personal curiosity and to test whether Git’s existing configuration was misaligned with the tool’s capabilities. His reply does not propose a solution to the line-wrapping problem but suggests that the community might need to accept imperfect automation in exchange for the benefits of reduced manual style nitpicks.

The email also references a past incident where `clang-format` produced odd formatting in a patch from Han-Wen Nienhuys, hinting at broader concerns about the tool’s consistency with Git’s manual style. This ties into Phillip Wood’s earlier critique about struct formatting and underscores the tension between automation and stylistic fidelity. Ævar’s tone is resigned rather than optimistic: he implies that `clang-format` may never perfectly match Git’s style, but that the trade-off could still be worth it if the community is willing to tolerate minor deviations.

No new technical details or alternatives are introduced here, and the reply does not engage with Randall S. Becker’s counterproposal, Eric Sunshine’s reflow constraint, or the platform-compatibility issues raised by brian m. carlson. The focus remains narrowly on the line-wrapping question, leaving the broader debate unresolved.
