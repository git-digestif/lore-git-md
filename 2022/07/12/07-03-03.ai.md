This is a follow-up in the ongoing `clang-format` adoption discussion, specifically addressing Junio C Hamano’s concern about whether automated formatters can replicate Git’s nuanced line-wrapping conventions. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason acknowledges the limitation—no formatter can fully capture Git’s flexible, context-dependent line-breaking rules—and proposes punting on the issue as a pragmatic compromise to advance the broader `clang-format` effort. He frames the RFC patch series as a response to brian m. carlson’s original proposal, motivated by curiosity about whether Git’s `.clang-format` configuration could be improved, rather than a personal push for adoption.

The email does not resolve the core tension: Git’s style guidelines prioritize human judgment in line breaks (e.g., wrapping at "good" places even if it slightly exceeds 80 columns), while `clang-format` enforces rigid rules. Ævar’s suggestion to disable `ColumnLimit` (setting it to `0`) is a temporary workaround, not a solution—it avoids enforcing *any* line-length limit, which conflicts with brian’s insistence on a specific cutoff (80 or 132 columns) and Junio’s preference for flexibility. The unresolved question remains: whether to reintroduce a stricter `ColumnLimit` later (e.g., via a separate `make style` target), accept `clang-format`’s rigid behavior, or abandon automated line-length enforcement altogether. Ævar’s reference to Han-Wen Nienhuys’ patches (where `clang-format` produced "odd styling") underscores the tool’s limitations in handling edge cases, though he does not propose an alternative.

This reply advances the thread by clarifying the trade-offs: incremental progress on `clang-format` adoption requires accepting some deviations from Git’s style, including line-wrapping flexibility. However, it does not address Junio’s broader skepticism about whether *any* automated formatter can replicate Git’s conventions, nor does it engage with Eric Sunshine’s later observation about `clang-format`’s reflow behavior (which actively rewraps code to fill available width). The discussion remains stuck on whether the benefits of automation outweigh the costs of style compromises, with no clear path forward for reconciling the two.
