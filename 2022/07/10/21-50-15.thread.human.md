The Git mailing list is discussing whether to adopt an automatic code formatter to reduce contributor friction and eliminate manual style nitpicks in reviews. The conversation has two competing proposals: one to use `clang-format` (advocated by brian m. carlson) and another to build a pluggable, language-agnostic formatting system directly into Git (proposed by Randall S. Becker). The `clang-format` approach has made measurable progress, with a patch series from Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason introducing tooling to test and refine the `.clang-format` configuration. This series has reduced the formatting delta between `clang-format` output and Git’s current style by about 60%, from roughly 32,000 to 13,000 lines of diff, with the latest patch addressing bit-field colon spacing and further trimming the delta by 36 lines.

Junio C Hamano has signaled cautious openness to the `clang-format` proposal, contingent on its ability to reformat the existing codebase without excessive disruption. However, Randall’s counterproposal remains unaddressed by other participants, and the discussion is polarized between the two approaches. The latest patch also highlights a new constraint: the bit-field spacing rule requires `clang-format` v12 or newer, raising questions about version compatibility and whether to standardize on a specific version for CI enforcement. Meanwhile, Randall’s pluggable system—designed to support multiple languages and platforms, including NonStop—has yet to gain traction, leaving the platform compatibility issue unresolved.

Key open questions include whether further `.clang-format` tweaks can close the remaining formatting delta, how to handle enforcement (e.g., CI or pre-commit hooks), and whether the community will accept minor style deviations in exchange for automation. The tooling introduced by Ævar’s series provides a way to measure progress, but fundamental incompatibilities between `clang-format` and Git’s style may persist. No decisions have been made, and the thread remains in the exploratory phase, with no clear path forward for either proposal.
