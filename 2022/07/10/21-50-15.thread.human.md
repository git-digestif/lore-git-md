This thread is exploring whether Git should adopt an automatic code formatter to reduce contributor friction and free up reviewer time for substantive work. The discussion centers on two competing approaches: using `clang-format` (proposed by brian m. carlson) or building a pluggable, language-agnostic formatting system directly into Git (Randall S. Becker’s counterproposal). The `clang-format` proposal has made measurable progress—Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason’s patch series has reduced the formatting delta by about 60% through iterative rule tweaks, and tooling now exists to test and refine the configuration. However, the remaining ~12,700 lines of diff highlight unresolved style incompatibilities, particularly around struct formatting, and the tool’s unavailability on the NonStop platform remains a hard blocker for Randall.

Elijah Newren’s recent reply reframes the debate around the net benefit of automation, arguing that even contributors who can’t use `clang-format` directly (due to platform or IDE constraints) aren’t harmed by its adoption—it merely automates existing style requirements. This aligns with the pragmatic trade-off of the `clang-format` proposal: minor style deviations are acceptable if they reduce reviewer burden. However, Randall’s counterproposal, which envisions a portable, pluggable system built into Git, remains unaddressed by other participants. Junio has signaled cautious openness to `clang-format` if it can reformat the codebase without excessive disruption, but no consensus has emerged on enforcement, cleanup, or how to handle edge cases like alignment-sensitive structs. The discussion remains polarized, with progress on the `clang-format` front but no movement on the pluggable system alternative.
