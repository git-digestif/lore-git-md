The Git mailing list thread on adopting an automatic code formatter continues, with a recent update on the long-standing platform-compatibility blocker for the `clang-format` rule `BitFieldColonSpacing: None`. Originally proposed by brian m. carlson, the idea of using `clang-format` to automate Git's code formatting has sparked a wide-ranging discussion about trade-offs between efficiency gains and style fidelity, platform inclusivity, and the tool's ability to replicate Git's nuanced conventions—particularly around line wrapping and struct formatting.

The latest contribution comes from Tuomas Ahola, who points out that the version constraint brian raised in 2022—clang-format v12+ being unavailable on Debian stable—may no longer apply, as four years have passed and Debian stable has since advanced. This suggests the `BitFieldColonSpacing: None` rule could now be viable without excluding major platforms, though Tuomas does not provide current version data or propose concrete next steps. The reply is narrowly focused on this compatibility question and does not engage with other unresolved issues, such as Junio C Hamano's concerns about `clang-format`'s rigid line-wrapping behavior, Eric Sunshine's observation that the tool actively rewraps code to fill available width, or Randall S. Becker's alternative proposal for a pluggable, language-agnostic formatter built into Git.

The discussion remains in early stages, with no enforcement mechanism or implementation plan agreed upon. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason's incremental patch series has reduced the formatting delta between `clang-format` output and Git's current style by about 60%, but key challenges persist: struct formatting in edge cases (e.g., `tar.h`), line-wrapping conventions, and the tool's reflow behavior. Junio has signaled cautious openness to exploring `clang-format`, contingent on its ability to align with Git's style without excessive disruption, while also suggesting `checkpatch` as a complementary tool. Meanwhile, Randall's counterproposal—a pluggable system written in a portable language like Perl—has yet to gain traction, and the broader question of whether the benefits of automation justify the costs (style deviations, platform constraints, and enforcement friction) remains unresolved. The thread now hinges on whether the community can reconcile these competing priorities or if the effort will stall over fundamental incompatibilities.
