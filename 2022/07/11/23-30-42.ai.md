This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting `clang-format` for Git’s codebase, focusing on the tool’s handling of line-wrapping conventions. Junio C Hamano responds to Eric Sunshine’s concern about `clang-format`’s reflow behavior by emphasizing that Git’s documented style guidelines prioritize *human judgment* over rigid line-length limits. The project’s convention—wrapping lines "at around 80 columns"—is not a hard rule but a flexible guideline that allows wrapping at "good" places even if it slightly exceeds 80 columns (e.g., at column 82 instead of 65). Junio questions whether any automated formatter can replicate this nuanced approach, suggesting that `clang-format`’s rigid reflow behavior (which fills the available width, e.g., 132 columns) may fundamentally conflict with Git’s style.

The reply underscores a key tension in the proposal: while `clang-format` can enforce *consistency*, it may not respect the *flexibility* that Git’s contributors rely on when wrapping lines for readability. Junio’s skepticism is not a rejection of automation but a call for a tool that aligns with the project’s existing practices. The question of whether such a tool exists—or whether `clang-format` can be configured to approximate this behavior—remains unresolved. This exchange adds a critical dimension to the discussion: even if the project adopts a longer line limit (e.g., 132 columns), the lack of support for context-aware wrapping could make `clang-format` a poor fit for Git’s needs. The reply does not address Randall S. Becker’s pluggable formatter counterproposal or other open questions (e.g., struct formatting, bit-field spacing), but it narrows the debate to a specific, high-stakes technical requirement.
