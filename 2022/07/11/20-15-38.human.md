This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason pushes back on Elijah Newren’s optimistic framing of automated formatting, offering a more nuanced view based on his experience with other projects. While he doesn’t outright oppose the idea, he highlights practical pitfalls: the risk of turning minor formatting issues into rigid enforcement hurdles, the importance of portable tooling for fast feedback (especially for contributors who don’t use GitHub CI), and the potential for endless debates over formatting overrides (e.g., `/* clang-format off */`). He also notes that some visually pleasing code (like aligned struct tables) may clash with a formatter’s rigid rules, creating friction rather than reducing it.

Ævar suggests a more incremental approach, such as phasing in formatting rules one at a time (e.g., fixing brace placement before tackling line wrapping) or exploring alternatives like GNU `indent` for better portability. His tone is pragmatic—he’s not dismissing the goal but warning that the transition could be bumpier than proponents expect. The reply doesn’t resolve the core tension between automation and inclusivity but adds weight to the argument that the project should proceed cautiously, if at all.
