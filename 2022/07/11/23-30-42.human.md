Junio C Hamano responds to Eric Sunshine’s concern about `clang-format`’s reflow behavior by emphasizing that Git’s style guidelines are not rigidly tied to an 80-column limit. Instead, they allow flexibility to wrap lines at "good" places—even if that means slightly exceeding 80 columns—rather than forcing awkward breaks at arbitrary limits. Junio questions whether any automated formatter can replicate this nuanced, human-judged approach to line wrapping, suggesting that `clang-format`’s rigid reflow behavior may be fundamentally incompatible with Git’s style.

The reply does not propose a solution or engage with the technical question of whether `clang-format` offers a configuration option to disable reflow. Instead, it reframes the problem as a broader philosophical mismatch: Git’s style prioritizes readability and context-aware line breaks over mechanical enforcement, while `clang-format` appears to prioritize consistency and full-width utilization. This adds a new dimension to the discussion—whether the project should accept `clang-format`’s limitations or seek an alternative tool that better aligns with Git’s flexible line-wrapping conventions.
