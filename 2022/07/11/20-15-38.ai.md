This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason responds to Elijah Newren’s reframing of the `clang-format` proposal as a net win, offering a more nuanced perspective on the practical challenges of automated formatting. While not outright rejecting the proposal, Ævar highlights real-world friction points—such as delayed feedback for contributors without CI access, the risk of turning minor formatting issues into hard enforcement gates, and the potential for prolonged debates over formatting overrides (e.g., `/* clang-format off */`). He also notes that some formatting preferences (e.g., struct alignment) may not align with `clang-format`'s output, even when the code is visually clear, and suggests that these edge cases could spark contentious discussions. The reply proposes a more incremental approach: testing `clang-format` in a limited mode (e.g., adjusting only specific patterns like `}\n\telse {` to `} else {`) or exploring alternative formatters like GNU `indent` for better portability. This positions Ævar as a pragmatic voice advocating for measured experimentation rather than a wholesale adoption of `clang-format`, while acknowledging the tool’s potential benefits. The response does not engage with Randall S. Becker’s pluggable system counterproposal but focuses on refining the `clang-format` approach to mitigate its downsides. Key technical details include the idea of phasing in formatting rules incrementally and the mention of GNU `indent` as a possible alternative, though no concrete testing or comparison has been shared yet.
