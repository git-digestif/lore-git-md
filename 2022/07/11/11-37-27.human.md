This is the third patch in Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason’s four-part RFC series refining Git’s `.clang-format` configuration to reduce the gap between its output and the project’s current style. The patch disables the `ColumnLimit: 80` rule, which previously enforced an 80-character line limit. This change shrinks the formatting delta by nearly 60%—from 32,065 lines of diff to 13,042—by allowing `clang-format` to leave long lines untouched rather than aggressively wrapping them.

The commit message acknowledges that Git’s `Documentation/CodingGuidelines` still nominally recommend an 80-character limit, but notes that the codebase already violates this rule extensively. The patch sidesteps the question of whether to enforce the limit in the future, leaving it open for later discussion. This is a pragmatic trade-off: it prioritizes reducing the immediate formatting mismatch over strict adherence to the project’s stated style, at least for now.

The patch itself is trivial—one line changed in `.clang-format`—but its impact is significant. By removing the column limit, it eliminates a major source of friction between `clang-format` and Git’s existing code, making the tool’s output far more palatable. The accompanying Makefile targets (`style-all-diff-apply`) introduced in earlier patches let the community measure and iterate on this progress, which is key to the broader debate about whether `clang-format` can ever fully align with Git’s style.

This patch doesn’t resolve the larger controversy—whether to adopt `clang-format` at all, or pursue Randall S. Becker’s pluggable-formatter counterproposal—but it provides concrete evidence that the delta can be reduced without drastic compromises. The remaining 13,000-line diff is still substantial, but the trend is encouraging for proponents of the `clang-format` approach. For skeptics, the question remains whether the remaining deviations are acceptable or fundamental.
