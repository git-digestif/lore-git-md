This is a design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git, continuing the thread between brian m. carlson and Randall S. Becker.

Brian responds to Randall’s counterproposal for a pluggable, language-specific formatting system built into Git. He acknowledges Randall’s concern about platform exclusivity but pushes back on the idea that Git should avoid useful developer tools because of uncommon platforms. Brian argues that `clang-format` is widely supported on major desktop and laptop systems (Linux, Windows, macOS, and several BSDs), which cover the vast majority of development environments. He also notes that LLVM (the foundation for `clang-format`) is highly portable and increasingly necessary for modern languages like Rust, suggesting that platforms like NonStop will eventually need to support it. While Brian is open to supporting less common platforms as build targets, he believes that requiring access to a modern toolchain (like LLVM) for development is a reasonable expectation.

The email does not propose a new technical direction or patch, but it clarifies brian’s position: the trade-off of excluding a small number of platforms is worth the efficiency gains of adopting `clang-format`. He frames the discussion as a practical matter of toolchain support rather than a philosophical debate about inclusivity. The tone is matter-of-fact, but the underlying message is that the project should not hold itself back for the sake of platforms that lack modern tooling. No other participants have weighed in on this specific exchange yet, so the discussion remains a two-way debate between the original proposal and Randall’s counterproposal.
