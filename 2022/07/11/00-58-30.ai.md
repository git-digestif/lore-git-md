This is a design discussion reply in the ongoing thread about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Brian m. carlson responds to Randall S. Becker’s platform-compatibility counterproposal, defending the original `clang-format` approach.

Brian acknowledges Randall’s concern about NonStop’s lack of `clang-format` support but argues that the tool is widely available on all major desktop and laptop platforms (Linux, Windows, macOS, and several BSDs), which cover the overwhelming majority of development environments. He suggests that developers on less common platforms (like NonStop) can use a VM or a separate system with `clang-format` installed to format their patches before submission. He also notes that LLVM (the project behind `clang-format`) is highly portable and increasingly necessary for modern languages like Rust, implying that NonStop will likely need to support it eventually.

The response frames the `clang-format` proposal as a pragmatic trade-off: while it may not be universally available today, it is the most practical solution for the majority of contributors, and platform-specific workarounds (like VMs) are reasonable for edge cases. Brian emphasizes that Git should not avoid useful developer tools simply because they are unavailable on uncommon platforms, while still expressing willingness to support those platforms as build targets if they meet basic POSIX requirements. No new technical direction is proposed here—this is a defense of the original RFC’s feasibility and scope.
