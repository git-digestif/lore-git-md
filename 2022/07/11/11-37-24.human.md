This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git, specifically focusing on `clang-format`. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason contributes a four-patch RFC series that makes incremental progress toward aligning Git’s `.clang-format` configuration with its existing coding style.

The series introduces a `make style-all` family of Makefile targets to test how `clang-format` behaves across the codebase. These targets reveal that, even after Ævar’s proposed tweaks, `clang-format` still suggests a ~13,000-line diff against `master`. The patches themselves adjust four specific formatting rules: disabling `BitFieldColonSpacing`, removing the `ColumnLimit` constraint, and preventing indentation of `goto` labels. These changes reduce the suggested diff by about 60 % compared to the current `.clang-format` file, but leave many discrepancies—such as `}\nelse {` instead of `} else {`—that still violate Git’s style guide.

Ævar is cautiously supportive of automation in principle but argues that declaring `clang-format` the canonical style source is premature. He suggests a gradual path: first whitelist files that already pass `clang-format` cleanly, then incrementally fix or exempt the remaining cases. He also raises the risk of “landmine checks”—CI or pre-commit hooks that flag style violations only when a patch touches already-nonconforming code—warning that these could unfairly burden contributors. The series is offered as a foundation for further experimentation rather than a final solution.
