This is a **design proposal** and **patch series** advancing the `clang-format` discussion. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason contributes a concrete, incremental step toward making Git’s `.clang-format` file produce output that more closely matches the project’s current style. The series does not yet declare `clang-format` as the canonical formatter, but it reduces the delta between its output and Git’s manual style by roughly 60 % (from ~32 k lines of diff to ~13 k). The patches add Makefile targets (`style-all`, `style-all-diff-apply`) to test formatting across the entire codebase, and tweak four specific `.clang-format` rules: disabling column limits, relaxing bit-field colon spacing, and preventing indentation of `goto` labels.

The cover letter frames the series as a pragmatic middle ground: it acknowledges that `clang-format` still produces many deviations, but argues that partial progress is valuable. Ævar suggests that the community could start by whitelisting files (or directories) that already pass `clang-format` without changes, then gradually expand coverage as the configuration is refined. He also raises the specter of “landmine checks”—CI or pre-commit hooks that fail a patch simply because it touches code already violating the formatter’s rules—and urges that any enforcement mechanism proceed in parallel with a plan to clean up the remaining violations, rather than leaving them as traps for future contributors.

The series itself is small and focused: two files changed, four patches, no new symbols or on-disk changes. It is clearly marked as RFC and does not attempt to settle the larger debate between `clang-format` and Randall S. Becker’s pluggable-formatter counterproposal. Instead, it provides a tangible artifact for that debate—an updated `.clang-format` file and tooling to measure its impact—so that participants can assess whether further rule tweaks might close the remaining gap, or whether the deviations are fundamental enough to require a different approach.
