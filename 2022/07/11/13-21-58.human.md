This is part of the ongoing discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git, specifically the `clang-format` proposal. Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason responds to Phillip Wood’s critique of `clang-format`’s inconsistent struct formatting by clarifying that the specific example Phillip raised—an irregularly indented struct definition from Han-Wen Nienhuys’ refs backend work—has already been resolved in a prior patch. That patch converted the struct to use designated initializers, which `clang-format` handles cleanly. Ævar suggests this pattern (designated initializers) as a general solution for large struct assignments, noting that many such cases have already been converted or are slated for conversion.

The response also acknowledges that `clang-format` still has outstanding issues, particularly with ASCII-art alignment in structs (e.g., in `tar.h`), but frames these as manageable trade-offs. Ævar proposes two ways to handle such cases: either accept the tool’s consistent (if less visually aligned) output or use `/* clang-format off */` directives to preserve manual formatting where necessary. This positions the remaining formatting delta as a solvable problem rather than a fundamental incompatibility, though it leaves open whether the community will accept minor deviations from Git’s current style.

For a busy reader, the key takeaway is that the `clang-format` proposal is making incremental progress—Phillip’s specific concern about struct formatting is already addressed in some cases, and the tooling introduced in Ævar’s RFC series allows the community to measure and refine the remaining differences. The discussion remains focused on practical alignment with Git’s style, with no movement on Randall S. Becker’s alternative pluggable formatter proposal. The thread is still early-stage, but the concrete tooling and measurable progress (a ~60% reduction in formatting delta) suggest the `clang-format` approach is gaining traction, even if adoption is not yet certain.
