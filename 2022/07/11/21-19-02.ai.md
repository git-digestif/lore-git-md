This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Elijah Newren pushes back against Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason’s pragmatic concerns, reaffirming his support for the `clang-format` proposal while acknowledging the complexity of transitioning. The exchange sharpens the contrast between two perspectives: Elijah’s focus on the end-state benefits (reduced reviewer burden, consistent style enforcement) and Ævar’s emphasis on the practical challenges of adoption (delayed feedback, enforcement friction, edge-case debates).

Elijah argues that the barrier to submitting patches does not increase after the switch—contributors still only need to match the surrounding code, just as they do today. He dismisses the idea that platform portability is a blocker, pointing out that even if some contributors (e.g., on NonStop) cannot run `clang-format` locally, they are no worse off than they are now, since they already rely on CI or reviewers to catch formatting issues. He also counters Ævar’s 30k-line diff example by noting that such discrepancies are inherent to any formatter adoption and that compiler-associated tools (like `clang-format`) are the most practical choice, despite their imperfections.

On struct alignment and other edge cases, Elijah concedes that overrides (e.g., `/* clang-format off */`) may be necessary but sees this as a manageable trade-off rather than a dealbreaker. He agrees with Ævar that the transition will be difficult—estimating it at "multiple man months" of work—but argues that the long-term benefits justify the effort. His position remains that if others are willing to drive the transition, they should be allowed to proceed, even if he does not plan to contribute to the work himself. The reply does not engage with Randall S. Becker’s pluggable system counterproposal, focusing instead on defending the `clang-format` approach against Ævar’s incrementalist objections.
