This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Elijah Newren responds to Randall S. Becker’s platform-compatibility objection by reframing the proposal as a net win for reviewers and contributors, regardless of individual tooling preferences or platform constraints. He argues that even contributors who do not use the formatter directly (e.g., due to IDE limitations or platform restrictions) are not harmed by its adoption, because the formatter merely automates what they would have to do manually anyway: conform to Git’s coding style. The key point is that the formatter does not impose new requirements—it only enforces existing ones more consistently. Elijah’s position aligns with the `clang-format` proposal’s pragmatic trade-off: minor style deviations are acceptable if they reduce reviewer burden and contributor friction, and platform-specific workarounds (e.g., VMs, cross-platform development) are a reasonable expectation for contributors on less common platforms. This reply does not engage with Randall’s counterproposal for a pluggable, language-agnostic formatting system built into Git, nor does it address the technical constraints of NonStop’s permanent lack of `clang-format` support. Instead, it shifts the focus to the broader benefit of automation, even if some contributors opt out of using the tool directly. The argument is substantive but does not resolve the fundamental tension between inclusivity and automation.
