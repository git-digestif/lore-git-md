This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about adopting an automatic code formatter for Git. Elijah Newren pushes back against Randall S. Becker’s platform-compatibility objection, arguing that the lack of `clang-format` on NonStop does not meaningfully disadvantage NonStop contributors. He points out that even on platforms where `clang-format` is available, some developers choose not to use it, yet still manage to contribute by adhering to the project’s style guidelines. Elijah sees the formatter as a net win for reviewers, reducing manual nitpicking without imposing additional burdens on contributors—whether they use the tool or not. He frames the formatter as a one-time style adjustment (a "flag day") followed by business as usual, where contributors simply follow the surrounding code’s format, just as they do today. The reply does not engage with Randall’s counterproposal of a pluggable, language-agnostic formatting system built into Git, nor does it address the broader question of whether Git should solve a general formatting problem rather than a Git-specific one. Instead, it treats the formatter as a reviewer-focused efficiency tool with minimal downside for contributors on unsupported platforms.
