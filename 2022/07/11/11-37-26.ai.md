This is the second patch in Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason’s four-part RFC series refining Git’s `.clang-format` configuration to reduce the delta between its output and the project’s manual style. The patch addresses bit-field colon spacing, a small but measurable contributor to the formatting mismatch.

The change adds `BitFieldColonSpacing: None` to `.clang-format`, enforcing the project’s preferred style (`unsigned foo:1` instead of `unsigned foo : 1`). This tweak reduces the total formatting diff by roughly 36 lines (from 32,029 insertions/deletions to 32,065/29,818), a modest but tangible improvement. The commit message highlights a key trade-off: the rule requires clang-format v12 or newer (released April 2021), cutting off users with older versions. Ævar raises the question of whether to dynamically detect versions or standardize on a “blessed” version for CI enforcement, leaving it as an open question for the thread.

The patch itself is trivial—four lines added to `.clang-format`—but the version-compatibility issue it surfaces is not. It underscores the tension between incremental progress and platform inclusivity, a recurring theme in the broader formatting debate. The series’ tooling (`make style-all-diff-ok`) now allows the community to measure the impact of such tweaks, but this patch also reveals how minor rule changes can introduce new constraints. No new files or symbols are introduced, and the change is purely stylistic, with no behavioral impact. The patch is clearly marked as RFC and does not attempt to resolve the larger debate between `clang-format` and Randall S. Becker’s pluggable-formatter counterproposal.
