This is the third patch in Ævar Arnfjörð Bjarmason’s four-patch RFC series refining Git’s `.clang-format` configuration to reduce the delta between `clang-format` output and Git’s current style. The patch disables the `ColumnLimit` rule (setting it to `0`), which removes `clang-format`’s enforcement of an 80-character line limit. This change alone cuts the formatting diff from ~32,000 lines to ~13,000 lines, a ~60% reduction, by allowing `clang-format` to leave long lines untouched rather than aggressively wrapping them.

The commit message acknowledges the tension between Git’s documented 80-character guideline and the reality that many existing lines exceed it. It raises an open question: whether to reintroduce a stricter line-length rule later (e.g., as a separate `make style` target) or accept that `clang-format` will not enforce this aspect of Git’s style. The patch does not resolve this; it simply removes the rule to make progress on other formatting dimensions while leaving the door open for future discussion.

This change is purely mechanical—one line in `.clang-format`—and introduces no new tooling or behavior. It is part of a broader effort to assess whether `clang-format` can be made compatible with Git’s style without requiring major compromises. The remaining ~13,000-line delta suggests that further rule tweaks (like those in the other patches in the series) will be needed to close the gap, but this patch demonstrates that significant progress is possible with small, targeted adjustments.

The patch does not address Randall S. Becker’s counterproposal for a pluggable, language-agnostic formatter built into Git, nor does it propose an enforcement mechanism. It remains a focused, incremental step toward making `clang-format` a viable option for the project. The version-compatibility constraint introduced in the next patch (requiring clang-format v12+) is not yet relevant here.
