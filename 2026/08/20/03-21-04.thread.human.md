This thread is about a bugfix patch that preserves `WWW-Authenticate` headers during HTTP redirects in Git’s credential subsystem. The issue, reported and fixed by Aaron Plattner, breaks authentication when a redirect target requires OAuth2 Bearer tokens or similar credentials not embedded in the URL. The patch is now in its second version (`v2`), which centralizes the preservation logic into a reusable `credential_update_url()` helper in `credential.c`.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has twice called for broader review, noting the patch is uncontroversial but at risk of being dropped from the `seen` branch due to lack of engagement. Jeff King (Peff) recently provided the substantive review Junio sought, confirming the patch is technically sound ("OK as-is") but suggesting minor improvements: clarifying the commit message to explain why preserving `wwwauth_headers` is safe (they are server-provided, not URL-derived) and reconsidering the design of the new `credential_update_url()` helper. Peff’s feedback also highlights the niche nature of the `wwwauth_headers` mechanism, which may explain the initial lack of review, and he cc’d Matthew Cheetham, the feature’s original author, to bridge the knowledge gap.

The patch touches `http.c` and `credential.c`, adds a test in `t/t5563-simple-http-auth.sh`, and leaves the on-disk format unchanged. No regressions or objections have been raised, and the security model has been validated. The patch remains in `seen` (proposed updates) and is now effectively unblocked, pending incorporation of Peff’s suggestions before the next integration cycle. Key open questions are whether to keep the helper (Peff prefers reverting to the v1’s inline approach) and how to document the `wwwauth_headers` safety invariant.
