Kristoffer Haugsbakk has responded to D. Ben Knoble's proposal to split `git format-rev` by mode, clarifying that the text mode -- which scans arbitrary input and replaces hashes with human-readable references -- is the unique capability that justifies keeping the command at all. He agrees that the revs mode, which formats revision ranges, adds little value and could be removed, leaving `git format-rev` as a simpler text-only tool without the mode flag. However, he pushes back on using `git diff-tree` as the replacement home for the revs functionality, arguing that the required invocation (`git diff-tree -v --always --no-patch`) is confusing and undiscoverable for commit-log-style usage, since the flags read as contradictory ("do a diff verbosely, always, without a diff") and rely on non-obvious behavior around merge commits.

Haugsbakk states he would be entirely comfortable with removing the revs mode or the command altogether, provided no other contributor wants to preserve it as a wrapper around the diff-tree options; for his own workflow the practical impact is minimal since he can use the diff-tree invocation directly or write wrappers. The v2 series adding `--abbrev`, `--color`, and `--date` options remains under review in `seen`, with all prior feedback from Junio C Hamano addressed, but the thread now hinges on whether the broader community sees enough value in the simplified text-only `git format-rev` to justify merging the current options series, or whether the revs functionality should be preserved through a different interface. No substantive technical reviews of the patches themselves have arrived yet, and Junio has reiterated that he is not the best reviewer for this experimental command.
