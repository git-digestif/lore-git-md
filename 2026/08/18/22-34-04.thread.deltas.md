## 2026/08/24

- [2026/08/24/04-48-22 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King argued that non-QUICK callers are not affected by the "unable to read object" race because the second read reloads packfiles, and that repreparing packs on a QUICK miss would reintroduce the performance problems OBJECT_INFO_QUICK was introduced to avoid, so low-level lookups work as designed; he also noted that Elijah Newren's unsubmitted QUICK fix only triggers when opening the .idx fails, but that the mmapped-idx/missing-pack variant is fine because packfile_fill_entry() calls is_pack_valid().
- [2026/08/24/04-55-29 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King proposed avoiding a repeated bsearch_midx() by having fill_midx_entry() return a tri-state distinguishing "not in the MIDX", "in the MIDX but unloadable", and "available", so the fallback would only run for the middle case, while questioning whether the fallback is worth pursuing given the analogous regular .idx/.pack race is already solved.
- [2026/08/24/05-13-39 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt agreed QUICK works as designed and that callers passing it without tolerating false negatives are buggy, but maintained the patch fixes a real bug: an object present in two MIDX-covered packs is not found at all when the preferred pack vanishes concurrently, because the MIDX lookup fails and the non-MIDX lookup skips all MIDX-covered packs — broken regardless of QUICK.
- [2026/08/24/05-40-22 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt clarified that the non-MIDX lookup ignores every MIDX-covered packfile, hiding secondary copies, and proposed an alternative fix of not ignoring all MIDX'd packs but only the one that should have contained the object, which he noted is equivalent to Jeff King's tri-state suggestion.
- [2026/08/24/06-55-39 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King acknowledged Patrick's explanation resolved his confusion, observing that the stale MIDX foils the usual SECOND_READ reprepare strategy because it is not flushed, and that refreshing it would be more complete but complicated by cached "struct multi_pack_index *" borrowers (use-after-free risk); he said skipping the fallback when SECOND_READ is unset would be acceptable since QUICK callers accept false negatives, and that the bsearch gate means the fallback triggers only for known racy negatives.
- [2026/08/24/07-03-17 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King clarified that in the main code path one should not search all MIDX-covered packs after a MIDX miss — the object may be in a new pack Q, but the other MIDX packs should not be searched since most objects have no duplicate there.
- [2026/08/24/07-06-01 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King refined his position: normally the SECOND_READ scan surfaces a new pack at the front of the reverse-mtime-sorted packfile list, and only the case this patch addresses (an existing duplicate hidden in the MIDX) forces rescanning old packs, so the fallback probably should kick in only for SECOND_READ.
- [2026/08/24/07-23-22 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King corrected his previous message's final sentence: the SECOND_READ scan finds the object "without having to open the (million) old packs", not new packs.
- [2026/08/24/14-45-36 by Derrick Stolee <stolee@gmail.com>] Derrick Stolee explained that 'git multi-pack-index expire' exists for exactly this race, keeping "stale" packs in the MIDX to halt reads by new processes while allowing existing processes to read them (used by incremental repacks, more important on Windows where read handles block deletion), and while supporting the robustness fix, suggested leaving redundant data around for a time interval to reduce how often the fallback logic triggers.
- [2026/08/24/14-46-22 by Derrick Stolee <stolee@gmail.com>] Derrick Stolee repeated the same explanation about 'git multi-pack-index expire' and the trade-off of retaining redundant packs to avoid the race.
