Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-patch series to make Git’s object-database fsck checks pluggable is now complete. The goal is to move all backend-specific verification logic out of `builtin/fsck.c` and into the respective ODB backends (loose, packed, multi-pack, etc.), preparing the codebase for future pluggable backends such as reftable or cloud storage. The series is a pure refactoring with no user-visible behavior changes, though it does fix a long-standing discrepancy in the `--full` flag’s behavior (ensuring it controls loose-object verification as documented) and a latent bug in the progress-meter logic (restoring incremental updates).

All feedback from the first two iterations has been addressed. Karthik Nayak provided surface-level reviews, focusing on minor wording and spelling nits, while Toon Claes raised several technical points, most of which have been resolved. The architectural question about whether the `ODB_FSCK_FULL` flag should filter sources in the central `odb_fsck()` helper or in backend-specific callbacks was settled in favor of the latter, making the infrastructure more flexible for non-local backends. The final patch, which moves loose-object verification into `odb/source-loose.c`, introduces the `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` flag to preserve the original `--verbose` behavior and fixes the progress-meter initialization bug. Toon’s remaining questions—whether `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` and `ODB_FSCK_PROGRESS` should be documented as mutually exclusive, and whether the `object_cb` callback should be guarded against NULL—are minor and do not affect the patch’s correctness. Patrick has declined both changes, noting that the flags’ outputs do not interfere and that no current callers pass a NULL callback.

The series is now under final review, with all ten patches posted as v2 on August 26. The scaffolding for pluggable fsck checks is fully in place, and `builtin/fsck.c` has been reduced to a thin wrapper around the new `odb_fsck()` helper. No substantive objections remain, and the unresolved review points are unlikely to block integration.
