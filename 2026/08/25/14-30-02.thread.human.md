This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 10-patch series to make Git’s object integrity checks (fsck) pluggable, allowing each ODB backend to implement its own consistency checks. The series is now complete with v2, incorporating all feedback from the first iteration. The goal is a pure refactoring—no user-visible behavior changes—preparing the codebase for future pluggable ODB backends like reftable or cloud storage.

The key architectural change moves all backend-specific fsck logic out of `builtin/fsck.c` and into their respective ODB backends (`source-loose.c`, `source-packed.c`, etc.). Along the way, the series fixes a few latent issues: the `--full` flag now correctly controls verification of loose objects and alternates (patch 4/10), and a progress-meter bug in loose-object verification is resolved (patch 10/10). The scaffolding for pluggable checks is fully in place, with `odb_fsck()` as the central entry point.

Reviews have been mixed in depth. Karthik Nayak provided surface-level feedback on most patches, focusing on wording and minor code organization, while Toon Claes raised substantive technical questions on patches 5/10 and 9/10. The most significant open issue is Toon’s architectural concern about where the `ODB_FSCK_FULL` filtering logic should reside—whether in the central `odb_fsck()` helper or in each backend’s callback. This affects the series’ future extensibility but isn’t blocking integration. For the final patch (10/10), Toon’s review raises two narrower points: whether the `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` flag should document mutual exclusivity with `ODB_FSCK_PROGRESS`, and whether the `object_cb` callback should be guarded against NULL for robustness. Both are minor and easily addressed.

The series is under active review, with all feedback either resolved or scoped to small, actionable items. No major objections remain, and the refactoring is structurally sound. Once the final two review points are addressed, the series should be ready for Junio to queue.
