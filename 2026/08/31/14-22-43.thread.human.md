This thread is a design discussion about whether Git’s porcelain commands (`checkout -b`, `switch -c`, `branch`) should reject `@` as a branch name. The inconsistency arises because `git check-ref-format --branch` accepts `@` while the non-branch variant rejects it, creating a usability trap: you can create a branch named `@` but can’t check it out afterward (since `@` is interpreted as `HEAD`).

The debate remains split between two positions. Bence Csókás, who reported the issue, argues that `@` is a valuable shorthand for `HEAD` in complex expressions (e.g., `git range-diff long-branch-name{^..otherbranch,..@}`) and removing it would harm usability. He recently reinforced this stance with real-world usage data from his bash history, showing frequent reliance on `@` over `HEAD`. Jeff King (Peff) agrees the current behavior is a footgun but proposes a targeted fix: treating bare `@` like `HEAD` (already rejected in `--branch` mode) while preserving compound names like `foo@bar`. Others, like D. Ben Knoble, have confirmed the inconsistency and highlighted the usability trap but haven’t pushed the discussion toward a resolution.

Junio C Hamano clarified that his 2024 comments about deprecating bare `@` were incomplete and shouldn’t dictate the current debate, leaving the discussion to focus on the trade-offs between safety and convenience. No patch has been proposed yet, and there’s no clear consensus on whether to prioritize stricter validation or preserve `@` for ergonomics. The thread remains civil and technical, but the next step will likely depend on whether a patch emerges—and how it balances the usability trap against the convenience argument.
