The Git version numbering discussion has taken a turn toward user-facing changes, with Harald Nordgren proposing a list of default configuration tweaks he’d like to see in a 3.0 release. The thread, originally focused on whether to bump to 3.0, 2.99, or 2.57 after 2.56, now includes a new sub-thread: whether 3.0 should be used as an opportunity to introduce breaking changes to defaults, not just technical ones like SHA-256 support.

Harald’s suggestions—enabling autostash for `checkout` and `rebase`, more intuitive branch/tag sorting, switching to the `histogram` diff algorithm, and making `git status` compare against both `@{upstream}` and `@{push}`—are all usability improvements, but they’re also likely to spark debate. Some of these, like autostash, have been contentious in the past, and changing defaults is always disruptive. His framing is pragmatic: if these don’t land in 3.0, he jokes, he might have to wait "another 10 years for 4.0." No patches have been submitted yet, so this remains a design-level proposal.

The broader discussion remains unresolved. Junio’s original call for input on version numbering (3.0, 2.99, 2.97, or 2.57) hasn’t yielded a consensus, and key questions—like GitHub’s SHA-256 support and the readiness of the lowercase-only object IDs series—are still open. Emily Shaffer’s emphasis on GitHub’s market dominance and Brian Carlson’s advocacy for a cautious delay (e.g., 2.97) still anchor the debate, but Harald’s proposal adds a new dimension: whether 3.0 should also signal a shift in user-facing behavior. For now, the thread is still in the discussion phase, with no maintainer decision in sight.
