Thread identity
Subject: Update CI to use Debian 12 instead of Debian 11
Series version: standalone patch (no series)
Originating author: Jeff King (Peff)
Thread type: CI/build system maintenance

Core problem or goal
Replace Debian 11 with Debian 12 in GitHub Actions and GitLab CI workflows because Debian 11 reached end-of-LTS (2024-07-10). The goal is to keep CI testing on a supported distribution for the next two years (until 2028-06-30). The proposal to use the `oldstable` Docker tag was explicitly rejected by Peff due to misalignment between Debian's release cycle and LTS window; Junio C Hamano has endorsed this rationale. Additionally, Junio initially proposed removing the `linux32` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs (both using Ubuntu 20.04) due to lack of i386 support and questionable value in testing exotic configurations en masse, but this proposal is now contested.

Current status
The Debian 12 update patch is queued in `seen`; Junio C Hamano has signaled intent to merge it. The patch touches `.github/workflows/main.yml` and `.gitlab-ci.yml`, updating `jobname` and `image` fields from `debian:11` to `debian:12` (not `oldstable`) and comments to reflect the new support window. The fate of the `linux32` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs is now unresolved: Junio's follow-up patch to remove them is on hold pending further discussion, as Patrick Steinhardt has pushed back, arguing for their preservation or adaptation.

Key technical details
Files: `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`
Symbols: none (CI configuration only)
Behavior: no user-visible change; CI environment now runs on Debian 12 instead of Debian 11. The `linux32` job (Ubuntu 20.04, i386) and `linux-TEST-vars` job (Ubuntu 20.04, exotic configurations) are unchanged for now but may be removed or updated depending on the outcome of the discussion.
On-disk format: none
Test coverage: none (CI infrastructure change)
Additional context: The `linux32` job was the last Ubuntu LTS with i386 support, but i386 has been dropped by both Ubuntu and Debian. The `linux-TEST-vars` job tests exotic configurations en masse, which Junio argues is unrealistic, while Patrick Steinhardt notes it has caught regressions in the past. Alpine Linux (already present in CI via `linux-musl-meson`) remains as an alternative for 32-bit coverage, though it lacks glibc.

Open questions and loose ends
- Whether the `linux32` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs should be removed, preserved, or adapted. Junio favors removal, while Patrick Steinhardt argues for their continued value and is willing to send patches to adapt them.
- If preserved, how to address the lack of i386 support in Ubuntu 20.04 (e.g., by switching to a Debian-based image or Alpine Linux).
- Whether Alpine Linux should be extended or merged into the `linux-musl-meson` job to provide 32-bit coverage; this remains a potential future task but is deferred as out-of-scope for the current discussion.
- The long-term value of testing exotic configurations individually versus en masse; no consensus has been reached.
