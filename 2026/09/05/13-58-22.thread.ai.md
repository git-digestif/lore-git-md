Thread identity
Subject: Update CI to use Debian 12 instead of Debian 11
Series version: standalone patch (no series)
Originating author: Jeff King (Peff)
Thread type: CI/build system maintenance

Core problem or goal
Replace Debian 11 with Debian 12 in GitHub Actions and GitLab CI workflows because Debian 11 reached end-of-LTS (2024-07-10). The goal is to keep CI testing on a supported distribution for the next two years (until 2028-06-30). The proposal to use the `oldstable` Docker tag was explicitly rejected by Peff due to misalignment between Debian's release cycle and LTS window; Junio C Hamano has endorsed this rationale. Additionally, Junio has proposed removing the `linux32` and `linux-TEST-vars` jobs (both using Ubuntu 20.04) due to lack of i386 support and questionable value in testing exotic configurations en masse.

Current status
Queued in `seen`; Junio C Hamano has signaled intent to merge both the Debian 12 update and the removal of `linux32`/`linux-TEST-vars`. The Debian 12 patch touches `.github/workflows/main.yml` and `.gitlab-ci.yml`, updating `jobname` and `image` fields from `debian:11` to `debian:12` (not `oldstable`) and comments to reflect the new support window. The removal patch deletes the `linux32` job entirely and updates the `linux-TEST-vars` job to use `ubuntu:rolling` instead of `ubuntu:20.04`. Both patches are expected to graduate to `next` in the next integration cycle.

Key technical details
Files: `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`
Symbols: none (CI configuration only)
Behavior: no user-visible change; CI environment now runs on Debian 12 instead of Debian 11, and two Ubuntu-based jobs (`linux32` and `linux-TEST-vars`) are removed or updated to `ubuntu:rolling`
On-disk format: none
Test coverage: none (CI infrastructure change)
Additional context: The `linux32` job was the last Ubuntu LTS with i386 support, but i386 has been dropped by both Ubuntu and Debian. The `linux-TEST-vars` job was testing exotic configurations en masse, which Junio argues is unrealistic and not representative of real-world usage. Alpine Linux (already present in CI via `linux-musl-meson`) remains as an alternative for 32-bit coverage, though it lacks glibc.

Open questions and loose ends
- Whether Alpine Linux should be extended or merged into the `linux-musl-meson` job to provide 32-bit coverage; this is acknowledged as a potential future task but deferred as out-of-scope for the current patches.
- The long-term value of testing exotic configurations individually versus en masse; Junio's patch removes the `linux-TEST-vars` job's all-at-once approach, but no alternative is proposed for targeted testing of specific configurations.
