This thread began with Jeff King (Peff) updating Git's CI to use Debian 12 instead of Debian 11, now that Debian 11 is out of LTS. That patch is queued and expected to merge soon. Junio C Hamano has now posted a follow-up patch removing two CI jobs that have become problematic: `linux32` (which relied on Ubuntu 20.04's i386 support, now dropped) and `linux-TEST-vars` (which tested exotic configurations en masse on Ubuntu 20.04, also out of LTS). The patch deletes `linux32` entirely and updates `linux-TEST-vars` to use `ubuntu:rolling` instead of `ubuntu:20.04`.

Junio's rationale is straightforward: the `linux32` job is no longer viable, and the `linux-TEST-vars` job's approach of testing all exotic configurations at once is unrealistic and not representative of real-world usage. The patch is small, mechanical, and unlikely to be controversial, as both Peff and Patrick Steinhardt had already flagged the `linux32` job as a latent maintenance issue. The patch is now queued in `seen` alongside the Debian 12 update and is expected to graduate to `next` in the next integration cycle. The only remaining loose end is whether Alpine Linux (already used in CI for musl builds) could serve as an alternative for 32-bit coverage, but that discussion is deferred until a concrete need arises.
