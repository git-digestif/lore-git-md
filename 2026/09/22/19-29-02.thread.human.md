This thread covers two related but distinct documentation efforts in Git. The first, a cross-reference modernization across 38 man pages, is already approved and queued in Junio’s `next` branch since mid-July 2024. It replaces informal section references with formal AsciiDoc links to improve HTML navigation, though it may break some external links due to anchor changes. The patch itself is stable, with no technical objections remaining, but a broader question about whether to prioritize link stability or toolchain consistency remains open for future discussion.

The second effort, a proposed rewrite of Git’s introductory tutorial, is still in the design phase. Julia Evans has shifted from defending the rewrite’s content to refining how it will land, now proposing a two-phase approach: first remove the current tutorial and all references to it, then add the new, simplified version. This addresses Junio’s concern about inadvertently losing useful content or leaving broken references, and his latest reply cautiously endorses the plan as a practical way to avoid repeating the project’s historical pattern of accumulating redundant documentation. The rewrite itself—focusing on a smaller set of commands and deferring branches to a follow-up tutorial—has drawn positive early feedback but no detailed technical review yet. No patch series has been posted, and the conversation remains focused on logistics rather than content.
