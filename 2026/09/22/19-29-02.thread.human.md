This thread is about modernizing cross-references in Git’s man pages to make them clickable in HTML output. Julia Evans proposed a patch that systematically replaces informal plain-text references (like "see EXAMPLES below") with formal AsciiDoc link syntax (`<<target,text>>`) across 38 man pages. The goal is to ensure consistent navigation, especially for cross-file references, where the single-form link (`<<target>>`) can produce broken or inconsistent output (e.g., rendering as `[REMOTES]` in some contexts).

The patch has been approved by Junio Hamano and is queued for integration in the `next` branch. Jeff King (Peff) recently provided a substantive review, confirming the technical necessity of the two-part link form. He tested both AsciiDoc and Asciidoctor, demonstrating that the single-form link behaves unpredictably across backends—sometimes duplicating text or failing to render correctly. While he explored workarounds like custom macros, he agreed the two-part form is the pragmatic choice for now, though it slightly reduces source readability. His feedback also suggested clarifying the commit message to better explain the rationale for the two-part form.

No open questions remain, and the patch is ready for integration. The only other participant, Kristoffer Haugsbakk, offered a surface-level endorsement but did not engage with the technical details. The effort aligns with the broader documentation modernization led by Jean-Noël Avila.
