The Git mailing list is discussing two related documentation efforts by Julia Evans. The first, already approved and queued in `next`, modernizes cross-references in 38 man pages by replacing informal plain-text references with formal AsciiDoc links, improving HTML navigation. This patch is stable and awaiting merge to `master`, though a minor design question remains about whether to prioritize link stability or readability in anchor style.

The second effort, still in the early proposal stage, is a ground-up rewrite of the Git tutorial to simplify it for beginners. Julia proposes focusing on a smaller set of commands and deferring branches to a follow-up tutorial, aiming to reduce cognitive load. Junio C Hamano supports the rewrite in principle but has raised concerns about how to handle topics omitted from the new tutorial—whether to discard them, relocate them within Git’s documentation, or link to external resources. Julia favors a mix of new internal guides and external links via `git-scm.com/learn`, but Junio now elaborates on his skepticism, arguing that external links risk pointing to outdated or irrelevant information, which he sees as actively harmful. He prefers a self-contained solution, drawing a parallel with `giteveryday`, a guide he considers stale and unusable. The discussion remains unresolved, with no concrete plan for triaging omitted topics or revising the draft. Early feedback from test readers, including Jeff King, has been positive, but the external links question could become a blocker for integration.
