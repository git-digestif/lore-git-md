## 2026/09/23

- [2026/09/23/19-06-44 by Kristoffer Haugsbakk <kristofferhaugsbakk@fastmail.com>] Kristoffer Haugsbakk, after reading the commit message, said the two-part AsciiDoc cross-reference form seemed like an obvious idea in hindsight.
- [2026/09/23/21-40-38 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King reported that, per the asciidoc docs and his tests with asciidoc 10.2.1 and asciidoctor 2.0.26, `<<FOO,BAR>>` simply means "link to FOO, using the text BAR" and is not affected by the reference's location; the single-item `<<FOO>>` behaves like `<<FOO,FOO>>` but vanilla asciidoc renders the text as "[FOO]" while asciidoctor uses "FOO", and in manpage output both expand to "the section called FOO", so switching to the single form produces nonsense like "the section called "PRUNING" section". He concluded the real reason for the doubled text is to control the expanded text across implementations and output backends, that using `<<PRUNING,PRUNING>>` is probably OK for a first pass rather than configuring both asciidoc implementations, and that the commit message's explanation (after Julia Evans had written that included-page headers caused broken-link false positives) might be misleading.
- [2026/09/23/22-00-53 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King noted that section auto-ids are enabled, so explicit anchors like `[[EDITING_PATCHES]]` are not strictly necessary (the auto-generated form would require `<<_editing_patches,EDITING PATCHES>>`), but warned that the ids are user-visible in final HTML fragment links, so the patch would change `https://git-scm.com/docs/git-add#_editing_patches` to `#EDITING_PATCHES` and break existing links across the web — a change he prefers (all-caps) but thinks should be made consciously rather than accidentally. He also flagged typographic inconsistencies in the patch, such as `<<OBJECT_PREREQUISITES,"OBJECT PREREQUISITES">>` and `<<BATCH_OUTPUT,\`BATCH OUTPUT\`>>` where the backticks cannot be moved outside the xref and probably ought to be dropped entirely, saying those can be punted and fixed later.
