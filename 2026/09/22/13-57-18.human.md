This is a **substantive review follow-up** in the ongoing bugfix series for `git stash` autostash handling. Phillip Wood and D. Ben Knoble continue their technical discussion of the second patch, which replaces subprocess-based index merging with in-core logic via `merge-ort.c`. The exchange clarifies several key design choices and edge cases that will shape the upcoming v2 submission.

The conversation resolves the diff algorithm question: Knoble agrees to standardize on the `_ui` variant for both index and worktree merges, addressing Wood’s concern about potential behavioral mismatches. On the `verbosity` field in `merge_options`, Knoble opts to retain it (set to 0) despite Wood’s observation that it is unused by the ort strategy, citing a desire to keep the merge quiet and potential future use. The most critical discussion centers on the removed `assert()` calls in `merge-ort.c`—both agree that fixed ancestor/branch labels must be restored in `do_apply_stash()` to prevent crashes, and Knoble confirms plans to add a test verifying graceful failure if the index merge fails.

The remaining open question is whether `merge_finalize()` should replace `clear_merge_options()`. Wood argues for `merge_finalize()` to ensure the strmap in the merge result is freed, while Knoble notes it doesn’t clear the merge options struct itself. Wood clarifies that the struct contains no allocations needing cleanup, and Knoble suggests conditionally reinitializing the merge options only when `!has_index`. The discussion leaves this as a minor implementation detail to be resolved in v2.

This follow-up advances the patch toward correctness but does not yet finalize all edge cases. The next steps will likely involve a revised implementation with fixed labels, a new test for merge failures, and a decision on the merge options cleanup. Reviewers familiar with `merge-ort.c` (e.g., Elijah Newren) may weigh in further on the assertion and algorithm questions. The series remains a well-motivated bugfix with clear test coverage, though the v2 will need to address these technical refinements.
