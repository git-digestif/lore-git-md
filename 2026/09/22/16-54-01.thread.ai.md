Updated AI thread summary:

This thread discusses the addition of lifecycle hooks for Git worktrees, initiated by Adrian Ratiu with a patch series proposing `pre-worktree-add` and `post-worktree-add` hooks. The goal is to enable automation around worktree creation and deletion, particularly for managing external resources (e.g., Docker containers, databases) tied to worktrees. The series introduces new hook names (`pre-worktree-add`, `post-worktree-add`) and plumbing in the `worktree.c` and `hook.c` subsystems. The current version is v1, with no merge status reported (under review).

A key point of discussion is the absence of a `pre-worktree-remove` hook, which Michał Papis advocates for in a follow-up. He argues that post-removal hooks fire too late—after the worktree is deleted—leaving no reliable way to map external resources to their worktrees for cleanup. A real-world example is provided: AI-generated worktrees (created and discarded rapidly) led to orphaned resources consuming significant disk space, requiring manual cleanup scripts. A `pre-worktree-remove` hook would allow deterministic teardown while the worktree’s configuration is still accessible. Michał clarifies that the hook is not intended to veto removal but to enable informed cleanup, though it would not cover manual deletion followed by `git worktree prune` (making post-removal hooks still necessary for those cases).

Open questions include:
- Whether the hook set is complete (e.g., should `pre-worktree-remove` be added).
- How to handle edge cases like manual worktree deletion, where hooks may not fire.
- The scope of hook applicability (e.g., `git worktree remove` vs. manual deletion).

Participants:
- Adrian Ratiu (author): Proposed the initial patch series (v1) with `pre-worktree-add` and `post-worktree-add` hooks.
- Michał Papis: Advocated for adding `pre-worktree-remove` to address gaps in resource cleanup, providing a real-world use case.

No substantive technical reviews or maintainer decisions have been recorded yet. The thread remains focused on design completeness and practical gaps in the proposed hook set.
