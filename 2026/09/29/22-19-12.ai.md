This is a substantive review of Hanan Arshad's RFC for sharing Git stashes through remotes. Brian M. Carlson engages directly with the proposal's core design questions, offering both technical critique and practical suggestions.

Carlson endorses the general idea of adding porcelain tooling around the existing `git stash export`/`import` plumbing, calling it "helpful" and expressing support for the goal. However, he raises significant concerns about the feasibility of standardizing the workflow across different environments. His feedback centers on three key points: (1) the lack of a universal ref namespace for shared stashes, (2) the impracticality of listing or importing stashes without fetching their data, and (3) the need for user customization in naming conventions.

Specifically, Carlson argues that the ref namespace (`refs/stashes/`) cannot be standardized because users in different contexts (e.g., solo developers vs. teams) will need different naming schemes. He also notes that listing shared stashes without fetching their commits would be "lighter" but incomplete, as stash messages and sizes (which can be large due to untracked files or history references) would be unavailable. For command naming, he suggests `git stash push` as a more intuitive alternative to the proposed `publish`.

The review is substantive because it identifies real-world usability challenges—such as the tension between lightweight listing and displaying stash metadata—that the RFC must address. Carlson’s perspective is informed by his own workflow (e.g., using `--to-ref` for stash exports), which adds weight to his suggestions. While he does not provide a tested alternative or detailed counter-proposal, his feedback highlights areas where the design may need to be more flexible or opinionated to work across diverse use cases. The thread now awaits Hanan’s response or further discussion on how to reconcile these concerns.
