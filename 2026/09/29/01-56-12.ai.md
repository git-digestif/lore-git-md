This is a workflow discussion in the `gitmergeconflicts(7)` documentation series thread. Junio C Hamano expands on Jeff King’s earlier advice about pre-submission integration testing, clarifying the mechanics and benefits of trial-merging a topic branch into a more advanced integration branch (like `next` or `jch`) rather than applying patches directly onto it.

Junio explains that the goal is not to build or test the topic in isolation on the integration branch, but to merge it into the branch to uncover interactions with other in-flight work. This can reveal textual merge conflicts, API changes, or even useful helper functions introduced by other topics. He emphasizes that this approach is especially valuable for contributors working in areas where multiple topics overlap, though it may yield little for isolated documentation work like Julia’s. Junio also notes his personal rule: topics must be in his `jch` branch before he marks them for `next`, reinforcing that `jch` is a stable subset of `seen` and a reliable target for early integration testing.

The exchange is procedural and collaborative, with no direct impact on the series’ content or pending revisions (e.g., `command-list.txt` registration or cherry-pick-specific adjustments). It provides concrete guidance for contributors who want to proactively catch integration issues, while acknowledging that such steps are optional and may not always be necessary.
