Patrick Steinhardt, the primary maintainer of the reftable backend, confirms the urgency of fixing the inconsistency between Git’s reflog timezone encoding (signed HHMM) and the published reftable specification (signed minutes). The fix is now actively being implemented, with Patrick aiming to post a patch later in the week. This follows the earlier consensus that the issue is a clear bug with minimal real-world impact, as reflogs are short-lived and the misinterpretation is bounded. No new technical questions or dissenting views are introduced; the focus is purely on execution.

The patch will target the `refs/reftable/` subsystem and adjust the reflog timezone offset encoding to match the specification (e.g., +05:30 → 330 instead of 0530). The existing test case in `t/t0610-reftable-basics.sh` and the provided reproducer (a temporary SHA-1 reftable repository with a Python script) will likely be used to validate the fix. Interoperability with JGit and other reftable consumers remains the primary motivation, as JGit already implements the specification correctly.

The thread’s earlier discussion established that no backwards-compatibility handling or format version bump is needed, given the limited scope of the issue. Patrick’s commitment to posting a fix this week signals the transition from design consensus to implementation, with no further debate expected unless the patch surfaces unforeseen complications. The urgency is framed as preventing further interoperability problems rather than addressing an immediate crisis.
