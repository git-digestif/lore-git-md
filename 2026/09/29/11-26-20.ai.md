This is a **substantive review** of Harald Nordgren’s patch that extends `` `git branch --delete-merged` `` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The email is D. Ben Knoble’s follow-up in the thread, offering technical observations and asking for clarification.

The review explores two implementation angles: whether the rebase case could leverage `` `git log --cherry-mark` `` (or `` `git range-diff` ``) to identify rebased commits, and whether the squash case could short-circuit if an upstream commit’s tree exactly matches the branch tip. Knoble notes that the patch currently treats both cases uniformly, which he finds reasonable because the squash check subsumes the rebase check and avoids a priori guessing. He also flags that the algorithm may be expensive on large repositories, though he questions whether a performance caveat in the documentation would be worth maintaining.

Additionally, Knoble asks where the post-2.56 user feedback was seen, expressing pleasant surprise at the quick uptake. The question is purely informational and does not challenge the patch’s motivation. No new edge cases or objections are raised; the review remains additive and focused on implementation trade-offs.
