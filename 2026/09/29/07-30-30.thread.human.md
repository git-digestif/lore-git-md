This thread is about Harald Nordgren’s patch series (now at v7) that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes—though not their exact commit tips—have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows. The new `--delete-integrated` flag offers a one-step cleanup command for common forge and local workflows, handling cases where upstream modified the same files before integration.

The series is technically complete and ready for Junio’s queue. The CLI design is finalized, with `--delete-merged` preserving strict tip-reachability (unchanged behavior) and `--delete-integrated` opting in to change-based detection for both rebased and squashed branches. All substantive review feedback—performance inefficiencies, `.gitattributes` resolution behavior, edge-case robustness, and blob-fetching transparency—has been addressed in v7. The first two patches (refactoring and landing-message helper) are uncontroversial and ready for integration, while the third (core flag) and fourth (squash-detection with upstream modifications) are now technically sound after fixes to index handling, documentation, and test coverage.

The sole major gap remains the author-check mitigation proposed by Jasper Mayone to reduce false positives in forge workflows, though it’s not yet implemented. Minor usability nits (outdated cover letter flag names, silent skipping of checked-out worktrees) and Phillip Wood’s call for clearer documentation of limitations (e.g., lack of rename detection) are still open but don’t block integration. Tuomas Ahola’s recent email adds three surface-level wording suggestions for the commit message and documentation, none of which affect the patch’s functionality or correctness. The series is now awaiting Junio’s decision to queue it.
