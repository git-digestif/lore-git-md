This thread introduces `git branch --delete-integrated`, a new opt-in flag that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes (but not their tips) have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows—even when the upstream branch modified the same files before integration. The series, now at v6, is authored by Harald Nordgren and is under final review.

The CLI design is settled: `--delete-merged` retains its strict tip-reachability behavior (unchanged), while `--delete-integrated` performs change-based detection for both rebased and squashed branches, handling upstream modifications via a path-by-path three-way merge approximation. The first two patches—a pure refactoring and a landing-message helper—are uncontroversial and ready for integration. The third patch, introducing the core `--delete-integrated` flag, is also uncontroversial after addressing Junio’s stylistic feedback. The fourth patch, which implements squash-detection with upstream modifications, now correctly respects `.gitattributes` merge drivers after Junio identified and Harald fixed a latent correctness issue in v5.

The series is technically complete, with the author-check mitigation (Jasper Mayone’s proposal) remaining the sole major gap. This mitigation would require that the landing commit’s author also authored at least one commit on the branch being deleted, addressing false positives in forge workflows and the "npm update" scenario. Minor usability nits—such as outdated flag names in the cover letter and silent skipping of checked-out worktrees—are still open, but the core functionality is stable. Junio has not raised further concerns, and the series is poised for merging once he signs off.
