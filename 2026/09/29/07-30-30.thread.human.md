This thread is about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes (but not their tips) have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows—even when upstream modified the same files. The series, now at v5, introduces `--delete-integrated` as an opt-in flag to handle these cases, while keeping the original `--delete-merged` behavior unchanged for strict tip-reachability.

The CLI design is finalized and confirmed by Junio, with two of Jasper Mayone’s usability nits addressed in v5 (help output and error messages). The first two patches (refactoring and landing-message helper) are uncontroversial and ready for integration. The third patch (core `--delete-integrated` flag) received minor stylistic feedback from Junio, which the author has agreed to address. The fourth patch (squash-detection with upstream modifications) is the focus of current discussion: Junio identified a correctness issue where `.gitattributes`-driven merge behavior is silently ignored because the index state passed to `ll_merge()` may not be populated. Harald has acknowledged the issue but not yet proposed a fix.

Key participants include Harald (author), Junio (maintainer), Jasper (proposed author-check mitigation), and Phillip Wood (raised performance and correctness concerns). The author-check mitigation remains unimplemented but is the sole major technical gap for the series. Open questions include how to resolve the `.gitattributes` issue, whether to implement author checks or patch IDs for rebase detection, and whether to document the algorithm’s limitations more explicitly. The series touches `builtin/branch.c`, adds new test coverage, and introduces a path-by-path three-way merge approximation for squash detection.
