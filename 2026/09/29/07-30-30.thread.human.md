This thread is about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes (but not their exact commits) have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows—a common pain point for users of GitHub-style workflows and local `git merge --squash`. Harald Nordgren has posted three iterations of the series, with v3 splitting the implementation into separate patches for rebase detection and squash detection, preserving performance optimizations, and adding a `--no-squashed` flag.

The discussion has taken a sharp turn with Junio C Hamano’s latest email, which questions the *fundamental goal* of the feature. He argues that the current `--delete-merged` (which only deletes branches whose exact commits are reachable) is a "stable and strict" tool with clear safety guarantees, while detecting rebased or squash-merged branches introduces ambiguity—what if the changes were independently solved by someone else? His proposal is to **decouple the two concepts entirely**: leave `--delete-merged` unchanged and introduce a new `--delete-irrelevant` option for the looser, change-based detection. This would address his earlier concerns about CLI scalability (the current `--no-squashed` flag doesn’t scale to future workflows like selective cherry-picks) and preserve the safety of the existing command. The email implies that the squash-merge and rebase-merge logic may need to be **revisited or deferred** until the project agrees on whether `--delete-irrelevant` is worthwhile.

Key participants have staked out positions: Harald Nordgren has implemented v3 with a three-patch reorganization and agreed to explore `git range-diff`’s commit-matching logic for rebase detection, but hasn’t yet responded to Junio’s latest proposal. Phillip Wood has focused on performance and correctness, advocating for `--no-squashed` and clearer documentation of the algorithm’s limitations (e.g., lack of rename detection). D. Ben Knoble has proposed using **patch IDs** for rebase detection, arguing that log-message matching (as in `git range-diff`) is insufficient for local workflows with edited commit messages. The thread is now at a crossroads: whether to proceed with the current unified approach, adopt Junio’s `--delete-irrelevant` split, or defer squash detection entirely. The next steps will likely hinge on whether the project accepts Junio’s framing of the problem as one of **user intent and safety**, not just technical implementation.
