This thread is about Harald Nordgren’s patch series (now at v5) that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes—rather than just their tips—have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows. The new `--delete-integrated` flag (opt-in) handles both rebased and squashed branches, including cases where upstream modified the same files before integration, using a path-by-path three-way merge approximation via Git’s low-level merge machinery (`ll_merge`).

The series is under active review, with the first two patches (refactoring and landing-message helper) marked as uncontroversial and ready for integration. The third patch (core `--delete-integrated` flag) received only stylistic feedback from Junio, while the fourth patch (squash-detection with upstream modifications) now faces its first substantive technical concern: Junio identified that the `ll_merge()` call in `merge_keeps_upstream()` may silently ignore `.gitattributes` because the index (`the_repository->index`) is uninitialized at that point. This could lead to incorrect merge results or false negatives/positives in squash detection. The issue does not affect the existing `--delete-merged` behavior but risks undermining the new logic’s correctness.

Harald has addressed most prior feedback, including usability nits (adding `--delete-integrated` to help output, simplifying error messages) and the upstream modification problem (via three-way merge). However, the **author-check mitigation**—proposed by Jasper Mayone to prevent false positives in forge workflows (e.g., "npm update" scenarios)—remains unimplemented and is the sole major technical gap. Jasper’s testing confirmed the series works as designed, but Junio’s new review raises a correctness issue that will need resolution before the fourth patch can proceed. The CLI design (`--delete-integrated` vs. `--delete-merged`) is finalized and explicitly approved by Junio, and the series is otherwise feature-complete. Open questions include whether to implement the author-check mitigation, how to handle Git attributes in the merge logic, and whether to document the squash-detection algorithm’s limitations more explicitly.
