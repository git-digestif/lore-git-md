This thread is about Harald Nordgren’s patch series (now at v4) that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes—rather than just their tips—have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows, even when upstream modified the same files. The series introduces a new `--delete-integrated` flag alongside the existing `--delete-merged` (which remains unchanged for strict tip-reachability). The CLI design is finalized and explicitly approved by Junio, with the two flags cleanly separated to avoid false positives: `--delete-merged` will never delete a branch whose changes were independently implemented by another author, while `--delete-integrated` opt-in handles rebased *and* squashed branches.

The series is now functionally complete and tested. Jasper Mayone’s recent review confirms the implementation works as documented across a variety of real-world scenarios, including renames, reverts, partial landings, and the "npm update" case. The output format (`Deleted branch topic (was 1a2b3c4, landed as 9f8e7d6)`) consistently identifies the correct landing commit, and the safety split between the two flags is effective. The first two patches (preparatory refactoring and a landing-message helper) are uncontroversial and ready for integration. The remaining two patches (the core `--delete-integrated` flag and squash-detection with upstream modifications) are technically sound but still await the author-check mitigation (Jasper’s proposal) to fully address forge workflows and manually applied patches. Minor usability nits (missing `--delete-integrated` in `git branch -h`, verbose error messages, and outdated flag names in the cover letter) are the only remaining blockers before the series can be merged.

Key participants include Harald (author), Junio (maintainer, who approved the CLI design), Jasper (who proposed the author-check mitigation and provided a tested review), D. Ben Knoble (who raised the "npm update" safety concern), and Phillip Wood (who reviewed algorithmic trade-offs). The open question remains whether to implement the author-check mitigation, patch IDs, or `git range-diff` for rebase detection—though Jasper’s testing shows the current implementation is already robust for most workflows. The config variable `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` applies to both flags, and the test suite now includes Junio’s false-positive case, Knoble’s "npm update" scenario, and coverage for upstream modifications.
