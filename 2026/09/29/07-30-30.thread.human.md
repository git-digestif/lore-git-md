Harald Nordgren's v10 series of five patches proposes extending `git branch` deletion to catch branches whose changes were integrated upstream via squash merges or rebases rather than tip-reachable commits. The CLI design is settled: `--delete-merged <pattern>` keeps its strict tip-reachability meaning, while the new opt-in `--delete-integrated <pattern>` performs change-based detection. Patch 4/5 handles rebase-merged branches with cheap tree-based comparison, and the final patch 5/5 adds squash-merge detection using a three-way `xdl_merge()` per changed path, deliberately ignoring `.gitattributes` merge drivers and rejecting binary or oversized files. Patch 3/5 also relaxes `--dry-run` so it works with plain `-d`/`-D`, a change Junio C Hamano has endorsed as a step in the right direction.

Reviewers have engaged substantively: Phillip Wood pressed on performance and the lack of rename detection, D. Ben Knoble and Jasper Mayone both flagged the "npm update" false-positive scenario where another author independently makes identical changes, and Jasper supplied a `Tested-by:` confirming the v10 build is clean and the tests pass, including the merge-driver and submodule edge cases. Junio has accepted the patch 3/5 direction but in his latest email frames a binary choice: either `--dry-run` is out of scope for this series entirely, or, since it is now accepted for deletion modes, users will expect operation-specific "not supported yet" errors for branch creation and renaming rather than generic incompatibility messages. The series remains under review with no integration status announced.

Still open are the config-key scope (whether `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` should govern `--delete-integrated` or a separate key is needed), the unimplemented author-check or patch-ID mitigation for false positives, and the broader question of whether universal `--dry-run` belongs in this series or a separate effort -- a point on which Harald and Junio now appear to agree it should be separate, pending Junio's confirmation.
