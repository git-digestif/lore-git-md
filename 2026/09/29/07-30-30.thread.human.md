Harald Nordgren has posted v9 of his four-patch series extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes were integrated via squash merges or rebases, now offered under the separate `--delete-integrated` flag. The series remains under review with no integration status announced, though Junio C Hamano has blessed the v8-to-v9 diff fixing a gitlink-to-blob crash via a `base_mode` guard and regression test. In his latest reply on patch 1/4, Harald accepted Junio's concern that the extracted helper `branch_opted_out()` was too generically named for an operation tied specifically to the `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` config key, and renamed it to `opted_out_of_delete_merged()` to explicitly bind the helper to that operation -- resolving one of three resolution paths Junio had proposed while leaving open the question of whether patch 3/4's `--delete-integrated` should reuse this same config key or adopt a more generic `deleteDone` alternative.

Junio's substantive review of patch 3/4 remains the main open front: he questions whether `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` should govern `--delete-integrated`, whether `--dry-run` should function without a delete-mode flag to preview non-deletion errors like checked-out branches, and he has identified test-coverage gaps in `t/t3200-branch.sh` for `--dry-run` with the new flag, the opt-out config combined with `--delete-integrated`, and `--quiet` behavior. Earlier design concerns about the default semantics of the opt-out helper -- whether branches should be treated as precious absent explicit consent -- also remain unresolved. Meanwhile, the "npm update" false-positive scenario still lacks the author-check or patch-id mitigation that Jasper Mayone, D. Ben Knoble, and others have urged, and Phillip Wood's call for clearer documentation of limitations like missing rename detection is only partially addressed.
