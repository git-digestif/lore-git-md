Harald Nordgren has posted v9 of a four-patch series extending `git branch` to detect branches whose changes were integrated via squash merges or rebases rather than tip-reachability, introducing the `--delete-integrated` flag alongside the existing `--delete-merged`. The CLI design is settled and confirmed by Junio C Hamano, but the series remains under review with no integration status announced. V9 specifically incorporates a fix for a gitlink-to-blob type-confusion crash that Jasper Mayone identified in v8, adding a `base_mode` guard so `read_mmblob()` is not called on submodule commit OIDs, plus a regression test where both a branch and upstream independently replace a submodule with a regular file.

Junio's latest review of patch 4/4 acknowledges the new regression test but expresses mild disappointment that the author appears to be reacting to reviewer-discovered corner cases rather than proactively hunting for additional edge cases himself. Beyond this, substantive open questions remain: Junio has raised design concerns about the `branch_opted_out` helper in patch 1/4 regarding its naming and default semantics, questioned whether `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` is the right config key for `--delete-integrated` or whether a more generic key is preferable, and identified test-coverage gaps for `--dry-run` combined with the new flag. He also argues `--dry-run` should work without requiring a delete-mode flag.

The major unresolved technical gap is the "npm update" false-positive scenario, where rebase-detection might incorrectly consider an unrelated branch landed because upstream happened to make identical changes; reviewers including Jasper Mayone and D. Ben Knoble have proposed author-check mitigations or patch IDs, but these remain unimplemented. Phillip Wood's call for clearer documentation of limitations such as the lack of rename detection is only partially addressed. Patches 1/4 and 2/4 are considered uncontroversial and ready for integration pending Junio's naming feedback, while patches 3/4 and 4/4 continue to draw substantive review.
