Harald Nordgren has posted v10 of a five-patch series that extends `git branch --delete-merged` with a new opt-in flag, `--delete-integrated <pattern>`, which deletes branches whose changes -- but not necessarily their tips -- have been landed upstream through squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows. The CLI design, which separates strict tip-reachability (`--delete-merged`, unchanged) from change-based detection (`--delete-integrated`), was confirmed by Junio C Hamano earlier in the thread. Patch 4/5 implemented rebase detection using purely tree-based comparison of changed paths, blob OIDs, and modes against upstream history, keeping the operation cheap in partial clones; patch 5/5, the final email in the series, adds squash-merge detection via a two-pass check that falls back to a plain three-way text merge using `xdl_merge()` when upstream contains a modified version of a changed file rather than the branch's exact blob. That merge deliberately ignores `.gitattributes` merge drivers and rejects binary or oversized files, preventing both false positives and arbitrary command execution, at the cost of potentially downloading blobs in partial clones. The series also relaxes `--dry-run` to work with plain `-d`/`-D` (patch 3/5), extends `delete_branches()` to consult a landed-commits map (patch 2/5), and extracts the `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` config opt-out into its own helper (patch 1/5), with roughly 440 lines of new tests in `t/t3200-branch.sh`.

The series remains under review with no integration status announced. Substantive reviewers include Phillip Wood, who pressed on performance, algorithmic trade-offs, and clearer documentation of limitations such as the lack of rename detection; Jasper Mayone, who proposed an author-check mitigation and raised usability nits; and D. Ben Knoble, who reinforced the safety concern. The most significant open gap is the "npm update" false-positive scenario, where another author independently makes identical changes and tree-based detection would wrongly treat the branch as landed; mitigations such as author checks or patch IDs have been proposed but not implemented. Junio's question about whether `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` is the right config key for the new flag, versus something more future-proof like `deleteDone`, also remains unanswered in v10.
