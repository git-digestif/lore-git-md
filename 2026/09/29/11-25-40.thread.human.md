This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s existing SHA-1 collision-detection implementation (`sha1dc`) with a new, faster `sha1dc-accel` subsystem. The goal is to reduce the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), delivering a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and cutting `index-pack` runtime by about half.

The series has been under review and is now effectively ready for integration. Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of large tables in `ubc_check.c`—but both have been addressed. Schindelin later provided a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend, further improving performance, and now explicitly endorses merging the series. Junio C Hamano had raised a policy blocker around licensing and provenance of the AI-generated code, but this has been fully resolved: Sam Reis (the original Rust author) endorsed the port, and Junio confirmed that Git’s GPL-2-only stance is compatible with incorporating MIT-licensed code like `sha1dc-accel`, as long as the original license and copyright notices are preserved. The last remaining question—whether Reis’s endorsement covers the *specific C implementation* or just the *idea* of porting—has now been rendered moot by Junio’s licensing clarification, which removes the final policy hurdle.

The series is now technically and legally cleared for integration, pending minor adjustments like resolving the `compress_portable()` duplication or deciding whether to fold in Schindelin’s optimization patch. Key participants include Scott Chacon (author), Johannes Schindelin (reviewer and optimizer), Junio C Hamano (maintainer), and Sam Reis (original Rust author), with supportive input from Sebastian Thiel (gitoxide maintainer) and policy clarifications from Todd Zullinger and D. Ben Knoble. The main open questions are now practical: how to handle the remaining code cleanup and whether to include Schindelin’s Perl script for table regeneration.
