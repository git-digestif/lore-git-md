This thread is about Scott Chacon’s four-patch series introducing `sha1dc-accel`, a vectorized and hardware-accelerated replacement for Git’s existing SHA-1 collision-detection code. The goal is to cut the collision-detection overhead from 1.5–2.5× down to 0.2× relative to OpenSSL SHA-1, yielding a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and roughly halving `index-pack` runtime. The series is still at v1 and under active review.

Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the portable compression function and maintainability of the large UBC-check tables—but has since shifted to a clear supportive stance after providing a Perl script to regenerate the tables and a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that closes most of the remaining performance gap. Sebastian Thiel (gitoxide) and Sam Reis (original Rust `sha1dc` crate author) have both endorsed the effort, with Reis explicitly stating he is “very happy to see Scott’s patch here land.”

Junio C Hamano previously blocked the series on licensing and provenance grounds, but those concerns are now addressed in principle by Reis’s confirmation that the UBC tables are autogenerated from the Stevens-Shumow paper and by Chacon’s policy proposal aligning with Software Freedom Conservancy guidelines. The only remaining policy question is whether the new `sha1dc-accel` code should retain the MIT license (matching the existing `sha1dc` code) or be relicensed to GPL-2 to align with Git’s packaging practices; D. Ben Knoble raised this point, and it remains unresolved. In the latest email Junio asks Reis for explicit clarification: does his endorsement cover the *idea* of porting the Rust work to Git, or the *specific C implementation* Chacon submitted? The answer will determine whether the series can move forward or needs further revision.
