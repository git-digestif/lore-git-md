This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s existing SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, hardware-accelerated version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× compared to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), speeding up SHA-1 operations by 2.7–2.85× and halving the runtime of `index-pack`.

The series has cleared all substantive technical and policy hurdles. Johannes Schindelin initially raised concerns about code duplication and maintainability of large autogenerated tables, but these were addressed—Schindelin even contributed an optimization patch for the x86-64 backend that further improved performance. Junio Hamano’s licensing questions were resolved after Scott confirmed the provenance of the AI-generated code, Sam Reis (the original Rust author) endorsed the port, and Todd Zullinger clarified how distributions like Fedora handle Git’s mixed-license components. The series is now technically and legally ready for integration, with only minor cleanup left (e.g., resolving a redundant function in patch 1 or folding in Schindelin’s optimization).

Key participants include Scott (the author), Schindelin (who shifted from critic to supporter after his concerns were addressed), Junio (who greenlit the licensing), and Todd Zullinger (who detailed how distributions manage Git’s licenses). The latest exchange between D. Ben Knoble and Zullinger reaffirmed that Git’s GPL-2-only stance doesn’t block MIT-licensed code like `sha1dc-accel`, as long as the original notices are preserved. The only remaining open questions are logistical: whether to include Schindelin’s Perl script for regenerating tables and how to handle his optimization patch. Real-world testing on non-Apple ARM hardware (e.g., Graviton) could provide additional validation, but the series is otherwise poised for merging.
