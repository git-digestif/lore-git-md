This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, hardware-accelerated version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to reduce the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), delivering a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and cutting `index-pack` runtime by about half.

The series has cleared all substantive technical and policy hurdles. Johannes Schindelin initially raised concerns about code duplication and maintainability of large autogenerated tables, but these were addressed with a Perl regeneration script and a follow-up optimization patch that further improved performance. Junio Hamano had a policy question about the licensing of AI-generated code, but this was resolved after confirmation that the original Rust author (Sam Reis) endorsed the port, and Junio clarified that Git’s GPL-2-only stance is compatible with MIT-licensed components like `sha1dc` and `sha1dc-accel`. The series is now technically and legally ready for integration, with only minor cleanup left—resolving a small code duplication in patch 1 and deciding whether to fold in Schindelin’s optimization patch.

The latest exchange between D. Ben Knoble and Todd Zullinger clarified how distributions handle Git’s mixed licensing. Fedora lists all involved licenses (BSD, GPL, LGPL, MIT), while Gentoo tags the package as GPL-2-only, reflecting the license of the built output rather than the source. This aligns with Junio’s earlier confirmation and reinforces that the series can proceed without licensing issues. The remaining open questions are purely practical: whether to include Schindelin’s Perl script for table regeneration, and whether his optimization patch should be part of this series or a follow-up.
