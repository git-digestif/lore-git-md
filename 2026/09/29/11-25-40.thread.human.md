This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, vectorized version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance penalty of collision checks from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL SHA-1, which should speed up `index-pack` by about 50 %. The series is at its first version and under active review.

Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the portable compression function and maintainability of the large UBC-check tables—but both have since been addressed. He later contributed an optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that closed most of the remaining gap between the C and Rust implementations, and he now explicitly supports merging the series. Junio C Hamano had a policy blocker around licensing and provenance of the AI-generated code, but that was resolved after Sam Reis (the original Rust author) endorsed the work and Junio confirmed that MIT-licensed code can be included in Git’s GPL-2-only codebase as long as the original notices are preserved. The only remaining question is whether Reis’s endorsement covers the specific C implementation or just the idea of porting the Rust code; Junio is waiting for clarification on that point.

Todd Zullinger recently chimed in to explain how Fedora packages Git’s mixed-licensed components—listing every license (BSD, GPL-2-only, GPL-2-or-later, LGPL, and MIT) rather than reducing them to a single “effective” license. This confirms that the licensing setup is already workable in practice, leaving the provenance question as the last hurdle before the series can move forward.
