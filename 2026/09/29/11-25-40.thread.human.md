This thread is about a patch series introducing `sha1dc-accel`, a faster SHA-1 collision-detection subsystem for Git. The series replaces the existing `sha1dc` implementation with vectorized (SSE2, AVX2, NEON) and hardware-accelerated (x86-64 SHA-NI, ARMv8 SHA-1) versions, aiming to reduce collision-detection overhead from 1.5–2.5× down to 0.2× relative to OpenSSL SHA-1. This delivers a 2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and a ~50% reduction in `index-pack` runtime.

The series is currently under review and remains at its initial v1 submission. Johannes Schindelin initially raised two substantive technical objections—code duplication in patch 1 and maintainability concerns about the large `ubc_check.c` tables—but later provided a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend, closing most of the performance gap between the C and Rust implementations. He now explicitly endorses merging the series. Junio C Hamano, however, raised a policy blocker regarding the licensing and provenance of the AI-generated C code, requiring assurance that it complies with Git’s AI policy and the legal vetting performed by Software Freedom Conservancy lawyers.

Scott Chacon has now addressed Junio’s blocker by confirming that the original Rust author (Sam Reis) is aware of and supportive of the port, and that both the Rust and C implementations were produced using AI tooling within the Conservancy’s updated guidelines. He has also submitted a proposed update to Git’s AI policy to align with those guidelines. While this resolves the policy concern in principle, no formal legal sign-off or maintainer Ack has been recorded yet. The series is still under review, with the licensing question now the sole remaining gate before technical evaluation can proceed. No Reviewed-by or Acked-by trailers have been added so far.
