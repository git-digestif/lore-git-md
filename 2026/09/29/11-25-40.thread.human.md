This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, vectorized version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1, speeding up `index-pack` by about 50%. The series is now at v1 and under active review, with most technical and policy hurdles resolved.

Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication and maintainability of large tables—but both are now addressed. He later contributed a tested optimization patch that nearly closes the performance gap between the C and Rust implementations, and he now explicitly supports merging the series. Junio C Hamano’s earlier policy questions about AI-generated code provenance were settled after Sam Reis, the original Rust author, confirmed his awareness and support for the port. Sebastian Thiel, the gitoxide maintainer, also endorsed the effort, citing real-world performance benefits.

The only remaining open question is a licensing one raised by D. Ben Knoble: whether the new `sha1dc-accel` code should keep its MIT license (matching the existing `sha1dc` code) or be relicensed to GPL-2 to align with Git’s broader licensing stance. Sam Reis weighed in to argue that MIT is GPL-compatible and that proper attribution should suffice, but no formal decision has been made. The series is otherwise ready for integration, pending a maintainer ruling on this licensing point.
