This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s existing `sha1dc` SHA-1 collision-detection code with a new `sha1dc-accel` subsystem. The goal is to use vectorized (SSE2, AVX2, NEON) and hardware-accelerated (x86-64 SHA-NI, ARMv8 SHA-1) implementations to cut collision-detection overhead from 1.5–2.5× down to 0.2× relative to OpenSSL SHA-1, delivering a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and roughly halving `index-pack` runtime.

The series is still under review at v1, but the technical objections have largely been addressed. Johannes Schindelin initially raised two concerns—code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of the large `ubc_check.c` tables—but both are now resolved: the tables are regenerated by a Perl script he provided, and he later contributed a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that nearly closes the performance gap with the Rust implementation. Schindelin now explicitly endorses merging the series. Junio C Hamano’s policy blocker about the licensing and provenance of the AI-generated C code has also been addressed in principle by Scott’s confirmation that the original Rust author is supportive and that the work aligns with the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated AI guidelines, though no formal legal sign-off has been recorded yet.

The only remaining open questions are minor: whether to fold Schindelin’s optimization patch into the series or submit it separately, whether to include his Perl script in the patch set, and build-system integration details across platforms. Sebastian Thiel, the maintainer of gitoxide, has added a supportive endorsement, confirming the real-world impact of the slower `sha1dc` implementation on gitoxide and welcoming the optimizations. With no new objections and strong cross-project alignment, the series appears technically ready for integration once Junio records a final position on the licensing and provenance questions.
