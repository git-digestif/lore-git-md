This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, hardware-accelerated version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance penalty of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1, which lacks collision checks, yielding a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and roughly halving `index-pack` runtime.

The series is now at v1 and under active review. Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of large lookup tables—but both have been addressed: the tables are now regenerated by a Perl script he provided, and he has since contributed a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that closes most of the remaining performance gap with the Rust implementation. Schindelin now explicitly endorses merging the series. Junio C Hamano previously blocked progress on licensing and provenance grounds, but those concerns are now resolved in principle: Scott confirmed the original Rust author (Sam Reis) is aware of and supports the port, and both implementations were produced using AI tooling within the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidelines. Sam Reis himself has now weighed in with a brief but clear endorsement, removing any lingering doubt about his stance.

The main open questions are now logistical: whether to fold Schindelin’s optimization patch into the series or submit it separately, whether to include his Perl script in the patch set, and ensuring the build system works correctly across all platforms. The code duplication in the first patch still needs to be resolved, but the technical path forward is clear. With licensing and provenance settled and strong support from Schindelin, Reis, and gitoxide maintainer Sebastian Thiel, the series appears ready for maintainer consideration once the remaining integration details are ironed out.
