This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection code with a faster, vectorized implementation called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance penalty of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× compared to OpenSSL’s SHA-1, which should speed up `index-pack` by about 50 %. The series is still at its first version and under review.

Johannes Schindelin has been the most active reviewer. He initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of the large lookup tables—but later provided a Perl script to regenerate those tables and a small optimization patch that closes most of the remaining performance gap. He now supports merging the series. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has so far only acknowledged Schindelin’s help without taking a position on the technical or policy questions. The main blocker is still the licensing and provenance of the AI-generated C code; Scott has addressed this in principle, but Junio has not yet recorded formal approval. Other open questions include build-system correctness across platforms and whether to fold Schindelin’s script and optimization into the series or handle them as follow-ups.
