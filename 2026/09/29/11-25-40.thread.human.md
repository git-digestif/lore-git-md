This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, hardware-accelerated version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to reduce the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), yielding a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and cutting `index-pack` runtime by about half. The series is now technically and legally ready for integration after resolving all substantive objections.

Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns: code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of the large `ubc_check.c` tables. Both were addressed—Schindelin even provided a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that further reduced `index-pack` runtime by 1.8%, leaving the C implementation only ~1% slower than the Rust version. He now explicitly endorses merging the series. Junio C Hamano had a policy blocker around licensing and provenance of the AI-generated code, but this was fully resolved after Scott Chacon confirmed the original Rust author’s support, and Junio clarified that Git’s GPL-2-only stance is compatible with MIT-licensed components as long as notices are preserved. Todd Zullinger and D. Ben Knoble later confirmed that distributions like Fedora and Gentoo already handle mixed-license components transparently.

The remaining loose ends are minor: resolving the `compress_portable()` duplication, deciding whether to include Schindelin’s Perl script for table regeneration, and whether to fold his optimization patch into the series or submit it separately. The series touches the new `sha1dc-accel/` directory, build system files, and maintains identical collision-detection behavior—only performance improves. Key supporters include Schindelin, Sebastian Thiel (gitoxide maintainer), and Sam Reis (original Rust author). No further objections are outstanding, and the series is poised for integration once the last technical adjustments are made.
