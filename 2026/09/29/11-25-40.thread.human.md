This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, vectorized version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), delivering a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and roughly halving the runtime of `index-pack`. The series is at its first version and under active review.

The main technical objections have been resolved. Johannes Schindelin initially raised concerns about code duplication and maintainability of large lookup tables, but those were addressed by a Perl script he provided to regenerate the tables. He later contributed a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend, closing most of the performance gap between the C and Rust implementations and now explicitly endorses merging the series. The only remaining technical loose end is whether to fold Schindelin’s optimization into the original series or submit it separately.

The licensing and provenance questions that Junio C Hamano raised as a policy blocker are now effectively settled. Junio confirmed that Git’s GPL-2-only stance is compatible with incorporating MIT-licensed code like the existing `sha1dc` submodule and the new `sha1dc-accel`, as long as the original license and copyright notices are preserved. D. Ben Knoble’s follow-up confirmed acceptance of this resolution. The sole remaining hurdle is Junio’s request for explicit clarification from Sam Reis, the original Rust author, on whether his endorsement covers the *idea* of porting the work or the *specific C implementation* submitted by Chacon—this distinction is critical for finalizing the provenance record. Once that clarification arrives, the series appears ready to proceed.
