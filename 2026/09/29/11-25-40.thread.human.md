This thread is about a patch series from Scott Chacon that replaces Git’s SHA-1 collision-detection implementation with a faster, hardware-accelerated version called `sha1dc-accel`. The goal is to cut the performance overhead of collision detection from 1.5–2.5× down to just 0.2× relative to OpenSSL’s SHA-1 (which lacks collision detection), delivering a 2.7–2.85× speedup in SHA-1 throughput and roughly halving the runtime of `index-pack`.

The series is now in its first version and under active review. Johannes Schindelin initially raised two technical concerns—code duplication in the first patch and maintainability of large lookup tables—but both have since been addressed, the latter by a Perl script he provided to regenerate the tables. Schindelin has also contributed a tested optimization patch for the x86-64 SHA-NI backend that closes most of the remaining performance gap between the C and Rust implementations, leaving the C version only about 1 % slower than Rust. He now explicitly supports merging the series.

The only remaining blocker is a licensing and provenance question: Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has asked for explicit clarification from Sam Reis (the original Rust `sha1dc` crate author) on whether his endorsement covers the *idea* of porting the work to Git or the *specific C implementation* submitted by Chacon. This distinction matters for Git’s policy on AI-generated code and its GPL-2-only licensing stance. All other policy concerns—including compatibility between Git’s GPL-2 and the MIT-licensed `sha1dc` code—have been resolved. The series is otherwise ready to proceed once Reis’s clarification is received.
