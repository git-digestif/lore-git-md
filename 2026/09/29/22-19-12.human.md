This is a substantive review of Hanan Arshad's RFC for sharing Git stashes through remotes. Brian M. Carlson engages directly with the proposal's core design questions, offering both critique and suggestions.

The review focuses on the practical challenges of standardizing a stash-sharing workflow. Carlson agrees that publishing stashes (operation 1) is useful but argues that the other operations—listing, importing, and removing shared stashes—face significant hurdles due to the lack of a standardized ref namespace. He notes that users in different environments (e.g., personal vs. shared remotes) will need different naming conventions, making it difficult to design a one-size-fits-all solution. For example, he points out that listing shared stashes (operation 2) would require downloading data to verify whether a ref is actually a stash, which could be inefficient, especially since stashes can be large (containing untracked files or historical references).

Carlson’s feedback is pragmatic and grounded in real-world usage. He suggests that user-provided names (rather than object-derived identifiers) would be more intuitive, aligning with Git’s existing conventions for branches and tags. He also recommends that listing should avoid fetching export commits to keep the operation lightweight. For command naming, he proposes `git stash push` as a natural extension of the existing `git stash` interface.

While Carlson supports the goal of improving stash-sharing tooling, his review highlights the tension between flexibility and standardization. The proposal is well-motivated and leverages existing plumbing, but the design questions—particularly around ref namespacing and listing behavior—will need careful resolution to avoid usability or performance pitfalls. This thread is still in the early stages, and Carlson’s input suggests that the implementation may need to accommodate diverse workflows rather than enforce rigid conventions.
