This thread is about a bugfix patch from Ariel Keselman that addresses a minor but real issue in Git's refs/files backend. When deleting root refs (like `HEAD` or `refs/heads/main`), the code was unnecessarily taking a lock on `packed-refs`, which can cause spurious errors in linked worktrees where the shared metadata directory is read-only. The fix is simple: skip the lock when the ref being deleted is a root ref, since those can't be packed anyway.

Ariel's patch includes seven new regression tests that verify the fix, and broader test suites (ref, worktree, and sequencer) also pass, suggesting no unintended side effects. The change is narrowly scoped to `files_delete_refs()` in `refs/files-backend.c` and is motivated by a real-world scenario. The author used AI assistance to generate the patch, tests, and commit message, but the contribution itself is well-scoped and technically sound. There's no controversy or discussion yet—this appears to be a straightforward fix that should be uncontroversial.
