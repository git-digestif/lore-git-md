This is a substantive review of the final patch in the `git last-modified` Bloom filter optimization series. Patrick Steinhardt questions the fragility of the current approach—restoring `bloom_filter_settings` in the revision machinery after `prepare_revision_walk()` clears it—and proposes an alternative: storing the settings in `struct last_modified` instead of forcing them into the revision subsystem. His patch on top of the series moves the settings to the `last_modified` struct, updates all references, and removes the post-walk restoration, while still passing all tests.

The core concern is maintainability: the current design relies on a side effect (restoring settings after they’re cleared) that might break if the revision machinery evolves. Patrick’s alternative is cleaner—it isolates the Bloom filter state within `last_modified`—but he acknowledges there could be reasons the original approach was chosen. The change is minimal (12 lines) and doesn’t alter behavior, only where the settings are stored.

This feedback doesn’t block the series but suggests a design improvement. The author will need to evaluate whether the revision machinery *must* have the settings (e.g., for other callbacks) or if Patrick’s approach is viable. The review is narrowly scoped to code structure, not correctness or performance, and the proposed fix is straightforward. If adopted, it would make the patch more robust without changing its observable effects.
