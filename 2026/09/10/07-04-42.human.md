This is the final patch in Toon Claes’s v4 series optimizing `git last-modified` performance by reusing Bloom filters. Patrick Steinhardt raises a maintainability concern about the current approach: the patch restores `bloom_filter_settings` in the revision machinery after `prepare_revision_walk()` clears it, which feels fragile. He suggests an alternative design where `last-modified` manages its own Bloom filter settings pointer instead of forcing it into the revision machinery, and provides a working patch that passes all tests.

The proposed change is a refactoring of the same functionality: store the settings in `struct last_modified` rather than in `struct rev_info`. This avoids the need to restore the settings after `prepare_revision_walk()` and keeps the Bloom filter logic self-contained within the `last-modified` builtin. The patch is small (12 lines changed) and mechanical, touching only `builtin/last-modified.c`. It does not alter behavior or performance, only the internal organization.

Patrick’s feedback is substantive but not a correctness issue; the current implementation works, but his alternative is cleaner and less surprising. The author will need to decide whether to adopt this refactoring or keep the current approach. Either way, the series remains ready for integration once this design choice is resolved.
