This thread is about a two-patch series that fixes resource cleanup in Git’s low-level merge driver infrastructure when interrupted by signals like SIGINT (Ctrl+C). The series, originally authored by Michal Koutný and submitted as v3 by Jeff King (Peff), ensures temporary files (`.merge_file_*`) are reliably removed even if the merge process is abruptly terminated. The solution replaces custom signal handling with Git’s `tempfile` API for atomic, signal-safe cleanup, while also modernizing adjacent code—for example, replacing manual buffer management with `strbuf` and addressing latent robustness issues in file operations.

The series is now **ready for merging** after incorporating all prior feedback. v3 includes a stylistic improvement to split a combined error check in `create_temp()` into two separate `die_errno()` calls for clarity, a commit-message tweak to use `Reported-by:` trailers for Jean Delvare and Michal Koutný, and a type-safety fix for oversized files in `strbuf_detach()`. The core logic remains unchanged from v2, which received substantive approval from Elijah Newren. Junio C Hamano has explicitly approved the design and implementation, noting the backward-compatibility trade-off (preserving relative paths for shell metacharacters) as well-crafted, and plans to queue the series in `next`. No substantive discussion remains, and the series is uncontroversial, aligning with Git’s established patterns for resource cleanup.

Key participants include Peff, who drove the series through v2 and v3, addressing edge cases and cleanups; Michal Koutný, the original author, who tested the interrupt scenarios and agreed automated tests were unnecessary; and Elijah Newren, who provided substantive review, including identifying and proposing a fix for a latent type-safety bug.
