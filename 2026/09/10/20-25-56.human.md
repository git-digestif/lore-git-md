This is a brief but substantive reply from Junio in the ongoing discussion about whether Git's advice-scope hints should default to `--global` or allow `--local` (or other) scopes.

Junio acknowledges that he did not contribute to the philosophical design of the `advice.*` config system when it was introduced in 2009, and that the original "use `git config advice.foo false` to disable" instruction was likely intended as a general guideline rather than a cut-and-paste-ready command. This aligns with Jeff King's historical argument that the system was designed with global user preferences in mind, not per-repository customization.

The email does not introduce new technical details or take a firm stance on the boolean `is_global_hint` simplification proposed in Vsevolod Myalitsin's v4 series. Instead, it reinforces the idea that the current design (and its lack of scope hints) was never deeply considered at a philosophical level. This leaves the door open for either the simplified boolean approach (which defaults all advice to `--global` except where explicitly overridden) or a more flexible mechanism that could support `--local` or other scopes in the future. The thread remains unresolved, with no clear maintainer decision yet.
