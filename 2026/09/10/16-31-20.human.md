This is a short follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the advice subsystem refactoring. Jeff King (Peff) and Junio C Hamano briefly exchange views on whether the turn-off instructions shown by `advise_if_enabled()` might feel visually intrusive to users. Peff acknowledges Junio’s point that users who dislike the instructions can simply disable the advice, but notes that the instructions themselves might *increase* the desire to turn them off. Junio reiterates the project’s design principle: advice messages should either be *always* beneficial (shown unconditionally via `advise()`) or *so rarely triggered* that turn-off instructions are unnecessary (also favoring `advise()`). For cases in between, `advise_if_enabled()` remains appropriate. Peff agrees with the principle but wonders if any current advice items truly belong in the "always beneficial" category, suggesting that real-world user feedback would be the best test. The exchange effectively closes the design discussion for now, leaving the refactoring patch’s approach intact. The only remaining technical question for the patch is the mechanical completeness of the Coccinelle rule, which missed some call sites in `commit.c`.
