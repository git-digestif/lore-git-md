This is a short follow-up in the ongoing advice subsystem refactoring thread. Jeff King (Peff) and Junio C Hamano briefly revisit the design question of whether the turn-off instructions shown by `advise_if_enabled()` are overly verbose or visually intrusive. Peff acknowledges Junio’s earlier point that users who find the instructions unnecessary can simply disable the advice, but notes that the instructions themselves might increase the desire to do so. He then restates Junio’s broader design principle: advice messages should either be *always* beneficial (shown unconditionally via `advise()`) or *so rarely triggered* that turn-off instructions are unnecessary (also favoring `advise()`). For intermediate cases, `advise_if_enabled()` remains appropriate. Peff agrees with the principle but suggests that real-world user feedback would be the best way to determine whether any advice items currently fall into the "always beneficial" category. The exchange effectively closes the design discussion for now, leaving the refactoring patch’s approach intact. The only remaining technical question for the patch is the mechanical completeness issue (missed call sites in `commit.c`), which Peff previously identified.
