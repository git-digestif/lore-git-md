This is part of Patrick Steinhardt’s ODB abstraction series, specifically patch 7/9, which implements the core change: writing alternates during ODB creation using Git’s lockfile API. The patch is a well-executed refactoring that resolves earlier security concerns about TOCTOU races and concurrent access, and it directly advances the series’ goal of consolidating alternates handling into the ODB backend.

Karthik Nayak’s review is substantive but narrowly focused on error handling in the new alternates-writing logic. He raises two specific concerns: first, that `ferror()` checks should occur inside the loop where `fprintf()` is called (rather than after the loop), to catch write errors immediately; second, that backends should formalize error reporting for cases where they cannot handle alternates (e.g., a backend that doesn’t support alternates at all). The first point is a clear correctness issue—delayed error detection could lead to partial writes or silent failures. The second is a forward-looking design question about the ODB interface’s clarity and extensibility, though it doesn’t block this patch.

The review does not challenge the patch’s core design (lockfile adoption, deduplication, or the reprepare step) or its integration with the rest of the series. The error-handling feedback is actionable but minor, and the patch remains a strong candidate for integration. If Patrick addresses the `ferror()` placement, the patch will be even more robust. The broader question about backend error reporting may resurface in future ODB abstraction work but isn’t urgent here.
