This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing advice-scope-hint thread. Junio C Hamano responds to Jeff King’s argument that all advice settings should default to `--global` because they are about silencing chatty help for a *user* rather than a specific repository.

Junio acknowledges the merit of Peff’s position but introduces a nuanced counterpoint: while advice messages are often user-specific, they can also be workflow-dependent. A user working on multiple projects with different workflows might want to silence certain advice messages *per project* rather than globally. For example, a developer who frequently works on both Git’s own codebase (where detached HEAD advice might be noise) and a client project (where it is useful) could benefit from repo-local advice settings. This suggests that `--local` (or even `--worktree`) could have legitimate use cases, even if they are rare.

Junio also concedes that the original design of customizable advice messages leaned more toward the workflow-dependent use case, but he now believes the user-centric argument ("I understand this advice and want it silenced everywhere") should have been prioritized. This shift in perspective aligns with Peff’s stance but stops short of fully endorsing a `--global`-only design. The email leaves the door open for a boolean `is_global_hint` (as proposed by Vsevolod Myalitsin) while acknowledging that the mechanism might need to support other scopes in the future if real-world use cases emerge.

The reply does not resolve the design debate but clarifies the trade-offs: simplicity (a boolean) versus flexibility (supporting multiple scopes). Junio’s tone suggests he is leaning toward the simpler design but wants to see concrete examples of non-global advice settings before committing to it. The series remains under review, with no indication yet of whether Junio will pick it up for integration.
