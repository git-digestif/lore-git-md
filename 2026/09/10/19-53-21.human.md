This is the cover letter for Patrick Steinhardt’s 9-patch series refactoring ODB alternates handling, now in its fifth iteration. The series is part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort and is ready for integration, with all substantive review feedback addressed in v5. Karthik Nayak’s reply here is a brief, surface-level observation: he notes that Patrick overlooked one of his earlier comments (a question about error handling in patch 7/9) but otherwise approves the series as "looks good."

The missed comment refers to Karthik’s substantive review of patch 7/9 in v4, where he raised two technical points about the alternates-writing logic: (1) whether `ferror()` should be checked after each `fprintf()` call rather than once at the end, to avoid partial writes, and (2) whether ODB backends should formalize error codes to distinguish "alternates not supported" from other failures. Patrick’s v5 cover letter does not mention these points, and Karthik’s follow-up here suggests they remain unaddressed. However, Patrick has previously indicated that these concerns do not block the series, as the lockfile mechanism ensures safety and the error handling is deemed sufficient for now.

The series itself is uncontroversial and well-documented, with v5 incorporating minor naming and clarity improvements (e.g., renaming `add_one_alternate` to `collect_one_alternate`, converting a boolean-like `int` to `bool`, and clarifying commit messages). The core design—consolidating alternates handling into ODB creation via `odb_create_on_disk_options` and removing the `write_alternates()` callback—remains unchanged. The lockfile API adoption in patch 7/9 resolves the primary security concern from earlier versions, and the latent bug fix for worktree alternates handling (patch 6/9) adds robustness.

For a busy reader, this series is a low-risk, high-value refactoring that simplifies the ODB interface and aligns with the broader abstraction goals. The only open question is whether the error handling in patch 7/9 will see follow-up work, but this does not affect the series’ readiness. Karthik’s approval, while surface-level, confirms the series is in good shape.
