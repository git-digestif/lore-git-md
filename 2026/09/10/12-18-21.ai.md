This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing advice subsystem refactoring thread. Junio C Hamano responds to Jeff King’s concern about whether the turn-off instructions shown by `advise_if_enabled()` would be perceived as visually intrusive. Junio suggests that if users find the instructions unnecessary, they can simply disable the advice—implying the instructions serve their purpose. He then outlines a broader design principle: advice messages should either be *always* beneficial (and thus shown unconditionally via `advise()`) or *so rarely triggered* that turn-off instructions are unnecessary (also favoring `advise()`). For cases in between, `advise_if_enabled()` remains appropriate. This effectively closes the design discussion for now, reinforcing the existing pattern without requiring immediate changes to the refactoring patch. The mechanical completeness issue (missed call sites in `commit.c`) remains the only open technical question for the patch.
