This is a **substantive review reply** in the ongoing advice-scope-hint thread, specifically addressing Junio’s tangential documentation patch and the broader design question of which config scopes (`--global`, `--local`, `--worktree`, `--system`) the advice-scope-hint mechanism should support.

Jeff King (Peff) begins by admitting he was unaware of the `--worktree` config scope until this thread, underscoring how obscure these options are even to experienced contributors. He reiterates his skepticism about the practical need for `--worktree` or `--system` in advice settings, arguing that `--global` is the only scope that makes sense for advice messages: they are about silencing chatty help for a *user*, not for a specific repository, so repo-local or worktree-specific advice settings are unlikely to be useful. This directly challenges the design complexity introduced by Vsevolod’s original `scope_hint` enum, which attempted to handle all `CONFIG_SCOPE` values.

Peff then proposes a minimal documentation patch to clarify the `<file-option>` placeholder in `git-config.txt`, grouping `--system`, `--global`, `--local`, `--worktree`, and `--file <filename>` under a single term. His patch is more concise than Junio’s and avoids the awkward pluralization (`<file-options>`) that Junio introduced. Peff also notes that `--blob` is a special case (a "location" option, not a "file" option) and suggests that the man page could benefit from a larger rewrite to organize options by command mode, though he does not attempt that here.

The email advances the thread by sharpening the design debate: Peff’s position that *all* advice settings should default to `--global` (rather than just `defaultBranchName`) simplifies the mechanism to a boolean (`is_global_hint`), which would resolve the build failures and eliminate the need to handle unhandled `CONFIG_SCOPE` values. This aligns with Vsevolod’s later proposal to replace the `scope_hint` enum with a boolean, though Peff has not yet responded to that specific patch. The documentation patch itself is a side discussion but reinforces the point that the scope options are poorly understood, making a strong case for a simpler design.
