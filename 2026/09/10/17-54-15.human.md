This is the second patch in Vsevolod Myalitsin’s three-part series that adds a scope hint to Git’s advice messages, helping users disable them with the correct config scope (e.g., `--global` instead of `--local`). The series is narrowly focused on the advice subsystem in `advice.c` and does not change any existing behavior—only the accuracy of the disablement instructions shown to users.

In this reply, Vsevolod responds to Jeff King’s question about whether `--system` scope is ever needed for advice settings. He agrees with Peff’s skepticism and proposes simplifying the patch by replacing the `enum config_scope scope_hint` field with a simple boolean `is_global_hint`. This would limit the hint to either `--global` or the default (local) scope, avoiding the complexity of handling `--system`, `--worktree`, or other rarely relevant scopes. The change reflects the practical reality that most advice settings are either repository-specific (local) or user-wide (global), and that `--system` is unlikely to be useful in this context.

The series is otherwise well-motivated and has addressed earlier feedback, but this simplification could reduce implementation complexity and sidestep the unresolved build failure around unhandled `CONFIG_SCOPE` values. The next revision will likely adopt this boolean approach unless Junio or others object.
