This is a bugfix patch targeting the low-level merge driver infrastructure.

Michal Koutný addresses a long-standing cleanup issue: when a user interrupts a long-running external merge driver with Ctrl+C, the temporary `.merge_file_*` files are left behind because the signal kills the entire process group, preventing Git’s normal cleanup routines from running. The patch borrows a signal-handling idiom already used in `editor.c`—detecting whether the child process died from SIGINT or SIGQUIT and re-raising the signal after performing cleanup.

The change is small (16 insertions, 3 deletions) and focused: it wraps the external driver invocation with `sigchain_push`/`sigchain_pop` to temporarily ignore SIGINT/SIGQUIT, checks the child’s exit status for signal-induced termination, and explicitly removes the temporary files before re-raising the signal. The commit message clearly explains the problem, cites the prior art, and includes a Reported-by trailer crediting Jean Delvare.

This looks like a well-motivated, low-risk fix that should be uncontroversial. It’s a standalone patch, not part of a larger series.
