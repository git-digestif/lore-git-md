This is the second patch in the v5 series adjusting when auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations (`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`). The patch implements the core behavior change: deferring auto maintenance until sequence completion for the merge backend, matching the apply backend’s behavior.

The patch modifies `builtin/rebase.c` and `builtin/revert.c` to call `run_auto_maintenance()` exactly once at sequence completion, rather than during intermediate steps. The sequencer itself cannot reliably trigger maintenance at a single exit point because single-pick cases (e.g., `cherry-pick <commit>` or `revert <commit>`) bypass `pick_commits()` entirely, exiting early through `continue_single_pick()` or `skip_single_pick()`. This design choice—delegating maintenance to the built-in commands—was previously contested but is now settled and explicitly documented in the commit message.

The tests in `t/t3418-rebase-continue.sh` and `t/t3510-cherry-pick-sequence.sh` verify that auto maintenance runs only at sequence end, not during intermediate steps. The rebase test now explicitly confirms it triggers a conflict (both topic and main branches add `F2`), and the failing exec ensures maintenance only runs at the very end. The cherry-pick tests cover both single-pick and sequence cases, including `--continue` and `--skip` scenarios.

The patch is technically sound and addresses all prior reviewer feedback. The commit message is concise and clearly explains the design rationale, and the tests are well-annotated to clarify edge cases. This version is likely to proceed to `next` unless new objections arise. The unresolved hypothetical about long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might improve performance remains noted but unaddressed, as no concrete issue has been identified.
