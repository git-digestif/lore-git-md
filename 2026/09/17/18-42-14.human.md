This is the third and final patch in Thomas Bachem’s v5 series adjusting when auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations (`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, `git revert`). The patch disables auto maintenance for all commands spawned by the sequencer—`git commit`, `git merge`, and exec commands—by passing `maintenance.auto=false` via `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS`. This ensures auto maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion, matching the apply backend’s behavior and preventing interference with intermediate steps.

The implementation is straightforward: a new `disable_auto_maintenance()` helper in `sequencer.c` builds and caches the config string on first use, appending it to the environment of every spawned command. The cached string is stored in the `replay_ctx` struct as a `const char *` to emphasize immutability, addressing Junio C Hamano’s feedback from earlier iterations. The helper’s format and purpose are now clearly documented in the header comment, resolving Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier concern about undocumented assumptions.

The tests in `t/t3418-rebase-continue.sh` and `t/t3510-cherry-pick-sequence.sh` have been updated to verify that `git maintenance run --auto` is executed exactly once at sequence end and not during intermediate steps. The rebase test now explicitly confirms the conflict scenario (both topic and main branches add `F2`) and the failing exec command, while the cherry-pick tests cover single-pick, sequence, `--continue`, and `--skip` scenarios. The test annotations clarify the sequence flow and conflict triggers, addressing Phillip Wood’s earlier concern about test coverage.

The patch is code-identical to v4; only commit messages, header comments, and test annotations have been updated. The series is now in `seen` after Junio signaled intent to queue it ("Will queue"), and the design controversy—whether to delegate maintenance to built-in commands or trigger it in the sequencer—has been settled in favor of the current approach. Thomas’s latest commit message explicitly documents why the sequencer cannot reliably trigger maintenance at a single exit point: single-pick cases (`cherry-pick <commit>`, `revert <commit>`, and their `--continue`/`--skip` variants) bypass `pick_commits()` entirely, exiting early through `continue_single_pick()` or `skip_single_pick()`.

The patch looks technically complete and well-motivated. The only unresolved hypothetical—a potential performance benefit from mid-sequence repacking in very long sequences—remains noted but unaddressed, as no concrete issue has been identified.
