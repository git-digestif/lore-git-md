This is a **documentation patch review** in the ongoing `gitmergeconflicts(7)` series. Patrick Steinhardt provides a **substantive review** of the first patch, engaging deeply with the content, structure, and technical accuracy of the new man page. His feedback is constructive and focused on improving clarity, consistency, and user comprehension, with several actionable suggestions.

Patrick’s review centers on three main themes: **terminology and jargon**, **technical completeness**, and **user experience**. He questions whether terms like "area" (instead of "hunk") or "ours/theirs" are sufficiently intuitive for users, and suggests rewording the "ours/theirs" explanation to clarify that `HEAD` always determines "ours" during a merge, regardless of the command (`git merge` vs. `git rebase`). This is a key point for avoiding confusion, as the current wording risks implying that `git rebase` inverts the meaning of "ours" and "theirs" in a way that might not be immediately clear to users. His proposed alternative is more precise and aligns with Git’s internal logic.

On technical completeness, Patrick raises two notable gaps. First, he suggests expanding the introduction to briefly explain how Git performs a 3-way merge, including the role of the common ancestor (base). While he acknowledges this might be too technical for some users, it’s a foundational concept that could help users understand why conflicts occur and how to resolve them. Second, he proposes making the commits involved in a conflict more accessible, such as by including `git rev-parse --reference` commands to let users inspect the conflicting versions directly. This would be a practical addition, though it might require careful implementation to avoid cluttering the guide.

Patrick also flags a few **surface-level issues**, such as a typo ("the the" -> "the") and a missing `linkgit:` macro for cross-referencing other man pages (e.g., `linkgit:git-merge[1]`). These are minor but important for consistency. He further suggests mentioning `git merge-tool --tool-help` as a way to list available merge tools, which would complement the existing reference to `merge.tool` in `git-config(1)`.

The review is **well-informed and user-centric**, reflecting Patrick’s deep familiarity with Git’s internals and his attention to how users interact with the tool. His feedback does not challenge the series’ goals but instead aims to refine the guide’s clarity and utility. Julia Evans is likely to incorporate most of these suggestions, particularly the rewording of "ours/theirs" and the addition of `linkgit:` macros, as they address potential sources of confusion without requiring major structural changes. The discussion about explaining 3-way merges and making commits more accessible may lead to further iteration, but these are enhancements rather than blockers. The patch remains on track for eventual inclusion, pending these refinements.
