This is a maintainer's review of a small feature patch that extends `git stash pop` with the same custom conflict-label options (`--label-ours`, `--label-theirs`, `--label-base`) already supported by `git stash apply`. Junio questions the fundamental motivation behind exposing these options in user-facing commands at all, not just the specific addition to `pop`.

The review is substantive: Junio traces the feature's origin to the underlying machinery created for `git checkout` (which uses stash internally during branch switching) and asks whether the user-facing exposure in `stash apply` was ever justified beyond debugging convenience. He challenges the patch's rationale by pointing out that the commit message does not explain why a user would routinely want to customize conflict markers when popping a stash, and suggests that the options might be better removed from `stash apply` rather than propagated to `pop`. The tone is skeptical but open-ended, inviting the author to articulate a real-world use case rather than relying on technical symmetry. No technical flaws in the implementation are raised; the concern is entirely about whether the feature belongs in the user interface.
