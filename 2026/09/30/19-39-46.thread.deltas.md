## 2026/09/30

- [2026/09/30/19-39-46 by Harald Nordgren via GitGitGadget <gitgitgadget@gmail.com>] Harald Nordgren posted a patch to make `@{p}` (in any case) resolve as a short form of `@{push}` in `object-name.c`, with documentation in `revisions.adoc` and a new test in `t/t1514-rev-parse-push.sh`, since `git log @{p}..` currently fails with "unknown revision" while `@{u}` works for `@{upstream}`.
- [2026/09/30/21-39-03 by D. Ben Knoble <ben.knoble@gmail.com>] D. Ben Knoble said he has often wanted this, noted there is no `@{pull}` so the push/pull ambiguity of a `p` alias does not apply, and wondered whether an older discussion explains why `@{u}` got a shorthand but `@{p}` did not.
- [2026/09/30/22-07-44 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano observed that letting `@{push}` take `@{p}` would block future `@{pull}` or other `p`-initial notations, but noted only two at_marks (upstream, push) have existed for a long time; digging history, he found `@{upstream}` (2010) had `@{u}` from inception while `@{push}` (2015) did not, and nobody questioned the asymmetry in the v3 review of the `@{push}` series.
- [2026/09/30/22-39-06 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King added that an earlier `@{publish}` proposal with different semantics was asked not to use `@{p}` to avoid confusion, and there was also discussion of `@{pull}` as an alias to `@{upstream}`; he has no particular objection now since neither conflict is under current discussion, just noting the history.
- [2026/09/30/22-52-39 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano called the "unknown revision" justification weak (the same argument could remove `@{u}`), asked that the change's log message record the history — `@{upstream}` had `@{u}` from the beginning, `@{push}` did not, and no concrete reason against `@{p}` was given when the feature was added, so the change is justified as symmetry — and marked the patch "Queued."
