This thread is about Taylor Blau’s eight-patch series fixing corner cases in Git’s multi-pack-index (MIDX) and cruft-pack machinery where MIDXs can end up containing objects not closed under reachability. The series, now at v2, addresses all feedback from the initial submission and is under active review. The core issue is that MIDXs must include all objects reachable from their tips to ensure bitmap generation works correctly, and the patches handle four specific scenarios: `--stdin-packs=follow`, incremental repacks, `.keep` packs, and `--write-midx=incremental`.

Most of the series is uncontroversial and ready for integration. Patches 1/8 (a refactoring introducing `stdin_packs_context`), 3/8 (fixing an edge case in `write_midx_incremental()`), 4/8 (a mechanical refactoring replacing a linear search with a binary search), 6/8 (a refactoring in `repack-midx.c` to track the preferred pack explicitly), 7/8 (deferring allocation of the write step until needed), and 8/8 (ensuring required packs are included in incremental MIDX writes) have all received maintainer or reviewer approval in spirit or are explicitly endorsed as correct. Jeff King’s latest follow-up on patch 6/8 confirms the refactoring’s necessity after Taylor clarified that the existing code’s behavior is order-dependent, resolving the last open question about that patch.

The main remaining discussion centers on patch 2/8, where the choice of data structure for `extra_roots` is contested. Elijah Newren’s test cases show that an `oid_array` preserves critical path and namehash ordering needed for delta compression and attribute handling, while an `oidset` would cause functional regressions. Jeff King now sides with Newren, emphasizing the importance of preserving pack-order correlation, though he acknowledges the memory trade-offs. The conditional logic in patch 5/8 also remains a minor point of contention, with Peff suggesting a refactor for clarity and Taylor proposing a configuration variable rename to resolve the confusion. These are implementation details rather than blockers, and the series as a whole is well-motivated and narrowly scoped. No other open questions or design objections remain, and the patches are expected to graduate to Junio’s integration branches soon.
