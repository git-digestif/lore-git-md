This is a brief but substantive discussion about the design direction of Phillip Wood’s patch series that teaches `git checkout -m` to remember the original conflict labels from a merge. Junio C Hamano responds to Johannes Sixt’s suggestion to use an index extension instead of the `.git/MERGE_LABELS` file proposed in the series.

Junio’s reply is measured and open-ended. He notes that replacing all the special `*_HEAD` files (like `MERGE_HEAD`, `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`, and even `ORIG_HEAD`) with index extensions would "unclutter" the `.git` directory, which he finds appealing. However, he also points out that index extensions would make the information less accessible—presumably because they are binary and not human-readable like plain files—so he is unsure how he feels about the trade-off. The tone is exploratory rather than decisive, treating the idea as an interesting possibility rather than a clear improvement.

This exchange does not resolve the question but elevates it: the choice of where to store the labels is now a live design discussion, not just an implementation detail. The next step will likely depend on whether Phillip or others engage with the trade-offs (accessibility vs. integration, binary vs. text, cleanup complexity) and whether anyone tests or prototypes the index-extension approach. For now, the series remains under review, and the original `.git/MERGE_LABELS` file is still the baseline.
