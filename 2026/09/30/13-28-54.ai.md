This is a **documentation patch** in the ongoing RFC series to convert Git’s `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage (`gitbreaking-changes(7)`). This is the second patch in the four-part series, focusing on usability: it replaces all message-ID references (e.g., `<xmqqa59i45wc.fsf@gitster.g>`) with clickable URLs pointing to the corresponding threads on `lore.kernel.org`. Two message-IDs required URL encoding to avoid breaking AsciiDoc parsing.

The patch is purely mechanical, updating every reference in the document and tweaking the surrounding explanatory text to clarify that the links point to the original email’s `Message-ID`. The motivation is straightforward: users no longer need to manually construct URLs, making it easier to jump directly to the relevant discussion. The change is uncontroversial and aligns with the series’ goal of improving discoverability.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is **substantive but narrow**. He endorses the usability improvement, noting that while `lore.kernel.org` URLs could theoretically break in the future, the message-ID can still be extracted and adapted. His only technical critique is a minor formatting nit (two spaces after a URL), which is easily fixed. He also suggests moving the "how to add new entries" section to the end of the document, arguing that most readers—now that the document is more public-facing—won’t care about the internal process. This is a reasonable usability suggestion but not a blocker.

The patch itself is ready to land if the series is accepted, though the RFC tag indicates the author is still seeking feedback on the overall approach. No new patch version has been posted yet, so this remains a point-in-time review. The files touched are limited to `Documentation/gitbreaking-changes.adoc`, and the changes are confined to URL formatting and explanatory text. There are no new symbols, CLI options, or behavior changes, and no test coverage is needed. The key names introduced are the URLs themselves, which are unlikely to be referenced in future discussion.
