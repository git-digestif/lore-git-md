This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s packed-refs backend, submitted by Karthik Nayak. The patch aims to speed up operations that rewrite the packed-refs file (like deleting references) by avoiding redundant reformatting of unchanged refs—writing them directly as raw bytes instead of using `fprintf()` for each entry. The benchmark shows a ~20% speedup when deleting 100,000 packed refs.

Toon Claes has raised substantive concerns about the patch’s approach. While the performance gain is clear, Toon points out four subtle behavioral changes introduced by bypassing `fprintf()`: case sensitivity in OIDs, whitespace handling, preservation of invalid refnames, and peeled OIDs for broken refs. None of these are likely to cause real-world issues, but Toon suggests documenting them in the commit message for clarity. They also propose a structural improvement—splitting `write_packed_entry()` into two functions to avoid mixing raw and formatted write logic—though they acknowledge this is a matter of maintainability rather than correctness.

The patch is still under review, with no other feedback recorded yet. The discussion is narrowly focused on the packed-refs subsystem, and there are no open questions beyond Toon’s suggestions. If Karthik addresses the feedback, this could be a straightforward optimization, but the trade-offs between performance and maintainability will need to be weighed.
