This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s packed-refs backend, authored by Karthik Nayak. The patch speeds up operations that rewrite the packed-refs file—such as deleting references—by avoiding redundant formatting of unchanged refs. Instead of using `fprintf()` for every entry, it tracks the raw byte positions of unchanged refs in the snapshot iterator and writes them directly with `fwrite()`. The change is confined to `refs/packed-backend.c` and introduces a `record_start` field to the `packed_ref_iterator` struct, along with modifications to `write_packed_entry()` to handle raw writes when possible.

Benchmarks show a consistent ~20% improvement when deleting 100,000 packed refs, reducing runtime from ~28.7 ms to ~23.8 ms. The patch is well-motivated, narrowly scoped, and unlikely to be controversial, as it preserves existing behavior while improving performance. No new dependencies or platform-specific code are introduced, and the risk of regression appears low. The optimization is part of ongoing work on the packed-refs subsystem, which Karthik and Patrick Steinhardt actively maintain. So far, there are no review comments or objections, so the patch is likely to proceed smoothly toward integration.
