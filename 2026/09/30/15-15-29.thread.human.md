This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s packed-refs backend, now in its third iteration. The patch, authored by Karthik Nayak, speeds up operations that rewrite the packed-refs file (like deleting references) by avoiding redundant formatting of unchanged refs. Instead of using `fprintf()` for every entry, it tracks raw byte positions and writes them directly with `fwrite()`, yielding a consistent ~20% speedup in benchmarks—deleting 100,000 packed refs now takes ~23.8 ms instead of ~28.7 ms.

The implementation is narrow and focused, touching only `refs/packed-backend.c`. It introduces a `record_start` field in `struct packed_ref_iterator` and splits the write logic into two functions: `write_packed_entry()` for formatted writes and `write_packed_entry_raw()` for raw writes. This addresses feedback from Toon Claes, who reviewed the previous version and is now satisfied with the changes. The commit message explicitly documents the side effects of bypassing `fprintf()`, such as preserving original formatting (case, whitespace, malformed refnames, and peeled OIDs for broken refs), which could affect tools parsing packed-refs files. The patch is backward-compatible with the on-disk format, though external tools may observe subtle formatting differences.

The patch has undergone two rounds of review, with Toon Claes’s feedback fully addressed. No maintainer decision has been recorded yet, and Junio has not queued it in `seen` or commented on it. The primary open question remains whether the documented formatting side effects will cause issues for tools like JGit or libgit2, though no specific compatibility concerns have been raised so far. The patch is likely to be uncontroversial, as it preserves behavior while improving performance, but the interoperability implications may warrant further scrutiny.
