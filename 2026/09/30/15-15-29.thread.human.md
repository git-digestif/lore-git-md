This thread is about a performance optimization for Git’s packed-refs backend, authored by Karthik Nayak. The patch, now in its second version, speeds up operations that rewrite the packed-refs file (such as deleting references) by avoiding redundant formatting of unchanged refs. Instead of using `fprintf()` for every entry, it tracks raw byte positions and writes them directly with `fwrite()`, resulting in a consistent ~20% speedup when deleting 100,000 packed refs.

The patch has undergone substantive review from Toon Claes, who initially raised concerns about the side effects of bypassing `fprintf()` (such as preserving original formatting, including case, whitespace, and malformed refnames) and proposed splitting the write logic into separate functions for clarity. These issues were addressed in v2, and Toon’s latest email confirms he is satisfied with the current version, marking the end of his feedback. The patch touches only `refs/packed-backend.c` and introduces a new `write_packed_entry_raw()` function alongside the existing `write_packed_entry()`. The trade-off—preserving original formatting—is now clearly documented in the commit message, though it remains an open question whether this could affect tools like JGit or libgit2 that parse packed-refs files.

The patch is narrow, well-motivated, and benchmarked, making it a strong candidate for merging once Junio picks it up. No new technical concerns have been raised, and the only remaining loose end is the potential interoperability impact of the formatting changes. The series has no dependencies on other in-flight topics and is part of the packed-refs subsystem maintained by Karthik Nayak and Patrick Steinhardt.
