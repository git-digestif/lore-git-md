This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on the fourth patch in Taylor Blau’s four-patch series fixing MIDX reachability closure corner cases. The patch itself unconditionally includes cruft packs in MIDXs whenever any kept packs are present, addressing a scenario where `.keep` packs can reference objects whose only copy is in a cruft pack, breaking MIDX reachability closure.

Peff’s review raises two technical questions that could impact the patch’s correctness and performance. First, he asks whether the fix might trigger **racily** during pushes due to the temporary `.keep` files Git installs to prevent pruning. If the MIDX includes these transient `.keep` packs, it could unexpectedly pull in cruft packs, potentially degrading performance in large repositories where cruft packs are sizable. This is a real-world concern: temporary `.keep` files are short-lived but could coincide with MIDX updates, leading to inconsistent behavior or unnecessary overhead.

Second, Peff questions why the patch opts to **unconditionally include cruft packs** rather than treating `.keep` packs like other included follow-packs (i.e., traversing them during the reachability walk). The current approach sidesteps the need to verify reachability closure for kept packs, but Peff suggests that traversing them might be a more principled solution. This could avoid over-inclusion of cruft packs when kept packs are already reachability-closed, though it would require deeper changes to the pack-selection logic.

The review is **tested in spirit**: Peff engages with the patch’s design rather than its implementation details, and his questions are narrowly scoped to the `.keep` pack handling. Neither question challenges the patch’s core goal (ensuring MIDX reachability closure), but both could lead to follow-up discussion or revisions. The first question is particularly actionable, as it surfaces a potential edge case that might not have been considered in the original test coverage. The second is more speculative but could influence the long-term design of the MIDX pack-selection logic.

This patch remains the last piece of the series addressing MIDX reachability closure, and Peff’s feedback does not undermine its correctness. However, the questions he raises could prompt further iteration, especially if the performance implications of temporary `.keep` files prove significant.
