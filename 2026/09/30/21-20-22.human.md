This is a brief but clarifying follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Phillip Wood’s patch series to preserve original conflict labels in `git checkout -m`. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) responds to Junio C Hamano’s earlier musing about replacing Git’s special `*_HEAD` files with index extensions.

Hannes draws a sharp distinction: while `MERGE_HEAD` and friends are pseudo-refs that track state (and could plausibly move to index extensions), `.git/MERGE_LABELS` is merely auxiliary data that should not be treated as a ref at all. His point is narrow but important: the architectural discussion about index extensions is orthogonal to the conflict-label series, and conflating the two risks muddying the design space. He also signals that if accessing index extensions proves prohibitively difficult, the idea is non-starter—though he doesn’t elaborate on what “prohibitively difficult” means in practice.

The exchange leaves the thread’s core question unresolved: whether to stick with `.git/MERGE_LABELS` or explore an index extension. Hannes’s comment suggests that the latter would require a separate, broader conversation about Git’s state-management architecture, while the former remains a pragmatic, self-contained solution. No new technical objections or alternatives are raised, but the clarification helps scope the discussion.
