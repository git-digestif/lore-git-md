The `git backfill --dry-run` feature is shaping up smoothly. Pablo Sabater sent the initial five-patch series to add a `--dry-run` option that estimates how many blobs—and their total uncompressed size—would be fetched before actually running `git backfill`. The goal is to give users a preview of the download cost.

So far, the discussion has been light and cooperative. Karthik Nayak reviewed the series and suggested a few cosmetic tweaks: squashing an enum addition into the API conversion patch (which Pablo accepted, reducing the series to four patches in v2) and three stylistic improvements to the dry-run implementation—using `bool` instead of `int` for the flag, renaming `dry_run_batch()` to `count_batch()`, and simplifying the control flow. Pablo agreed to all three, though he noted a small concern about the "count" name not perfectly matching future size-accumulation logic and plans to add a clarifying comment. No technical objections have been raised; the feature remains feature-complete and ready for deeper review.

The series touches `transport.c`, `fetch-object-info.{c,h}`, `builtin/backfill.c`, and adds test coverage in `t/t5620-backfill.sh`. It gracefully handles servers without the `object-info` capability by reporting only the blob count. The output format—“After backfill, N blobs would be fetched (M KiB).”—is still open for feedback, as is the overall usefulness of the feature. Pablo explicitly asked whether users find this preview valuable. The next revision (v2) will incorporate the stylistic changes and is expected soon, after which substantive technical review can proceed.
