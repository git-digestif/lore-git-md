This is a documentation patch review in the ongoing series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s merge conflict resolution guidance. Patrick Steinhardt provides a **substantive review** of the first patch, focusing on clarity, technical accuracy, and user experience. His feedback is constructive and detailed, addressing both mechanical and conceptual aspects of the new guide.

Patrick’s comments center on improving the guide’s precision and usability. He suggests using `linkgit:` macros for command references (e.g., `linkgit:git-merge[1]`) to ensure consistent linking, and proposes replacing the term "area" with "hunk" when describing conflicted regions, as the latter is more idiomatic in Git’s documentation. He also raises a conceptual point about whether the guide should briefly explain how Git performs a 3-way merge, arguing that understanding the three sides (base, ours, theirs) is foundational to resolving conflicts. While he acknowledges this might be too technical for some users, the suggestion highlights a potential gap in the guide’s introductory material.

The review includes several practical suggestions. Patrick notes that the guide could benefit from showing how to access the commits involved in a conflict (e.g., `git rev-parse --reference <commit>`), which would help users inspect the changes more easily. He also corrects a minor typo ("the the" -> "the") and clarifies the explanation of "ours" and "theirs" during a rebase, proposing a rewrite to make the distinction clearer. His alternative wording explicitly ties "ours" and "theirs" to `HEAD` and the commit being merged, which avoids confusion about the direction of the operation. Additionally, he points out that the terms "stage 1/2/3" refer to index entries, not commits, and should not be conflated with "ours" or "theirs."

Patrick’s feedback is well-informed and aligns with the series’ goal of improving documentation clarity. While some suggestions (e.g., explaining 3-way merges) may require balancing technical depth with accessibility, his comments are actionable and likely to strengthen the guide. The review does not raise any major objections but instead focuses on refining the content to better serve users. Julia Evans has already demonstrated responsiveness to feedback in earlier rounds, so these adjustments are likely to be incorporated in the next revision. The patch remains a strong contribution to Git’s documentation, and Patrick’s input will help ensure it is both accurate and user-friendly.
