This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Phillip Wood’s patch series to preserve original conflict labels in `git checkout -m`. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) clarifies his earlier suggestion about using an index extension to store merge labels.

Hannes now explicitly distinguishes between *pseudo-refs* (like `MERGE_HEAD`) and *auxiliary information* (like the proposed `MERGE_LABELS`). He argues that `MERGE_LABELS` should not be treated as a pseudo-ref at all, but rather as ephemeral data that doesn’t belong in the refs namespace. His comment implies that an index extension could still be a viable storage mechanism for this kind of transient, non-ref data, but he concedes that if accessing index extensions is "prohibitively difficult," the idea is non-starter.

The exchange remains exploratory. Hannes does not provide concrete examples of how index extensions are accessed or why they might be cumbersome, nor does he address Junio’s earlier concern about reduced accessibility for users and scripts. The thread is now at a standstill on this point: the architectural trade-offs (cleanliness vs. usability) are acknowledged but unresolved, and no one has yet volunteered to prototype or benchmark an index-extension approach. Phillip Wood has not responded to either Junio’s or Hannes’s comments, so the next move likely rests with him—either to address the open questions in a v2 or to defer the index-extension idea for future work.
