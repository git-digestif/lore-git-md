This is a maintainer response to a small feature patch that adds `@{p}` as a shorthand for `@{push}` in Git revision syntax. Junio C Hamano has queued the patch for integration, signaling that it will likely graduate to `next` and eventually `master` in a future cycle.

The email itself is brief and procedural. Junio acknowledges the patch’s motivation—eliminating the inconsistency between `@{u}` (upstream) and `@{push}` (no shorthand)—but dismisses the original commit message’s phrasing ("users who reach for the one letter spelling by analogy get an error") as too weak. Instead, he directs the author to strengthen the justification by incorporating the historical context he and Peff previously clarified: `@{upstream}` and `@{u}` were introduced together in 2010, while `@{push}` was added in 2015 without a shorthand, not because of any deliberate decision against it, but simply because the asymmetry was overlooked. This framing—"just for symmetry, add missing `@{p}`"—is now the recommended rationale for the change.

No technical objections or requests for code changes are raised; the queuing is unconditional. The patch touches `object-name.c`, `Documentation/revisions.adoc`, and adds test coverage in `t/t1514-rev-parse-push.sh`, following established patterns for revision suffix aliases. The change is uncontroversial and aligns with Git’s existing case-insensitive handling of revision suffixes (e.g., `@{U}` also works for `@{upstream}`).
