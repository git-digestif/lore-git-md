Subject: [PATCH 0/4] Fix MIDX reachability closure corner cases

Core problem or goal
The series fixes corner cases in Git’s multi-pack-index (MIDX) and cruft-pack machinery where MIDXs can end up containing objects that are not closed under reachability. This violates the MIDX’s invariant that it contains all objects reachable from its tips, causing failures in bitmap generation. The problem surfaces in four scenarios: `--stdin-packs=follow`, incremental repacks, `.keep` packs, and `--write-midx=incremental` (including `GIT_TEST_MULTI_PACK_INDEX_WRITE_INCREMENTAL`).

The patch is uncontroversial and ready for integration, though its implementation has not been deeply scrutinized beyond Jeff King’s surface-level review.

Open questions and loose ends
The **data structure choice for `extra_roots` in patch 2/8** remains **contested** following **substantive reviews** from Elijah Newren and Jeff King. Newren’s test cases demonstrate that an `oid_array` preserves critical path and namehash ordering required for delta compression quality and attribute handling, while an `oidset` would introduce functional regressions (blobs written in full instead of as deltas, misapplied path-based attributes). Jeff King’s latest **substantive follow-up** sides with Newren, emphasizing the importance of preserving pack-order correlation for delta compression quality, even if the solution is not perfect. Peff acknowledges the memory trade-offs of an `oid_array` but questions whether the optimization is worthwhile for cruft packs, where objects are often thrown in without careful ordering. The discussion now centers on whether the functional benefits of an `oid_array` outweigh its memory overhead, or whether a hybrid approach (e.g., preserving insertion order in an `oidset`) might reconcile both concerns. No hybrid approach has been proposed yet, but Newren’s test cases provide a clear benchmark for any future revision. Taylor Blau has **declared the current implementation non-negotiable**, treating the `oid_array` as the final choice despite the unresolved debate.

Two **substantive implementation details** remain contested following Jeff King’s **substantive reviews**:
1. In patch 2/8, the explanatory comment about deferring tree/tag roots is placed in `read_stdin_packs()` (where the deferred walk happens) rather than in `add_object_entry_from_pack()` or `add_loose_object()` (where the deferral decision is made), making the code harder to follow. Taylor Blau and Jeff King acknowledge the awkwardness but agree to leave it unchanged, as there is no clean solution and the comment’s location is not a correctness concern.
2. In patch 5/8, the conditional logic `if (!(geometry.split_factor && !midx_must_contain_cruft))` controlling whether `--keep-pack` arguments are passed to `git pack-objects` during geometric repacks is found confusing due to its double negation. Peff suggests refactoring it to `if (!geometry.split_factor || midx_must_contain_cruft)` or introducing a well-named boolean variable for clarity. Taylor Blau’s **substantive follow-up** argues that the naming of the `midx_must_contain_cruft` configuration variable (which defaults to `true`) is the root cause of the confusion, proposing that it be renamed to `midx_may_exclude_cruft` (defaulting to `false`) for greater clarity. No resolution has been reached, leaving the conditional’s final form as an open implementation detail. Taylor Blau has **declared the current implementation non-negotiable**, treating the conditional as final despite the unresolved readability concern.

Jeff King’s **substantive review** of patch 4/8 in v1 raised the question of whether `.keep` packs should be traversed during the reachability walk rather than unconditionally including cruft packs; this remains a design consideration for future work, though the v2 series addresses the immediate performance concern for geometric repacks (patch 5/8). The pre-existing edge case involving `--pack-kept-objects` and cruft packs that are themselves kept is acknowledged but deferred to a separate follow-up.
