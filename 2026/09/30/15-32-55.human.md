This is the final patch in Jeff King’s five-patch series refactoring the xdiff subsystem’s buffer handling. The patch adds a defensive NUL-terminator to buffers read by `read_mmfile()`, aligning them with Git’s common practice of NUL-terminating ptr/len pairs to guard against off-by-one errors or accidental string-function use. The change is minimal: replacing `xmalloc(sz ? sz : 1)` with `xmallocz(sz)` in `xdiff-interface.c`, which allocates `sz + 1` bytes and zeroes the last byte. This also removes the special case for empty files, since `xmallocz(0)` safely returns a one-byte buffer.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review expresses mild ambivalence about the defensive measure, noting that while the xdiff code’s complexity and pointer arithmetic might justify it, the inconsistency with other non-defensive code paths feels arbitrary. He suggests fuzzing as a more systematic way to catch bugs, though acknowledges that platform-specific integer issues would still be hard to detect. His counterpoint—that the patch essentially restores a safety guard removed in patch 3/5—softens the objection, and he doesn’t oppose the change. The review is substantive but non-blocking, focusing on design philosophy rather than implementation flaws.

The patch itself is uncontroversial and mechanically sound. It touches only one line of code, introduces no new symbols, and changes no user-visible behavior. The series as a whole is now complete, addressing the original goal of switching buffer sizes to `size_t`, fixing a latent memory leak, consolidating redundant types, and adding this final defensive measure.
