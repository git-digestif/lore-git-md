This thread is about adding `@{p}` as a shorthand for `@{push}` in Git's revision syntax, mirroring the existing `@{u}` shorthand for `@{upstream}`. The feature was proposed by Harald Nordgren to address a long-standing asymmetry: `@{u}` was introduced in 2010 alongside `@{upstream}`, but `@{push}` (added in 2015) lacked a shorthand.

The patch, now in its second version, touches `object-name.c`, updates documentation, and adds test coverage. It introduces no new behavior—just a case-insensitive alias (`@{p}` or `@{P}`) for `@{push}`. Junio Hamano, the maintainer, initially queued it in his `seen` branch and has now confirmed it will move to `next`, meaning it’s in the final integration-testing phase before reaching `master`. The commit message was praised as "very well written," and no further changes are expected. The patch has no remaining technical or design objections, and all historical context (including past discussions about `@{publish}` and `@{pull}`) has been addressed.

Key participants include Harald (the author), D. Ben Knoble (who expressed support and curiosity about the historical rationale), and Jeff King (who clarified past discussions). Junio’s latest email is the procedural green light that the feature is effectively done and will ship in the next Git release.
