This thread is about a small but convenient feature addition: making `@{p}` a shorthand for `@{push}` in Git’s revision syntax, mirroring the existing `@{u}` shorthand for `@{upstream}`. The patch, submitted by Harald Nordgren, addresses an inconsistency that arose when `@{push}` was introduced in 2015 without a shorthand, while `@{upstream}` and `@{u}` had been available since 2010.

The discussion has focused on the historical context behind the asymmetry. Junio Hamano clarified the timeline of when each feature was introduced, while Jeff King (Peff) added depth by recounting a 2015 discussion where `@{publish}` (with different semantics) was proposed, and he had requested that `@{p}` be reserved to avoid future confusion. Neither `@{publish}` nor a proposed `@{pull}` alias for `@{upstream}` materialized, so the historical concerns no longer apply. Peff explicitly stated he has no objection to the patch, and no other participants have raised technical or design concerns.

The patch itself is straightforward: it adds `@{p}` and `@{P}` as case-insensitive aliases for `@{push}`, touching `object-name.c`, the revisions documentation, and adding test coverage. There are no open questions or requests for changes, and the thread remains uncontroversial. The only remaining question is whether the project considers the asymmetry worth addressing now, but no one has opposed the idea. The patch is still under review, with no indication of when or if it will be merged.
