This is a **documentation-focused review reply** in the ongoing RFC thread to convert Git’s `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage (`gitbreaking-changes(7)`). The email is a short, substantive exchange between Patrick Steinhardt and Kristoffer Haugsbakk about the mechanical steps for the file conversion.

Patrick endorses Kristoffer’s proposed three-step approach for the file transformation: first convert the existing `BreakingChanges` document to the manpage format in-place, then rename it to `gitbreaking-changes.txt`, and finally resurrect `BreakingChanges` as a one-line pointer to the new manpage. He notes that the rename and pointer steps can likely be combined into a single patch without breaking Git’s rename detection. This is a minor but practical suggestion to streamline the patch series.

The discussion remains focused on implementation details rather than the broader RFC questions (e.g., the document’s lifecycle post-Git 3.0), which were previously addressed in the thread. The tone is collaborative, and the feedback is already accepted by the author. This email does not introduce new concerns or open questions; it simply clarifies the preferred mechanical approach for the file conversion.
