This is a **documentation patch discussion** in the ongoing `gitmergeconflicts(7)` series. Julia Evans responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s **substantive review** of the first patch, engaging with his suggestions on terminology, technical completeness, and user experience.

Julia agrees to most of Patrick’s **surface-level fixes**—adding `linkgit:` macros for cross-references, correcting a typo ("the the" → "the"), and mentioning `git merge-tool --tool-help` as a way to list available merge tools. These are straightforward improvements that will be incorporated.

On **terminology**, Julia acknowledges Patrick’s concern about "area" versus "hunk" but opts for "lines of code" instead, avoiding jargon while keeping the meaning clear. For the "ours" and "theirs" explanation, she appreciates Patrick’s proposed rewording but plans a shorter, more direct alternative. Her goal is to avoid over-explaining a concept that users often find confusing, even with more context. Instead, she leans toward reinforcing the use of tools (like `diff3` or graphical merge tools) to provide practical help rather than theoretical clarity. This aligns with her earlier comment that users often find tool-based explanations more actionable than conceptual ones.

Julia pushes back slightly on **technical completeness**, particularly Patrick’s suggestion to explain how Git performs a 3-way merge. She shares her experience that users struggled with this concept in past attempts, and that the guide’s focus on tools (like `git diff --check` and `diff3`) has been more effective in practice. While she leaves the door open for adding this explanation in a future iteration, she prioritizes the guide’s current utility over theoretical depth. She also declines Patrick’s idea to include commit references (e.g., `git rev-parse --reference`), feeling that commit messages would be more helpful than IDs but not essential for the guide’s purpose.

The discussion about **stages** (e.g., "stage 1", "stage 2") highlights a subtle but important distinction: stages refer to file versions in the index, not commits. Julia plans to clarify this, possibly by cross-referencing `gitdatamodel(7)` for users who want deeper technical context.

Overall, Julia’s responses are **pragmatic and user-focused**, balancing Patrick’s suggestions with her empirical observations about what helps users resolve conflicts effectively. The patch remains on track for inclusion, with minor refinements pending. The series continues to aim for a practical, tool-centric guide that centralizes conflict-resolution advice while preserving command-specific details where necessary.
