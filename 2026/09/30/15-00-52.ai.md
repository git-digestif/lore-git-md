This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Thomas Bachem’s three-part v5 series fixing a `rerere` race condition. The patch introduces `--auto` mode for `git rerere gc`, allowing auto-maintenance tasks to skip the operation if the lock is held. Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback focuses on two key issues: **documentation clarity** and **flag naming**.

First, Patrick questions the documentation’s phrasing in `git-rerere.adoc`, which currently explains *who* calls `git rerere gc --auto` (`git maintenance run --auto` and `git gc --auto`). He suggests rewording to focus on *why* the flag is useful—likely to avoid disrupting concurrent operations—rather than listing callers. This is a minor but valid point about man-page conventions.

More significantly, Patrick challenges the `--auto` flag’s name itself. He argues that "auto" misleadingly implies heuristic behavior (like `git pack-refs --auto`), whereas the flag’s sole purpose is to skip the operation if the lock is held. He proposes `--skip-locked` as a more accurate alternative, even suggesting it could be hidden (undocumented) since it’s primarily for `git maintenance`. This critique touches on a design tension: whether the flag should be a general-purpose option or a maintenance-specific workaround. Patrick leaves the final call to Thomas but hints at a preference for `--skip-locked`.

The review also notes a tangential code-quality issue: the `builtin/rerere.c` file’s structure is messy, with subcommand handling logic that could benefit from refactoring. Patrick explicitly sets this aside as out of scope, but the observation underscores the patch’s narrow focus.

No objections block merging, but the flag naming discussion could lead to a v6 tweak. The patch remains **well-motivated** and **technically sound**, addressing a real race condition with clear test coverage. The open question is purely about user-facing semantics.
