Thread identity: standalone patch and follow-up series addressing GitLab CI documentation job failures and Asciidoctor version pinning modernization, originating with Patrick Steinhardt’s minimal fix (`ps/ci-depends-on-ruby`). The thread evolved into a discussion about CI policy and broader documentation tooling, culminating in Jeff King’s two-patch series (`jk/ci-use-system-asciidoctor`) and a subsequent resend by Tuomas Ahola of Jeff’s second patch as a standalone update.

Core problem or goal: restore and modernize GitLab CI’s documentation build. The immediate goal was to fix a broken CI job by ensuring Ruby is available for Asciidoctor installation. The broader goal was to eliminate an outdated version pin and simplify CI configuration by using the system-provided Asciidoctor package, reflecting what users are likely to have installed locally and reducing maintenance overhead. The thread also opened a tangential discussion about the long-term viability of supporting both Asciidoc and Asciidoctor.

Current status: the original fix (`ps/ci-depends-on-ruby`) and Jeff King’s follow-up series (`jk/ci-use-system-asciidoctor`) are fully merged to `master`. The first patch (removal of dead `ALREADY_HAVE_ASCIIDOCTOR` code) and the second patch (replacing manual version-pinned installation with system package) were both applied in the integration cycle between v2.56.0-rc0 and rc1, as confirmed by Junio C Hamano in commit `47ce80527c`. The dependency chain—Patrick’s Ruby fix, Jeff’s dead-code removal, then the system-package switch—has been respected. No further action is required for the CI modernization effort. The broader Asciidoc/Asciidoctor discussion remains exploratory and does not propose immediate changes.
