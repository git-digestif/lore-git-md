This thread is about whether and how to formally document Git’s governance structure—roles, responsibilities, delegation, and succession processes—to improve clarity and reduce the project’s bus factor. Antonin Delpeuch kicked off the discussion, proposing a *descriptive* document that records current practices rather than imposing new rules, though even a factual write-up could highlight gaps like understaffed roles or unclear renewal processes.

The conversation is still in the early stages. Antonin plans to draft the document unless objections arise, basing it on publicly available information and filling gaps by asking other contributors (rather than Junio C Hamano directly). Christian Couder recently endorsed this approach, suggesting Antonin send a draft as a patch to the mailing list—aligning with Git’s usual review process—and noting that even if the document isn’t accepted into the official codebase, it could be published elsewhere (e.g., Git Developer Pages or Git Rev News). Junio has previously expressed cautious openness to a descriptive document but warned against prescriptive rules, and neither he nor the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) has yet engaged with the drafting process.

Key open questions remain: whether the project wants formal governance documentation at all, how detailed it should be (especially around succession planning), and whether leadership will participate in its creation. Antonin’s fallback plan to publish externally if needed suggests the document’s final home is still undecided. For now, the next step is Antonin’s initial draft, which will likely clarify the document’s scope and structure.
