This is a governance documentation discussion, specifically Junio C Hamano’s reply to Antonin Delpeuch’s proposal to formally document Git’s governance structure.

Junio responds with cautious openness. He acknowledges that Git’s governance is informal and emergent—clout is earned through sustained contribution, and there is no official list of reviewers or privileged roles. He points to the existing `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` (which references the Git PLC at SFC as “Community leaders”) as the closest thing to an official structure. While he does not oppose the idea of a descriptive document outlining “how it currently works,” he expresses concern that any attempt to make the document *prescriptive* could spark unproductive debate, as participants might lack shared experience to ground the discussion.

The tone is pragmatic: Junio neither endorses nor rejects the proposal outright, but he signals that the project is unlikely to adopt a formal, rule-based governance document. His response leaves the door open for a factual, low-stakes write-up that simply records current practice. No merge status or next steps are implied; the thread remains exploratory.
