This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in D. Ben Knoble’s two-patch bugfix series for `git stash`. The patch replaces subprocess-based index merging (`git diff-tree` and `git apply`) with in-core logic via `merge-ort.c` to fix a race condition in autostash handling when `stash.index=true` is set. Phillip Wood’s review engages deeply with the implementation, identifying correctness risks and suggesting improvements.

The review focuses on three key areas:
1. **Merge algorithm consistency**: Wood notes that the patch uses the default diff algorithm for index merging, while the worktree merge uses the `_ui` variant. He recommends standardizing on the `_ui` variant to avoid potential behavioral mismatches.
2. **Assertion safety**: The patch removes two `assert()` calls in `merge-ort.c` that guarded against NULL pointers (`opt->branch1`, `opt->branch2`, and `opt->ancestor`). Wood expresses concern that these assertions may have been protecting against real crashes, as the code later dereferences these pointers. He suggests adding fixed ancestor/branch names in `do_apply_stash()` to ensure safety and proposes a new test to verify the command fails gracefully (without SIGSEGV) if the index merge fails.
3. **Verbosity option**: Wood observes that the `verbosity` field in `merge_options` is unused by the ort strategy and suggests dropping the assignment.

The review also praises the patch’s cleanup of subprocess calls and confirms the test coverage (checking autostash application and stash list cleanup) is appropriate. Wood’s feedback is technical and actionable, targeting edge-case correctness rather than style or superficial issues. The open questions—assertion safety and merge algorithm choice—are likely to require author follow-up, but the underlying approach (in-core merging) appears sound. Reviewers familiar with `merge-ort.c` (e.g., Elijah Newren) may weigh in further.
