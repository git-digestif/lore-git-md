This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Maciej Ciemborowicz’s three-patch v2 series addressing the `reference-transaction` hook regression. Karthik Nayak’s feedback focuses on a **minor implementation detail** in `builtin/tag.c` but otherwise endorses the patch.

The patch updates `git branch` and `git tag` to pass resolved old OIDs to `refs_delete_refs()`, restoring the pre-regression behavior where the hook receives meaningful old OIDs (instead of all-zeros) and enabling conditional deletion (compare-and-delete) to prevent overwriting concurrent updates. Karthik’s review identifies a **readability nit**: the patch collects old OIDs in `delete_tags()` by iterating over `refs_to_delete` *after* it’s populated, rather than appending them directly in the `collect_tags()` callback. This could be streamlined by modifying the callback’s data parameter to include the `oid_array`, but Karthik does not insist on the change—only suggesting it be documented if left as-is. The rest of the patch is uncontroversial: the core logic (passing old OIDs to `refs_delete_refs()`), the new regression tests (verifying hook behavior and concurrent update safety), and the commit message (clearly explaining the motivation and trade-offs) all meet project standards.

The patch is **ready for integration** pending Maciej’s response to the nit. The broader series remains under discussion for two unresolved questions: (1) whether the project accepts the intentional race condition trade-off (conditional deletion for safety vs. unconditional deletion for simplicity), and (2) whether `refs_delete_refs()` should treat all-zero OIDs as equivalent to `NULL` (Karthik’s earlier concern). Neither question blocks this patch, which is narrowly scoped to the `git branch`/`git tag` plumbing.
