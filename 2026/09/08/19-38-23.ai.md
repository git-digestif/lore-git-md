This is a reply in the thread about the sequencer auto-maintenance deferral series (v3). Kristoffer Haugsbakk offers a perspective on the recent trend of increasingly verbose commit messages, responding to Junio C Hamano’s observation that proposed log messages are growing longer with "irrelevant details."

Kristoffer notes that six months ago, a thorough commit message typically included the problem, the solution (conceptually and sometimes concretely), and occasionally alternatives considered. These messages were concise and relied on project conventions (e.g., adding regression tests, updating documentation) without explicitly stating them. Recently, however, he has observed commit messages that resemble a checklist, explicitly addressing points like test coverage, documentation updates, and even benchmarks—even when these are standard practice. The most noticeable examples include a final paragraph with implied checkmarks, which he describes as overly formal and redundant. He also mentions that some commit messages read like "code-to-English descriptions," making them difficult to evaluate for correctness or coherence.

The reply does not engage with the technical content of the series itself, nor does it take a position on Junio’s critique. Instead, it provides context for the broader trend of verbose commit messages, framing it as a shift toward explicitness rather than a decline in quality. This observation may help future contributors understand the maintainer’s preference for brevity while acknowledging that some verbosity stems from an attempt to be thorough. The thread remains focused on commit message style rather than the patch series, which is already slated for queuing.
