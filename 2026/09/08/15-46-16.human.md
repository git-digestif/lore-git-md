This is a brief meta-discussion reply in the ongoing thread about what version number Git should use after 2.56. Randall S. Becker (the NonStop port maintainer) chimes in with a short but intriguing update on Rust platform support.

Becker acknowledges brian m. carlson’s point that Rust support for platforms without existing toolchains is not a blocker for Git 3.0, but adds that there may be progress on this front soon — though he cannot share details due to NDA restrictions. He remains "very hopeful" that Rust support for NonStop (and presumably other platforms) could arrive shortly after a 3.0 release, if not in time for it.

The comment is light on specifics but suggests that the Rust platform-compatibility landscape may shift in the near future. It does not directly engage with the other readiness criteria (SHA-256 on forges, libgit2, or the lowercase-only object IDs series) or advocate for a particular version-numbering strategy. Instead, it serves as a quiet counterpoint to Carlson’s earlier dismissal of Rust platform support as a non-issue, hinting that the situation may not be as static as it appears. For now, though, it remains a footnote rather than a decisive factor in the discussion.
