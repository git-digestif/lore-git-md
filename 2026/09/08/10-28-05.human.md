This is the second patch in a three-patch series (v3) that adjusts when auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations (`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, `git revert`). The goal is to defer maintenance until sequence completion, matching the apply backend’s behavior and preventing maintenance from running during intermediate steps.

This patch moves the `run_auto_maintenance()` call out of the sequencer and into the built-in commands (`run_specific_rebase()` in `builtin/rebase.c` and `run_sequencer()` in `builtin/revert.c`). This consolidates maintenance into a single exit path per command, aligning with the apply backend’s design and addressing Patrick Steinhardt’s preference for a unified exit path. The change ensures auto maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion, regardless of whether the sequence involves multiple commits, conflicts, or exec commands.

The patch touches `builtin/rebase.c` and `builtin/revert.c`, adding logic to invoke `run_auto_maintenance()` only after the sequencer has successfully completed and removed its state directory (for rebase) or after any successful sequencer operation (for cherry-pick/revert). Test coverage is expanded in `t/t3418-rebase-continue.sh` and `t/t3510-cherry-pick-sequence.sh` to verify that maintenance runs exactly once at sequence end and not during intermediate steps, using `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT` to trace maintenance invocations.

The commit message explains the design choice clearly: the sequencer itself lacks a single exit point for all sequences, so maintenance is delegated to the built-in commands, which are the only entry points for sequencer operations. The message also notes that this change makes the behavior consistent with the apply backend and prepares for the next patch, which will disable auto maintenance during intermediate commands.
