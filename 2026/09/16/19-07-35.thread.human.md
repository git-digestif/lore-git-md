The September 2026 "What's cooking" report from Junio Hamano gives a snapshot of Git's integration pipeline, with Git 2.56-rc1 tagged and the final release imminent. Several topics have recently graduated to `master`, including Rust build fixes (`jc/rust-cargo-build-target`), ODB backend refactoring (`ps/odb-pluggable-fsck` and `ps/odb-stop-registering-in-memory-sources`), a new `receive-report` hook (`kn/receive-report-hook`), and the `git history squash` command (`hn/history-squash`).

Among the new topics, `jc/advice-config-set-global` (suggesting `--global` for advice-squelching) and `of/commit-reach-repo-awareness` (fixing submodule merging) are under review but look likely to merge. The `sg/precompile-git-compat-util` series, which speeds up builds by precompiling `git-compat-util.h`, has now been confirmed to work with Clang and other LLVM-based compilers (including Intel's oneAPI), removing a portability concern and making its graduation to `next` and eventually `master` all but certain. Other topics cooking in `next` include ref storage terminology cleanup (`ps/ref-storage-format`) and rerere lock handling (`tb/rerere-wait-for-merge-rr-lock`), while stalled efforts like `mh/rust-crate-subdir` (Rust directory restructuring) and `mm/diff-process-hunks` (external diff hunk processing) await review or revisions. No major controversies are brewing, but several topics are blocked on reviewer feedback or author updates.
