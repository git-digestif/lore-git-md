This is the final review in Tyler Cipriani’s v5 bugfix series for `--force-if-includes`. The series fixes two long-standing flaws: the feature incorrectly checked the reflog of the wrong branch, and it unnecessarily rejected fast-forward pushes of non-branch refs (tags, detached HEADs). This third patch exempts fast-forwards from reflog checks, resolving a backwards-compatibility issue raised by D. Ben Knoble in v4.

Knoble’s review is substantive but narrowly focused. He catches a minor wording nit in the commit message (“push force” → “force push”) and questions whether the `deferred_reject_reason` variable name clearly conveys its purpose—specifically, that it only applies to non-fast-forward cases. His concern is readability for future maintainers, not correctness. The patch itself he describes as “making sense” once he traces the control flow, and he does not identify any functional bugs or edge cases the tests miss.

The implementation is clean: it defers the reflog check until after fast-forward determination, discards the deferred rejection if the push is a fast-forward, and otherwise promotes it to the active rejection reason. The change is minimal (10 lines in `remote.c`) and integrates smoothly with the rest of the series. The 27 new test lines cover expired reflogs and tag-based pushes, which are the most common edge cases.

This patch completes the series. All prior review feedback is resolved, and Knoble’s comments are surface-level. The series is technically sound, well-tested, and ready for final scrutiny. The only remaining question is whether other reviewers will request additional test coverage for mixed fast-forward/non-fast-forward pushes or symbolic references, but those would be nice-to-have rather than blockers.
