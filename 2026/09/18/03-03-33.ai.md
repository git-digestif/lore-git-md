This is the third patch in a five-patch bugfix series targeting a data-loss race in `git repack -d`. The series replaces the `--honor-pack-keep` mechanism with a snapshot-based approach to prevent concurrent pushes from causing objects to vanish despite `.keep` files.

This specific patch optimizes the `--keep-pack` lookup in `git pack-objects` by sorting the list of kept packs once and replacing linear scans with binary search (`string_list_has_string`). The change is internal-only: the comparison function (`fspathcmp`) and matching semantics remain unchanged, so what matches does not change. The optimization yields an 11× speed-up (11 seconds → 1 second) when matching 20,000 kept packs against 20,000 repository packs, addressing a scalability bottleneck introduced by the next patch in the series, which will let callers name every kept pack in the repository.

The patch touches only `builtin/pack-objects.c` and is mechanical: it initializes the `keep_pack_list` with `fspathcmp` as its comparison function, sorts the list once in `add_extra_kept_packs()`, and replaces the inner loop with `string_list_has_string()`. No new plumbing is exposed, and the change is unlikely to be controversial. The commit message is clear and includes the performance measurement, making the motivation obvious.

This is a well-motivated, narrowly scoped performance fix that enables the series’ main safety improvement. It is production-tested and merges cleanly into `next` and `seen`. Reviewers are likely to focus on whether the sorted list interacts poorly with future callers expecting insertion order, but the patch itself is straightforward and uncontroversial.
