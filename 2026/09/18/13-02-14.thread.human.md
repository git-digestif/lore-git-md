This thread began as an RFC patch series by Derrick Stolee proposing a "safe" subset of the `strbuf` API that cannot call `die()` or `exit()`, motivated by the trace2 subsystem's need for crash-free operation under memory pressure. The series introduced new `sstrbuf_*` functions and a two-phase transition plan, but drew substantive critiques from Jeff King (Peff) and Phillip Wood. Peff argued the current approach was solving the wrong problem, proposing instead fixed-size, stack-allocated buffers to avoid `malloc()` entirely, while Phillip raised concerns about naming, usability, and a bug in the implementation. Junio C Hamano endorsed the direction of making core routines safer but questioned the architectural framing.

The discussion has now concluded with Stolee setting the RFC aside. He acknowledged the feedback as requiring a fundamental rethink and stated he lacks time to pursue Peff’s alternative or address the architectural concerns. The series will not be revised or resubmitted in the near term, leaving the broader question of how to make `strbuf` operations safe for critical code paths unresolved. The thread is dormant from the author’s side, and future progress would likely need to start from Peff’s proposal or a new design. Key unresolved issues include the trade-offs between dynamic allocation hardening, fixed-size buffers, and signal safety, as well as the ergonomics of error handling in a "safe" API.
