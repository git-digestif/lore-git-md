This is a **refactoring and safety-hardening RFC series** introducing a "safe" subset of the `strbuf` API that cannot transitively call `die()` or `exit()`. The series is motivated by a real problem: the trace2 subsystem (via `json-writer`) can trigger recursive `die()` loops if memory allocation fails, which is unacceptable for low-level libraries.

The series is **well-motivated and technically sound**. Derrick Stolee frames the problem clearly (trace2’s dependence on `strbuf` can lead to fatal recursion), proposes a solution (a `strbuf-safe` API that avoids `die()`), and uses CodeQL to verify the safety property. The commits are logically structured: first moving struct definitions to shared headers, then addressing the undocumented `GIT_ALLOC_LIMIT` environment variable (proposing to rename it to `GIT_TEST_ALLOC_LIMIT` for clarity), then implementing the first safe methods (`sstrbuf_grow()`, `sstrbuf_init()`, `sstrbuf_release()`), and finally integrating them into `json-writer.c`. The naming convention (`sstrbuf_*` instead of `strbuf_*_gently`) is explained and justified.

The **transition risk is acknowledged**: some `json-writer` methods become "safe" but their callers are not yet updated to handle errors. Stolee plans to split this into two phases if the RFC is accepted: first landing the safe API, then refactoring callers. The series also documents `GIT_ALLOC_LIMIT` (previously undocumented) and moves its initialization to process startup to avoid implicit `die()` calls.

**Key files introduced**: `strbuf-safe.h` and `strbuf-safe.c`, which define the safe API. The series touches `strbuf.h` (moving definitions), `wrapper.c` (memory limit checks), `json-writer.c` (early adoption), and `common-init.c` (proactive initialization). The build system (`Makefile`, `meson.build`) is updated to include the new source file.

**Likely points of discussion**:
- Whether the "safe" API is the right approach (vs. hardening the existing API or using a different error-handling strategy).
- The naming convention (`sstrbuf_*` vs. `strbuf_*_gently`).
- The fate of `GIT_ALLOC_LIMIT` (rename to `GIT_TEST_ALLOC_LIMIT` or keep as-is due to Hyrum’s Law).
- The transition plan for `json-writer` and trace2 (how to handle errors in callers).

The series is **part of a larger effort to make Git’s low-level APIs more robust**, particularly for libraries like trace2 that must not crash. It is **not yet ready for merging** (hence the RFC), but the direction is promising and the implementation is careful. The use of CodeQL to verify the safety property is a strong positive signal.
