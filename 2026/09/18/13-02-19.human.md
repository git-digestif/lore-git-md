This is **part 5 of Derrick Stolee’s RFC series introducing a "safe" `strbuf` API** that cannot call `die()`. The series aims to harden low-level libraries like trace2, which must not crash even under memory pressure.

This patch **prepares `json-writer` for the safe API** by switching its header from `strbuf.h` to `strbuf-safe.h`. The change is mechanical but has ripple effects: `json-writer.h` no longer implicitly includes `strbuf.h`, so two trace2 files (`tr2_tgt_event.c` and `tr2_tgt_perf.c`) now explicitly include `strbuf.h` to compensate. The patch is small (4 lines changed) and uncontroversial in isolation, but it’s a necessary step toward making `json-writer` fully "safe" in later patches.

The motivation is clear: `json-writer` is a critical dependency of trace2, and trace2 must not trigger fatal errors. By including `strbuf-safe.h`, the header gains access to `struct strbuf` and `STRBUF_INIT` without pulling in the unsafe `strbuf` API. The commit message notes the awkwardness of this transition (implicit dependencies in headers) but justifies it as a pragmatic trade-off.

This patch **does not yet convert any `json-writer` methods to the safe API**—that happens in patch 6/6. It’s purely preparatory, but it’s a good sign that the series is structured incrementally. The change is unlikely to break anything, as the safe API is designed to be a drop-in replacement for the struct definitions.

For a busy reader, this patch is worth noting as a **harbinger of the safe API’s adoption** in a real subsystem. The real discussion will center on patch 6/6, where the rubber meets the road.
