This is a **refactoring and robustness RFC** targeting Git’s string-buffer (`strbuf`) and memory-allocation subsystems. Derrick Stolee proposes a new "safe" variant of the `strbuf` API that cannot transitively call `die()` or `exit()`, motivated by a recent discovery that the trace2 subsystem can trigger recursive fatal errors if memory allocations fail.

The series is exploratory: Stolee is asking whether the project wants to pursue this direction at all. The six patches are a proof-of-concept that (1) splits the `strbuf` API into a safe subset (`sstrbuf_*`) that returns errors instead of dying, (2) documents and proactively initializes the undocumented `GIT_ALLOC_LIMIT` environment variable to avoid allocation-time `die()` calls, and (3) begins migrating `json-writer.c` (used by trace2) to the safe API. The patches are small and mechanical, but the commit messages make it clear that the real work—auditing all callers to handle the new error returns—is not yet done.

The motivation is concrete: Peff identified a real bug (recursive `die()` in trace2) that this RFC would fix. The approach is cautious: safe functions are named with an `s` prefix (`sstrbuf_grow`) rather than the usual `_gently` suffix to avoid confusion with existing gentle variants. CodeQL is used to verify that the safe functions cannot reach `die()` or `exit()`, and Stolee suggests making this a CI build-time check if the effort proceeds.

If the project adopts this direction, the next steps would be a full audit of `json-writer.c` and trace2, followed by a gradual roll-out to other subsystems. For now, the series is marked RFC and is explicitly seeking feedback on whether the idea is worth pursuing.
