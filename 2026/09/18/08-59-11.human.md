This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about the second patch in Johannes Schindelin’s seven-patch series fixing Coverity-reported issues in Git for Windows. The patch in question makes GPG signature-prefix matching length-aware by propagating a `slen` (signature length) parameter through `get_format_by_sig()` and its callers, including `check_signature()`.

Junio C Hamano acknowledges Johannes’s audit showing that all six current callers of `check_signature()` pass a NUL-terminated buffer (five from `strbuf` and one from an offset sub-buffer of a `strbuf`), making the `slen` parameter redundant for prefix matching. However, Junio argues that the parameter is not just redundant but actively harmful: it invites future callers to pass non-NUL-terminated buffers, risking buffer overreads. His proposed alternative is to remove the `slen` parameter entirely, forcing all callers to pass NUL-terminated strings and eliminating the need for length-aware checks in `get_format_by_sig()`.

Johannes counters that `slen` is not only used for prefix matching but also to determine the exact byte count passed to external verifiers (`gpg`/`ssh-keygen`), preventing truncation of signatures with embedded NULs. He also points to a prior incident where `parse_signed_buffer()` was incorrectly assumed to be safe from buffer overreads because it was always called with a `strbuf` -- an assumption invalidated by a new caller (`convert_tag_object()`) in 2024. Junio dismisses this as a straw man, arguing that the audit was about redundancy, not about forcing a `strbuf` interface.

The exchange ends with Junio conceding that the difference between the two approaches is not large enough to justify rewriting and re-reviewing the patch. This leaves the patch’s current design intact, but the underlying disagreement remains unresolved: whether the GPG subsystem should enforce NUL-termination at the interface level (Junio’s preference) or maintain a length-aware interface for correctness and safety (Johannes’s position). The patch is likely to proceed as-is, but the discussion may resurface if future callers introduce similar defects.
