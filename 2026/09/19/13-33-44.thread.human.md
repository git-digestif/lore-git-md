This thread is about a bug in Git’s `reference-transaction` hook, which fails to report the creation of a new branch when an existing one is renamed (`git branch -m`) or copied (`git branch -c`). The hook only sees the deletion of the old branch name, not the creation of the new one, breaking the expected atomicity of ref transactions. Maciej Ciemborowicz posted a 4-patch series (v4) to fix this, addressing earlier test breakages and preserving architectural improvements like per-ref-update state and centralized hook suppression. The series is now technically sound—passing all tests on macOS for both files-based and reftable backends—but introduces performance regressions (up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs), which are deemed acceptable for correctness.

The series has stalled, however, over a non-technical issue: Patrick Steinhardt raised a process concern after Maciej posted v4 without replying to feedback on v3, warning that this risks disengaging reviewers and may indicate the author is acting as a "meat proxy" for AI-generated patches. Maciej’s recent reply acknowledges the issue and reflects on the tension between AI-assisted development and the project’s review culture, but does not directly address the expectation to engage with feedback before posting new versions. While patch 1/4 is uncontroversial and ready to proceed, patches 2–4 remain under review, and the unresolved process concern may delay further progress. No merge status has been reported, and the thread’s focus has shifted from technical fixes to broader questions about contributor workflows in an era of AI tooling.
