This thread began as a bug report about the `reference-transaction` hook omitting the new ref when a branch is renamed or copied (`git branch -m` or `-c`). Maciej Ciemborowicz’s four-patch v4 series fixes the issue by introducing `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL` infrastructure, a unified reflog replacement mechanism, and backend-agnostic copy/rename logic, while removing 656 lines of backend-specific code. The series passes all tests on macOS with both files-based and reftable backends, though it introduces a ~137% performance slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs.

The technical review has stalled, however, as the discussion has shifted to process and contributor workflow. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both reinforced a long-standing project norm: authors should engage substantively with reviewer feedback *before* posting a new version, not after or alongside it. Maciej’s latest emails disclose a seven-step AI-assisted workflow and frame his participation as a moral dilemma about submitting patches he did not write by hand. While he has accepted Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s suggestion to disclose AI assistance upfront, he has not addressed the maintainer’s expectation for pre-version engagement. Patrick’s newest reply draws a line based on patch complexity, arguing that large, intricate series like this one are unsuitable for AI-assisted development early in a contributor’s journey. The series remains under review, with patch 1/4 uncontroversial but the rest stalled until the process concerns are resolved or a new reviewer steps in.
