This thread is about a bug in Git’s `reference-transaction` hook: when you rename or copy a branch (`git branch -m` or `git branch -c`), the hook only reports the deletion of the old branch name, not the creation of the new one. Maciej Ciemborowicz has posted a v3 series of four patches to fix this, splitting the work into logical steps after Patrick Steinhardt’s objections to the earlier v2 design.

The series is now ready for substantive review. The key architectural change in v3 is moving from a transaction-wide copy/rename state to per-ref-update tracking, which addresses Steinhardt’s concerns about mixing refactoring and logic. The first patch in the series (just posted) introduces an internal transaction flag to suppress hook invocations for backend-internal updates, ensuring hooks only see the outer logical operation. This is a prerequisite for the later patches, which will handle reflog replacement and copy/rename integration.

Performance regressions are acknowledged—up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs—but are considered acceptable given the correctness fix. The series is independent of other ref backend work and has no on-disk format changes. Junio hasn’t raised new objections, but the series won’t merge until Steinhardt reviews it. Karthik Nayak has helped with domain expertise, and Maciej has incorporated human oversight into the AI-assisted patches. The next step is for Steinhardt and others to review the full series, particularly the refactoring in `refs.c` and the backend-specific logic.
