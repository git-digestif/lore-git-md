This thread began as a bug report and fix for the `reference-transaction` hook, which omits the creation of a new ref when a branch is renamed (`git branch -m`) or copied (`git branch -c`). Maciej Ciemborowicz’s four-patch series (v4) addresses the issue by introducing `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL` infrastructure, a unified `ref_transaction_replace_reflog()` mechanism, and backend-agnostic copy/rename logic, while removing 656 lines of backend-specific code. The series resolves earlier test breakages and passes the full test suite on macOS with both files-based and reftable backends, though performance regressions persist (up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs).

The technical work is largely complete, but the series has stalled over process concerns. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both emphasized the project norm that authors must engage substantively with reviewer feedback *before* posting a new version, not after or alongside it. Maciej’s disclosure of a heavily AI-assisted workflow—where he acts as a "meat proxy" for patches largely generated by agents—has deepened the impasse. While Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s suggestion to disclose AI assistance upfront in initial submissions was accepted by Maciej as a potential project norm, it does not address the maintainer’s expectation for pre-version engagement. Patch 1/4 remains uncontroversial and ready to proceed, but the series as a whole awaits either a demonstration of active engagement with v3 feedback or a new reviewer taking up the technical discussion. Until then, the meta-discussion about contributor workflow overshadows the technical review.
