Here’s the updated human-mode summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread is about a bug in Git’s `reference-transaction` hook, which fails to report the creation of a new branch when an existing one is renamed (`git branch -m`) or copied (`git branch -c`). The hook only sees the deletion of the old branch name, not the creation of the new one, breaking expectations for tools that rely on transactional ref updates. Maciej Ciemborowicz posted a four-patch series (v4) to fix this by introducing infrastructure to track rename/copy operations atomically, replacing backend-specific callbacks with a unified approach. The series addresses earlier test breakages and now passes the full test suite, though it introduces performance regressions (up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs) that are deemed acceptable for correctness.

The technical fix itself is uncontroversial—patch 1/4 is ready to proceed—but the series has stalled due to a process dispute. Patrick Steinhardt and Junio C Hamano have both criticized Maciej’s workflow: v4 was posted without substantive replies to feedback on v3, violating the project’s norm of engaging with reviewers *before* sending a new iteration. Junio’s latest email reinforces this expectation, framing it as a long-standing social contract rather than a new rule. The meta-discussion about AI-assisted development (Maciej acknowledged using AI tools, with Karthik Nayak providing human oversight) has overshadowed the technical review, leaving reviewers hesitant to invest further time until the process concern is resolved. No new technical objections have been raised, but the series remains in limbo pending either a response from Maciej or a new reviewer stepping in.
