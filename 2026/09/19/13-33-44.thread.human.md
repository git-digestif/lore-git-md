This thread began as a bug report and fix for the `reference-transaction` hook, which omits the creation of a new ref when a branch is renamed (`git branch -m`) or copied (`git branch -c`). Maciej Ciemborowicz posted a four-patch series (v4) addressing the issue, which affects both the files-based and reftable backends. The series introduces infrastructure to ensure hooks receive both deletion and creation events in the same transaction, resolving test breakages from earlier versions and preserving architectural improvements like per-ref-update state and centralized hook suppression.

The technical review has stalled, however, due to a growing meta-discussion about contributor workflow and AI-assisted development. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both raised concerns that Maciej posted v4 without first engaging substantively with feedback on v3, treating this as a long-standing project norm. Junio’s latest email reinforces this expectation, while Maciej’s response shifts the thread further into meta territory, disclosing his AI-assisted workflow in detail and framing his participation as a moral dilemma. He describes a seven-step process relying heavily on AI agents for code generation, testing, and review, and expresses discomfort with acting as a "meat proxy" for patches he did not write by hand. While he emphasizes transparency, he does not directly address the expectation to engage with feedback before posting new versions, nor does he propose a path forward for the technical discussion.

The series itself remains under review, with no merge status reported. Patch 1/4 (infrastructure) is uncontroversial and ready to proceed, but the series as a whole is stalled pending resolution of the process concerns. Performance regressions (~137% for files-based rename with large reflogs, ~20-25% for reftable) are deemed acceptable for correctness, and the fix is backward-compatible with no on-disk format changes. No new reviewers have engaged with the technical content since the meta-discussion took over.
