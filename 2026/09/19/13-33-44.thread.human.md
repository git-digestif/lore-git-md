This thread began as a bug report and fix for the `reference-transaction` hook, which omits the creation of a new ref when a branch is renamed or copied (`git branch -m` or `-c`). Maciej Ciemborowicz’s four-patch series (v4) addresses the issue by introducing `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL` infrastructure, a unified reflog replacement mechanism, and backend-agnostic copy/rename logic, while removing 656 lines of backend-specific code. The series passes all tests but carries a performance regression (up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs), which the author argues is acceptable for correctness.

However, the thread has shifted into a meta-discussion about AI-assisted development and project norms. Junio C Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, and Karthik Nayak have all reinforced the expectation that contributors engage substantively with feedback *before* posting new versions, framing this as a long-standing project norm. Karthik disengaged from reviewing after his feedback was met with new versions rather than discussion, while Patrick distinguished between small changes (acceptable for AI assistance) and large, complex series (risky without deep author context). Maciej disclosed a seven-step AI-assisted workflow and offered to step aside if others wanted to take over, but this did not resolve the procedural impasse.

Brian M. Carlson’s recent email adds a pragmatic perspective: contributors should only submit patches they can stand behind, whether written by hand or with AI assistance. He reassures Maciej that reporting bugs without patches is valuable and common, noting that the issue may eventually be addressed by someone with deeper expertise. The series remains stalled, with no new technical review or merge status reported, and the meta-discussion now dominates the thread. The core technical fix is uncontroversial, but the process concerns—particularly around AI-assisted contributions and pre-version engagement—remain unresolved.
