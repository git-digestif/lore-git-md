This thread began as a bug report and fix for the `reference-transaction` hook, which omits the creation of a new ref when a branch is renamed or copied (`git branch -m` or `-c`). Maciej Ciemborowicz’s four-patch series (v4) addresses the issue by introducing `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL` infrastructure, a unified reflog replacement mechanism, and backend-agnostic copy/rename logic, while removing 656 lines of backend-specific code. The series passes all tests on macOS with both files-based and reftable backends, though it introduces performance regressions (up to 137% slowdown for files-based rename with large reflogs) that are deemed acceptable for correctness.

The technical review has stalled, however, due to process concerns. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both emphasized that authors are expected to engage substantively with reviewer feedback *before* posting a new version—a long-standing project norm. Maciej’s disclosure of a seven-step AI-assisted workflow, where he acts as a "meat proxy" for externally generated patches, has further complicated the discussion. While Patch 1/4 remains uncontroversial and ready to proceed, the series as a whole is on hold until Maciej demonstrates active engagement with the feedback on v3 or the project clarifies norms for AI-assisted contributions. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s recent suggestion—that authors state upfront if they’re using AI agents—offers a procedural tweak but doesn’t resolve the underlying tension between automated workflows and the project’s expectation of human review engagement. The thread’s focus has shifted almost entirely to this meta-discussion, with no clear path forward.
