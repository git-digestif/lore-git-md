This thread is about a bug in Git’s `reference-transaction` hook, which fails to report the creation of a new branch when an existing one is renamed (`git branch -m`) or copied (`git branch -c`). The issue affects both the files-based and reftable backends and has been present since at least Git 2.28. Maciej Ciemborowicz posted a 4-patch series (v4) to fix it, introducing infrastructure like `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL` and `ref_transaction_replace_reflog()` to ensure hooks see complete transactions. The series resolves earlier test breakages but introduces performance regressions (up to 137% slowdown for files-based renames with large reflogs), which the author deems acceptable for correctness.

The series is now stalled, not on technical grounds but on process concerns. Patrick Steinhardt previously objected to Maciej posting v4 without replying to feedback on v3, framing it as a structural tension between AI-assisted development and the project’s review culture. Maciej acknowledged the discomfort of acting as a "meat proxy" for AI-generated patches but did not directly address the expectation to engage with feedback before posting new versions. Junio C Hamano’s latest reply reinforces this concern, critiquing the patch’s size and structure as indicative of "insufficient thought" due to its reliance on AI. While he does not reject the series outright, his focus on style over substance suggests reviewers may hesitate to invest further time. Patch 1/4 remains uncontroversial and ready to proceed, but the series as a whole is shadowed by questions about AI-assisted development practices. No merge status has been reported, and the thread awaits either a direct reply to Patrick’s feedback or a new reviewer stepping in.
