This thread is about a bugfix series for `git stash` that addresses a race condition in autostashing with staged index entries. The series, now at v3, replaces subprocess-based logic with in-core merging via `merge-ort.c` to properly preserve staged entries during merge operations. While the technical implementation is complete and prior feedback resolved, two critical issues have emerged, temporarily halting progress.

The first issue is a test breakage in `t5520-pull.sh` when the series is merged into Junio’s `seen` branch. Phillip Wood’s recent analysis pinpoints the root cause: an interaction with commit `2d1fa0323f` from the `tb/rerere-lock-grace` topic, which introduces auto-maintenance operations during rebase. The auto-maintenance step (`git maintenance run --auto`) expires reflog entries under the test’s fixed timestamp, disrupting `git pull --rebase`’s fork-point detection. This isn’t a direct flaw in the stash series but an indirect conflict that could affect real-world workflows. The author, D. Ben Knoble, is investigating why the stash series triggers this behavior, and coordination with Thomas Bachem (author of the conflicting topic) may be needed to resolve it.

The second issue is a NULL-dereference risk in Patch 5/5, where `merge_incore_nonrecursive()` may return with `result.tree` uninitialized on catastrophic failure, leading to a SIGSEGV. The author has confirmed this and plans to fix it by checking `result.clean` before dereferencing `result.tree`. Additionally, a verbosity regression in Patch 5 was resolved by preserving the original verbosity setting for the worktree merge.

Junio has ejected Patches 2–5 from `seen` pending resolution of these issues, though this is procedural rather than a rejection. Phillip Wood and Junio have provided substantive reviews, and the series is otherwise ready for integration. The next steps involve fixing the NULL-dereference risk and addressing the reflog expiration issue, either by adjusting the stash series’ tests or modifying the auto-maintenance logic.
