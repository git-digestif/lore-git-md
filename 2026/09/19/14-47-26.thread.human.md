Harald Nordgren’s fourteen-part patch series to introduce `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` and a matching-inspired fetch mode for shallow repositories is now complete and under final review, with two strategic questions still unresolved. The series aims to solve a long-standing performance problem: shallow repositories (created with `git clone --depth`) experience slow or hanging `git fetch` operations when the remote has many branches, because a refspec-less fetch negotiates history for all remote branches. The v14 design, endorsed by Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood, decouples the “what” (which refs to fetch) from the “where” (where to store them) using the new `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` configuration key, resolving earlier objections to overloading `remote.<name>.refmap`.

The implementation is now finalized, with all high-weight design questions settled. Command-line refspecs override `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` entirely, not coexist with it, and forced updates (`+`) belong on the refmap side. The series adds a `--limited-fetch` flag to `git remote add` for opting into the new mode, and the fifth and latest patch makes this flag the default in shallow repositories. Three key participants—Harald, Junio, and Phillip—have converged on the technical details, but two strategic decisions remain: whether to eliminate the dynamic branch-enumeration logic (Patch 3/5) on architectural grounds, and whether to merge only the foundational patches (1–3) now to unblock Collin’s dependent topic, deferring the ergonomic additions (Patches 4–5) for later. Matt Hunter’s recent clarification that Patches 1–3 are sufficient to resolve the textual conflicts strengthens the case for a partial merge, while also weakening Phillip’s architectural objection, since Patch 3/5 is now known to be required for unblocking the other work. The series remains queued in `next` as v11, but v11 and v12 are deprecated in favor of v14.
