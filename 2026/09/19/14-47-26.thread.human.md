The thread is about a patch series (now at v6) by Harald Nordgren that introduces a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories. The goal is to speed up `git fetch` operations by dynamically fetching only the branches tracked by local branches at the remote, rather than negotiating history for all remote branches. This is achieved through a new `remote.<name>.refmap` configuration that separates namespace mappings from fetch selection logic.

The series is technically complete but blocked on a design decision: what should happen when both `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap` are configured? Harald proposed that `remote.<name>.fetch` should take precedence, resolving a fatal error by ignoring the `refmap` setting, but Junio C Hamano has not yet responded to this proposal. Junio’s latest email notes a textual conflict with another merged topic (`followRemoteHAD`) and asks Harald to review the resolution, but does not address the design question. The series remains under active review, with no indication of when the design ambiguity will be resolved.

Key participants include Harald (the author), Junio (the maintainer, who raised the design concern), Phillip Wood (who provided design feedback in earlier versions), and D. Ben Knoble (who contributed to the design discussion). The series touches `remote.c`, `builtin/fetch.c`, `builtin/remote.c`, documentation, and tests, and introduces new behavior for shallow repositories while preserving backward compatibility for non-shallow ones. Open questions include edge-case handling (e.g., remotes with no default branch) and whether existing shallow repositories should be migrated automatically. The next step is likely Harald’s review of the conflict resolution in Junio’s `seen` branch.
