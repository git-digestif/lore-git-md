Harald Nordgren’s thirteen-iteration patch series introducing `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` to speed up fetches in shallow repositories is now in the final stages of review, with the v13 implementation complete and queued in `next`. The core idea—decoupling *which* refs to fetch from *where* to store them—has been endorsed by Junio and Phillip Wood, resolving the earlier design objection to overloading `remote.<name>.refmap`. The series is now blocked by two interlinked issues: the unresolved override semantics for command-line refspecs (or colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries) versus `fetchRefmap`, and an inadequate cover letter that Junio and Phillip insist must be expanded to include a patch-by-patch summary and design context.

The override semantics discussion has converged toward command-line refspecs overriding `fetchRefmap` entirely, but D. Ben Knoble’s latest review surfaces a new ambiguity: how should overlapping configurations behave, and how does this affect `@{push}` resolution? The v13 implementation preserves the current dual-storage behavior (where a colon-separated `fetch` entry stores the ref in both the explicit destination and the refmap’s destination), but this is now deprecated in favor of override semantics. Knoble’s question highlights that the interaction between `@{push}` and override semantics is not yet documented or implemented, which could become a new blocker if left unaddressed. Harald will need to clarify this in the v14 revision, either by updating the precedence rules or expanding the cover letter to explain the behavior.

The cover letter remains the most immediate blocker. Junio’s critique—that it fails to provide technical orientation for reviewers—is now compounded by the need to address Knoble’s questions about override semantics and `@{push}`. The urgency is heightened because the series blocks Collin’s `ch/fetch-followremotehead-lazy-validation` topic, and Knoble’s suggestion to merge Patches 1–3 (the foundational plumbing) separately offers a potential path forward if the override semantics edge case proves intractable. However, Junio has not signaled openness to partial merges, and the dependent topic’s requirements are not yet clear.

The series is otherwise ready for merging. The `@{push}` resolution fix (Patch 6/6) is mechanical and uncontroversial, the `--limited-fetch` flag and shallow-repository default (Patches 4–5) are well-tested and uncontentious, and the dynamic branch enumeration logic (Patch 3/6) is thoroughly covered by tests. The only remaining technical work is to finalize the override semantics and revise the cover letter, both of which are expected in v14. If these issues are resolved, the series is likely to graduate in the current cycle.
