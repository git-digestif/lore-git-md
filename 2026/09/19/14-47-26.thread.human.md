This thread is about Harald Nordgren’s patch series introducing `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`, a new configuration key that enables a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories. The goal is to speed up `git fetch` in shallow clones by dynamically fetching only the branches tracked by local branches at the remote, rather than negotiating history for all remote branches. After eleven iterations and a major redesign (v12), the series is now under active review for merging to `master`.

The v12 design is finalized and endorsed by Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood. It decouples the "what" (which refs to fetch) from the "where" (where to store them) by introducing `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` for the former and restoring `remote.<name>.refmap` to its original purpose (namespace mapping only). The precedence rule is settled: `remote.<name>.fetch` takes priority, followed by `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`, with dynamic branch enumeration as the fallback. Command-line refspecs override both. The series also adds a `--limited-fetch` flag to `git remote add` and makes it the default in shallow repositories.

The latest exchange between Phillip Wood and Junio clarified a minor detail: the `+` prefix for forced updates belongs on the refmap side (the "where"), not the `fetchRefmap` side (the "what"). This aligns with existing refspec behavior but introduces a small inconvenience for users who want to reject non-fast-forward updates for some branches while mapping all refs the same way. The discussion does not raise new objections or block the series, which remains on track to graduate in the current cycle. The only remaining work is addressing final review feedback on the v12 implementation.
