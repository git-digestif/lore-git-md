Here’s the **human-mode summary** for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread implements a long-awaited improvement for shallow repositories: a "matching-inspired" fetch mode that avoids the slowdowns and hangs of `git fetch` when the remote has many branches. The core idea is to dynamically fetch only the branches tracked by local branches at the remote, using a new configuration key, `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`, to separate *what* to fetch from *where* to store it. After twelve revisions, the design is finalized and endorsed by Junio and Phillip Wood, with the series now queued in `next` (as v11) but effectively superseded by v12, which is under active review for merging to `master`.

The last major hurdle is nailing down how command-line refspecs and colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries interact with the new `fetchRefmap`. The discussion has converged on "override semantics"—where explicit refspecs replace the refmap’s mapping for that ref—rather than the earlier "dual storage" model, which would have stored the ref in both destinations. This aligns with how command-line refspecs already work and avoids ambiguity. Junio’s latest feedback frames this as a choice between "do more than the refmap" (the deprecated behavior) and "do something else instead" (the proposed override), leaving the door open for Phillip Wood’s preferred model. The documentation also needs to clarify the minor inconvenience of forced-update placement (`+` on the refmap side), which requires users to split their configuration if they want to reject non-fast-forward updates for some branches.

Harald Nordgren is the author, and the series has strong support from Junio and Phillip Wood, who drove the redesign after objecting to an earlier version that overloaded `remote.<name>.refmap`. Ben Knoble’s suggestions on commit message clarity), and the series is expected to graduate in the current cycle once the override semantics are finalized and the documentation is polished.
