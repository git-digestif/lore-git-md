Harald Nordgren’s patch series introducing a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories has reached its eleventh iteration but is now at a crossroads. The series aims to speed up `git fetch` in shallow clones by dynamically fetching only the branches tracked by local branches at the remote, rather than negotiating history for all remote branches. While the v11 implementation is feature-complete and correctly handles edge cases—such as configuring both `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap`—it remains queued in `next` and blocked from graduating to `master` due to a fundamental design flaw.

The maintainer, Junio C Hamano, and key reviewer Phillip Wood have agreed that overloading `remote.<name>.refmap` to control both *which refs to fetch* and *where to store them* repeats the same inflexibility the series seeks to fix. The solution, now endorsed by both, is to introduce a dedicated `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` configuration key in v12. This will decouple the "what" (e.g., `*@{upstream}`) from the "where" (handled by `remote.<name>.refmap`), resolving the refspec-matching inconsistency and enabling per-branch overrides. The precedence rule will remain: `remote.<name>.fetch` overrides `remote.<name>.fetchRefs`, which in turn overrides dynamic branch enumeration. Forced updates (`+`) will belong on the refmap side, as they only make sense when storing refs.

The v11 series addressed all prior feedback, including Junio’s control-flow reorganization and D. Ben Knoble’s clarity suggestions, and introduced the `--limited-fetch` flag and shallow-repository default. However, these features will need to be reworked in v12 to use `fetchRefs` instead of overloading `refmap`. While the maintainer has validated the v11 implementation’s correctness, the series cannot proceed without the redesign. No timeline for v12 has been set, and Harald has yet to respond to the design discussion or commit to implementing the next iteration. The open questions now focus on implementation details—such as how `fetchRefs` will integrate with dynamic branch enumeration and whether it will support negation—rather than the overarching design.
