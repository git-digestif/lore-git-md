The thread tracks Harald Nordgren's v14 patch series, which introduces a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories by way of a new `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` configuration key. The series aims to speed up `git fetch` in shallow clones by fetching only the branches that local branches track at the remote, decoupling "which refs to fetch" from "where to store them." The design is now finalized and endorsed by maintainer Junio C Hamano and reviewer Phillip Wood, with the last major disagreement -- override semantics -- resolved in this latest exchange. Both now agree that any refspec carrying an explicit destination (`src:dst`), whether given on the command line or in configuration, ignores the refmap entirely, while destination-less refspecs remain subject to `fetchRefmap` or `--refmap`. Phillip Wood confirmed this implicit-override model in his most recent email, providing examples showing that command-line `--refmap` and config `fetchRefmap` behave equivalently, and corrected a slip in which he had previously referred to `.fetch` instead of `.fetchRefmap`.

The v14 implementation is complete and under final review; the series was last queued in `next` as v11, with v14 now the current iteration. Beyond the resolved override question, two strategic items remain open. First, Phillip Wood and Junio both favor explicit configuration over Patch 3/5's implicit dynamic branch enumeration, though Matt Hunter noted that Patches 1--3 are textually required to resolve conflicts with Collin's dependent topic `ch/remote-defer-follow-remote-head-validation`. Second, Phillip has endorsed a partial-merge path -- landing Patches 1--3 now to unblock that dependent topic while deferring Patches 4--5 and revisiting the implicit fallback behavior later. Until Junio decides on these final points and the series graduates to `master`, it remains under review.
