The RFC patch series proposing a warning in `git commit` for commits with dates preceding their parents has been withdrawn by its author, Yashwanth Sai. The withdrawal follows brian m. carlson's objection to the commit message's AI co-authorship, which violates Git's contribution guidelines under the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO). While the feature was designed to address a real usability issue—where out-of-order commit dates break history traversal commands like `git log --since`—the procedural blocker proved insurmountable in this iteration.

The patch introduced a new `advice.clockSkew` config key (defaulting to `true`) to warn users during `git commit` when a commit's date is earlier than its parent(s). The implementation handled timezones, skipped unparseable commits, and included test coverage, but it was warning-only and did not enforce date ordering. brian m. carlson had previously endorsed the feature as "useful," noting that the problem had been discussed on the list before, but the series will not proceed in its current form. If Yashwanth revisits the idea, they will need to rewrite the patch without AI assistance. For now, the thread is closed, and no further technical review or integration activity is expected.
