This thread is about a regression in Git 2.50 that swapped the default expiry times for reachable and unreachable reflog entries in `git reflog expire`. The bug, introduced by commit `85658275702b` in April 2025, caused reachable entries (documented to expire after 90 days) to expire after 30 days, while unreachable entries (documented to expire after 30 days) were incorrectly retained for 90 days.

Pushkar Singh posted a fix, now in its second version, which restores the correct default expiry periods by reversing the misordered fields in `reflog.h`. The updated patch also addresses feedback from Junio C Hamano and Jeff King by expanding test coverage to verify both reachable and unreachable expiry behavior. The new tests confirm that 60-day-old reachable entries are retained (validating the 90-day default), 100-day-old reachable entries are expired, 20-day-old unreachable entries are retained (validating the 30-day default), and 40-day-old unreachable entries are expired. The commit message now clearly explains the regression's origin and the fix's scope.

The fix is uncontroversial, narrowly targeted, and ready for merging. It touches only the misordered defaults without altering other logic or on-disk formats. Junio and Peff have reviewed the updated patch and raised no new technical concerns, though final nitpicks may still be addressed before merging. The original reporter, r.norouzi, identified the root cause and provided a reproduction case. The thread does not address whether the regression affects `git gc`, which internally calls `reflog expire`.
