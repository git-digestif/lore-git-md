This is a brief follow-up in the **reftable reflog timezone encoding** discussion. The thread remains a bugfix targeting the reftable backend, now focused on migration strategy and interoperability concerns.

Josh McKinney, the original reporter, clarifies his motivation: the issue surfaced while developing a third-party tool (referred to as "clanker") that interacts with Git repositories. His concern centers on how multiple tools interpret the same on-disk format, but he emphasizes the issue is not urgent and was caught in a development context. This reinforces the earlier consensus that the practical impact is limited—reflogs are short-lived and not shared across repositories—but underscores the importance of interoperability with other reftable implementations like JGit.

The discussion is now settled on aligning Git with the published reftable specification (signed minutes rather than signed HHMM). Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt agree the discrepancy is a bug, not a design choice, and that a one-time offline conversion might be the simplest migration path. However, given the bounded scope of the issue (at most 10 hours of misinterpretation, reflogs typically garbage-collected within 30 days), they question whether such a conversion is worth the effort. No patch has been posted yet, but the path forward appears clear: a straightforward fix without special backwards-compatibility handling.

The thread remains in the design-discussion phase, with no new technical details or test cases introduced in this reply. The key takeaway is that the consensus is forming around a low-risk, specification-compliant fix.
