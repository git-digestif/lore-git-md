This is a **bugfix series** targeting a long-standing issue in `git stash` where autostashing fails to correctly preserve staged index entries when `stash.index=true` is set. The series is now in its final form (v3), with only one critical issue remaining before it can be merged: a NULL-dereference risk in the error path of the new in-core merge logic.

This email is a **substantive technical follow-up** in the thread, where Phillip Wood and Thomas Bachem (author of the `tb/rerere-lock-grace` topic) confirm the root cause of the `t5520-pull.sh` test breakage that previously blocked the series. The issue stems from an interaction between the stash series and auto-maintenance: the stash series reduces the number of reflog entries generated during failed merges by eight, shifting the timing of reflog expiration under the test’s fixed timestamp (2005). This causes `git pull --rebase` to misidentify the upstream commit due to missing reflog entries. The fix—pinning `gc.reflogExpire` and `gc.reflogExpireUnreachable` to `never` in the test setup—has already been submitted and merged to `master`, fully resolving the breakage.

The email also clarifies a minor detail about the reflog entries: the reduction comes from `git reset --quiet --refresh` calls in the stash series, which no longer write reflog entries, rather than from the autostash tests themselves. This is a harmless side effect of the cleanup, not a design flaw. The discussion underscores that the interaction is purely a test-environment quirk, with no implications for real-world usage.

With the `t5520` breakage resolved, the series is now blocked only by the NULL-dereference risk in Patch 5/5, where `merge_incore_nonrecursive()` may return with `result.tree` uninitialized on catastrophic failure (e.g., missing objects). The author has confirmed the issue and will address it by checking `result.clean` before dereferencing `result.tree`. This fix is straightforward and expected to be uncontroversial.

The series itself is well-motivated and thoroughly tested, addressing a real bug with clear edge cases. The in-core merge approach eliminates a race condition in the original subprocess-based logic, improving robustness. If the NULL-dereference fix lands as planned, the series should be ready for integration into `next`. For now, it remains a high-priority bugfix with no unresolved technical objections.
