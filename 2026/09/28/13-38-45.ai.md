This is the fourth patch in Christian Couder’s five-part v4 series introducing `uploadpack.lazyFetchTrusted`, a server-side mechanism to prevent infinite recursion when lazy fetching from promisor remotes. The patch adds a recursion depth counter (`GIT_INTERNAL_LAZY_FETCH_DEPTH`) to the promisor-remote subsystem, capping the maximum depth at 5 to avoid the pathological case where a repository is configured as its own promisor remote. The safeguard is internal and not user-configurable, addressing Junio’s earlier concern about recursion risks in the v2 review.

The implementation touches `promisor-remote.c` and `environment.h`, introducing a new environment variable (`GIT_INTERNAL_LAZY_FETCH_DEPTH`) that tracks recursion depth across child processes. The depth counter is incremented in `fetch_objects()` and checked in `lazy_fetch_objects()`; if the limit is exceeded, a warning is emitted and the fetch is aborted. The patch also updates `try_promisor_remotes()` to pass the depth parameter through the call chain. The choice of 5 as the maximum depth balances safety with legitimate nesting (e.g., `git fetch` invoking `index-pack` to lazily fetch REF_DELTA bases), and the cast from `git_env_ulong()` to `int` follows Junio’s acceptance of this approach in v4.

A new test in `t/t0410-partial-clone.sh` verifies the recursion safeguard by configuring two partial clones as mutual promisor remotes and confirming that lazy fetching fails with the expected warning after exactly 5 nested fetches. The test also checks that the recursion is bounded by counting the number of `git fetch` subprocesses spawned. The patch is a clean, targeted fix with no behavioral impact outside the recursion case, and the test coverage is thorough. Junio’s v3 review nits (type choice for the depth counter) are fully addressed. This patch is a prerequisite for the final patch (5/5), which activates the server-side trust model by conditionally setting `GIT_NO_LAZY_FETCH=0` for trusted repositories.
