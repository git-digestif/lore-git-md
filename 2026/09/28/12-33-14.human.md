This is a brief update in the ongoing effort to fix a bug in `git stash` where autostashing fails to correctly handle staged index entries when `stash.index=true` is set. The series, now in its third version, has hit a snag: patches 2–5 were ejected from Junio’s `seen` branch after causing a test breakage in `t5520-pull.sh`.

D. Ben Knoble shares the results of a bisect he ran on the `seen` branch to isolate the interaction causing the breakage. The culprit appears to be `4f65642eb0` (Merge branch 'tb/rerere-lock-grace' into jch), a merge commit from Thomas Bachem’s work on rerere lock handling. The breakage manifests as an unexpected change in `git pull --rebase`'s fork-point detection, shifting the upstream commit argument from `f29aa667ce` to `ae9857430e`—even though the test doesn’t involve stashing. This suggests the stash series inadvertently alters rebase’s fork-point logic, likely through a side effect in index or ref-handling code paths. Knoble’s bisect script (included in the email) confirms the interaction but doesn’t yet explain *why* it happens.

The next step is to dig into how `tb/rerere-lock-grace` and the stash series might interfere with each other. Thomas Bachem is cc’d, but no immediate resolution is proposed. Meanwhile, two other issues remain unresolved: a NULL-dereference risk in Patch 5/5 (where `merge_incore_nonrecursive()` could return with `result.tree` uninitialized) and the already-fixed verbosity regression. Until the `t5520` breakage is understood, the series won’t re-enter `seen`. The bisect result narrows the scope but leaves the root cause—and the fix—still unclear.
