This is a reply in the ongoing **Git tutorial rewrite** thread, a documentation effort focused on pedagogy. Junio C Hamano responds to Julia Evans' proposal for a **two-phase approach** (first removing the current tutorial, then adding the new one) with cautious approval, but clarifies a key distinction: his concern was never about broken links (404 errors) but about **stale links**—those that still resolve but point to outdated or irrelevant content.

Junio explains that while broken links are easy to fix (just remove them), stale links require human judgment to assess whether the linked material is still accurate and useful. His worry about external links (like those on `git-scm.com/doc/ext`) is that they might silently become stale, offering misleading guidance to beginners. He agrees with Julia’s two-phase plan as a practical way to audit deletions and avoid broken references, but his underlying concern about stale content remains unresolved.

The email also reveals Junio’s historical reluctance to remove the original tutorial (written by Linus Torvalds) when `gittutorial-2.adoc` was added, framing it as a missed opportunity to modernize. He now sees this rewrite as a chance to correct that, but his xkcd 927 reference ("Standards") hints at skepticism about whether the new tutorial will truly replace the old one cleanly—or just add another layer of complexity. The thread is now at a crossroads: the logistics of landing the change are settled, but the deeper question of how to handle stale content (both internal and external) lingers.
