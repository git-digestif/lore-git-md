This is a **follow-up discussion** in the ongoing `stash: fix autostash with staged index entries` series, focusing on the root cause of the `t5520-pull.sh` test breakage. D. Ben Knoble (the series author) digs deeper into the mechanics of the interaction between `git pull --rebase` and reflog expiration, expressing surprise at how `--fork-point` behaves in this context.

The email confirms the earlier diagnosis: the breakage stems from a timing shift in reflog expiration caused by the stash series’ tests generating fewer reflog entries (eight, from failed merges) than expected. This moves the expiration point past a critical fetch in test 68, leaving the reflog empty when test 69’s `git pull --rebase` tries to compute the fork point. The fix—pinning `gc.reflogExpire` to `never` in the test setup—remains the agreed solution, but Knoble’s confusion highlights an unexpected edge case in `git pull`’s behavior.

The key revelation here is that `git pull --rebase` *does* use `--fork-point` (and thus reads reflogs) even when an `<upstream>` is explicitly provided. This contradicts Knoble’s initial assumption (based on the `git-rebase` man page) that `--fork-point` would be disabled in such cases. The discrepancy suggests either a documentation gap or a subtle logic error in `builtin/pull.c:run_rebase()`, though Knoble does not speculate further. His plan to trace the test locally implies this is more of a curiosity than a blocker—the fix is already in hand, and the breakage is understood as a test-environment quirk rather than a design flaw in the stash series.

For a busy reader, this email clarifies that the `t5520` issue is resolved in principle, but it also flags an oddity in `git pull`’s behavior that might warrant future attention. The stash series itself remains on track for integration once the NULL-dereference risk in Patch 5/5 is addressed.
