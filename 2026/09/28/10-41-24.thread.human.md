This thread proposes converting Git's plain-text `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage, `gitbreaking-changes(7)`, so that upcoming breaking changes are discoverable via `git help` and `man`. Kristoffer Haugsbakk authored the five-patch v2 series, which moves the content into `Documentation/gitbreaking-changes.adoc`, leaves a pointer at the old location, and wires the new page into the build system and command list. The series also adds a "living draft" admonition to clarify that the document is not a permanent record, and addresses the post-Git-3.0 lifecycle by proposing either a living draft (old entries deleted after each release) or retaining only the most recent breaking-change discussion.

Review has converged. Patrick Steinhardt endorsed the goal and suggested a pragmatic retention window until Git 3.0 is widely adopted; Junio C Hamano raised questions about long-term utility but has signaled tentative approval, contingent on the post-transition awkwardness being manageable. D. Ben Knoble offered only surface-level support. The only known blockers are minor merge conflicts with `ps/ref-storage-format` (in `master`) and `bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase` (in `seen`), which require moving their additions to the new manpage during integration. No merge to any integration branch has been announced yet.

Kristoffer has now sent a status inquiry to Junio noting that the topic has not appeared in `seen` or the "What's cooking" reports and that no actionable feedback remains, explicitly asking what the next step is. The series is technically complete and unblocked from the author's side; it now awaits the maintainer picking it up for integration.
