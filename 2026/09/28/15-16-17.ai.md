This is a reply in the **Git tutorial rewrite** thread, a documentation effort focused on pedagogy. Junio C Hamano responds to Julia Evans' proposal for a two-phase approach (removing the current tutorial first, then adding the new one) with cautious approval, framing it as a practical way to avoid repeating past mistakes.

He agrees that splitting the change into smaller, independent pieces makes review easier and acknowledges that the end goal—replacing the outdated tutorial—is the right one. His reference to the xkcd comic ("Standards") underscores a long-standing project tendency: when faced with outdated content, contributors often add new material rather than remove the old, leading to redundancy. The current tutorial (`gittutorial.adoc`) is a prime example—it remains in the tree despite being widely recognized as obsolete, while a follow-up (`gittutorial-2.adoc`) was added years later without removing the original. Junio’s concern is that the project has historically lacked the confidence to *remove* outdated documentation, even when it’s no longer useful, and he wants to avoid perpetuating that pattern.

His reply does not raise new technical or pedagogical objections to Julia’s rewrite itself. Instead, it reinforces the logistical wisdom of her two-phase approach: by first removing the old tutorial and its references, the project can audit what is being lost and ensure no useful content is inadvertently discarded. This aligns with his earlier preference for self-contained documentation and avoids the pitfalls of relying on external links or half-measures. The thread remains in the design phase, but Junio’s response signals that the proposed workflow is a viable path forward—provided the removal phase is thorough and the new tutorial is ready to take its place. No timeline or integration status is implied; this is still a discussion about *how* to land the change, not *when*.
