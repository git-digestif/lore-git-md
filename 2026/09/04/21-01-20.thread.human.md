This thread fixes a subtle but important bug in Git's `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism for `git push`. The feature is meant to prevent accidental overwrites by checking that the remote branch's tip is present in the local reflog—but it was incorrectly checking the reflog of a *local* branch with the same name as the remote destination, rather than the branch actually being pushed. This could cause false rejections (e.g., when pushing from a differently named branch) or even unintended data loss in edge cases.

Tyler Cipriani posted a two-patch v2 series that corrects the logic to check the reflog of the pushed ref, adds special handling for `HEAD`-based pushes and detached HEAD states, and improves advice messages. The v2 commit message clarifies that the existing behavior of rejecting detached HEAD pushes (when the same-named local branch lacks the remote tip) is intentional, and the reflog limitation for detached HEAD is accepted as a necessary trade-off. D. Ben Knoble, who provided a substantive review of v1, confirmed the root cause and verified the fix, and his only remaining question—whether the detached HEAD rejection was intentional—was resolved by the v2 clarification. His "Thanks!" reply to v2 signals the series is now ready for maintainer attention.

The patches touch core push logic in `remote.c`, update all transport layers for consistency, and add eight new test cases. No open questions remain, and the series is based on the `maint` branch, indicating it's a bugfix suitable for backporting. The fix doesn't change on-disk formats and leaves `--force-with-lease` behavior unaffected.
