This thread fixes two critical bugs in Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism for `git push`. The feature is meant to prevent force-pushing unless the remote branch’s tip has been integrated into your local work, but it had two flaws: it checked the wrong reflog (the local branch matching the *remote* name, not the branch you’re actually pushing) and incorrectly rejected fast-forward pushes of non-branch refs like tags or detached HEADs. Tyler Cipriani’s v5 series—now three patches—corrects the reflog lookup, exempts fast-forwards from checks, and provides clear advice for rejected cases.

The series is technically complete and has addressed all prior feedback. Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt reviewed earlier versions, focusing on implementation robustness and documentation clarity, while D. Ben Knoble raised a backwards-compatibility concern that v5 resolved by exempting fast-forwards. Knoble’s recent review of the fast-forward logic confirmed the implementation is correct but suggested a more descriptive variable name for maintainability; Tyler agreed and will adjust it in v6. The patches touch `remote.c` and add 108 lines of test coverage in `t/t5533-push-cas.sh`, with no on-disk format changes or external dependencies. The series is ready for final review and likely merger.
