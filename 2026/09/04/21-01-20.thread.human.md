This thread is a two-patch bugfix series by Tyler Cipriani that fixes a long-standing flaw in Git's `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism for `git push`. The issue causes the feature to incorrectly check the reflog of a local branch matching the *remote* destination branch name (e.g., `refs/heads/main`) rather than the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `refs/heads/src` in `src:main`). This can lead to false rejections or unintended data loss, particularly when pushing from a differently named local branch or a detached HEAD.

The first patch (previously summarized) fixes the core logic in `remote.c` to consult the reflog of the pushed ref, adds special handling for `HEAD`-based pushes and detached HEAD states, and clarifies the existing rejection policy for detached HEAD pushes. The second patch, now under discussion, improves the advice message shown when a `--force-if-includes` push is rejected due to a detached HEAD. It introduces a new `ref->unverifiable` flag to distinguish these rejections from remote-update rejections and adds a configurable advice message (`advice.pushRefUnverifiable`) that guides users to either specify the expected remote tip with `--force-with-lease` or bypass the check entirely. The patch updates all transport layers and the test suite for consistency.

The series is now in its second version (`v2`), incorporating minor polish from the initial review by D. Ben Knoble, who provided substantive feedback confirming the root cause and verifying the fix. The detached HEAD policy (reject by default) and the reflog limitation for detached HEAD states are now explicitly documented as settled trade-offs. No open questions remain, and the series is ready for final review. It is based on the `maint` branch and does not affect `--force-with-lease` behavior.
