This thread fixes a long-standing bug in Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism, which incorrectly rejected fast-forward pushes of non-branch refs (like tags or detached HEADs) and checked the wrong reflog when pushing differently named branches. The v5 series, now three patches, is technically complete and addresses all prior review feedback from Junio Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, and D. Ben Knoble.

The core issue was that `--force-if-includes` checked the reflog of the local branch matching the remote destination (e.g., `refs/heads/main` when pushing to `origin/main`), rather than the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `refs/heads/src` in `src:main`). This caused false rejections for workflows like `git push origin <TAG>:hotfix`, even when the push was a fast-forward. The v5 series fixes this by (1) consulting the reflog of the pushed ref, (2) providing actionable advice for rejected cases (e.g., `--force-with-lease`), and (3) exempting fast-forward pushes from reflog checks entirely, resolving the existing bug.

Knoble’s latest review focuses on the fast-forward exemption (patch 3/3), questioning whether the `deferred_reject_reason` variable name clearly communicates its purpose. While he confirms the logic is correct, he suggests a more descriptive name or comment to aid future maintainability. This is a minor readability concern, not a blocker, and the series remains uncontroversial. All prior feedback—including Knoble’s earlier backwards-compatibility concern—has been resolved.
