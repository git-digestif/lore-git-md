This thread fixes two critical bugs in Git's `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism for `git push`. The feature, designed to prevent force-pushing unless the user has integrated upstream changes, was incorrectly checking the reflog of the *local* branch matching the remote destination (e.g., `refs/heads/main`) rather than the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `refs/heads/src` in `src:main`). It was also unnecessarily rejecting fast-forward pushes of non-branch refs like tags or detached HEADs, breaking workflows.

Tyler Cipriani has posted the sixth and final version of the series, which is now technically complete. The fixes consult the reflog of the pushed ref (`ref->peer_ref->name`), exempt fast-forward pushes from checks, and introduce actionable advice for unverifiable refs (e.g., detached HEADs or tags). The v6 update includes only readability improvements: a clearer variable name (`needs_force_reject_reason`), typo fixes, and minor documentation tweaks. All prior feedback from Patrick Steinhardt, Junio C Hamano, and D. Ben Knoble has been addressed, and the series includes 108 lines of new test coverage for edge cases.

The series is ready for merge unless new issues are raised. There are minor textual conflicts with another topic and a recent `maint` commit, but these are not blockers. The implementation is backwards-compatible with `--force-with-lease` and maintains the feature’s safety guarantees while fixing its long-standing UX flaws.
