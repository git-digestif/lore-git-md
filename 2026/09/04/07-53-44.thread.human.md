This thread is about a patch series (v2, 3 patches) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations—`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`—so it defers until the sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend. The goal is to prevent maintenance from interfering with intermediate steps and ensure consistency across all three commands.

The series is under review and technically complete, with all prior feedback from Phillip Wood and Patrick Steinhardt addressed. Junio C Hamano reviewed the third patch and suggested a mechanical change to store the cached `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` string as a `const char *` instead of a `struct strbuf` for clarity, which the author has agreed to include. The remaining discussions focus on polish: commit message clarity (avoiding phrases that imply active interference, like "opposition" when it’s really lock contention), parameter naming (`env` vs. `buf` in a helper function), and whether to consolidate maintenance calls into a single exit path. Phillip Wood also suggests improving test coverage by asserting that maintenance *doesn’t* run during intermediate steps and consolidating tests to avoid duplication.

No substantive objections remain. The only open questions are minor: a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might help (not a blocker), whether disabling both `maintenance.auto` and `gc.auto` is necessary (Patrick Steinhardt questions the redundancy), and the undocumented format of `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` (a clarity gap, not a functional issue). The series is on track for integration once the author addresses the latest feedback on commit messages and tests.
