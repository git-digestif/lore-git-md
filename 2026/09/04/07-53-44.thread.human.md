This thread is about a patch series (v5) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git's auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the entire sequence completes, preventing interference with intermediate steps and ensuring consistent behavior across all three commands. The series is now in Junio C Hamano's `seen` branch with intent to queue, meaning it's under review but likely to move to `next` soon unless new objections arise.

The design controversy—whether to trigger maintenance from the sequencer or delegate it to built-in commands—has been fully settled in favor of the latter. Thomas documented why the sequencer can't reliably handle this: single-pick cases (like `cherry-pick <commit>`) bypass the main `pick_commits()` loop entirely, exiting early through other paths. The implementation uses a reusable helper to disable auto maintenance for all spawned commands (`git commit`, `git merge`, exec commands) by setting `maintenance.auto=false` via `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS`. Tests in `t/t3418-rebase-continue.sh` and `t/t3510-cherry-pick-sequence.sh` verify maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion, not during intermediate steps.

Key reviewers—Patrick Steinhardt, Phillip Wood, and Junio—have all had their feedback addressed. Patrick initially objected to delegating maintenance to built-in commands but was convinced by Thomas's analysis of single-pick edge cases. Phillip Wood's concerns about test coverage were resolved with clearer annotations. The series is code-identical to v4, with only commit message and test improvements. The only unresolved item is a hypothetical concern about very long sequences potentially benefiting from mid-sequence repacking, but no concrete issue has been raised. The series appears ready to proceed.
