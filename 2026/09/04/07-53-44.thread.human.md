This thread is about a patch series (v4) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git's auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend and avoiding interference with intermediate steps. The series was previously marked "Will queue" by Junio Hamano, indicating it was on track for merging, and all prior feedback—including from Phillip Wood and Patrick Steinhardt—had been addressed.

The latest development is a substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt challenging the series' design. He argues that the sequencer *does* have a single exit point (`pick_commits()` in `sequencer.c`) where maintenance could be triggered consistently, rather than scattering calls across the built-in commands (`builtin/rebase.c` and `builtin/revert.c`). His alternative proposal would simplify the implementation and reduce the risk of missing edge cases, such as when the sequencer is aborted. This is the first significant pushback on the series' architecture, and the author's response will determine whether a v5 is needed or if the current design's trade-offs are justified.

The series is technically complete, with all three patches on-list and prior feedback incorporated, including a reusable `git_config_append_parameter()` helper, caching of the config string in `replay_ctx`, and removal of a redundant `gc.auto=0` setting. The latest iteration (v4) also addressed Junio's and Patrick's critiques about commit message verbosity, tightening them significantly. However, Phillip Wood recently identified a test coverage gap in the rebase test, which doesn't verify suppression during conflict resolution. The thread is now at a crossroads: while Junio signaled intent to queue the series, Patrick's review raises a legitimate architectural concern that could require a rewrite. The outcome will shape how auto maintenance is handled across all sequencer commands.
