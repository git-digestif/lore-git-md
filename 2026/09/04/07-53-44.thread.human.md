This thread is about a patch series (v4) by Thomas Bachem that defers Git’s auto maintenance until the completion of sequencer-driven operations like `rebase`, `cherry-pick`, and `revert`. The goal is to prevent maintenance from running during intermediate steps, ensuring consistent behavior across all three commands. The series was previously marked as "Will queue" by Junio C Hamano, placing it in the `seen` branch, but a recent **substantive review** from Patrick Steinhardt has reopened the design discussion.

The core dispute centers on *where* to trigger maintenance. Thomas’s current design delegates this responsibility to the built-in commands (`rebase.c` and `revert.c`), while Patrick argues the sequencer itself has a single exit point (`pick_commits()`) where maintenance could be triggered consistently. Thomas has now clarified that single-commit operations (`cherry-pick <commit>`, `revert <commit>`) bypass `pick_commits()` entirely, exiting early through `continue_single_pick()` or `skip_single_pick()`. This means Patrick’s proposal would miss these cases unless the sequencer also hooks those early-return paths. Thomas has offered to implement either approach but is waiting for Patrick to decide whether the series needs a v5 rewrite or can proceed as-is.

All prior feedback—from Phillip Wood, Junio, and earlier Patrick reviews—has been addressed, including test coverage clarifications and minor wording tweaks. The technical foundation (a reusable `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` helper) is uncontroversial and ready. The series touches `sequencer.c`, `config.c`, and the built-in commands, with tests verifying maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion. The outcome hinges on Patrick’s response to Thomas’s latest clarification.
