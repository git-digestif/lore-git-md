This thread is about a patch series (v3) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git's auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the entire sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend and avoiding interference with intermediate steps. The series is now technically complete, with all feedback from reviewers—including Phillip Wood, Patrick Steinhardt, and Junio C Hamano—addressed.

The key change moves the `run_auto_maintenance()` call from the sequencer to the built-in commands (`builtin/rebase.c` and `builtin/revert.c`), consolidating maintenance into a single exit path per command. This aligns with Patrick Steinhardt’s preference for a unified design and simplifies the rule to "maintenance runs once when done." The series also introduces a reusable helper, `git_config_append_parameter()`, to build the `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` string, and caches it in `replay_ctx` as a `const char *`. Tests have been updated to verify maintenance runs only at sequence completion, not during intermediate steps.

The only remaining task is a v4 with polished commit messages. Patrick Steinhardt has emphasized the need for human-written messages that justify the design choices (e.g., why maintenance is delegated to callers) and avoid LLM-generated phrasing. Once this is addressed, the series is likely to be merged to `next`. No open technical objections remain, though Phillip Wood has noted a hypothetical edge case about long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might help performance—this will be monitored in real-world usage rather than blocking the series.
