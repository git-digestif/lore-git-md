This thread is about a two-patch series (v2) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git’s auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend and avoiding interference with intermediate steps. The series is technically complete and under review, with all prior feedback addressed.

Key participants include Thomas Bachem (author), Phillip Wood (substantive reviewer who approved the approach and suggested expanding scope to all three commands), and Junio C Hamano (maintainer, who provided a minor mechanical suggestion about storing the cached config string as a `const char *`). The latest feedback from Patrick Steinhardt is surface-level, focusing on commit message clarity and naming in the new `git_config_append_parameter()` helper. No technical objections remain, though Phillip Wood has noted a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might improve performance—this is being monitored rather than blocking progress.

The series touches `sequencer.c`, `config.c`, and test files, introducing a reusable helper for building `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` strings and caching the config in `replay_ctx`. Tests verify maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion. The next revision will incorporate Junio’s and Patrick’s minor suggestions, but no major changes are expected. The series is on track for integration once these final tweaks are made.
