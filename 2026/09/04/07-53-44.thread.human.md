This thread is about a patch series by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations (`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, `git revert`). The goal is to defer maintenance until sequence completion, matching the behavior of the apply backend and preventing interference with intermediate steps. The series is now in its third version (v3) and is technically complete, with all prior feedback from reviewers—including Phillip Wood, Patrick Steinhardt, and Junio C Hamano—addressed.

The key change moves the `run_auto_maintenance()` call from the sequencer to the built-in commands (`builtin/rebase.c` and `builtin/revert.c`), consolidating maintenance into a single exit path per command. This aligns with Patrick Steinhardt’s preference for a unified design and simplifies the rule to "the command runs maintenance once when done." The series also introduces a reusable helper, `git_config_append_parameter()`, to build the `GIT_CONFIG_PARAMETERS` string and caches it in `replay_ctx` as a `const char *`. Tests have been extended to verify maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion and not during intermediate steps.

Junio C Hamano has signaled intent to queue the series ("Will queue"), meaning it will enter `seen` shortly and is likely to graduate to `next` in the next integration cycle. The only remaining prerequisite is a v4 with revised commit messages to address Patrick Steinhardt’s concerns about clarity and human authorship, as well as Junio’s stylistic critique about verbosity. No open technical objections remain, though Phillip Wood has noted a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might improve performance—this is a watch item rather than a blocker. The author has agreed to revise the commit messages to justify the design choice of delegating maintenance to callers and ensure they read as human-written. Once v4 is posted, the series is expected to be merged.
