This thread is about a patch series (v2) by Thomas Bachem that adjusts when Git's auto maintenance runs during sequencer-driven operations like `rebase`, `cherry-pick`, and `revert`. The goal is to defer maintenance until the entire sequence completes, matching the behavior of the apply backend and avoiding interference with intermediate steps. The series is technically complete and under review, with all prior feedback addressed.

Key participants include the author (Thomas Bachem), who implemented the deferral logic and expanded its scope to cover all three sequencer commands; Phillip Wood, who reviewed the initial version and suggested broader coverage; and Patrick Steinhardt, whose latest review raises a substantive technical point about the series' third patch. Steinhardt questions whether disabling both `maintenance.auto` and `gc.auto` is necessary, arguing that disabling only `maintenance.auto` should suffice since Git's maintenance infrastructure now relies exclusively on `git maintenance`. This is a correctness concern about the maintenance subsystem's behavior. Junio C Hamano also reviewed the series and suggested a minor mechanical change to improve code clarity, which the author plans to incorporate.

The series touches the sequencer, auto maintenance, and config subsystems, introducing a reusable helper (`git_config_append_parameter()`) and caching the config string in `replay_ctx`. Tests verify that maintenance runs exactly once at sequence completion. Open questions include Steinhardt's technical point about redundant config settings and a hypothetical edge case about very long sequences where mid-sequence repacking might improve performance, though no concrete issue has been identified. The series is otherwise ready for integration, pending resolution of these minor points.
