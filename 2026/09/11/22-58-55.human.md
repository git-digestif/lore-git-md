This is a **bugfix patch** addressing a long-standing flaw in Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism. The author, Tyler Cipriani, responds to **substantive review feedback** from Patrick Steinhardt on the first patch of a two-patch series (v3 1/2). The core issue—checking the wrong reflog when pushing from a differently named local branch—remains unresolved in `master`, but the series is technically complete and ready for merge once these edge cases are clarified.

Tyler accepts most of Steinhardt’s suggestions: he will add `RESOLVE_REF_READING` to the `HEAD` resolution logic (though he disagrees on `RESOLVE_REF_NO_RECURSE`, arguing that symref chains like `STABLE -> HEAD -> main` should be followed), and he plans to generalize the check to reject any non-reference revision (e.g., `HEAD~`, object IDs, tags) rather than special-casing `HEAD`. He also agrees to document the rationale for allowing deletions, which Steinhardt flagged as a potential risk. The only surface-level change is adding a blank line between tests in `t/t5533-push-cas.sh`.

The discussion reveals two key design decisions that may need broader consensus: whether symref chains should be resolved recursively (Tyler’s preference) or not (Steinhardt’s), and whether tags/object IDs should be rejected outright (both agree, but the implementation is still pending). The series already includes 65 lines of new test coverage, but Steinhardt’s feedback highlights a gap: the test suite lacks explicit coverage for push-to-delete scenarios under `--force-if-includes`. Tyler’s responses suggest he will address these in v4, which should resolve the last technical concerns before Junio considers merging. The patch touches `remote.c`, `t/t5533-push-cas.sh`, and introduces no new on-disk formats.
