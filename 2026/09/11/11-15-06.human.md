This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-part series refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) fsck checks to make them pluggable. The patch moves loose-object verification from `builtin/fsck.c` into the loose ODB backend (`odb/source-loose.c`), completing the architectural shift begun in earlier patches. The change is a pure refactoring with no user-visible behavior changes, but it fixes a latent bug in the progress-meter logic where the meter jumped from 0 to 256 at completion instead of updating incrementally.

Toon Claes’s review raises two technical points. First, he suggests documenting that the new `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` flag is mutually exclusive with `ODB_FSCK_PROGRESS`, since `--verbose` disables the progress meter. Second, he asks whether the code should guard against a NULL `object_cb` in the `fsck_loose` helper, though he acknowledges the callback is required by the existing contract. Both points are minor and do not challenge the patch’s core design. Toon also notes the use of an `error_found` flag to propagate errors is inherited from the old code and is not a new issue.

The patch itself is clean and well-structured, removing 90 lines from `builtin/fsck.c` and adding 89 lines to `odb/source-loose.c`. It introduces the `ODB_FSCK_VERBOSE` flag to preserve the original behavior of printing status messages when `--verbose` is passed, and threads the progress meter and callback through the new `odb_fsck_options` structure. With this patch, all fsck checks are now implemented in their respective ODB backends, and `builtin/fsck.c` no longer contains any backend-specific verification logic. The series is now complete and ready for integration, with all feedback from earlier iterations addressed. Karthik Nayak’s earlier reviews were surface-level, focusing on wording and spelling rather than substantive correctness, and Toon’s feedback here is similarly focused on clarity and robustness rather than design.
