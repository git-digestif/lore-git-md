This thread is about Harald Nordgren’s patch series adding a `--matched-only` option to `git range-diff`, which filters the output to show only commits present in both input ranges. The feature aims to help users focus on how surviving commits changed during rebases or other range comparisons, rather than seeing added or dropped commits. The series is now in its third and final version, with all technical concerns resolved—most notably, a layering violation in error handling was fixed by reverting to `error()` in the library layer and letting the CLI front end call `die()`.

The patch is ready for merging and currently sits in Junio’s `seen` branch, but it risks being dropped due to lack of review rather than any technical flaw. Junio has signaled that the patch will be removed soon unless a reviewer steps in, though his feedback has been procedural rather than substantive since the v3 update. Harald has implemented all requested changes and considers the series complete.

The only new development is Ben Knoble’s substantive review, which questions the feature’s motivation. He asks why a user would want to hide added or deleted commits, suggesting that clearer use cases in the commit message or documentation could improve the patch’s reception. While he doesn’t oppose the feature outright, his feedback highlights a potential gap in justification that might explain why the patch has received little attention. The thread is now at a crossroads: either Harald or another contributor could address Ben’s concern by expanding the motivation, or the patch may stall further due to the lack of broader engagement.
