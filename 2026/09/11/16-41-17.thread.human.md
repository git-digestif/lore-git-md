This thread is about a patch series by Harald Nordgren that adds a `--matched-only` option to `git range-diff`. The feature filters the output to show only commits present in both input ranges, addressing a common pain point when reviewing rebases: the default output often buries relevant changes under hundreds of unrelated commits. Harald’s v3 patch is technically complete, resolving the last architectural concern about error-handling layering, and is currently queued in Junio’s `seen` branch. However, Junio has signaled it will be dropped soon unless a reviewer steps in to validate its conceptual fit.

The discussion has clarified that while some workflows can achieve similar filtering with existing syntax (e.g., symmetric difference or careful input selection), these workarounds don’t cover the general rebase case—particularly when the base has advanced since the previous round. Junio’s emails confirm that the three-revision form of `range-diff` cannot replace `--matched-only` in this scenario, reinforcing the feature’s necessity for the workflow it targets. Ben Knoble and Matt Hunter raised questions about redundancy, but their points were addressed by Junio and Harald, who demonstrated the feature’s unique value. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s recent reply adds anecdotal support for the use case but doesn’t shift the debate.

The patch is low-risk and additive, touching only the presentation layer with no on-disk format changes. It’s ready for merging but stalled due to lack of review rather than technical objections. The ball is now in the court of potential reviewers to confirm whether the feature’s utility justifies its addition to `git range-diff`. Without that validation, Junio will likely drop it from `seen`.
