This is the latest reply in the ongoing bugfix series for `--force-if-includes`. The patch targets a long-standing flaw in `git push` where the safety mechanism incorrectly checks the reflog of the *local* branch matching the *remote* destination name (e.g., `refs/heads/main`) instead of the branch actually being pushed (e.g., `refs/heads/src` in `src:main`). The series also improves advice messages for detached HEAD scenarios.

In this email, Tyler Cipriani responds to Junio C Hamano’s **substantive review** of the first patch in v3. Junio’s questions focused on whether silent returns for edge cases (missing `peer_ref` and deletions) were intentional or potential bugs. Tyler clarifies that the silent return for `NULL` `peer_ref` is an ordinary path—triggered when `apply_push_cas` checks all advertised refs, even those not being pushed—and proposes moving this check upstream to `apply_push_cas` for clarity. He also confirms that the silent return for deletions is intentional (deletions have no local history to verify) and notes that existing tests cover push-to-delete scenarios, though the test suite lacks explicit coverage for `--force-if-includes` deletions. For the `get_local_ref` guard, Tyler agrees to remove it in v4, as it never triggers in practice.

The exchange resolves Junio’s technical questions without introducing new concerns. Tyler plans to incorporate this feedback, along with Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier suggestions (generalizing `HEAD` resolution and tightening symbolic reference validation), into v4. The series remains technically complete and ready for merge, with no objections to its core design. The forthcoming v4 will also include minor documentation and test formatting improvements. If you’ve been following this thread, this email confirms the fix is on track; if you’re new, it’s a well-motivated bugfix with thorough test coverage and active maintainer engagement.
