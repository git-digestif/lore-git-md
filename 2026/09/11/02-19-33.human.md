This is a CI/build system follow-up in the thread updating Git's CI from Debian 11 to Debian 12. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Patrick Steinhardt's suggestion to use the `oldstable` Docker tag instead of a hard-coded `debian:12`. Peff explains that the Debian release and LTS cycles are misaligned: the `oldstable` tag does not consistently point to the oldest supported LTS release, and using it would require annual CI updates or risk running on unsupported releases. He concludes that the current approach—manually updating the version number every two years when support ends—remains the most reliable, despite the minor maintenance burden.

The discussion is pragmatic and narrowly focused on CI infrastructure. Peff's analysis is thorough and grounded in Debian's release cadence, making it unlikely to be controversial. The thread remains on track for merging the original patch (now in `seen` with Junio's intent to merge), with this exchange serving as a refinement rather than a blocker. The open questions about 32-bit testing and older Ubuntu LTS jobs remain unaddressed, but this reply keeps the focus on the immediate Debian update.
