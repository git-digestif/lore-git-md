This is patch 2/10 of Patrick Steinhardt’s v3 series refactoring Git’s ODB fsck checks to make them pluggable. The patch merges `fsck_obj_buffer()` and `fsck_obj()` into a single function, eliminating redundancy and simplifying control flow.

The change is mechanical and targeted: `fsck_obj()` had no callers other than `fsck_obj_buffer()`, and the only difference between the two was that `fsck_obj_buffer()` parsed the object buffer before calling `fsck_obj()`. The merged function, still named `fsck_obj_buffer()`, now handles both parsing and verification in one place. This removes an early return check for the `SEEN` flag that was always cleared by the caller, making the code’s behavior more obvious. The patch deletes 27 lines and adds 20 in `builtin/fsck.c`, with no behavior changes.

No technical concerns or edge cases were raised, and the patch’s design rationale — consolidating two nearly identical functions to reduce redundancy — was acknowledged as sound.

This patch is a small but necessary step in the series, setting up the infrastructure for later patches that move fsck logic into individual ODB backends. It touches only `builtin/fsck.c` and introduces no new symbols or user-visible changes. The refactoring is clean and well-motivated, with no unresolved feedback.
