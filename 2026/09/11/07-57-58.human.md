This is the second and final patch in Souma’s v2 series teaching `git history` to sign rewritten commits. Patrick Steinhardt’s review here is surface-level, focusing on minor documentation and style nits rather than the substance of the feature.

The patch itself extends `git history`’s `drop`, `fixup`, `reword`, and `split` subcommands to respect `commit.gpgsign` and the `-S/--gpg-sign` and `--no-gpg-sign` command-line options, matching the behavior already available in `git commit` and `git rebase`. The implementation reads the signing configuration early so that later command-line options can override it, and it plumbs the signing flag through the replay machinery. Documentation and GPG-gated tests are included, covering all flag/config combinations, explicit key selection, and the no-new-commit edge case for `drop`.

Patrick’s feedback is limited to two small points: he suggests dropping a sentence in the commit message that merely notes the `drop` subcommand creates no replacement commit (calling it an unnecessary edge-case distraction), and he flags a minor style nit about backslash alignment in the `OPT_HISTORY_GPG_SIGN` macro definition. Neither comment questions the design or implementation, and both are easily addressed. The series now looks ready for maintainer consideration unless further substantive review surfaces.
