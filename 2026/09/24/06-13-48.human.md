This is a CI/build system patch, the second in a two-patch series aimed at reducing resource exhaustion in GitHub Actions CI jobs. The series targets Linux runners where the full test suite—including long-running tests—is enabled, leading to out-of-memory kills and disk-space exhaustion.

This email is a substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt on the second patch, which dynamically sets the parallelism level (`-j`) for `make` and `prove` to the number of available CPU cores on Linux runners. The patch itself is minimal and aligns with existing practice in GitLab CI, but Patrick raises two open questions: first, he requests a link to a CI failure demonstrating the ENOSPC errors the patch aims to fix; second, he asks for benchmark data comparing CI runtime before and after the change, and wonders whether some degree of oversubscription might be beneficial.

Patrick also suggests unifying the parallelism logic between GitLab and GitHub CI, noting there is no obvious reason for the two to differ. The review is constructive and focused on evidence and consistency, rather than questioning the overall goal. The patch looks sensible and well-motivated, but the thread is now waiting on the author to provide the requested data to move forward.
