This is the first patch in a seven-part documentation series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s merge conflict resolution guidance. The patch adds the new guide itself (`Documentation/gitmergeconflicts.adoc`) and updates the build system (`Makefile`, `meson.build`) to ensure it’s processed correctly.

The new page is a direct response to user feedback about Git’s scattered and inconsistent conflict-resolution documentation. It addresses specific pain points: inconsistent terminology ("ours" vs. "theirs" vs. `HEAD`/`MERGE_HEAD`), unclear references (e.g., "2. and 3." in the original `git merge` man page), and outdated analogies (RCS). The guide prioritizes practical examples—like resolving a fruit-list conflict—over technical minutiae, and explains key concepts such as `diff3`/`zdiff3` and the unified `--abort`/`--continue` workflow. It also clarifies edge cases, such as why "ours" and "theirs" reverse roles during a rebase.

The writing is clear and user-focused, with a tone that assumes no prior knowledge of Git internals. The structure (e.g., sections like "MERGE CONFLICT MARKERS" and "TOOLS FOR HANDLING MERGE CONFLICTS") mirrors established man-page conventions, though the `SYNOPSIS` section’s "Guide to handling merge conflicts" phrasing may spark discussion about whether non-command guides need a synopsis at all. The patch leaves two open questions for reviewers: whether `git commit` and `git merge --continue` are always equivalent, and whether `git apply`/`git am` conflicts should be covered.

This is a well-crafted contribution that fills a long-standing gap in Git’s documentation. The user-centric design—rooted in real feedback—makes it likely to be well-received, though reviewers may nitpick technical details (e.g., `diff3`/`zdiff3` explanations) or man-page formatting. The series as a whole (patches 2–7 update existing man pages to link here) should reduce duplication and improve clarity across Git’s documentation.
