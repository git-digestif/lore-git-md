## 2026/09/29

- [2026/09/29/01-32-33 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King answered Julia Evans's question about whether to apply patches to `seen` before submitting: in general no — it rarely turns up useful changes, and the more likely outcome is a textual or semantic conflict with another topic that the maintainer must resolve; knowing about such conflicts and warning in the cover letter can help distribute the load. If looking ahead, he suggested "next" or "jch" as more useful targets than `seen`, since a topic on `seen` only means the maintainer saw it and it might not pass all tests, whereas "next" is fairly stable and "jch" is what Junio runs day to day.
- [2026/09/29/01-56-12 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano noted that since Julia Evans works mostly on documentation modernization, the advantage Jeff King described may not be as relevant to her as to those working with code. He advised not applying patches _on_ an advanced branch, but instead applying them on a stable base (a release tag or the tip of the current 'master') and making a trial merge of the topic into the advanced target; even without building, merge conflicts can reveal what others are working on (e.g., via `git log --merge --left-right -p`), and even without textual conflicts a build may reveal that an API the topic depends on has changed. He also stated his personal rule that topics must be in 'jch' before being marked "Will merge to 'next'".
