This thread is about Julia Evans’ patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s scattered merge-conflict resolution guidance. The series aims to replace redundant sections in existing man pages (`git merge`, `git rebase`, etc.) with cross-references to the new guide, improving clarity and reducing maintenance overhead. The initial version (v1) was posted via GitGitGadget and is under review, with mechanical integration fixes pending (e.g., Meson build updates, `.gitattributes` inclusion, and registration in `command-list.txt` to resolve a `make check-docs` failure).

Junio C Hamano reviewed the first patch and requested build-system adjustments, then merged the series into his `seen` branch for integration testing. Jeff King (Peff) identified the `check-docs` failure’s root cause—missing registration in `command-list.txt`—and provided the fix. D. Ben Knoble has been the most substantive reviewer, validating the series’ design (e.g., deferring `git apply` coverage) and raising technical clarifications, such as the equivalence of `git commit` and `git merge --continue`. His latest feedback highlights two omissions in the new guide: a foundational explanation of *why* conflicts occur (non-overlapping vs. overlapping changes) and a practical tip about `git log --merge -p <path>` for inspecting branch diffs during resolution. These suggestions aim to improve the guide’s completeness without altering its structure.

The series is otherwise well-received, with no major objections. The immediate next steps are mechanical: addressing Junio’s requested changes, registering the new man page in `command-list.txt`, and incorporating Knoble’s content refinements. The guide’s example-driven approach and focus on user pain points (e.g., "ours" vs. "theirs" clarification) have been praised, though its balance between brevity and completeness remains an open question. No revised version has been posted yet, but the pending adjustments are straightforward and unlikely to derail the effort. The series is self-contained and doesn’t interact with other in-flight topics.
