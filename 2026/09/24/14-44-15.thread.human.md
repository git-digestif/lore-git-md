This thread introduced `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. Authored by Julia Evans and posted as a seven-patch series, the effort responds to long-standing user confusion caused by duplicated, incomplete, or inconsistent conflict-resolution guidance across existing man pages. After two iterations and thorough review from Junio C Hamano, Jeff King, D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt, the series is now complete and ready for merging.

The new guide is organized as an example-driven document, covering foundational concepts (what a merge conflict is, when it occurs), resolution workflows (identify, edit, stage, continue), tooling advice (merge tools, `diff3`/`zdiff3` conflict styles), and command-specific nuances (e.g., `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` in cherry-picks, `--skip` in rebases). Key improvements in v2 include a rewritten "OURS" AND "THEIRS" section to clarify terminology, a new "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" introduction, and active promotion of `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice, supported by Junio’s detailed examples demonstrating its utility. The series retains command-specific details in the man pages while linking to the new guide for general advice, striking a balance between centralization and precision.

All feedback from v1 has been addressed, including mechanical fixes (e.g., `command-list.txt` registration, `.gitattributes` integration) and content refinements. The only minor disagreement—Julia’s decision not to document the niche rationale behind `conflict-marker-size=32`—does not affect the series’ readiness. The guide is now fully integrated into the documentation build system, with no unresolved technical or build issues. While follow-up discussions about commit accessibility (Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to embed commit details in conflict markers) and potential `git status` wording improvements (Junio’s exploratory questions) remain open, these are deferred to future patches. For developers catching up, this is a straightforward, uncontroversial documentation improvement that directly addresses a usability gap.
