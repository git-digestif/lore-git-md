This thread is about the final version (v3) of a patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s merge conflict resolution guidance into a single, user-friendly guide. The series, originally authored by Julia Evans with collaborative input from Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan, addresses long-standing documentation gaps: scattered advice across multiple man pages, inconsistent terminology (e.g., "ours" vs. "theirs"), and unclear explanations of merge conflict markers. The new guide is comprehensive, covering foundational concepts, step-by-step resolution workflows, tools for handling conflicts (e.g., `git diff AUTO_MERGE`, `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`), and command-specific nuances like the "upside down" behavior of "ours" and "theirs" during rebases. It also explicitly deprecates `zdiff3` and promotes `diff3` as a best practice.

The series has undergone thorough review, with high-weight feedback from Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt. Junio’s v2 and v3 reviews confirmed the series is ready for merging, pending minor refinements to the new guide’s tone and completeness. Julia Evans has incorporated all feedback, including an exhaustive list of commands that can cause conflicts and formal phrasing adjustments. Junio’s `Reviewed-by:` can now be added to all patches once the final adjustments are applied. The series is purely additive—no existing behavior or on-disk formats are changed—and integrates seamlessly into the documentation build system.

The only remaining open questions are forward-looking: whether `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` will become the default, and potential follow-up patches for conflict marker labels or `git status` wording improvements. These were deferred to future work. The series aligns with ongoing documentation modernization efforts, such as Jean-Noël Avila’s synopsis-style conversion, and has no conflicts with other in-flight topics. With all substantive feedback addressed, the series is now poised for merging into Git’s master branch.
