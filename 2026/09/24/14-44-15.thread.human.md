This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, user-friendly guide. The series is now in its first version and under active review, with key participants including Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The goal is to replace redundant conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (like `git merge` and `git cherry-pick`) with cross-references to the new guide, while preserving command-specific details where necessary. Early feedback identified mechanical issues—like missing build-system updates and a `make check-docs` failure due to an unregistered man page—which Julia has committed to fixing. More substantive discussions have focused on balancing centralization with command-specific nuances, particularly for `git cherry-pick`, where Junio clarified that critical details like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` must remain in the command’s man page rather than being moved to the guide.

The latest development is Junio’s concrete contribution to the guide itself: two real-world examples demonstrating why `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` is often essential for correct conflict resolution. These examples show how the common ancestor (visible in `diff3`’s middle section) reveals the *intent* behind conflicting changes, helping users decide whether to include or exclude them. This aligns with the guide’s practical, user-tested approach and fills a gap in the current draft, which mentions `diff3` as a tool but doesn’t explain its value. Julia is likely to incorporate these examples into the next revision, and the discussion reinforces the broader push to promote `diff3` as a best practice—possibly even as Git’s default conflict style in the future.

Other open questions include how to make commit information (like messages or IDs) more accessible during conflict resolution, with Patrick Steinhardt proposing an innovative enhancement to embed this data directly into conflict markers. Julia has enthusiastically endorsed this idea, though the exact implementation remains under discussion. The series is otherwise progressing smoothly, with incremental refinements building on a solid foundation. The next steps are mechanical fixes (like registering the man page in `command-list.txt`) and addressing the remaining content gaps, such as the new "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section Julia has proposed to improve the guide’s foundational clarity.
