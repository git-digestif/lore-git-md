This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. The series is now in its first version and under active review, with key participants including Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The core goal is to replace duplicated conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (like `git merge`, `git rebase`, and `git cherry-pick`) with cross-references to the new guide, while preserving command-specific details where necessary. For example, Junio and Patrick have clarified that cherry-pick-specific details—such as the `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` pseudo-ref and index updates for auto-resolved paths—must remain in the cherry-pick man page, as the new guide focuses on general advice. The series has already addressed mechanical issues like build system integration (Makefile and Meson) and `.gitattributes` updates, but a `make check-docs` failure revealed the need to register `gitmergeconflicts(7)` in `command-list.txt`, which Julia has committed to fixing.

Recent discussion has shifted toward **user experience improvements**, with Patrick Steinhardt proposing two significant enhancements: (1) embedding commit details (IDs, messages, and dates) directly into conflict markers to make them more actionable, and (2) promoting `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice. Julia enthusiastically endorsed both ideas, particularly the commit-accessibility enhancement, which she sees as a major usability win. While she prefers making commit information *discoverable* (e.g., via `git show`) rather than embedding it in markers, the proposal is likely to be prototyped in GitLab and may evolve into a follow-up patch. Junio and Patrick also strongly advocate for `diff3` as the default conflict style, citing its clarity and user feedback, and the guide is now expected to actively promote it.

Open questions include how to balance centralization with command-specific nuances (e.g., retaining cherry-pick details in its man page), whether to include foundational explanations of merge conflicts, and how prominently to feature the new tooling advice (commit accessibility and `diff3`). The series is currently in Junio’s `seen` branch for integration testing, with no major technical blockers remaining. The next steps involve Julia addressing the `command-list.txt` registration, refining the guide’s content based on feedback, and potentially prototyping Patrick’s conflict-marker enhancement. The thread reflects a collaborative, user-focused effort to improve Git’s documentation, with a strong emphasis on practical utility and clarity.
