This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. The series is now in its first version and under active review, with recent discussion focusing on two key improvements: making commit information more accessible during conflict resolution, and promoting `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice.

Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have driven much of the recent feedback. Junio initially requested mechanical fixes—adding Meson build support and folding a `.gitattributes` change into the first patch—and later merged the series into his `seen` branch, where it triggered a `make check-docs` failure due to a missing `command-list.txt` entry. Jeff King identified the root cause, and Julia has committed to addressing it. Junio also raised substantive concerns about the removal of cherry-pick-specific details (like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) from the cherry-pick man page, arguing these should be retained rather than moved to the new guide. Julia has agreed to revise the patch to preserve these details while still linking to the guide for general advice.

The latest discussion, led by Patrick, proposes embedding commit messages and dates directly into conflict markers—a significant usability improvement that would make conflict resolution more intuitive. For example:
```
<<<<<<< HEAD: abcdefg (fruits: add apple, 2026-10-01)
    "cherry",
=======
    "banana",
>>>>>>> add-fruit: 12345678 (fruits: add banana, 2024-02-03)
```
This idea builds on Junio’s earlier suggestion to include commit references and is now being prototyped in GitLab for user feedback. Separately, both Junio and Patrick strongly advocate for making `diff3` the default conflict style, citing its clarity and positive user feedback. Julia has embraced this idea, and the guide is expected to actively promote `diff3` as a best practice.

Other contributors have weighed in constructively. D. Ben Knoble validated the series’ design choices (like deferring coverage of `git apply` conflicts) and clarified technical details, while Patrick’s earlier review focused on terminology and user experience, suggesting improvements like a foundational explanation of merge conflicts and clearer "ours/theirs" definitions. Julia has responded pragmatically, accepting some suggestions (like `linkgit:` macros and `git merge-tool --tool-help`) while pushing back on others (like using "hunk" instead of "lines of code") based on user feedback.

The series is now poised for a second version that will address the mechanical fixes, retain command-specific details, and incorporate the new tooling advice. Open questions include how prominently to feature commit messages in the guide and whether to include a brief explanation of 3-way merges. The thread remains collaborative, with no major objections, and the focus on practical, user-tested improvements aligns with Julia’s goal of making conflict resolution more accessible.
