This thread introduced `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. Authored by Julia Evans (with Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan as co-author) and sent via GitGitGadget, the series reached its fourth and final version after incorporating feedback from Junio Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, Jeff King, and D. Ben Knoble. The guide now covers foundational concepts (what a merge conflict is, when they occur), an exhaustive list of commands that can trigger them (including Junio’s clarification that `git am -3` is a three-way merge), resolution steps, conflict markers, and tools like `git diff AUTO_MERGE` and `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`. The `diff3` example was refined to avoid implying intent and now demonstrates a scenario where both sides removed an item from a list, clarifying how the common ancestor reveals the correct resolution.

The series is fully merged to Junio’s `next` branch, meaning it’s undergoing integration testing and will graduate to `master` in the next release cycle. All six patches carry Junio’s `Reviewed-by`, and the documentation build system (`make check-docs`) validates the new guide. The patches replace duplicated conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (e.g., `git-merge.adoc`, `git-rebase.adoc`) with cross-references to `gitmergeconflicts(7)` while preserving command-specific details like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` or `--skip`. The latest patch, updating `git-pull.adoc`, completes the series by replacing a generic mention of conflict resolution with a direct link to the new guide.

A few loose ends remain, though none block the series. The `zdiff3` conflict style was omitted from the guide, with Julia deferring a more thorough explanation to future work. Patrick Steinhardt’s suggestion to embed commit details in conflict markers and Junio’s questions about updating `git status` wording or conflict-marker labels are potential follow-ups. The guide actively promotes `diff3` as a best practice, but whether it will become the default is still an open question. The series aligns with Jean-Noël Avila’s ongoing documentation cleanup efforts and has no conflicts with other in-flight topics.
