Julia Evans’ patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating merge conflict resolution guidance, is under active review after its initial v1 posting. The series aims to replace scattered, duplicated advice across multiple command man pages (`git merge`, `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, etc.) with a centralized, example-driven guide. Junio C Hamano merged the series into his `seen` branch for integration testing, but it hit a `make check-docs` failure due to the new page not being registered in `command-list.txt`—a mechanical issue now resolved by Jeff King’s suggested fix.

The series has made progress on build system integration (updates to both Makefile and Meson, plus `.gitattributes` handling), but substantive content questions remain. Junio’s latest review highlights a critical gap: the new guide omits cherry-pick-specific details like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and index behavior for auto-resolved paths, which were removed from the `git-cherry-pick` man page. He argues these should either be added to the guide or retained in the original page. Meanwhile, D. Ben Knoble’s feedback has sharpened the guide’s focus, endorsing its structure while flagging omissions like a foundational explanation of what merge conflicts *are* and practical tools like `git log --merge -p <path>`. Julia has committed to addressing these, and Junio’s recent reply clarifies the correct usage of `git log --merge` (it operates on all conflicted paths, not just a specified file) and recommends `--left-right` as a more useful alternative for single-file inspection.

The immediate next steps are mechanical: registering `gitmergeconflicts(7)` in `command-list.txt` and folding the `.gitattributes` addition into the first patch. Once those are resolved, the series can move toward `next`, but content refinements—particularly around cherry-pick specifics and the guide’s completeness—will likely require a v2. The thread remains constructive, with no major objections to the series’ goals, but the balance between centralization and command-specific nuances is still being negotiated.
