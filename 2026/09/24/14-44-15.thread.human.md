This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. The series is now in its second revision (v2 pending), addressing feedback from Junio C Hamano, Jeff King, D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The core goal—replacing duplicated conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages with a central guide—has broad support, but the discussion has refined how to balance centralization with command-specific details. Junio’s review clarified that critical cherry-pick details (like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) must stay in the cherry-pick man page, while the new guide handles general advice. Julia has committed to retaining these details and adding a foundational "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section to address gaps in introductory context, with Junio’s wording refinements improving clarity. The latest exchange between Junio and D. Ben Knoble resolved the last stylistic question about the term "unstaged," leaving Julia’s phrasing intact for v2.

Two major improvements have emerged from the discussion: (1) **`merge.conflictstyle=diff3`** is now actively promoted as a best practice, with Junio providing detailed examples showing how the common ancestor reveals the *intent* behind conflicting changes, and (2) **commit accessibility**, where Patrick Steinhardt proposed embedding commit details (messages, dates) directly into conflict markers—a user-experience enhancement Julia enthusiastically endorsed. While the exact implementation (embedding vs. discoverability via commands like `git show`) remains under discussion, Patrick plans to prototype this in GitLab. The series also resolves mechanical issues (e.g., `command-list.txt` registration, Meson build integration) and folds the `.gitattributes` change into the first patch, as Junio requested.

The v2 revision will include the new foundational section, Junio’s `diff3` examples, and D. Ben Knoble’s recommendation to steer users toward `git merge --continue` as the default resolution command. Open questions about terminology (e.g., "ours/theirs") and omissions (e.g., 3-way merge explanations) have been deferred, with Julia prioritizing user-tested practicality over theoretical completeness. The series is now ready for v2, pending Julia’s final adjustments.
