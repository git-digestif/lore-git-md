This thread is about Julia Evans’s seven-patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. The series aims to reduce duplication, improve clarity (e.g., explaining “ours” vs. “theirs”), and provide user-tested examples, while preserving command-specific details where necessary—such as `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` in `git-cherry-pick.adoc`.

The latest update responds to D. Ben Knoble’s feedback by adding a “WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?” section to the new guide. This lightweight, example-driven explanation avoids technical jargon like “3-way merge” and instead focuses on what users see: overlapping changes that Git can’t combine automatically, leaving conflict markers in unstaged files. The addition directly addresses concerns that the updated `git-merge` man page might feel abrupt to newcomers, while keeping the guide’s tone accessible. It’s expected to appear in the next revision (v2), alongside previously agreed improvements like the `git log --merge -p` guidance.

The series is still under review, with mechanical fixes pending (e.g., registering `gitmergeconflicts` in `command-list.txt`, folding `.gitattributes` into the first patch) and substantive feedback on cherry-pick-specific content retention. Recent discussion has also highlighted two significant user-experience proposals: Patrick Steinhardt’s idea to embed commit details (messages, dates) directly into conflict markers, and Junio’s and Patrick’s advocacy for making `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` the default. Julia has enthusiastically endorsed both, suggesting the guide will actively promote them, though the exact implementation of the commit-accessibility enhancement remains under discussion. The series is now in Junio’s `seen` branch for integration testing, with no major controversies or blockers beyond the pending revisions.
