This thread introduced `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s scattered merge-conflict resolution guidance into a single, practical guide. The series, now at its fifth and final version, was authored by Julia Evans with collaborative input from Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan and feedback from Junio C Hamano, Jeff King, Patrick Steinhardt, and D. Ben Knoble. After addressing all substantive and editorial feedback—including Junio’s final polish on the `diff3` example’s wording and Jeff King’s fix for a mechanical build issue—the six-patch series has been merged to `next` and is cooking for graduation to `master`.

The new guide replaces duplicated conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (`git-merge`, `git-rebase`, `git-revert`, `git-cherry-pick`, and `git-pull`) with cross-references to `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, while retaining command-specific details like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and `--skip`. It organizes advice into clear sections: what merge conflicts are, how to identify and resolve them (including a step-by-step process and tool recommendations), the meaning of "ours" and "theirs," and practical examples demonstrating how `diff3` reveals the intent behind conflicting changes. The `diff3` example, now using consistent indentation and corrected phrasing ("removed one fruit *from* the list"), retains the word "probably" in its resolution rationale to emphasize the need for judgment in real-world scenarios—a choice explicitly endorsed by Junio.

All open questions from earlier versions have been resolved or deferred. The brief mention of `zdiff3` was removed, and Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed enhancement to embed commit details in conflict markers remains a potential follow-up. The series is now fully integrated into the documentation build system, with no unresolved technical or editorial issues. The next step is routine integration testing in `next` before the guide graduates to `master`.
