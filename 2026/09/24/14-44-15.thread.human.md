This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page designed to centralize Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, practical guide. The series is now in its first version and under active review, with key participants including Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The core goal is to replace redundant conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (like `git merge` and `git cherry-pick`) with cross-references to the new guide, while preserving command-specific details where necessary. For example, Junio’s feedback clarified that cherry-pick-specific details—such as the `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` pseudo-ref—must remain in the cherry-pick man page, as the new guide won’t cover them. The series has already addressed mechanical issues like build system integration (Makefile and Meson) and a `make check-docs` failure caused by missing registration in `command-list.txt`.

The latest exchange between Julia and Patrick Steinhardt reveals her user-centric approach to documentation. She prioritizes practical tools and examples over jargon or theoretical explanations, even when reviewers suggest otherwise. For instance, she rejected Patrick’s proposal to explain Git’s 3-way merge internals, citing past user feedback that such details were confusing. Instead, she focuses on actionable advice, like using `diff3` or `git log --merge -p` to resolve conflicts. This philosophy also shapes her terminology choices: she prefers "lines of code" over "hunk" and keeps the "ours/theirs" explanation concise, acknowledging its inherent confusion rather than trying to resolve it.

The series is now poised for a revised version that will incorporate minor fixes (e.g., `linkgit:` macros, `git merge-tool --tool-help` mentions) and address the cherry-pick-specific content retention. No major structural changes are expected, and the guide’s empirical, tool-focused design is unlikely to face further pushback. The remaining open questions—such as whether to include commit accessibility details or a foundational explanation of merge conflicts—are being resolved incrementally, with Julia leaning toward deferring non-essential additions. The thread’s tone remains collaborative, with reviewers aligning on the series’ goals even as they debate implementation details.
