Julia Evans’s new documentation series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`—a dedicated man page to centralize Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice—is nearing its second iteration after a productive first round of review. The series, now in its v1 form, has been merged into Junio C Hamano’s `seen` branch for integration testing, where it surfaced a mechanical issue: the new man page wasn’t registered in `command-list.txt`, causing a `make check-docs` failure. Julia has committed to fixing this, along with Junio’s earlier requests to update the Meson build system and fold the `.gitattributes` change into the first patch.

The core goal remains unchanged: replacing redundant conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (`git merge`, `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, etc.) with cross-references to the new guide, while preserving command-specific nuances. Junio’s review of the fifth patch clarified that cherry-pick-specific details—like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and index updates for auto-resolved paths—must stay in the cherry-pick man page, as the new guide won’t cover them. Julia has agreed to revise the patch to retain these details while still linking to the guide for general advice. Similarly, D. Ben Knoble’s feedback prompted her to add a foundational "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section to the guide, ensuring users have the necessary context before diving into resolution techniques.

Two major user-experience improvements have emerged from the discussion. First, Patrick Steinhardt proposed embedding commit details (IDs, messages, dates) directly into conflict markers, transforming them from passive indicators into active resolution tools. Julia enthusiastically endorsed this idea, highlighting the practical benefits of commit messages and dates, and the enhancement may evolve into a follow-up patch after Patrick prototypes it in GitLab. Second, Junio and Patrick strongly advocated for promoting `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice, with Julia’s user feedback ("every time I show people diff3 someone tells me how happy they are") reinforcing its impact. The guide is now expected to actively promote `diff3`, and this discussion may foreshadow a broader shift in Git’s default behavior.

Open questions remain, but they’re narrowing. Julia’s latest email addresses two technical ambiguities raised by Knoble: she’ll soften the claim about `git commit` and `git merge --continue` being equivalent (acknowledging edge cases) and remove the premature mention of `git history --squash` (which currently rejects conflict-creating operations). Other loose ends—like whether to include a 3-way merge explanation or make commit IDs more accessible—are still under discussion, but Julia’s pragmatic approach prioritizes user-tested practicality over theoretical completeness. The series is on track for v2, with Julia actively incorporating feedback to refine the guide’s clarity, completeness, and user-centricity.
