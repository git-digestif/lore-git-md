Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s merge-conflict resolution guidance, is nearing completion after several rounds of review. The series aims to replace scattered advice across multiple command man pages with a single, practical guide, improving clarity and reducing maintenance burden. Julia has addressed most of the substantive feedback, including Junio C Hamano’s request to retain cherry-pick-specific details (like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) in the cherry-pick man page rather than moving them to the new guide, and D. Ben Knoble’s suggestion to add a foundational "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section. The latter is now largely settled, with Julia incorporating Junio’s wording refinements—such as "it marks them as conflicted" and "since there’s no overlap"—while retaining "will not try to guess" to emphasize Git’s intentional conservatism in conflict resolution. Junio’s playful push for "cannot guess" (framing it as a technical limitation) was ultimately set aside in favor of Julia’s preference, which contrasts Git’s approach with more aggressive tools like `mergiraf`.

The series is now mechanically complete: Julia has confirmed she will fold the `.gitattributes` change into the first patch and register `gitmergeconflicts(7)` in `command-list.txt` to resolve the `make check-docs` failure. The guide itself is shaping up as a user-tested, example-driven document, with recent additions like Junio’s detailed `diff3` examples (demonstrating how the common ancestor reveals the *intent* behind conflicts) and Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed commit-accessibility enhancement (embedding commit details in conflict markers). Julia has enthusiastically endorsed both ideas, though the latter may evolve into a follow-up patch after Patrick prototypes it in GitLab. The guide now actively promotes `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice, supported by user feedback and Junio’s advocacy, and includes practical tools like `git log --merge -p <filename>` (with corrected wording) and `git merge-tool --tool-help`. Open questions are minor—such as whether to include commit messages or IDs in the guide—and the series is expected to graduate soon, pending final mechanical fixes and Junio’s integration.
