This thread is about the introduction of `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s merge conflict resolution guidance. The series, now at its fourth and final version, was authored by Julia Evans and has received broad support from key reviewers, including Junio C Hamano, Jeff King (Peff), Patrick Steinhardt, and D. Ben Knoble.

The goal of the series is to address the scattered and often duplicated advice across Git’s documentation by creating a dedicated, example-driven guide. The new man page covers foundational concepts (e.g., "ours" vs. "theirs," conflict markers, and the role of the common ancestor), provides an exhaustive list of commands that can cause conflicts, and offers practical resolution steps. Version 4 incorporated all prior feedback, including Junio’s refinements to the `diff3` example and Patrick Steinhardt’s editorial suggestions. The series also fixes a long-standing bug in `git-merge.adoc` (references to non-existent steps) and updates other man pages to cross-reference the new guide.

The series is now fully approved and has been merged to Junio’s `seen` branch, with the only remaining mechanical blocker—a missing `SYNOPSIS` section in `gitmergeconflicts.adoc`—resolved automatically during the merge. Junio’s latest email confirms no further action is needed from the author, and the series is ready for graduation to `next` and eventually `master`. Minor polish, such as fixing a typo and inconsistent indentation in the `diff3` example, will be addressed before the final merge.

While the series is complete, a few open questions remain for future work, including clarifying the behavior of `zdiff3`, improving conflict marker labels, and potentially making `diff3` the default merge conflict style. Julia Evans has also endorsed Patrick Steinhardt’s idea of embedding commit details in conflict markers but deferred it to a follow-up effort. For now, the focus is on finalizing the current guide, which is expected to significantly improve the user experience for resolving merge conflicts.
