Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating merge conflict resolution guidance, is under active review after its initial v1 posting. The series aims to replace scattered, duplicated advice across multiple command man pages (`git merge`, `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, etc.) with a single, centralized guide, improving clarity and reducing maintenance overhead. Junio C Hamano merged the series into his `seen` branch for integration testing, but it triggered a `make check-docs` failure due to the new page not being registered in `command-list.txt`—a mechanical issue Julia has committed to fixing by adding the entry under the "guides" section.

The core tension in the series is balancing centralization with command-specific nuances. Junio’s recent review of the fifth patch (updating `git-cherry-pick.adoc`) highlighted a critical gap: the new guide omits cherry-pick-specific details like the `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` pseudo-ref and how cleanly auto-resolved paths are recorded in the index. Julia initially planned to remove these sections entirely, assuming the new guide would suffice, but Junio argued that users need these details to understand cherry-pick’s unique workflow. In response, Julia agreed to revise the patch to retain at least the `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` explanation in the cherry-pick man page while still cross-referencing the new guide for general advice. This exchange underscores that the guide isn’t yet a complete substitute for all removed content, particularly for commands with unique pseudo-refs.

Other open questions remain, including whether to cover `git apply`/`git am` conflicts (deferred for now), the accuracy of `diff3`/`zdiff3` examples, and the foundational explanation of what a merge conflict *is*. D. Ben Knoble’s substantive reviews have helped clarify technical ambiguities, such as the behavior of `git log --merge -p <path>` (now approved with precise wording) and the equivalence of `git commit` and `git merge --continue`. The series is otherwise on track, with Julia addressing mechanical fixes (e.g., `command-list.txt` registration, Meson build updates) and refining content based on feedback. The next revision will likely strike a balance—centralizing general advice while preserving command-specific details where necessary.
