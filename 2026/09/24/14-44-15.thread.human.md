This thread is about a new `gitmergeconflicts(7)` man page, proposed by Julia Evans to consolidate Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, example-driven guide. The series is now in its first version, with seven patches touching documentation files and build systems. Junio C Hamano reviewed the initial patch and requested mechanical adjustments—adding Meson build support and folding the `.gitattributes` change into the first patch—which have since been addressed. The series was merged into Junio’s `seen` branch for integration testing, where it hit a `make check-docs` failure due to the new man page not being registered in `command-list.txt`; Jeff King (Peff) identified the fix, which is now the immediate next step.

D. Ben Knoble’s recent review engages deeply with the series’ content and design. He endorses the standalone-page approach, calling it a cleaner alternative to includes, and validates Julia’s decision to defer coverage of `git apply`/`git am` conflicts. On open questions, he clarifies that `git commit` and `git merge --continue` are generally equivalent but links to prior discussion for edge cases, and notes that the experimental `git history` command currently rejects conflict-creating operations, so it doesn’t yet need inclusion. Knoble also supports the guide’s de-emphasis of “ours” vs. “theirs” terminology for most users, while acknowledging its relevance for advanced workflows. The review is broadly positive, with no major objections, but surfaces areas where further reviewer input could help refine command-specific details and edge-case coverage. The series is now waiting on the mechanical `command-list.txt` fix before moving toward `next`.
