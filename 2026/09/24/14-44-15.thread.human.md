This thread is about Julia Evans’s patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, user-friendly guide. The series is now in its first version (v1) and under active review, with key participants including Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt.

The goal is to replace redundant conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages (like `git merge` and `git cherry-pick`) with cross-references to the new guide, while preserving command-specific details where necessary. For example, Junio and Julia have agreed that cherry-pick-specific details—such as `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and how Git handles auto-resolved paths—must remain in the `git-cherry-pick` man page rather than being moved to the new guide. The series has also grown to include foundational improvements, like a "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section (now approved with minor wording tweaks) and Junio’s detailed examples demonstrating why `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` is a best practice for revealing the *intent* behind conflicting changes.

Mechanical issues, like the `make check-docs` failure due to the missing `command-list.txt` entry, have been resolved, and Julia has committed to folding the `.gitattributes` change into the first patch. The series is currently in Junio’s `seen` branch for integration testing, though Jeff King and Junio have clarified that contributors should target `next` or `jch` for early testing of interactions with other in-flight work.

Open questions include how prominently the guide will promote Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed **commit-accessibility enhancement** (embedding commit details in conflict markers) and whether minor code changes—like rewording `git status` output or updating conflict marker labels—will be pursued. Julia has endorsed the idea of "documentation-driven development" and identified five specific pain points in `git status`’s current output, but she hasn’t committed to implementing these changes yet. The discussion about `git commit` vs. `git merge --continue` has also been resolved: the guide will note both as valid but steer users toward the latter, though `git status`’s recommendation remains unchanged for now.

The series is on track for graduation, with no major objections and a clear path to addressing the remaining refinements. The next steps are Julia’s revisions to retain cherry-pick-specific details, incorporate Junio’s `diff3` examples, and finalize the wording for the "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section. Follow-up patches may emerge for Patrick’s commit-accessibility idea or `git status` improvements, but these won’t block the current series.
