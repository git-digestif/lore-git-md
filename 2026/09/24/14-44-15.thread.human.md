This thread is about the finalized **v4 patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`**, a new man page that consolidates Git’s merge-conflict resolution guidance into a single, practical guide. The series, authored by Julia Evans (via GitGitGadget), addresses long-standing user confusion caused by scattered advice across multiple man pages, inconsistent terminology (e.g., "ours" vs. "theirs"), and unclear references. The new guide is now complete, incorporating all feedback from prior versions, including Junio’s refinements to the `diff3` example and Patrick Steinhardt’s editorial suggestions.

The core of the series is the new `gitmergeconflicts.adoc` file, a 339-line document structured as an example-driven guide. It covers foundational concepts (e.g., what a merge conflict is, Git’s behavior during conflicts), an exhaustive list of commands that can cause conflicts (including `git am -3`, confirmed by Junio as a valid three-way merge operation), and a clear four-step resolution workflow. The guide also explains merge conflict markers, tools for handling conflicts (with a strong endorsement of `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`), and the relationship between "ours" and "theirs" during merges and rebases. The `zdiff3` deprecation note has been removed, deferring a more thorough explanation to future work.

The series has received **final sign-off from Junio**, with all patches now cooking in the `next` branch and expected to graduate to `master` soon. Patches 2/6 through 6/6 carry Junio’s `Reviewed-by`, and patch 1/6 (the new guide) is finalized with all requested adjustments. The series is purely additive—no behavior or on-disk changes—and integrates seamlessly into the documentation build system. Key contributors include Jeff King (who fixed a `make check-docs` blocker), D. Ben Knoble, and Patrick Steinhardt, all of whom provided substantive feedback in earlier versions. While a few loose ends remain (e.g., potential follow-ups on `zdiff3` clarification, conflict marker labels, and `git status` wording), the series is now complete and ready for merging.
