This thread is about a new `gitmergeconflicts(7)` man page, proposed by Julia Evans to consolidate Git’s scattered merge-conflict advice into a single, user-friendly guide. The series is in its first version and has been merged into Junio’s `seen` branch for testing, but it hit a snag when `make check-docs` failed because the new page wasn’t registered in `command-list.txt`. Jeff King (Peff) identified the fix—adding `gitmergeconflicts` to the "guides" section of that file—and Junio requested mechanical tweaks to include Meson build updates and fold `.gitattributes` into the first patch.

The bigger discussion centers on whether the new guide can fully replace the conflict-resolution sections in existing man pages. Junio raised concerns about the fifth patch, which removes cherry-pick-specific details like `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` and auto-resolved path behavior, arguing these aren’t covered in the new guide and shouldn’t be deleted without a replacement. D. Ben Knoble’s reviews have been constructive, validating the series’ goals while flagging omissions—like a foundational explanation of what a merge conflict *is*—and helping refine technical details, such as the behavior of `git log --merge -p <path>`. Julia has been responsive, committing to address these gaps, including the now-resolved `git log --merge` mechanism (which uses a `HEAD...<other>` range to show commits from both sides of a conflict).

The series is close to being ready for `next`, but three things still need attention: (1) the `command-list.txt` registration to fix the `check-docs` failure, (2) Junio’s requested mechanical adjustments, and (3) a decision on how to handle cherry-pick-specific details—either by adding them to the new guide or retaining them in the cherry-pick man page. The thread has also clarified that `git commit` and `git merge --continue` are equivalent in conflict resolution, and that covering `git apply`/`git am` conflicts can wait. No major objections remain, but the guide’s completeness and accuracy are still being fine-tuned.
