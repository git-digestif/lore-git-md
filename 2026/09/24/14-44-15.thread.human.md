This thread introduced `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page consolidating Git’s merge conflict resolution guidance into a single, practical guide. Julia Evans authored the series, which has now reached its third and final version after incorporating feedback from Junio Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, D. Ben Knoble, and others. The goal was to replace scattered, inconsistent advice across multiple command man pages with a cohesive, example-driven document that clarifies concepts like "ours" vs. "theirs" and promotes best practices such as `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`.

The series is now fully approved and ready for merging. Junio’s latest email provides the final mechanical refinement: a squashed patch updating the `diff3` example in `gitmergeconflicts.adoc` to better illustrate how the common ancestor reveals the *intent* behind conflicting changes. This adjustment—replacing "code" with "lines" and revising the example to show both sides removing a fruit—addresses the last outstanding feedback from the v3 review. All other patches (2–6) already carry Junio’s `Reviewed-by:`, and the series has been cooking in `next` since his final sign-off. The new guide is purely additive, touching only documentation files and cross-referencing existing man pages where appropriate.

Key improvements in the final version include an exhaustive list of commands that can cause conflicts, a rewritten "OURS" AND "THEIRS" section tying the terms to `HEAD` and the other commit, and a side-by-side comparison of `diff3` vs. `zdiff3` output. The guide also deprecates `zdiff3`, promotes `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`, and clarifies resolution paths (fix or abort) with formal phrasing. No further substantive changes are expected; the remaining adjustments are mechanical and uncontroversial. Once merged, the series will resolve a 16-year-old bug in `git-merge.adoc` and provide users with a centralized, pedagogically sound resource for handling merge conflicts.
