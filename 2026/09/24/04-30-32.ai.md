This is a maintainer clarification in the Git 3.0 planning thread, correcting minor errors in Junio C Hamano’s previous email and restating the provisional timeline and technical strategy. The email refines the earlier proposal without altering its substance, offering a cleaner version of the same plan for community discussion.

Junio reiterates the three-phase timeline: Git 2.98 in December 2026, Git 2.99 in March 2027, and Git 3.0 in April 2027. The month-long gap between 2.99 and 3.0 remains an "advertisement" period for downstream projects, not a full development cycle. Git 2.99.1 (April 2027) will be a bugfix-only release, identical to Git 3.0 except for the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` Makefile knob, which will be enabled in 3.0. This approach minimizes divergence between the two releases, reducing maintenance overhead. The email also reaffirms that volunteers may maintain the 2.99.x series indefinitely for LTS distributions.

The technical strategy remains unchanged: retain the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` knob in Git 3.0 to keep the 2.99.1/3.0 diff minimal, deferring cleanup of disabled code to later 3.x releases. Junio’s preference for a short lead time (one month) between 2.99 and 3.0 is restated, along with his interpretation of the Contributor’s Summit consensus. The email does not address open questions about the scope of breaking changes (e.g., Rust support, SHA-256 interoperability) or downstream coordination, deferring those to future discussion.

This clarification advances the thread by providing a more polished version of the earlier proposal, but it does not introduce new information or resolve outstanding uncertainties. The tone remains pragmatic, emphasizing flexibility and downstream coordination. The next steps will likely involve community feedback on the timeline and technical approach, with refinements expected in Junio’s next "What’s cooking" report.
