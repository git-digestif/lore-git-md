This is the **substantive review** of the first patch in Maciej Ciemborowicz’s three-patch bugfix series (v5), which refactors `refs_delete_refs()` to allow callers to supply old OIDs for batch deletion. The series targets a regression in the reference-transaction hook where all-zero OIDs are passed on branch/tag deletion, breaking external tools like Gerrit and GitLab.

Karthik Nayak’s review engages deeply with the patch’s design and implementation. He raises **three technical concerns**, all of which are substantive and address correctness, safety, and maintainability:

1. **The `is_null_oid()` → `NULL` conversion in `ref_transaction_delete()`**
   Karthik argues this change weakens safety by masking a potential user bug (uninitialized `struct object_id` or ignored return codes). He questions whether `old_oid = NULL` (unconditional deletion) and `old_oid = null_oid` (ref should not exist) are being conflated, and suggests this change should be split into a separate precursor patch with a clearer rationale. His position is that the `BUG()` macro is valuable for catching misuse, and removing it without addressing the underlying concern risks introducing subtle bugs.

2. **Redundant failure counting**
   He notes that `rejection_data.failures` duplicates `failed_refs->nr` and could be eliminated, simplifying the code.

3. **Minor nit on loop variable**
   He suggests inlining the `size_t i` declaration in the `for` loop for conciseness.

The review is **not surface-level**: it challenges core assumptions (the safety of removing the `BUG()`), identifies redundancy, and suggests structural improvements. Karthik’s feedback aligns with his earlier high-weight review of v2, where he proposed the `REF_TRANSACTION_ALLOW_FAILURE` middle ground that the series now adopts. His concerns here are similarly technical and merit close attention, particularly the `is_null_oid()` handling, which could affect the robustness of the refs API.

The patch itself is **well-structured and narrowly scoped**, touching only the refs subsystem and its callers. It lays critical groundwork for the next two patches, which restore the pre-regression hook behavior. Karthik’s feedback does not question the patch’s necessity or direction, but rather its implementation details—specifically, whether the trade-off between safety and flexibility is justified. The author will need to address these points before the series can graduate from `next` to `master`.
