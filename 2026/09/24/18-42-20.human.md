This is a documentation discussion clarifying the technical rationale behind the AsciiDoc cross-reference patch that is already approved and queued in `next`. Jeff King (Peff) provides a detailed explanation of *why* the two-part AsciiDoc link form (`<<EXAMPLES,EXAMPLES>>`) is necessary, tracing the rendering behavior through the toolchain: AsciiDoc/Asciidoctor → DocBook XML → roff (for man pages) or HTML.

The key insight is that the short form (`<<EXAMPLES>>`) hands off rendering control to the DocBook layer, which produces inconsistent output like `the section called "EXAMPLES"` in man pages or `[EXAMPLES]` in HTML (depending on the backend). The two-part form bypasses this by embedding the desired text directly in the XML, ensuring consistent rendering as plain `EXAMPLES`. Peff’s breakdown confirms Julia Evans’s earlier observation that the behavior is backend-dependent and not fully predictable, but the two-part form reliably avoids the edge cases.
