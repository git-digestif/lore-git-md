This is a **bugfix** for the `git reflog expire` regression that was introduced in Git 2.50.0 (April 2025) and went unnoticed for over a year. The discussion here is a brief exchange between Jeff King and Junio C Hamano about whether to fast-track the fix for the upcoming Git 2.56 release. The regression swapped the default expiry times for reachable and unreachable reflog entries—reachable entries (documented to expire after 90 days) were incorrectly expiring after 30 days, while unreachable entries (documented to expire after 30 days) were being retained for 90 days.

The fix itself, authored by Pushkar Singh, is straightforward and uncontroversial: it restores the correct field order in `REFLOG_EXPIRE_OPTIONS_INIT` in `reflog.h` and includes thorough test coverage in `t/t1410-reflog.sh`. Both Jeff and Junio agree the patch is low-risk, but Junio notes the bug’s age suggests it has had minimal real-world impact. Neither objects to merging it for 2.56, though the urgency is now more about correctness than addressing an active problem. The patch has already been merged to `master`, so this email is essentially a post-merge discussion about release timing rather than a technical review. If you’re tracking the regression, this confirms it’s resolved and unlikely to generate further debate.
