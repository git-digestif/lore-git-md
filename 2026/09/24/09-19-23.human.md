This is the fifth patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-part refactoring series to simplify the `create_repository()` interface. It aligns `git clone` with `git init` by ensuring that neither command applies the `core.sharedRepository` configuration to leading directories when creating the repository’s parent directories.

The change is small—just two lines in `builtin/clone.c`—but significant: it replaces `safe_create_leading_directories_const()` with `safe_create_leading_directories_no_share_const()`, the same helper introduced earlier in the series for `git init`. The motivation is consistency: both commands now ignore the shared-repository setting when creating intermediate directories, regardless of whether the setting comes from global or system-level configuration. The patch also adds two new tests in `t/t1301-shared-repo.sh` to verify that the leading directories are created with the umask’s permissions, not the shared-repository value.

This patch is a clean, uncontroversial step in the larger effort to make `create_repository()` stateless. The tests are thorough and the rationale is clear: there’s no obvious reason for `git clone` and `git init` to behave differently here. If the series lands as-is, this patch should be a straightforward merge.
