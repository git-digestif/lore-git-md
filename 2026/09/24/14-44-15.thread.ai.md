Core problem or goal: Git’s current documentation scatters merge-conflict advice across multiple command man pages, leading to duplication, omissions, and user confusion. The series introduces a dedicated `gitmergeconflicts(7)` guide to centralize this information, improve clarity (e.g., "ours" vs. "theirs"), and provide practical examples. V2 explicitly balances centralization with preserving command-specific nuances, particularly for operations like `git cherry-pick` that involve unique pseudo-refs (`CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) and workflows. The guide’s terminology, foundational explanations, and tooling advice have been refined based on v1 feedback, with key improvements: (1) a new "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section, (2) a rewritten "OURS" AND "THEIRS" explanation, (3) active promotion of `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice (supported by Junio’s detailed examples), and (4) retention of command-specific details in the man pages while linking to the new guide for general advice. Junio’s latest contributions provide **detailed examples** for `diff3`, demonstrating how the common ancestor reveals the *intent* behind conflicting changes. Patrick Steinhardt’s **commit-accessibility enhancement**—embedding commit details (IDs, messages, dates) directly into conflict markers—remains under discussion as a potential follow-up, with Julia Evans endorsing its practical benefits.

- Structure of the new guide: Organized as a practical, example-driven document with sections covering:
  - "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" – A foundational explanation of what merge conflicts are, when they occur, and Git’s behavior during conflicts (e.g., staging successfully merged files, leaving conflicted files unstaged with markers). This section avoids technical jargon and focuses on practical, user-tested clarity.
  - When merge conflicts occur (`git merge`, `git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, `git pull`, `git revert`).
  - Resolution paths (fix or abort).
  - Merge conflict markers (`<<<<<<<`, `=======`, `>>>>>>>`) and the four-step resolution process (identify, edit, stage, continue).
  - Tools for handling conflicts (graphical merge tools, `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`/`zdiff3`, `git show :1:filename`).
  - "OURS" AND "THEIRS" – Explicitly ties "ours" and "theirs" to `HEAD` and the other commit, clarifies their relationship to conflict markers, and explains the "upside down" behavior during rebases. The updated section improves technical accuracy by separating commit-level terms from index stages.
  - `git diff AUTO_MERGE` for tracking progress.
  - Side-by-side examples of `diff3` vs. `zdiff3` output, including **Junio’s detailed examples** demonstrating how the common ancestor reveals the *intent* behind conflicting changes.
  - Sample `git status` output to guide users on `--abort`/`--continue` commands.
  - Approved additions/corrections:
    - The `git log --merge -p <filename>` section, with the approved wording: "will print out all commits which caused the merge conflict for `<filename>`, and the diff of how they changed the file."
    - Clarification that `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` is available any time a cherry-pick stops due to conflict, regardless of whether one or multiple commits are being picked.
    - Patrick Steinhardt’s rewording for "ours/theirs" (revised to explicitly tie "ours" and "theirs" to `HEAD` and the other commit).
    - Mention of `git merge-tool --tool-help` to list available merge tools.
    - Use of `linkgit:` macros for cross-referencing other man pages.
    - Note that `git <thing> --continue` (e.g., `git merge --continue`) is the modern and consistent choice for conflict resolution, with `git commit` as an older alternative.
  - **Deferred enhancements**:
    - **Commit accessibility**: Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to embed commit details (IDs, messages, dates) directly into conflict markers. Julia Evans has endorsed the practical benefits but deferred implementation to a follow-up patch after Patrick’s GitLab prototype.
    - **`merge.conflictstyle=diff3`**: Junio’s and Patrick’s strong endorsement for making `diff3` the default conflict style, with Julia’s user feedback and Junio’s **detailed examples** supporting its practical impact. The guide actively promotes `diff3` as a best practice, but a default change is deferred.
