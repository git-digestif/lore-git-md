This is a documentation feature introducing a new manual page, `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, aimed at improving Git's guidance on handling merge conflicts. The seven-patch series consolidates conflict-resolution advice that was previously scattered or incomplete across multiple command man pages into a single, dedicated guide with examples. The new page is then linked from `git merge`, `git rebase`, `git revert`, `git cherry-pick`, and `git pull`—all commands that can trigger conflicts.

Julia Evans explains that the motivation came from user feedback: existing documentation wasn’t giving people the practical help they needed. The new guide is structured to prioritize actionable advice and examples over abstract concepts like "ours" vs. "theirs," which many users find confusing. She acknowledges several open questions—whether `git commit` and `git merge --continue` are always equivalent, the accuracy of diff3 vs. zdiff3 explanations, and whether to include `git apply`/`git am`—but opts to defer those for now to avoid overcomplicating the initial version.

The series looks well-crafted and addresses a real pain point. The commit messages are clear, the new page is thorough (300 lines), and the cross-linking is sensible. No controversial changes are proposed—just better organization of existing information. If the community agrees the new guide is an improvement, this is likely to land smoothly.
