This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in a three-patch bugfix series that restores correct old-OID reporting in the reference-transaction hook when branches or tags are deleted. The series, now merged to `next`, fixes a regression introduced in Git 2.31 that broke external tools like Gerrit and GitLab by sending all-zero OIDs to the hook instead of the resolved old OID.

Patrick Steinhardt raises **fundamental design concerns** about the patch’s approach. His core argument is that `refs_delete_refs()` exists as a convenience wrapper for simple, unconditional deletions, and that callers needing more complex behavior—like preserving old OIDs or handling partial failures—should instead drive their own transactions directly. He sees the patch as overloading the function with responsibilities it wasn’t designed for, leading to awkward workarounds (e.g., treating null OIDs as a special case for unconditional deletion) and redundant mechanisms (e.g., manually tracking failed refs when transactions already provide this).

The review is **high-weight**: Patrick is a prolific contributor with deep expertise in the refs subsystem, and his objections target the patch’s architectural direction, not just implementation details. While the series has already been merged to `next`, his feedback suggests the design may need rethinking before graduating to `master`. The author will need to either defend the current approach or propose an alternative that aligns better with the refs API’s intended use. This could involve rewriting the patch to use explicit transactions for callers that need old-OID support, or clarifying why the convenience wrapper should evolve to handle these cases. Either way, the discussion is likely to shape how future ref-deletion logic is structured.
