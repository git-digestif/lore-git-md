This is a brief technical exchange in the ongoing `defaultBranchName` advice documentation fix thread, clarifying the broader advice subsystem design context. Jeff King (Peff) and Junio C Hamano agree that the existing pattern—checking `advice_enabled()` before expensive work and then calling `advise()`—is intentional and should not be mechanically converted to `advise_if_enabled()`. The latter helper is reserved for cases where the turn-off instructions are explicitly desired, while the former avoids unnecessary computation when the advice is disabled. Peff notes that a few callers already use `advise_if_enabled()` inside the block when they want to skip expensive work but still show the turn-off message, and Junio confirms this is an accepted pattern. The exchange also draws a parallel to `trace_want()`, reinforcing the idiom.

This discussion does not alter the core patch or its readiness; it merely documents the rationale behind the current subsystem design, which remains unchanged. The unresolved syntax debate (`--global` vs `set --global`) and the backward-compatibility mechanism (passing `NULL` to `vadvise()`) are unaffected.
