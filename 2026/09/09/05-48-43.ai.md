This is the first patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s 9-patch series refactoring ODB alternates handling during repository creation, part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort. The patch splits `init_db()` into three focused functions: `create_repository()`, `create_reference_database()`, and `create_object_database()`, addressing the growing complexity of `init_db()`’s skip flags and divergent behavior between `git init` and `git clone`.

The core change is mechanical but foundational: `init_db()` is renamed to `create_repository()` and stripped of its responsibility for creating the refdb and ODB. Instead, callers now explicitly invoke `create_reference_database()` and `create_object_database()` after repository skeleton creation. This refactoring eliminates the `SKIP_REFDB` flag (and a potential future `SKIP_ODB` flag) and moves the `QUIET` flag’s output logic into `git init`, where it belongs. The only remaining divergence is whether reinitialization is allowed, now handled via a `reinit_ok` pointer that also conveys whether reinitialization occurred.

The patch touches `builtin/clone.c` and `builtin/init-db.c` to adapt to the new interface, and updates `setup.c`/`setup.h` to implement the refactored functions. Documentation in `setup.h` clarifies the purpose of each helper and the `reinit_ok` parameter, addressing Justin Tobler’s feedback from v3. The changes are straightforward and well-motivated, with no user-visible behavior changes. The refactoring sets the stage for deferring ODB creation in `git clone` (patch 2) and consolidating alternates handling (patches 4-6), making this a key enabler for the series’ broader goal of simplifying the ODB interface. Reviewers may focus on the clarity of the new documentation and the correctness of the `reinit_ok` logic, but the patch appears uncontroversial.
