This is a **follow-up in the advice-scope-hint thread**, where Vsevolod Myalitsin responds to Jeff King’s (Peff) review of the v3 patch. The patch aims to make Git’s advice messages scope-aware, so users are told the correct config scope (e.g., `--global`) when disabling advice.

Vsevolod agrees with Peff’s two main suggestions. First, he will adopt the alternative approach for the translatable string, avoiding the confusing `set%s` placeholder in favor of a clearer implementation. Second, he will replace the hardcoded `0` comparison with `ADVICE_LEVEL_NONE` for better readability. However, he raises a concern about reusing Git’s existing `CONFIG_SCOPE` enum: since `CONFIG_SCOPE_UNKNOWN` is `0`, all existing `advice_setting` entries without an explicit `scope_hint` would default to `UNKNOWN` rather than `LOCAL`, which contradicts the current behavior where advice settings default to local scope. He leaves the final decision to Peff but notes that `UNKNOWN` might not be the right semantic fit.

The email advances the thread by clarifying the author’s intent to address Peff’s feedback while surfacing a subtle edge case in the enum-reuse proposal. The remaining open question is whether `CONFIG_SCOPE_UNKNOWN` is an acceptable default for uninitialized `scope_hint` fields, or if the patch should stick with a new `ADVICE_SCOPE` enum to preserve the existing local-scope default. No new technical details or files are introduced; the discussion remains focused on the `advice.c` implementation.
