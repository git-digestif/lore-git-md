This thread is about a patch series from SZEDER Gábor that introduces precompiled headers to Git's build process, targeting `git-compat-util.h` to reduce build times by around 35%. The series is now at version 2, which addressed Junio C Hamano’s editorial feedback on commit message clarity but made no technical changes. The first patch, a cleanup of obsolete `XDIFF_OBJS`, is uncontroversial and ready to merge. The second patch generalizes CMake’s Makefile parsing and is flagged for review from Windows/CMake builders. The third patch resurrects `REFTABLE_OBJS` to exclude reftable files from precompiled-header processing, and the fourth patch implements the core precompiled-header logic.

Jeff King (Peff) recently raised a substantive concern about the final patch. In his real-world setup, target-specific `EXTRA_CPPFLAGS` (like `-DNO_OPENSSL`) cause the precompiled header to be unused, triggering a warning (`-Winvalid-pch`) because the header was generated with different flags. While this doesn’t break the build for Peff, it highlights a limitation: precompiled headers are sensitive to flag mismatches, and specialized targets may not benefit from them. This isn’t a blocker, but it opens a discussion about whether to document the limitation, add a knob to suppress the warning, or explore dynamic precompiled-header generation. No fixes have been proposed yet, but the issue is now on the radar for further review. The series remains under review, with no maintainer decisions or merge signals for the full set.
