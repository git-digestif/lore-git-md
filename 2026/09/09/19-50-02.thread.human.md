This thread is about a patch series from SZEDER Gábor that introduces precompiled headers to Git's build process, targeting `git-compat-util.h` to reduce build times by about 35%. The series is now in its second version, with no technical changes—only an update to the commit message of Patch 3 to address Junio C Hamano’s editorial feedback.

The first patch, a cleanup of an obsolete `XDIFF_OBJS` initialization, is uncontroversial and ready to merge. The second patch generalizes CMake’s parsing of the Makefile to ignore all `*_OBJS` variables, but it’s flagged as needing review from Windows/CMake builders to confirm the regex behaves as intended. The third patch resurrects the `REFTABLE_OBJS` variable to exclude reftable files from precompiled-header processing, as they avoid including `git-compat-util.h` for embeddability reasons. The final patch, just posted, implements the core logic for precompiled headers, including dependency tracking, exclusion rules, and a knob to disable the feature.

The series is purely a build-system optimization with no runtime changes, but there are still open questions about benchmark reproducibility and cross-platform compatibility, particularly for Windows/CMake. Junio has reviewed the commit messages but hasn’t signaled a merge decision yet. The author has already explored and abandoned a more complex exclusion logic, as it yielded only minimal performance gains. The series stands alone and isn’t tied to any larger ongoing efforts like ODB abstraction or Rustification.
