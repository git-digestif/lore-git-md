This thread is fixing a long-standing race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where concurrent operations (like a rebase and background maintenance) can collide over the shared `rr-cache` lock, crashing the second process and leaving the rebase in an unrecoverable state. The issue became more noticeable after Git 2.54’s geometric maintenance strategy made background `git rerere gc` runs more frequent, but the root cause is a general flaw in `rerere` locking.

Thomas Bachem’s v4 series takes a two-part approach. The first patch introduces a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1 second) for acquiring the `MERGE_RR.lock`. Non-maintenance commands (like `git merge` or `git rebase`) wait up to the timeout before failing, while background maintenance skips its run entirely if the lock is held. The second patch refines this further: commands that stop at a conflict (e.g., `git rebase`) now proceed with a warning if the lock remains held after the timeout, preserving the user’s ability to recover conflict preimages manually. Post-resolution recording and explicit `git rerere` commands still fail to prevent silent corruption of the `MERGE_RR` file. The series also restores proper error handling for `rerere_clear()` and `rerere_forget()`, ensuring `--abort` and `--skip` operations fail if the lock is held.

Key participants—Phillip Wood, Patrick Steinhardt, and Junio Hamano—have all weighed in, with their feedback largely incorporated into v4. Phillip initially opposed the timeout-based fix but now accepts it as a pragmatic stopgap while deeper fixes are explored. Patrick clarified that the race is a broader `rerere` robustness issue, not just a maintenance-heuristic side effect, and his suggestions for configurable timeouts and unified error reporting are now part of the series. Junio’s concern about silently skipping `rerere` recording (risking lost conflict resolution work) led to the current compromise: warnings at conflict stops, failures elsewhere. The only remaining discussion centers on whether this trade-off between resilience and correctness is the right one. The series is under active review, with no outstanding objections to the locking patch itself.
