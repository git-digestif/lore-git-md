This thread is about a three-patch series (v6) from Thomas Bachem that fixes a long-standing race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where concurrent operations (like a rebase and background maintenance) contend for the shared `rr-cache` directory lock. The series has been through several iterations, with all prior feedback incorporated, and was considered ready for integration—until Patrick Steinhardt raised a late objection.

The first two patches introduce a configurable lock timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1 second) and a `--skip-locked` mode for `git rerere gc` (used by background maintenance to silently skip a held lock). The third patch, which Patrick now questions, allows conflict-stopping commands (e.g., `git rebase`, `git merge`) to proceed with a warning if the lock remains held after the timeout, while post-resolution recording and explicit `git rerere` commands still fail immediately. This compromise was meant to balance resilience (letting users continue their work) with correctness (preventing silent corruption of the `MERGE_RR` file).

Patrick’s objection centers on whether this third patch is necessary at all. He argues that the earlier changes (timeout and `--skip-locked`) may already address the problem sufficiently, and that the warning-and-proceed behavior could be premature without real-world evidence of user impact. He suggests dropping the patch from the current series while leaving the door open for future reintroduction if the issue arises in practice. His feedback introduces uncertainty about the architectural settlement—whether the trade-off between resilience and correctness is justified now or should wait for more data.

The series touches several files, including `rerere.c`, `rerere.h`, `apply.c`, `builtin/am.c`, `builtin/merge.c`, `builtin/stash.c`, `sequencer.c`, and the test suite. Phillip Wood and Junio C Hamano have previously reviewed the series, with their feedback fully incorporated into v6. Phillip initially opposed the timeout-based fix but now accepts it as a pragmatic stopgap, while Junio’s concerns about loud failures were addressed by the warning-and-proceed compromise. No other participants have weighed in on Patrick’s latest objection, leaving the question unresolved. The series remains technically ready, but its fate now hinges on whether the maintainers agree with Patrick’s assessment or prefer to proceed as planned.
