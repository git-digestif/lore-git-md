This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series (v7) that addresses a long-standing race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where concurrent operations contend for the shared `rr-cache` directory lock. The issue was exacerbated by Git 2.54’s geometric maintenance strategy but is fundamentally a robustness flaw in `rerere` locking.

Thomas Bachem’s final series introduces two key changes: (1) a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1 second) for all `rerere` writers to wait for the lock, and (2) a hidden `--skip-locked` flag for `git rerere gc` that allows auto-maintenance tasks (like `git maintenance run --auto`) to skip silently if the lock is held, matching the behavior of other maintenance operations. The first patch, which adds the timeout, has been confirmed as technically complete and ready for integration. The second patch, just summarized, implements `--skip-locked` and is also ready, with all prior feedback incorporated.

Key participants include Phillip Wood, who initially opposed the timeout-based fix but now accepts it as a pragmatic stopgap while deeper fixes (like disabling maintenance during rebase) are explored; Patrick Steinhardt, who clarified the root cause and influenced the design to prioritize correctness over resilience; and Junio C Hamano, whose feedback on earlier versions shaped the final approach. The series adopts a conservative stance: conflict-stopping commands fail outright if the lock remains held after the timeout, ensuring correctness. Open questions remain about whether garbage collection is useful mid-rebase and how future maintenance strategy changes will interact with rebase-specific config overrides, but these do not block the current patches. The series is now technically complete and awaiting Junio’s final review.
