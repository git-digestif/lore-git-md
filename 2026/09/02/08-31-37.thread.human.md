The `rerere` race-condition fix, a two-patch series by Thomas Bachem, is now ready for integration. The series addresses a long-standing robustness flaw in Git's `rerere` subsystem where concurrent operations—exacerbated by the geometric maintenance strategy introduced in Git 2.54—could collide while contending for the shared `rr-cache` directory lock. The solution introduces a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1000 ms) for all `rerere` writers, allowing processes to wait for the lock rather than failing immediately, and a hidden `--skip-locked` mode for `git rerere gc` that skips silently when the lock is held, matching the behavior of other auto-maintenance tasks.

After eight iterations, the series has incorporated all feedback from Junio C Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, and Phillip Wood. Junio’s final sign-off confirmed the series as technically complete, and Patrick’s latest reply explicitly endorses moving it to the `next` branch for integration testing. The design now fails outright if the lock remains held after the timeout, prioritizing correctness over resilience. Phillip Wood’s earlier critique about the futility of garbage collection mid-rebase remains unresolved but deferred, as the series focuses on the immediate locking issue rather than rebase-specific maintenance logic. With no substantive objections left, the thread is effectively closed, pending integration.
