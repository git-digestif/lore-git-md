This thread is about fixing a race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) can prune conflict-resolution entries while a foreground operation like `rebase` is recording them. The issue, initially thought to be tied to the geometric maintenance strategy introduced in Git 2.54, is now understood as a long-standing flaw in `rerere`’s locking design—any two concurrent writers can trigger it, not just maintenance.

Thomas Bachem’s two-part series (v4 of the locking patch in progress) proposes a compromise: conflict stops during rebase emit a warning and proceed, preserving the user’s ability to manually recover the conflict preimage, while post-resolution recording and explicit `git rerere` commands fail immediately if the lock is held. The patch also introduces configurable locking timeouts and disables background maintenance during rebase via config overrides. Junio Hamano and Phillip Wood have largely accepted this approach, though Phillip remains critical of the fork-based background maintenance design and insists on consistency between rebase backends.

Patrick Steinhardt’s latest review refines the patch’s framing, emphasizing that the race is a general robustness issue, not just a maintenance-heuristic problem. He also questions the wisdom of silently skipping `rerere` recording when the lock timeout expires, suggesting this change be split into a separate commit for clearer discussion. This aligns with Junio’s preference for loud, actionable errors and Phillip’s advocacy for failing fast. The series remains under review, with open questions about error messaging, backend consistency, and whether garbage collection is even useful mid-rebase. No objections block progress, but the trade-offs between resilience and correctness are still debated.
