This thread began with Thomas Bachem’s two-patch series fixing a long-standing race condition in Git’s `rerere` subsystem, where concurrent operations could contend for the shared `rr-cache` directory lock. The fix introduced a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`) and a `--skip-locked` mode for background maintenance, addressing both the immediate issue and the broader robustness flaw. That series is now in Junio C Hamano’s `next` branch, with all feedback incorporated and no substantive objections remaining.

A follow-up cleanup by Okhuomon Ajayi aligns the naming of Git’s credential-store lock timeout with the convention established by the `rerere` series. The patch renames `credentialStore.lockTimeoutMS` to `credentialStore.lockTimeout` (retaining the old name as a deprecated alias) and has just received Junio’s explicit sign-off, marking it ready for `next`. The v3 update resolved Junio’s earlier concern by demonstrating that all existing lock-timeout variables omit the unit from their names and are consistently measured in milliseconds, making the change uncontroversial and purely mechanical.

The thread also surfaced deeper questions about Git’s maintenance design—whether rebases should run maintenance at all, whether the fork-based background maintenance model is flawed, and how to reconcile the merge and apply backends—but these remain unresolved. Phillip Wood’s critique of mid-rebase garbage collection as useless and his call for consistency between backends have yet to be addressed, though Thomas plans to tackle the latter in a future sequencer patch. For now, the immediate fixes are complete and queued for integration.
