This is the v9 submission of the `git var` extension series, now split into four focused patches as requested by Junio and Phillip Wood. The series implements the long-discussed unified interface for Git’s identity and signing configuration by adding support for multiple variables, NUL-terminated output (`-z`), and broken-out identity components (`GIT_AUTHOR_NAME`, `GIT_AUTHOR_EMAIL`, `GIT_AUTHOR_DATE`, `GIT_COMMITTER_NAME`, `GIT_COMMITTER_EMAIL`, `GIT_COMMITTER_DATE`). The controversial `GIT_SIGNING_KEY` variable has been removed due to ambiguity across signing mechanisms (GPG/SSH/X.509).

The patches are structured as independent, buildable steps: (1) internal conversion of multi-valued variables to `string_list`, (2) `-z` output mode, (3) multi-variable support, and (4) the new identity variables. Each patch includes tests and maintains backward compatibility. Key behavioral changes include relaxed exit codes for multi-variable queries (omitting unset variables rather than failing) and strict argument ordering (options before variable names). The output format for multi-valued variables now emits repeated `VARIABLE=value` entries, matching `git var -l`, though Phillip Wood’s suggestion of a trailing delimiter remains an open question.

The series addresses all prior feedback from Junio, Phillip Wood, and others. Junio’s structural concerns are resolved by the split, and Phillip’s technical points (exit codes, multi-valued formatting, test robustness) are incorporated. The commit messages are now prose rather than development logs. The patches touch `builtin/var.c`, `Documentation/git-var.adoc`, and `t/t0007-git-var.sh`, with no new configuration options or on-disk format changes.

This iteration is technically complete and ready for integration. The series remains in `seen`; graduation to `next` depends on Junio’s review of the split and the resolution of the trailing delimiter question. The removal of `GIT_SIGNING_KEY` is not a blocker, and the exit-code behavior aligns with `GIT_CONFIG_NOSYSTEM` precedent. The implementation is well-tested, with comprehensive coverage for `-z` output, multi-variable queries, and edge cases like unset variables.
