This is a documentation patch, the fifth iteration (v5) of a series refining the explanation of `git log --follow`'s limitations. It supersedes the recently merged v4, which documented `--no-follow` and its interaction with `log.follow` but left the `--follow` constraints themselves under-specified.

The patch now explicitly distinguishes between *errors* (explicit `--follow` with zero or multiple paths) and *no-ops* (the same cases when `log.follow` is set). It also declares that directory arguments and wildcard patterns, while accepted, produce *unspecified* results, and directs users to `--no-follow` to disable following entirely. The ordering of `--follow` and `--no-follow` in the man page is reversed to list the positive option first, matching the project’s usual style. The changes are confined to `Documentation/git-log.adoc` and `Documentation/config/log.adoc`.

This iteration responds to Junio Hamano’s feedback on v4, which noted that the documentation should be more precise about what is *not* supported. The new text avoids over-specifying undefined behavior while making clear that `--follow` is only reliable for single-file, linear history. The patch is well-motivated and uncontroversial, but it is unusual to see a new version after a topic has already been merged to `next`; this suggests the author and maintainer agreed that the documentation could be improved further before graduating to `master`.
