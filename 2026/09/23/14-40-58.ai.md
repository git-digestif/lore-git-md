This is a follow-up discussion in the Git Contributor's Summit thread, focusing specifically on documentation improvements. Toon Claes responds to Johannes Schindelin's recap of the summit's documentation session, adding context, personal accountability, and concrete next steps.

The email centers on two main themes: **documentation quality** and **discoverability**. Toon echoes Schindelin's excitement about Julia Evans' work to make Git's documentation more approachable for beginners, emphasizing that while experienced contributors ("old-timers") may understand the need for better explanations, translating deep technical knowledge into beginner-friendly language is a distinct skill. He also acknowledges his own role in the stagnation of `git-scm.com` (the project's official website), admitting he hasn't devoted enough time to maintenance despite earlier commitments. A key pain point is the website's "UI list" (a curated list of Git GUI tools), which has seen little review activity; Toon links to a stalled PR he opened to address this, framing it as a symptom of broader maintainability challenges.

On **tooling and funding**, Toon agrees with the summit's consensus that iterative improvements are more practical than waiting for perfection. He suggests Discord as a better venue than the mailing list for gathering user feedback, though this risks fragmenting discussion. For diagrams, he endorses the idea of using Open Blocks (an Asciidoctor feature) to support both text-based man pages and richer HTML visuals, while noting that no diagram format (e.g., Mermaid, GraphViz) has been decisively chosen. Toon also references prior discussions about privacy-respecting traffic analytics for the website, linking to an open issue, but acknowledges that caching and AI scraping complicate data collection.

The tone is collaborative and self-critical, with Toon taking ownership of past inaction while proposing incremental solutions. His comments add nuance to Schindelin's recap, particularly around the practical challenges of maintaining `git-scm.com` and the trade-offs between different documentation formats. For readers tracking Git 3.0 planning, this email underscores that documentation improvements are a priority but remain under-resourced, with funding and tooling decisions still unresolved. The discussion is unlikely to derail the 3.0 timeline but highlights a long-standing pain point for users.
