This is a brief follow-up in the Git Contributor's Summit thread, where Junio C Hamano adds nuance to the discussion about documentation improvements. He responds directly to Toon Claes' earlier email, agreeing with the need for iterative progress but emphasizing a critical gap: **old-timers (experienced contributors) often lack awareness of what new users struggle to understand**, even if they possess deep technical knowledge and teaching skills. Junio clarifies the summit's consensus on documentation strategy, distinguishing between two approaches: **incremental updates for reference material (man pages, technical notes)** and **complete rewrites for tutorials and conceptual guides**. His comment underscores that Git's core concepts may not have changed, but the broader "world view" (e.g., how users interact with Git, common workflows, or misconceptions) has evolved, making some existing tutorials outdated or misleading.

The email is short but substantive, as it refines the scope of the documentation effort. For readers tracking Git 3.0 planning, this reinforces that documentation improvements are a priority but will proceed in parallel with technical work, with no expectation of delaying the release. Junio's focus on "rewriting from scratch" for conceptual material suggests a more ambitious approach than mere incremental fixes, though the timeline and resourcing for this remain unclear. The discussion remains collaborative, with no controversy or new action items proposed.
