This is a follow-up discussion email in the ongoing "What's cooking in git.git" and Git 3.0 planning thread. Daniele Sassoli, a contributor with JGit experience, responds to Johannes Schindelin’s summit recap with clarifications about JGit’s pluggable backend capabilities and SHA-256 support.

Sassoli confirms that JGit has supported pluggable backends since its early days (citing a 16-year-old implementation by Shawn Pearce) and notes that while production adoption has been limited, the infrastructure remains viable. He references a decade-old discussion about a Cassandra backend and Luca’s Gerrit User Summit talk on the topic, suggesting JGit’s architecture is still adaptable. On SHA-256, Sassoli acknowledges corporate interest in funding the work but states no active development is underway, attributing the delay to insufficient community demand rather than technical barriers. His tone is optimistic about future progress, framing JGit as a "hackable" project despite current gaps.

The email advances the thread by providing concrete context about JGit’s readiness for two key Git 3.0 goals: pluggable object databases and SHA-256 interoperability. Sassoli’s input suggests that while JGit’s technical foundation is sound, ecosystem coordination (e.g., funding, prioritization) is the primary bottleneck. This contrasts with Schindelin’s earlier note about JGit being a "gap" for SHA-256, reframing the issue as one of resource allocation rather than capability. The clarification is relevant for downstream projects planning migrations to Git 3.0, as it highlights JGit as a potential partner for interoperability efforts—if resources materialize. No new technical proposals or objections are raised; the focus is purely on correcting and expanding the factual record.
