This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing "What's cooking in git.git" thread, specifically a reply to Johannes Schindelin’s Contributor’s Summit recap. Daniele Sassoli, who led a breakout session at the summit but missed the afternoon discussions, chimes in to clarify and expand on the pluggable object database (ODB) topic, particularly regarding JGit’s capabilities and history.

Sassoli corrects and supplements Schindelin’s note about JGit’s pluggable backends, pointing out that JGit has supported pluggable storage backends since its early days (16 years ago, introduced by Shawn Pearce). While no production backends emerged, the infrastructure exists, and there have been community efforts—like Luca’s Cassandra backend proposal for the Gerrit User Summit. Sassoli also addresses JGit’s SHA-256 support, noting that while there’s corporate interest (including from GerritForge), no active development is underway, which he finds unfortunate. He emphasizes that JGit remains actively maintained and hackable, despite the lack of progress on SHA-256.

The email is not a patch or technical proposal but a factual clarification aimed at the broader Git community. It provides useful context for the pluggable ODB discussion, particularly for those considering interoperability between Git and JGit. Sassoli’s tone is constructive and collaborative, reinforcing that JGit is a viable target for future work on pluggable backends or SHA-256 support. For readers tracking the Git 3.0 roadmap or ODB abstraction efforts, this note highlights that JGit’s limitations (e.g., missing SHA-256) are not due to technical constraints but rather a lack of prioritization or funding.
