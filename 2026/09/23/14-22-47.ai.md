This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Git's security mailing list and process, specifically a reply from Toon Claes to Johannes Schindelin's summit recap. The email focuses on clarifying and expanding on the security process challenges raised at the Contributor's Summit, with Toon offering concrete next steps and commitments.

Toon acknowledges the problems Schindelin highlighted—AI-generated reports overwhelming triage, lack of documented processes, and knowledge bottlenecks—and agrees on the need for action. He commits to driving the documentation of Git's security model, a task he plans to tackle after the summit, and expresses interest in reviving a stalled conversation about Git's security boundary (originally initiated by Derrick Stolee). On staffing, Toon announces that GitLab plans to invest more resources in handling security reports, addressing the volunteer burnout concern.

The email also reveals a knowledge gap around the release process: Toon admits he doesn’t know how to merge, backport, or prepare advisories, and asks to shadow Schindelin to learn. This is a practical offer to distribute the workload, which has historically relied heavily on a few individuals. Toon suggests adopting Git for Windows’ release cadence (second Tuesday of the month, with patches settled three weeks prior) as a potential solution for the current backlog, though he notes that urgent issues (like Elijah Newren’s 0-day work) may still require exceptions.

The tone is collaborative and solution-oriented, avoiding the finger-pointing Schindelin critiqued. Toon’s proposal to collaborate on the next security release is a clear step toward addressing the process’s fragility. For readers tracking Git 3.0 planning, this email underscores that security process improvements are a prerequisite for smoother releases, especially as the project scales. The key takeaway: while the summit identified problems, Toon’s reply moves the discussion toward actionable fixes, with GitLab’s increased involvement as a positive signal.
