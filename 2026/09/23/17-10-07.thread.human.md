This thread is about a two-patch series by Tamir Duberstein that optimizes `git ls-files` by reusing the untracked cache already populated by `git status`. The first patch fixed a bug causing unnecessary cache invalidation, while the second (and most recent) patch implements the core optimization: expanding collapsed directories and filtering cached listings on demand without writing the expanded cache back to the index.

The series is under review and hasn't received any feedback yet. The key technical change is in `dir.c` and `builtin/ls-files.c`, with new performance tests in `t/perf/p3010-ls-files.sh` and regression tests in `t/t7063-status-untracked-cache.sh`. Benchmarks show significant speedups—up to 7× in synthetic tests—without regressing correctness for complex pathspecs. The optimization is particularly effective for repositories with many tracked files, as it avoids redundant directory traversals.

No objections or requests for changes have been raised so far, but reviewers may ask for additional real-world benchmarks to validate the impact of the first patch beyond synthetic tests. The series is self-contained and doesn’t modify the on-disk cache format or index state.
