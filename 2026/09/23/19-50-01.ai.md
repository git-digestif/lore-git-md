This is a design discussion reply in the ongoing thread about improving `git fetch` behavior in shallow repositories. Junio C Hamano responds to D. Ben Knoble’s refinement of the priming mechanism for the matching-inspired fetch mode, clarifying a long-standing design principle: the command `git fetch <remote> <branch>` has historically been treated as a "single-shot fetch" that does not create or update remote-tracking branches (`refs/remotes/<remote>/<branch>`). This behavior dates back 20 years and is deeply embedded in user expectations and scripts.

Junio acknowledges that D. Ben Knoble’s suggestion—automatically creating remote-tracking branches when fetching from a named remote (e.g., `second`) rather than a URL—has some intuitive appeal, especially for users who expect to interact with the remote repeatedly. However, he emphasizes that any change to this behavior would require a careful transition plan and an escape hatch (e.g., a `--single-shot` flag) to preserve backward compatibility for users who rely on the current behavior. The discussion highlights a tension between preserving existing workflows and simplifying the priming step for the matching-inspired fetch mode.

This reply does not propose a specific solution but sets a constraint: the priming mechanism must either work within the existing "single-shot" semantics or include a well-designed transition plan. The thread’s focus remains on refining the matching-inspired fetch mode, but this clarification narrows the design space for how the priming step could be implemented. No objections to the core idea (matching-inspired fetch mode) are raised, but the feasibility of D. Ben Knoble’s refinement is now in question. Future discussion will likely explore alternative priming mechanisms or transition strategies.
