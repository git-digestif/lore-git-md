This is a substantive review in the documentation cross-reference modernization thread. Jeff King (Peff) engages deeply with the AsciiDoc syntax and rendering behavior, providing technical analysis that clarifies why the two-part link form (`<<target,text>>`) is necessary in the patch. He tests both AsciiDoc and Asciidoctor implementations, demonstrating that the single-form (`<<target>>`) produces inconsistent or nonsensical output across backends—such as rendering as `[REMOTES]` in HTML or duplicating text in man pages ("the section called 'PRUNING' section"). His findings confirm the patch’s pragmatic choice to use the two-part form universally, though he notes the trade-off in source readability.

Peff also explores potential workarounds, such as configuring fallback text or introducing a custom macro (e.g., `section-link:`), but concludes these are premature optimizations for this patch. He suggests the commit message could better reflect the technical rationale—specifically, that the two-part form is needed to control rendered text across backends, not just for cross-file references. The review is tested (he explicitly mentions testing with AsciiDoc 10.2.1 and Asciidoctor 2.0.26) and substantive, addressing both the "what" and the "why" of the implementation. No objections are raised; the patch remains queued for integration in `next`.
