The review is **substantive but narrow**: Junio does not challenge the fix or the regression’s root cause, but instead focuses on the **structure of the test coverage**. He notes that the test script creates three separate repositories (`reachable-keep`, `reachable-expire`, and `unreachable`) and tests four scenarios (reachable entries at 60 and 100 days, unreachable entries at 20 and 40 days). While the reachable cases are split into two independent blocks, the unreachable cases are tested together in a single block. Junio finds this **asymmetry puzzling** and suggests two alternative approaches: either consolidate all four cases into a single repository (to reduce setup/teardown overhead) or split them into four separate `test_expect_success` blocks (for easier debugging). The current hybrid approach—three repositories, with one testing two scenarios—does not clearly align with either goal.

This feedback is **not a blocker** for merging, but it reflects Junio’s preference for test clarity and maintainability. The fix itself remains ready for integration, and the test coverage is already comprehensive; this is a **polish-level suggestion** rather than a functional critique.
