This is a review reply from Junio C Hamano in the ongoing thread about adding scope hints to Git’s advice messages. The email clarifies Junio’s stance on the design direction for Vsevolod Myalitsin’s patch series, which proposes a boolean `is_global_hint` to simplify scope hints for `advice.*` settings.

Junio acknowledges that the original 2009 design of the `advice.*` config system never intended the "use `git config advice.foo false`" instruction to be a literal cut-and-paste command. Instead, users were expected to adapt the command to their own needs (e.g., adding `--global` or `--local` as appropriate). This implies that the absence of a scope hint was intentional, and adding one now is an enhancement rather than a bugfix. Junio agrees that including `--global` in the hint is acceptable, as users can still tweak it if they prefer a different scope, but he suggests an even simpler approach: the hint could *always* recommend `--global` without needing to mark individual advice settings for their intended scope. This would eliminate the need for the `is_global_hint` field entirely, further simplifying the design.

The reply does not reject the boolean `is_global_hint` proposal outright, but it does signal a preference for an even more minimal solution. This leaves the door open for Vsevolod to either revise the series to use a uniform `--global` hint or continue advocating for the boolean approach, which Jeff King has twice endorsed as aligning with the original intent of `advice.*` settings. The thread remains unresolved on this point, with no indication yet of whether Junio will pick up the series for integration. The key technical detail here is Junio’s suggestion to avoid per-setting scope hints altogether, which would reduce the patch’s complexity but may conflict with Peff’s argument that some settings (like `defaultBranchName`) are inherently global.
