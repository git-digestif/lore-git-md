This thread is about a bugfix for Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism, which prevents unsafe force-pushes by checking if the remote ref has been updated since the local branch was last fetched. Aleksei Sviridkin identified an uninitialized variable bug in the files backend that causes the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog (e.g., in fresh clones), leading to false positives and potential unsafe pushes. The fix—a one-line initialization of `timestamp_t date` to `0` in `remote.c`—has been refined through three iterations, addressing all prior review feedback, including Junio’s requests to remove an `Assisted-by: LLM` trailer and adjust test cleanup placement.

The patch is technically ready for integration, but the discussion has shifted to a design trade-off: whether the fallback timestamp should be `0` (epoch start, ensuring a full reflog scan for safety) or a more recent cutoff like `gc.reflogExpire` (or 90 days) to avoid redundant work. Aleksei argues that `0` is the only safe choice, as older reflog entries can persist until explicitly expired, and a non-ancestor tip could slip through if the cutoff is too recent. Junio, however, questions whether this edge case—reflog entries older than 90 days persisting due to disabled `gc`—should dictate the default behavior for all users, framing the full scan as a performance penalty for those who follow Git’s default reflog expiration policies. The tension between safety and performance remains unresolved, and while Junio does not dispute the correctness of the fix, this exchange may delay the patch’s graduation to `next` until a consensus is reached. No further code changes are anticipated, but the discussion could inform future refinements to the safety mechanism.
