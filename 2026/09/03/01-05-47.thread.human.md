This thread is about a bug in Git’s `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism, where an uninitialized variable in the files backend causes the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog (common in fresh clones). This can lead to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check, potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes. Aleksei Sviridkin’s patch (v3) fixes the issue by initializing `timestamp_t date` to `0` in `remote.c`, ensuring the reflog traversal covers the entire local branch history when no remote-tracking reflog exists.

The discussion has centered on the fallback value for `date`: Aleksei and now Tyler Cipriani argue that `0` (epoch start) is the only safe choice, as it guarantees correctness even when older reflog entries persist (e.g., due to disabled `gc` or non-default expiration policies). Tyler’s review provides a concrete scenario showing how a cutoff-based approach (e.g., 90 days or `gc.reflogExpire`) can reject valid pushes, while `0` avoids this risk at a negligible performance cost. Junio Hamano, however, prefers a cutoff aligned with Git’s reflog retention design, arguing it avoids penalizing users who follow default expiration policies. The patch is technically complete, but this design trade-off remains the sole obstacle to integration.

Key participants include Aleksei (author), who addressed all prior feedback and justified the fallback value, and Junio, who remains unconvinced but has not objected to the core fix. Tyler’s review strengthens the case for `0`, leaving the final decision to Junio. The thread is now focused on whether the current implementation will graduate to `next` or prompt a follow-up patch adjusting the fallback value.
