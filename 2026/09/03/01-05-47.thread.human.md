This thread is about a bugfix for the `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism in `git push`. Aleksei Sviridkin identified an uninitialized variable in the files backend that causes the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog, leading to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check and potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes. The fix initializes `timestamp_t date` to `0` in `remote.c`, ensuring the reflog traversal covers the entire local branch history when no remote-tracking reflog exists.

The discussion has centered on the fallback value for the timestamp. Aleksei and Tyler Cipriani argue that `0` (epoch start) is the only safe choice, as older reflog entries can persist until explicitly expired, and a non-ancestor tip could slip through if the cutoff is too recent. Junio Hamano, however, prefers a 90-day or `gc.reflogExpire` fallback, aligning with Git’s reflog retention design and avoiding performance penalties for users who follow default expiration policies. Aleksei and Tyler have provided timing data and a concrete scenario showing that the cutoff is faster only when it produces the wrong result (rejecting valid pushes when the matching reflog entry is older than the cutoff), while `0` ensures safety at a negligible performance cost in typical repositories.
