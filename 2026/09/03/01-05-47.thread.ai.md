Subject: [PATCH] push: fix uninitialized variable in `--force-if-includes` reflog walk
Series version: standalone patch (v3 posted)
Originating author: Aleksei Sviridkin
Thread type: bugfix

Core problem or goal
The `--force-if-includes` safety mechanism in `git push` contains an uninitialized variable bug in the files backend that causes the reflog walk to terminate prematurely when the remote-tracking ref has no reflog (e.g., in fresh clones). This leads to false positives in the "remote ref updated since checkout" check, potentially allowing unsafe force-pushes. The patch initializes `timestamp_t date` to `0` in `remote.c` to ensure the reflog traversal covers the entire local branch history when no remote-tracking reflog exists. The discussion has focused on the design rationale for the fallback value: Aleksei argues that `0` (epoch start) is the only safe choice because reflog entries older than `gc.reflogExpire` (or a fixed 90-day cutoff) can persist until explicitly expired, and a non-ancestor tip could slip through if the cutoff is too recent. Junio’s alternative (90 days or `gc.reflogExpire`) is acknowledged but shown to risk unsafe pushes in edge cases, particularly when `gc` is disabled or reflog expiration is non-default. Aleksei’s latest reply provides concrete timing data demonstrating that the cutoff is faster *only* when it produces the wrong result (rejecting valid pushes when the matching reflog entry is older than the cutoff), while `0` ensures safety at a negligible performance cost in typical repositories. The choice is now framed as a deliberate trade-off between safety and performance, with Aleksei leaving the final decision to Junio.
