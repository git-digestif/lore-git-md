This is a substantive review comment on the second patch in Julia Evans’ seven-patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`. D. Ben Knoble raises two specific concerns about content that was removed from `git-merge.adoc` and not carried over to the new guide: a conceptual explanation of what merge conflicts *are* (why they occur), and a practical tip for resolving them using `git log --merge -p <path>`.

Knoble argues that both pieces of information are valuable and should be preserved in the new guide. The first—why conflicts exist—provides foundational context that may not be obvious to all users, while the second is a concrete, actionable command (`git log --merge -p`) that helps users understand the changes on each side of the conflict. He notes that he personally uses a similar alias (`conflict = log --oneline --graph --left-right --boundary --merge`) and believes the guide should help users discover `--merge` as a tool for conflict resolution.

The comment is constructive and grounded in real-world usage, suggesting that the new guide may have inadvertently omitted useful material in its effort to consolidate and streamline conflict-resolution advice. This feedback is likely to prompt a revision to the first patch (which introduces the new guide) rather than to this one, since the missing content belongs in `gitmergeconflicts(7)` rather than in `git-merge.adoc`. The review is substantive—it engages with the *content* of the documentation and its practical utility, not just its structure or formatting.
