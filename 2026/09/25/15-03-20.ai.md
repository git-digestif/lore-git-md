This is a CI/build system patch update in the ongoing series to reduce resource exhaustion in GitHub Actions. Tamir Duberstein responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s review of Patch 2 (dynamic parallelism for `make` and `prove` on Linux runners) by providing the requested benchmark data and a revised approach.

The new data compares CI runtime across three configurations: the current fixed `-j10`, the proposed `JOBS=$(nproc)` (1x CPU count), and a hypothetical `2x CPU count`. On Linux, `1x CPU count` shows a modest 2% speedup over `-j10`, while `2x CPU count` improves runtime by 7%. However, macOS and Windows results are inconsistent—macOS regresses with `1x CPU count` but improves with `2x`, while Windows shows negligible change. Tamir concludes that the optimal setting is unclear and opts for `1x CPU count` as a conservative, consistent baseline across CI providers (GitHub and GitLab), deferring further tuning to a future patch.

The revised Patch 2 (now in v2) unifies the logic for both CI platforms, addressing Patrick’s concern about divergent configurations. No new failure logs are provided, but the benchmark data gives reviewers concrete evidence to weigh the trade-offs. The discussion remains open on whether oversubscription (e.g., `2x CPU count`) might be worth exploring later, but for now, the simpler `1x` approach looks like a safe, incremental improvement. The ball is back in Patrick’s court to assess whether the data and unified logic satisfy his review concerns.
