Ben Knoble’s point that the `--merge` option in `git log` implicitly constructs a `HEAD...MERGE_HEAD` revision range, which is essential for understanding how the command works in conflict resolution. This detail was missing from Junio’s earlier explanation of what `git log --merge -p <path>` does, and it directly affects the accuracy of the proposed wording in the new guide.

The clarification is narrow but important: it confirms that the command’s utility comes from showing commits on both sides of the merge (ours and theirs), not just the conflicted paths. This will likely be incorporated into the guide’s "Tools to get more context" section to explain why `git log --merge -p` is more informative than a simple `git log -p` for conflict resolution.
