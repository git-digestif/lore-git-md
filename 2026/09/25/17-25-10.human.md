This is a follow-up in the ongoing **Git tutorial rewrite** thread, a documentation effort focused on pedagogy. Julia Evans responds to Junio C Hamano’s concern about the fate of topics omitted from the new `gittutorial.adoc` by proposing a two-pronged strategy: (1) writing new, clearer guides for material that still belongs in Git’s documentation, and (2) curating and linking to high-quality external resources (via `git-scm.com/learn`) for topics better suited to books or third-party guides. She frames this as a practical way to address the outdatedness of current tutorials like `gittutorial` and `giteveryday`, which she argues undermine trust in Git’s documentation by failing to deliver on their promises.

The email is light on technical detail but clarifies the author’s intent: the rewrite is not just about simplifying the tutorial but also about modernizing Git’s broader documentation ecosystem. Julia acknowledges that `git-scm.com/learn` is currently outdated and suggests it could be improved as part of this effort. The tone is constructive and forward-looking, but the proposal remains high-level—no specific topics are triaged, and no concrete plans for new guides or external links are outlined. This leaves the structural question Junio raised (how to handle omitted material) partially addressed but still open for further discussion.

The patch series itself is not yet sent to the list, so this remains a design-phase exchange.
