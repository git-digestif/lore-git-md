This is a follow-up in the **Git tutorial rewrite** thread, a documentation effort targeting pedagogy. Julia Evans responds to Junio C Hamano’s concern about the fate of topics omitted from the rewritten `gittutorial.adoc` by proposing a two-pronged strategy: (1) writing new, clearer guides for material that still belongs in Git’s documentation, and (2) curating and linking to high-quality external resources (via `git-scm.com/learn`) for topics better suited to books or third-party guides. She frames outdated guides like `gittutorial` and `giteveryday` as actively harmful, undermining user trust in Git’s documentation, and argues that replacing them with either improved internal guides or well-vetted external links is preferable to leaving them in their current state.

The email does not resolve the triage question Junio raised—it does not specify which omitted topics fall into which bucket—but it clarifies Julia’s intent to address the gap through a combination of internal and external resources. The tone is pragmatic, acknowledging that a mix of both strategies is likely necessary given the project’s current bandwidth. No technical implementation details or patch specifics are discussed; this remains a high-level design and documentation-planning exchange. The proposal to improve `git-scm.com/learn` is a concrete action item, though it lies outside the Git codebase itself.
