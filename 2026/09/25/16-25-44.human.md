This is a documentation patch series introducing a new `gitmergeconflicts(7)` man page. D. Ben Knoble’s review is a substantive, positive engagement with the series’ core design choices and open questions.

Knoble endorses the series’ approach of centralizing merge-conflict guidance in a dedicated page rather than using includes, agreeing that the ~300-line length would make includes unwieldy. He also supports the decision to defer coverage of `git apply` and `git am` conflicts, calling it a pragmatic way to avoid a "can of worms." On the question of whether `git history --squash` (once it lands) should be added to the list of conflict-generating commands, Knoble notes that the `git history` series currently rejects operations that create conflicts, so no immediate update is needed.

The review touches on two key user-experience decisions in the new guide. First, Knoble agrees with Julia Evans’ choice to de-emphasize "ours" vs. "theirs" terminology, observing that most users rely on tools like `diff3` or mergetools rather than reasoning about the labels. Second, he acknowledges the removal of `_HEAD` references (e.g., `MERGE_HEAD`) as a reasonable simplification, though he personally uses `REBASE_HEAD` to inspect the current patch during a rebase.

Knoble’s feedback is constructive and aligns with the series’ goals: improving clarity for users who find Git’s current conflict-resolution documentation scattered and confusing. The review does not identify technical inaccuracies or request major structural changes, but it does surface small points for future consideration (e.g., whether `git history --squash` might eventually need coverage). The series remains on track for integration once the mechanical `command-list.txt` fix is applied.
