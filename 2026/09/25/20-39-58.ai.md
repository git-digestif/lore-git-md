This is the second patch in Jeff King’s (Peff) two-patch series fixing a regression in `git shortlog` and related revision commands, where unknown options were incorrectly reported as `(null)` instead of their actual name (e.g., `--not-an-option`). The patch targets the `revision.c` subsystem and addresses the root cause of the regression introduced by commit cd439487 ("revision: manage memory ownership of argv in setup_revisions()", 2025-09-19).

The fix is surgical: it modifies `parse_revision_opt()` to look for the unknown option in the consolidated destination (`ctx->out[ctx->cpidx - 1]`) rather than the original, now-NULL location (`ctx->argv[0]`). This restores the pre-cd439487 behavior where error messages correctly display the option name. The patch also adds a test case to `t/t4201-shortlog.sh` to verify that unknown options are reported as expected (e.g., `error: unknown option `--no-such-option'`).

Peff’s commit message is unusually detailed, walking through the memory management logic and explaining why the fix is safe. He explicitly rules out other potential callers of `handle_revision_opt()` that might be affected, noting that `parse_revision_opt()` is the only other entry point besides `setup_revisions()` itself. The patch is minimal and avoids the risk of future breakage by not altering the broader memory ownership assumptions introduced in cd439487. The alternative approach—recording the string before passing it to `handle_revision_opt()`—was considered but rejected as clunkier.

This patch complements the first in the series, which fixed a latent bug where known-but-invalid options (e.g., `--max-count=foo`) were misreported as unknown. Together, the series resolves the `(null)` regression, the latent misreporting bug, and an even older latent bug where known options with invalid arguments (e.g., `--default`) were incorrectly labeled as unknown. The changes are confined to `revision.c` and the test suite, with no new symbols or on-disk format changes. The series is under review and appears to be a strong candidate for merging, given Peff’s thorough analysis and the clear problem statement. No objections or alternative proposals have surfaced yet, but the patch’s implications for shared revision command logic may prompt closer scrutiny.
