This is a follow-up in the ongoing bugfix series for merge driver resource cleanup. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Elijah Newren’s **substantive review** of the first patch, which identified a latent type-safety bug in the `strbuf`-based refactoring. The bug could cause buffer overreads on LLP64 platforms (e.g., Windows) when external merge results exceed `LONG_MAX` bytes, due to silent truncation of the file size to a negative value.

Peff accepts Elijah’s proposed fix—a simple `die()` check before `strbuf_detach()`—and plans to incorporate it into a v3 of the series. He also notes that the issue exists in other parts of the codebase (e.g., `read_mmfile()`) and suggests a broader cleanup to unify the handling of merge results using `mmfile_t`. However, he acknowledges this would be a separate effort and will focus on the immediate fix for now. The rest of the series (patches 2 and 3, which replace custom signal handling with Git’s `tempfile` API) remains unaffected and uncontroversial.

The patch is well-motivated and addresses a real problem, but the type-safety bug must be resolved before merging. Peff’s response is pragmatic and aligns with Git’s defensive programming practices. Expect a v3 with the fix and possibly a follow-up series for the broader `mmfile_t` cleanup. No test coverage changes are needed, as the fix is internal and the original issue was theoretical. The series is otherwise ready for review.
