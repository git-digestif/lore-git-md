This is the v4 cover letter for a two-patch bugfix series targeting the `rerere` subsystem. The series addresses a long-standing race condition in `rerere` locking that became easier to hit after Git 2.54’s geometric maintenance strategy made background `git rerere gc` runs more frequent. The race occurs when two processes (e.g., a rebase and a background maintenance task) contend for the shared `MERGE_RR.lock`, causing the second process to die and leaving the rebase in an unrecoverable state.

The series is now split into two patches as requested by reviewers. Patch 1 introduces a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1000 milliseconds) for acquiring the `MERGE_RR.lock`. Non-maintenance callers (e.g., `git merge`, `git rebase`) wait up to the timeout before failing, while background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) skips its run entirely if the lock is held. This ensures that a rebase can proceed even if a concurrent `rerere gc` is running, avoiding the previous deadlock. Patch 2 builds on this by allowing commands that stop at a conflict (e.g., `git rebase`, `git merge`, `git am --3way`) to proceed with a warning if the lock remains held after the timeout, while post-resolution recording and explicit `git rerere` commands still fail. This compromise preserves the user’s ability to recover conflict preimages manually while preventing silent corruption of the `MERGE_RR` file.

Key changes since v3 include rebasing onto `master` (to align with recent lockfile helper refactoring), clarifying the commit messages to emphasize that the race is a general `rerere` robustness issue (not just a maintenance-heuristic side effect), and refining the behavior of conflict-stopping commands. The documentation now accurately describes the outcome per command, and the test suite has been expanded to cover edge cases like `git rerere` manual recovery and the distinction between conflict stops and post-resolution recording. The series also restores the upstream signatures of `rerere_clear()` and `rerere_forget()`, ensuring that `--abort` and `--skip` operations fail if the lock is held, preventing stale `MERGE_RR` entries.

The patches touch `rerere.c` (locking logic and error handling), `rerere.h` (new flags), documentation, and test files. The implementation is well-motivated, with clear test coverage for both the timeout behavior and the new conflict-stop recovery path. The series is under active review, with no outstanding objections to the revised approach. The second patch (conflict-stop recovery) remains the primary point of discussion, particularly around the trade-off between resilience and correctness.
