This is the first patch in the v4 series that defers auto maintenance until sequencer operations complete. The email is a reply from the author, Thomas Bachem, to Patrick Steinhardt’s **substantive review** of the series’ design. The discussion centers on where to trigger auto maintenance: the current patch delegates this to the built-in commands (`git rebase`, `git cherry-pick`, `git revert`), while Patrick argues the sequencer itself has a single exit point (`pick_commits()`) where maintenance could be triggered consistently.

Thomas clarifies the technical details of the current design: single-commit cherry-picks and reverts bypass `pick_commits()` entirely, exiting early through `single_pick()`, `continue_single_pick()`, or `skip_single_pick()`. Only rebase consistently reaches `pick_commits()`. He acknowledges Patrick’s alternative—triggering maintenance at the end of `pick_commits()` and on abort—would require adding calls for cherry-pick and revert aborts, but notes rebase aborts already handle this in `finish_rebase()`. Both approaches are viable, and Thomas is open to either, asking Patrick to choose.

The exchange is constructive and technical, with no signs of sloppiness or under-motivation. The patch addresses a real consistency issue (matching the apply backend’s behavior) and has already resolved prior feedback from Phillip Wood and Junio C Hamano. The remaining question is purely architectural: whether to centralize maintenance in the sequencer or delegate it to built-ins. This is a **substantive design decision** that will likely require a v5 reroll, but the series remains well-crafted and worth attention.
