This thread is about a performance feature to add an opt-in incremental connectivity check for Git fetches and pushes, reducing the cost of verifying object connectivity. The series, now at v2, introduces `transfer.connectivityCheck=incremental` and a new internal `rev-list` flag (`--verify-trees-incremental`) to process incoming commits in topological order, skipping unchanged subtrees by tracking trusted objects from parent commits.

Kristofer Karlsson is the author, and the series has drawn substantive review from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt. Junio’s initial concerns about edge-case robustness (promisor objects and NULL-dereference risks) were addressed in v2, while Patrick’s feedback on documentation precision, maintainability, and clarity has been accepted in full. On the implementation side, Patrick raised three technical questions about trust boundary detection, error handling, and performance trade-offs. Kristofer’s latest reply clarifies that the verifier establishes its trust boundary using `git rev-list --not --all` to identify already-connected commits, processes incoming commits in reverse topological order, and memoizes trusted objects to avoid redundant checks. He defends the use of `die()` for cycles in the incoming commit graph, citing the existing `rev-list` pattern and subprocess architecture, but acknowledges that a single-process implementation with non-fatal variants would be a worthwhile long-term improvement. The discussion also resolved Patrick’s question about force pushes or new references: the verifier always compares a commit to its direct parents, ensuring correctness regardless of whether those parents are preexisting or incoming.

The series is still under review, with a v3 likely to split the implementation patch into smaller commits (option introduction, benchmark, `rev-list` flag, and strategy implementation) for better reviewability. Open questions remain about the long-term scalability of ref-parsing overhead and the subprocess architecture, but no new technical objections have been raised. The feature is positioned as a foundational step toward broader scalability challenges, with future directions like MIDX-based connectivity closure and parallel ref loading explicitly out of scope for now.
