This thread introduces an opt-in incremental connectivity check for Git fetches and pushes, aiming to reduce the performance cost of verifying object connectivity. The feature is now complete in its sixth iteration (v4), with all patches submitted and ready for integration. Kristofer Karlsson is the author, and the series has received substantive reviews from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt, both of whom have signaled strong support.

The core idea is to process incoming commits in topological order, tracking trusted objects from parent commits to skip unchanged subtrees. This shifts the cost from repository size to incoming change size. The feature is configurable via `transfer.connectivityCheck=full|incremental|auto[=<n>]` (default: `full`), with `auto` dynamically switching to incremental mode for histories up to a configurable 1,000-commit cutoff. Benchmarks show up to 190x speedups for small pushes to large repositories, with negligible overhead for small repos.

All prior feedback has been addressed, including refactoring to eliminate `the_repository` dependencies, improving error handling, and adding synthetic performance benchmarks. The latest discussion with Junio clarified promisor-object handling, leading to a minor technical refinement that simplifies the implementation without introducing new blockers. Patrick Steinhardt’s thorough reviews have already shaped the series into a robust, maintainable design. The only remaining open questions are forward-looking (stack overflow risk, lazy-fetching assumptions) and are planned for follow-up patches.

The series is effectively cleared for integration, pending final sign-off from Junio. No fundamental objections remain, and the tone of the discussion suggests the feature is poised to land in the near future.
