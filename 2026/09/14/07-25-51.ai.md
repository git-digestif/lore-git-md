This is the first patch in a two-patch bugfix series (v4) that addresses a heap-buffer-overflow and incorrect pathspec matching in Git’s directory traversal logic. The patch prevents `match_pathspec_with_flags()` from applying the common prefix (derived from positive pathspecs) to exclude pathspecs, which previously caused two distinct bugs: (1) false positives when an exclude pathspec matched a pathname only after the common prefix was stripped (e.g., `git ls-files a/b ':!x/b' a/c` incorrectly excluding `a/b/m`), and (2) a heap-buffer-overflow when an exclude pathspec was shorter than the common prefix, leading to out-of-bounds pointer arithmetic and potential crashes under AddressSanitizer.

The fix is minimal: the `do_match_pathspec()` call for exclude pathspecs now passes `0` as the prefix length instead of the common prefix derived from positive pathspecs. This ensures exclude pathspecs are matched against the full pathname, not a truncated version. The patch also adds two regression tests in `t/t6132-pathspec-exclude.sh`: one covering the false-positive case (exclude pathspec shorter than the common prefix) and another verifying that exclude pathspecs are matched against the full path (using a deterministic, non-crashing example).

The change is narrowly scoped to `dir.c` and the pathspec subsystem, with no broader impact on Git’s behavior. The commit message clearly explains the bugs, the fix, and the test coverage, and the patch itself is straightforward and well-motivated. The series is queued for merging, and this patch is the first half of the solution; the second patch (not shown here) refactors `common_prefix_len()` to correctly handle exclude pathspecs when calculating the common prefix. The regression tests added here complement the unit tests in the second patch, providing comprehensive coverage for both the memory-safety issue and the logical bug.
