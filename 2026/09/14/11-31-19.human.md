This is the second patch in a six-part **bugfix series** addressing a data-loss race in `git repack -d` when concurrent pushes are active. The series targets a production issue where dangling refs can point to nonexistent commits after a repack deletes packs whose objects were never copied elsewhere.

This patch adjusts `git pack-objects` behavior when using `--stdin-packs=follow`. Currently, packs named with `--keep-pack` are treated as closed under reachability (like `^` packs), stopping traversal at their objects. That’s incorrect: `--keep-pack` only means “don’t repack this pack,” not “this pack is self-contained.” When the pack holds a commit but not its tree, the tree is never rescued, and writing a bitmap over the result fails for lack of closure. The fix marks such packs as “kept-open” in follow mode, allowing traversal to continue through them to rescue dependent objects.

The change is small (20 lines in `builtin/pack-objects.c`) and adds a 41-line test in `t/t5331-pack-objects-stdin.sh` that verifies the fix: when a kept pack contains only a commit, the traversal now walks through it to include the commit’s tree and blob from another pack. This aligns `--keep-pack` behavior with on-disk `.keep` packs, which have never been traversal boundaries.

The patch is a **prerequisite for patch 6/6**, which eliminates the race by passing `pack-objects` the exact list of `.keep` packs observed at repack startup. Without this change, patch 6 would reintroduce the same closure bug it is trying to fix. The commit message is clear and the test is comprehensive, making the motivation and impact easy to follow. No controversy is expected; this looks like a straightforward correctness fix.
