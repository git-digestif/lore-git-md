This is the third patch in a six-patch **bugfix series** targeting a data-loss race in `git repack -d` when concurrent pushes are active. The series as a whole is well-motivated, production-tested, and addresses a subtle but serious issue where dangling refs can point to nonexistent commits after a repack deletes packs whose objects were never copied elsewhere.

This specific patch fixes a pre-existing bug in `pack-objects`’ cruft walk that was masked by `--honor-pack-keep`. When writing a cruft pack with an expiration, `pack-objects` first collects recent objects (marking any unlisted packs as "kept" so their objects aren’t copied), then unmarks those packs before walking reachability to rescue expired objects. However, the walk uses a cached list of kept packs built during collection, so it never sees the unmarking—stopping early and leaving expired objects unreachable. The bug only surfaces when `--honor-pack-keep` is *not* used (which a later patch in the series removes), making this a latent correctness issue.

The fix is straightforward: expose and call `packfile_store_invalidate_kept_pack_cache()` after re-marking the fresh packs, forcing the cache to rebuild with the current state. The patch includes a new test in `t/t5329-pack-objects-cruft.sh` that constructs an unreachable chain where the middle commit sits in an unlisted pack and the oldest objects have expired. Without the fix, the cruft pack only contains the recent tip; with it, the entire chain is rescued.

The change is narrowly scoped to `builtin/pack-objects.c`, `packfile.c`, and their headers, with no on-disk format or user-visible behavior changes. The commit message clearly explains the bug’s mechanics and why it went unnoticed, and the test provides a reproducible case. This is a **clean, necessary fix** that enables the later race-fix patches in the series. Reviewers familiar with the ODB or repack machinery (e.g., Patrick Steinhardt, Taylor Blau) may scrutinize the cache invalidation logic, but the patch itself looks solid and uncontroversial.
