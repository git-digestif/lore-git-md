This thread is about a critical bugfix series that plugs a race condition in `git repack -d` where concurrent pushes could cause data loss by deleting packs whose objects weren’t safely copied elsewhere. The series is now at v3, with five patches (down from six after the first was withdrawn in favor of a cleaner approach in Justin Tobler’s ODB-focused work).

The core fix replaces Git’s `--honor-pack-keep` mechanism with a snapshot-based approach, ensuring `.keep` files are managed consistently. The most urgent patch—2/5, which fixes a latent cruft-pack walk bug—has been acked by Junio Hamano after addressing his layering concerns. This patch ensures the cruft-pack walk sees the current kept-pack state, preventing objects from being incorrectly omitted and later deleted. The remaining patches (1/5, 3/5–5/5) are under review and introduce key improvements: correcting a long-standing traversal logic bug in `pack-objects`, optimizing kept-pack matching performance, and replacing the mid-repack directory scan with a snapshot file to eliminate the race entirely.

Key participants include Qin ShiCheng (the author), who withdrew the original patch 1/6 in favor of Tobler’s ODB work, and Junio, who acked patch 2/5 as a necessary fix for the immediate data-loss risk. The series touches low-level repack machinery, object traversal, and ODB internals, with tests covering edge cases like unreachable chains in untold packs. While the architectural question of whether the downcast grouping in patch 2/5 is sufficient for long-term maintainability remains open, the series is effectively ready for integration, pending reviews from repack/ODB maintainers like Patrick Steinhardt and Taylor Blau. The trade-off—packing alternate ODB packs instead of skipping them—prioritizes safety over disk space, a decision likely to draw discussion.
