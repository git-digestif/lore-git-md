This thread is about a critical bug in `git repack -d` that can cause data loss when concurrent pushes race with repack operations. The original six-patch series by Qin ShiCheng (qeesung) aimed to fix this by ensuring `.keep` files are only removed by the process that created them and by replacing the `--honor-pack-keep` mechanism with a consistent snapshot of kept packs. The series also included fixes for latent bugs in `pack-objects` traversal and cruft-pack walks, along with performance optimizations to handle large numbers of kept packs efficiently.

The thread has taken a significant turn following Justin Tobler’s review of the first patch. Justin proposed an alternative architectural approach: instead of patching the `.keep` file ownership logic, the ODB transaction’s commit phase should explicitly create `.keep` files, eliminating the need for pre-migration tracking. Qin has agreed to withdraw the first patch in favor of Justin’s approach, which simplifies the fix and aligns with a more maintainable long-term design. The two new tests Qin added to `t5547-push-quarantine.sh` will be incorporated into Justin’s series, as they cover edge cases like partial migration failures.

The remaining patches (2/6–6/6) in Qin’s series—addressing `pack-objects` traversal, cruft-pack walks, and the snapshot-based replacement for `--honor-pack-keep`—are still relevant and will proceed independently. These patches are under review, with no objections or approvals recorded yet. Key reviewers likely include Patrick Steinhardt and Taylor Blau (repack/ODB maintainers) and Jeff King (Peff) for low-level concerns. The collaboration between Qin and Justin ensures the fixes are coordinated without duplication, with Justin’s series handling the ODB transaction layer and Qin’s remaining patches focusing on the repack machinery.
