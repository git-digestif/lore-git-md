This thread is about a six-patch series from Qin ShiCheng (qeesung) that fixes a race condition in `git repack -d` where concurrent pushes can cause data loss by deleting packs whose objects weren’t safely copied elsewhere. The series is under review at v1, with all tests passing and no prior versions.

The core issue is that `.keep` files—used to protect packs from deletion—can be removed by the wrong process during concurrent operations, leaving dangling refs. The series addresses this by ensuring `.keep` files are only removed by their creator and replacing the `--honor-pack-keep` mechanism with a consistent snapshot of kept packs observed at repack startup. It also fixes a latent bug in the cruft-pack walk that would become critical once the old mechanism is removed.

Justin Tobler’s recent review of the first patch engages deeply with the problem and proposes an alternative: instead of tracking `.keep` files during migration, the ODB transaction’s commit phase should create them explicitly. This would simplify the fix by allowing early aborts if a `.keep` already exists, avoiding the need for pre-migration tracking. Justin has prototyped this approach locally and frames it as a preference rather than a demand, leaving room for discussion. His feedback introduces a higher-level architectural question about whether the incremental fix should proceed as-is or be replaced with a more fundamental change. Reviewers like Patrick Steinhardt or Jeff King may weigh in on the trade-offs between immediacy and long-term maintainability. No other reviews have been posted yet.
