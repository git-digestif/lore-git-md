This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Qin ShiCheng’s six-part bugfix series targeting a data-loss race in `git repack -d`. The reviewer, Justin Tobler, engages deeply with the problem and the proposed solution, offering both praise and a concrete alternative approach.

The patch prevents `receive-pack` from removing `.keep` files it didn’t create, which is a prerequisite for the full race fix later in the series. Justin confirms the problem—concurrent pushes of identical content can lead to one push deleting another’s `.keep` file, orphaning packs—and agrees the current timing of `.keep` registration is awkward. He notes that the `.keep` message itself (written by `index-pack`) can serve as a fingerprint: if the file’s contents don’t match what the current push wrote, it wasn’t created by this process and shouldn’t be removed.

Justin’s main suggestion is architectural: instead of registering the `.keep` path just before migration and verifying its contents afterward, he proposes eliminating the premature `.keep` creation by `index-pack` entirely. In his alternative design, the `.keep` file would be created explicitly during the ODB transaction’s commit phase, simplifying ownership tracking and making the collision check trivial (just attempt to create the file; if it already exists, abort). He mentions he already has local patches for a separate series that move in this direction and is willing to upstream them with adjustments to address this bug.

The review is **tested-level**: Justin demonstrates familiarity with the quarantine mechanism, ODB transactions, and the behavior of concurrent pushes, and he references his own in-progress work on the same subsystem. His feedback is narrowly scoped to patch 1/6 but has implications for the entire series, particularly if the alternative design is adopted. The tone is collaborative, and the suggestion is framed as a preference rather than a demand. Reviewers of later patches (especially Patrick Steinhardt and Taylor Blau) may weigh in on whether the alternative approach is preferable, but for now the thread remains focused on this first step.
