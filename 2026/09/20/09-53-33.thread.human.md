This thread is about a user-experience improvement for shallow clones: when a user tries to access an ancestor not present locally via `<rev>~N` or `<rev>^N` syntax, Git currently emits a generic error with no hint that the operation failed due to the shallow boundary. Harald Nordgren’s patch (now at v3) adds an advice message that explains the issue and suggests the exact `git fetch --deepen=<n>` command needed to resolve it. The patch is technically sound, addresses all prior feedback, and is ready for integration—but it remains stalled due to a lack of reviewers.

The core issue here isn’t the patch itself, which is uncontroversial, but the broader challenge of review bandwidth. Junio has repeatedly warned that the patch will be dropped from his integration branches unless a reviewer steps forward, and the thread has evolved into a discussion about how to address the structural mismatch between contributor volume and reviewer availability. Harald has proposed incremental tooling (like Codecov for coverage reporting) and a soft rule to defer nitpicking until the core idea of a patch is settled, while Phillip Wood has suggested contributors self-review their patches and the project experiment with a "review quota" policy. However, Junio’s latest reply reaffirms that the patch’s fate hinges on positive reviews, not process changes, and no reviewer has yet engaged with the technical content.

The patch touches `advice.c`, `object-name.c`, and related files, adding a new `advice.shallowHistory` enum value and context-aware advice for failing `<rev>~N`/`<rev>^N` syntax. It auto-detects the remote and branch, suppresses advice in non-shallow repos or during internal operations, and includes robust test coverage. While Junio’s early feedback questioned the inclusion of `<rev>^N` and a minor documentation inconsistency, these were addressed in v3, and the patch is otherwise ready for `next`. The only remaining loose ends are edge cases (like whether the advice behaves consistently for `origin/main^^^^^` vs. `origin/main~5`), but these are minor compared to the patch’s overall value. For now, the patch’s future depends entirely on whether a C-savvy reviewer steps up in the next few weeks.
