This thread is about a proposed improvement to Git’s error messages when users in shallow clones try to access ancestors not present locally using `<rev>~N` or `<rev>^N` syntax. The patch, now in its third version, adds an advice message (`advice.shallowHistory`) that explains the shallow boundary and suggests the exact `git fetch --deepen=<n>` command needed to resolve the issue. The feature is narrowly scoped to failing syntax only, addressing all prior feedback, and is uncontroversial in principle—Junio has repeatedly stated it is ready for the `next` branch if only a reviewer would step forward.

The patch itself is stalled, not because of technical objections or process flaws, but because no one has provided the required human review of its conceptual direction. Junio’s latest email reaffirms that the patch will be dropped from his integration branches unless a reviewer engages in the next few weeks. He also warns against proposals to incentivize reviews through quotas or automation, fearing they would flood the list with low-effort or AI-generated feedback rather than meaningful scrutiny. The thread has become a microcosm of a broader tension: contributors like Harald Nordgren see the review bottleneck as a structural problem, while maintainers like Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, and Phillip Wood insist that human judgment is irreplaceable and that Git’s standards are non-negotiable.

Key participants include Harald (the author), Junio (the maintainer), and several others who weighed in on process rather than the patch itself. D. Ben Knoble provided a minor documentation nit in an earlier version, but no substantive technical review has emerged. The patch touches several files, adds a new advice enum, and includes thorough test coverage, but its fate hinges on whether someone with domain expertise will vouch for it. For now, it remains in limbo—under review, but at risk of being discarded.
