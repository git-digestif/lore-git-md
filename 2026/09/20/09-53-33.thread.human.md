This thread is about a proposed improvement to Git’s error messages when users in shallow clones try to access ancestors not present locally via `<rev>~N` or `<rev>^N` syntax. The patch, now in its third version, adds an advice message (`advice.shallowHistory`) that explains the shallow boundary and suggests the exact `git fetch --deepen=<n>` command needed to resolve the issue. The v3 patch is narrowly scoped to the failing-syntax case, addresses all prior review feedback, and is technically uncontroversial—Junio has even issued a call for review, warning that the patch will be dropped unless a reviewer steps forward soon.

The core problem is a user-experience gap: Git’s current error ("is not a commit") gives no hint that the operation was limited by the shallow boundary. The patch fixes this by auto-detecting the remote and branch, calculating the correct `--deepen` value, and printing actionable advice. It touches several files (`advice.c`, `object-name.c`, `shallow.c`, etc.) and includes thorough test coverage. The implementation is robust, with fixes for edge cases like chained carets and pager awareness.

Despite its readiness, the patch remains stalled due to a lack of reviewer engagement. The thread has shifted to discussing structural solutions to Git’s review bottleneck, with proposals ranging from self-review and "review quotas" to deferring nitpicking until the core idea is settled. Harald has suggested tools like Codecov to automate coverage reporting, but the project’s stance—articulated by Patrick Steinhardt and Phillip Wood—is that human review is irreplaceable for assessing conceptual direction. The patch’s fate hinges on whether a C-savvy reviewer steps forward in the next few weeks; otherwise, Junio will drop it from his integration branches. No substantive objections exist, but the review logjam persists.
