This is a reply in the ongoing `post-worktree` hook design discussion. Domen Kožar responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s `--id=<string>` counterproposal with a use-case-driven argument for why ownership IDs alone are insufficient.

Domen clarifies the real-world scenario he is targeting: a separation of concerns between *creators* (IDEs, AI agents) and *provisioners* (devenv). The provisioner does not control the worktree lifecycle—it only reacts to it. Even if the creator supplies an ownership ID, the provisioner has no way to discover that a worktree was added or removed unless Git notifies it. Ownership IDs solve the *interference* problem (one tool accidentally removing another’s worktree) but not the *observability* problem (a third-party tool learning about lifecycle events it did not initiate). Domen also points out that uncontrolled callers—users running `git worktree remove` or `git worktree prune` directly—remain invisible to the provisioner unless Git emits a notification.

The email does not reject the counterproposal outright; instead, Domen suggests that ownership IDs and lifecycle hooks might be *complementary*.
