This is a substantive review reply from Patrick Steinhardt on Thomas Bachem’s v3 locking patch for the `rerere` race condition. The patch is part of a two-part series addressing a race between background maintenance (`git rerere gc`) and foreground operations like rebase.

Patrick’s key point is that the race is not specific to background maintenance—it can happen between any two writers, so the problem is a long-standing conceptual issue in `rerere`’s locking design, not just a side effect of the geometric maintenance strategy introduced in Git 2.54. He suggests clarifying this in the commit message to avoid over-focusing on the maintenance trigger.

He also questions whether silently skipping `rerere` recording after a timeout is the right fallback. While he acknowledges it may be better than aborting the entire command, he feels this behavioral change deserves its own preparatory commit to allow focused discussion on the trade-offs between graceful degradation and potential data loss. His tone is constructive but signals this is still an open design question.
