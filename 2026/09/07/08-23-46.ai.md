This is a meta-discussion reply in the ongoing thread about Git's version numbering after 2.56. Patrick Steinhardt responds to brian m. carlson’s cautious stance on delaying a major version bump, offering new information that weakens two of the key technical blockers Carlson identified.

Steinhardt’s reply is brief but substantive. He first quips that even after 2.99, there is still room for 2.100 — a lighthearted way of saying the version numbering scheme itself is not a hard constraint. More importantly, he provides concrete updates on two of Carlson’s three readiness criteria: **libgit2’s SHA-256 and reftable support**. He reports that reftable support has been upstreamed into libgit2 (PR #7117), and SHA-256 support was enabled by default in PR #7261, merged about a month ago. Once libgit2’s next release ships, both of these blockers will be resolved. This directly addresses Carlson’s concern about ecosystem compatibility and removes a significant technical hurdle for a 3.0 transition.

However, Steinhardt does not comment on the third criterion — **SHA-256 support on major forges**, particularly GitHub — beyond expressing curiosity about Carlson’s upcoming update at Git Merge. He also does not engage with Carlson’s advocacy for a cautious delay (e.g., 2.97) or the status of the lowercase-only object IDs series, which Carlson had flagged as needing soak time. The tone remains pragmatic and focused on achievable progress, reinforcing the idea that the project is closer to readiness than Carlson’s earlier assessment suggested.

In short: Steinhardt’s reply advances the discussion by removing two of the three technical blockers Carlson cited, but leaves the GitHub SHA-256 question and the lowercase-only object IDs series as open issues. The thread remains in the design phase, with no maintainer decision yet.
