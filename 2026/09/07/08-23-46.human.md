This is a meta-discussion reply in the ongoing thread about what version number Git should use after 2.56. Patrick Steinhardt weighs in with a pragmatic perspective, responding directly to brian m. carlson’s concerns about ecosystem readiness for a major version bump.

Steinhardt’s key point is that the version numbering scheme still has room for flexibility even after 2.99 — there’s no technical reason Git couldn’t continue to 2.100 if needed. This undercuts the urgency of rushing to 3.0 and aligns with a more gradual transition. On the technical side, he addresses two of Carlson’s readiness concerns: GitHub’s SHA-256 support (still uncertain, and a blocker in his view) and libgit2 compatibility. He reports that libgit2 now has both reftable support (merged in PR #7117) and SHA-256 enabled by default (merged in PR #7261), removing those as obstacles once the next libgit2 release ships.

The tone is constructive and fact-based, focusing on what’s achievable rather than ideological preferences. While he doesn’t explicitly endorse any of Junio’s four options, his comments suggest a preference for a cautious, staged approach — possibly option (3) (2.97/2.98) or a variant like 2.99 — that allows time for GitHub to catch up while acknowledging progress elsewhere in the ecosystem. The discussion remains open, with no consensus yet, but Steinhardt’s input narrows the technical blockers to a single major one: GitHub’s SHA-256 support.
