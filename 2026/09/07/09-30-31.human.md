This is a meta-discussion reply in the ongoing thread about what version number Git should use after 2.56. Emily Shaffer, a former Git contributor now focused on Jujutsu (jj), weighs in with a pragmatic perspective on the readiness for Git 3.0, particularly emphasizing the importance of GitHub’s SHA-256 support.

Shaffer’s main concern is the lack of SHA-256 support on GitHub, which she describes as the most significant remaining blocker for a 3.0 release. She acknowledges the tension between Git’s independence from GitHub and the reality that most users rely on it, making GitHub’s support a practical necessity. While she is eager to see progress, she also notes that waiting indefinitely for GitHub’s commitment is not ideal, especially since much of the 2026 work on SHA-256 was driven by the looming 3.0 deadline.

She also touches on the status of GitOxide (the Rust reimplementation of Git) and jj (Jujutsu), noting that while GitOxide has made progress on SHA-256 support, jj itself still needs work to fully support it. This is relevant because Google’s interest in SHA-256 is tied to jj’s adoption of it, but she clarifies that neither project is ready yet. Despite this, she suggests that Google would likely disable SHA-256 for new repositories via system configuration until GitHub and JGit support is available, which tempers her concern about releasing 3.0 without full ecosystem readiness.

Shaffer’s tone is balanced: she leans toward releasing 3.0 sooner rather than later, but her comments reflect an awareness of the broader ecosystem’s dependencies. Her input adds weight to the idea that while Git 3.0 is technically feasible, its practical impact hinges on external factors like GitHub’s support. The thread remains unresolved, but her perspective reinforces the need for a decision that accounts for both technical readiness and real-world adoption.
