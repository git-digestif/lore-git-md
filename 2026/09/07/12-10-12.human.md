This is a **test modernization patch** that has run into a fundamental design issue. Patrick Steinhardt replies to Mark C. Chu-Carroll’s first patch in the series, explaining why the mechanical replacement of `test -f` with `test_path_is_file` is incorrect in control-flow contexts.

The core problem: `test_path_is_file` and `test_path_is_missing` are assertion helpers that print error messages when the condition fails, whereas `test -f` is a silent conditional. When `test -f` is used to lazily create files or to check for platform-specific paths (e.g., `/proc/$shell_pid/winpid` on MINGW), converting it to `test_path_is_file` turns a silent check into a loud assertion failure, breaking the test logic.

Patrick’s review is **substantive and tested**: he identifies specific examples where the conversion is wrong (`t/t1007-hash-object.sh`, `t/t4032-diff-inter-hunk-context.sh`) and explains the semantic mismatch.
