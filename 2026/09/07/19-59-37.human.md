This is the fourth patch in a seven-patch RFC series proposing a breaking change for Git 3.0: restricting hex object IDs to lowercase only. The series is part of an ongoing design discussion about consistency, security, and ecosystem compatibility, with this patch serving as a purely preparatory step.

The patch introduces a new constant, `HEX_KIND_OID`, defined as `HEX_KIND_MIXED` for now, and updates all call sites in `diagnose.c`, `hex.c`, `http-push.c`, `notes.c`, and `object-file.c` to use it instead of `HEX_KIND_MIXED` when parsing object IDs. This change is purely mechanical and introduces no functional or behavioral differences—it simply labels the intended use of the hex parsing, making future enforcement of lowercase-only parsing easier to implement. The patch touches six files and is well-scoped, addressing Junio C Hamano’s earlier review feedback by ensuring `HEX_KIND_OID` is used consistently where object IDs are parsed.

The series as a whole remains in RFC limbo, with no final decision on whether the project will accept the breaking change. This patch is uncontroversial and ready for integration if the series proceeds, but its fate is tied to the broader discussion about whether the security and consistency benefits outweigh the costs of a breaking change. The next patch in the series will introduce a behavioral change (preserving original case during transition), while the final patch will activate enforcement when `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` is enabled. For now, this patch is a clean, low-risk step toward that goal.
