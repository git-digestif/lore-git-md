This thread is about a two-part effort to modernize and correct OpenSSL compatibility in `git imap-send`. Beat Bolli initially sent a v1 series with three patches addressing TLS certificate verification correctness, OpenSSL 4.1 API deprecation, and memory-safety issues. That series stalled due to unresolved technical concerns, including a memory-safety bug in patch 2 and incomplete RFC 6125 compliance in patch 3. Beat has now split the work into a narrower v2 series, posting two patches so far.

The first v2 patch, already summarized, replaces the deprecated `SSLv23_method()` with the modern `TLS_method()`—a straightforward, uncontroversial change. The second patch, just arrived, tackles the larger issue: it removes Git’s manual hostname verification logic in favor of OpenSSL’s built-in `SSL_set1_host()` API. This directly addresses brian m. carlson’s objections to the v1 series by ensuring full RFC 6125 compliance (including IP-type SANs) and reducing maintenance burden. The patch is clean, well-motivated, and ready to proceed independently of the still-stalled v1 series.

Key participants include Beat (the author), Junio C Hamano (who flagged the memory-safety bug in v1), brian m. carlson (who raised compliance and architectural concerns), and Patrick Steinhardt (who proposed an alternative fix for the memory-safety issue). The v2 patches have so far drawn no objections, but the v1 series remains open on three fronts: the memory-safety fix (Beat’s allocation-based approach vs. Patrick’s length-bounded alternative), the OpenSSL 4.1 compatibility macro, and the architectural question of manual vs. OpenSSL-native verification. For now, the v2 patches look like the path forward.
