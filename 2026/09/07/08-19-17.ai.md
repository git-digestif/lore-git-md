This is a **bugfix** reporting a serious data-loss scenario in `git pull`.

The email describes a real-world incident where a `.gitignore` pattern ending in a slash (`dir/`) fails to ignore a symlink of the same name. The symlink is committed and later, when another user pulls, Git silently replaces their local directory with the symlink, destroying all data inside it. The author reproduces the issue on both Linux (Git 2.43.0) and Windows (Git 2.53.0.windows.2).

The problem stems from two distinct but related behaviors: (1) `.gitignore` patterns with trailing slashes do not match symlinks, allowing them to be staged and committed, and (2) `git pull` overwrites a local directory with a symlink without warning or user confirmation. The author suggests two fixes: either make `.gitignore` patterns with trailing slashes also ignore symlinks, or detect the conflict during pull and refuse to overwrite without prompting.

The report includes a concrete reproduction case with shell output, making the issue easy to verify. The potential impact is severe—hundreds of gigabytes of local data could be lost silently during a routine pull. The email is well-structured, clearly motivated, and provides enough technical detail for a reviewer to understand the problem and test a fix. This is a high-priority issue that warrants immediate attention.
