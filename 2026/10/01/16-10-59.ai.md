This is a **contributor housekeeping / workflow tool** discussion, specifically about the proposed `git-rebase-walk` script and alternative approaches to incremental rebase conflict resolution.

Nico Williams replies to Alejandro Colomar’s RFC with a mix of technical feedback and an alternative proposal. He suggests a simpler pipeline for the script (using `git log --reverse --format=%H` instead of the multi-command chain) but immediately pivots to two major concerns: the script lacks conflict handling and is "very slow" when rebasing across thousands of commits. These are substantive critiques—performance and robustness are real-world issues, not nits.

Nico then shares his own solution, `bisect-rebase.sh` (linked in a GitHub gist), which he describes as a more efficient approach. Instead of linearly rebasing onto every commit, his script uses a bisection-like strategy to find the *first* upstream commit that conflicts with the local branch, resolves it, and then resumes rebasing. This reduces the number of conflict resolutions from O(n) to O(k), where k is the number of conflicting commits (e.g., 10 resolutions for 1,000 commits). He reports successfully using this to rebase a PostgreSQL fork across 1,000–2,000 commits with "significant conflicts," a scenario where a plain `git rebase` would produce unmanageable conflicts.

The email positions `bisect-rebase.sh` as superior to both `git-rebase-walk` and `git-imerge` (mentioned in the thread context) for rebase workflows, while acknowledging that `git-imerge` is a more general tool. Nico’s tone is confident, bordering on promotional, but the technical rationale is clear: his script is optimized for rebasing large histories with many conflicts, and his real-world experience with PostgreSQL lends credibility. The unresolved question for the thread is whether Git’s `contrib/` area should include a minimal shell script (`git-rebase-walk`), a more sophisticated one (`bisect-rebase.sh`), or neither—given the existence of third-party tools like `git-imerge`.
