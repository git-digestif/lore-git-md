This is a discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about modernizing Git’s tutorial documentation, specifically the fate of `gittutorial.adoc` (the older, first-part tutorial). Julia Evans clarifies her position on whether to preserve `gittutorial` while developing a replacement, responding to Junio C Hamano’s confusion about her earlier remarks.

Julia argues that `gittutorial` is not a viable interim resource for teaching branching and collaboration, citing its outdated structure, lack of foundational explanations (e.g., what a branch *is*), and impractical examples (e.g., multi-user systems). She presents four options for handling the transition: (1) directing users to the current `gittutorial` (which she rejects as ineffective), (2) shipping her new draft (covering only basic commit/push workflows) now and adding branching/collaboration later, (3) recommending external guides (problematic for maintainability), or (4) delaying the entire replacement until all material is ready (which she considers too slow). She favors option 2, acknowledging its trade-offs but prioritizing incremental progress.

The email provides concrete details about her draft’s scope (e.g., commands covered, user testing feedback) and the challenges of teaching Git’s core concepts. No new patches are proposed here—this is purely a scoping discussion. The tone remains collaborative, but the disagreement over sequencing (preserve `gittutorial` temporarily vs. remove it outright) is now sharper. Future work will likely hinge on whether the project accepts Julia’s incremental approach or insists on a more comprehensive replacement.
