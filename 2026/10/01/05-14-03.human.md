This is a **documentation patch discussion** in the ongoing `gitmergeconflicts(7)` series. The thread continues to refine the new man page’s practical utility, focusing on two key improvements: **making commit information more accessible during conflict resolution** and **promoting `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` as a best practice**.

Patrick Steinhardt builds on Junio C Hamano’s earlier suggestion to include commit references in conflict markers. He proposes a concrete format for embedding commit metadata directly in the markers themselves, e.g.:
```
<<<<<<< HEAD: abcdefg (fruits: add apple, 2026-10-01)
    "cherry",
=======
    "banana",
>>>>>>> add-fruit: 12345678 (fruits: add banana, 2024-02-03)
```
This approach avoids requiring users to learn special refs like `MERGE_HEAD`, provides context via commit messages and dates, and aligns with Git’s existing conflict-marker style. Patrick plans to prototype this in GitLab to gather feedback. His proposal is substantive: it transforms conflict markers from passive indicators into active tools for resolution, addressing a long-standing pain point for users.

The discussion also reinforces the push to make `merge.conflictstyle=diff3` the default. Both Patrick and Junio strongly endorse this, with Junio stating he feels "strongly enough to think it should become the default." This is notable—it suggests the new guide may not only document `diff3` but actively advocate for its adoption, potentially paving the way for a future default change. Julia Evans’ earlier observation ("every time I show people diff3 someone tells me how happy they are") underscores the practical impact of this feature.

The exchange is constructive and incremental, with no signs of controversy. The proposed commit-metadata format is a natural extension of existing conflict markers, and the `diff3` advocacy aligns with the series’ goal of improving user experience. The series remains on track, with these suggestions likely to be incorporated in the next revision. The focus on actionable, user-tested improvements (e.g., commit metadata, `diff3`) continues to strengthen the guide’s practical value.
