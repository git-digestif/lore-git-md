This is a **workflow tool discussion** in the ongoing thread about incremental rebase conflict resolution. Alejandro Colomar responds to Nico Williams’ critique of his `git-rebase-walk` script and alternative proposal (`bisect-rebase.sh`) with a mix of technical refinements, philosophical preferences, and curiosity about upstreaming.

Alejandro first addresses Nico’s suggestion to simplify the pipeline by adopting a revised version of his script that replaces the `git log`/`cut`/`tac` chain with `git rev-list` and an explicit `tac`. His rationale is readability and conceptual clarity: he prefers breaking the pipeline into discrete, well-known commands (e.g., `tac`) over relying on Git-specific flags (`--reverse`). This is a matter of taste rather than performance, which he notes is comparable. The change is minor but reflects his preference for maintainable, self-documenting code.

The core of the response pushes back on Nico’s two substantive critiques—lack of conflict handling and slowness. Alejandro concedes the performance issue (the script is slow for large histories) but frames it as acceptable for his use case: he runs it in the background and manually resumes after resolving conflicts. He also clarifies that the script *does* handle conflicts naturally by stopping at each rebase, allowing him to `--continue` or `--abort` before resuming the walk. This contradicts Nico’s claim that conflict handling is missing, though it remains manual rather than automated.

Alejandro then engages with Nico’s alternative, `bisect-rebase.sh`, which he acknowledges as more sophisticated (93 lines vs. his 10-line script). He expresses interest in trying it and even considers writing his own bisection-based version for comparison. His tone is collaborative, not defensive, and he seems open to the idea that a more efficient approach might be preferable—though he still values his script’s simplicity and robustness for his workflow.

The most significant new question in this email is whether Nico has attempted to upstream `bisect-rebase.sh` and, if so, what happened. Alejandro’s curiosity suggests he sees value in having *some* incremental rebase helper in Git’s `contrib/` area, whether his script, Nico’s, or neither. The thread’s unresolved tension—whether Git should endorse a minimal shell script, a more sophisticated one, or defer to third-party tools like `git-imerge`—remains open, but Alejandro’s response keeps the door open for either script to be considered.
