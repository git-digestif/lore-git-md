This is a **bugfix** patch in a four-part series addressing corner cases in Git’s multi-pack-index (MIDX) reachability closure. This specific email is Taylor Blau’s reply to Jeff King’s **substantive review** of patch 2/4, which ensures trees and tags in `--stdin-packs=follow` mode are treated as roots to maintain reachability closure.

Taylor confirms Peff’s understanding of the core problem: the patch fixes a "timebomb" scenario where earlier repacks fail to walk trees or tags not reachable from commits, creating MIDXs that aren’t closed under reachability. This can break later bitmap generation if a commit reaches those omitted objects. The discussion clarifies that the issue arises in *subsequent* repacks, not within the same process, and that the fix targets the initial generation of the problem rather than repairing existing broken MIDXs.

On the technical nuances Peff raised, Taylor agrees that blobs don’t need special handling (since they can’t reference other objects) but stands by the two-walk approach (commits first, then `extra_roots`) to preserve path prefixes for delta attributes and namehashes. While Peff questioned whether a single traversal with all objects might suffice, Taylor acknowledges that path context is inherently ambiguous—trees or tags might be visited multiple times with different prefixes (e.g., "a" vs. "sub/a")—and that the current approach is a pragmatic improvement. Both agree that rethinking the traversal machinery is beyond the scope of this patch, which Peff endorsed as a strict improvement despite its limitations.

The exchange is narrowly focused on `builtin/pack-objects.c` and doesn’t introduce new edge cases or challenges to the patch’s core goal. Taylor’s reply is concise and aligns with Peff’s feedback, suggesting the patch is nearing readiness for integration. The series as a whole remains under review, with patches 3/4 and 4/4 still awaiting deeper technical scrutiny.
