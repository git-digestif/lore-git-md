This is a discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about modernizing Git’s tutorial documentation. Julia Evans clarifies her position on the future of `gittutorial.adoc` (the older, first-part tutorial) and outlines her vision for a replacement, responding to Junio C Hamano’s earlier confusion about her stance.

Julia reiterates that the current patch series (removing `gittutorial-2`) should proceed regardless of the broader discussion. She then shifts focus to `gittutorial.adoc`, presenting a detailed breakdown of its current content, her draft replacement’s scope, and feedback from 22 testers who tried the new version. The draft currently covers only the basics (`git init`, `git add`, `git commit`, `git diff`, `git status`, `git remote add`, and `git push`), but testers overwhelmingly requested more material on branching and collaboration. Julia argues that the existing `gittutorial` is inadequate for teaching these topics—it introduces branches without explaining what they are and frames collaboration in outdated ways (e.g., multi-user systems).

Her preferred path forward is to merge the new tutorial in stages: first the basic material, then branching and collaboration later. She rejects the idea of directing users to the current `gittutorial` for those topics, calling it ineffective, and dismisses other options (external guides or waiting for a complete rewrite) as impractical. The tone is pragmatic, acknowledging the trade-offs of each approach while advocating for incremental improvement.

This email does not propose new patches but lays the groundwork for future work. The unresolved question is whether the project will accept a partial replacement now or insist on a more comprehensive solution upfront. Junio’s response will likely determine the next steps.
