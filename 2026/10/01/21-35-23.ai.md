This is a **workflow tool discussion** in the ongoing thread about incremental rebase helpers for Git’s `contrib/` area. Nico Williams clarifies the algorithmic behavior of his `bisect-rebase.sh` script (and by extension, Alejandro Colomar’s now-similar `git-bisect-rebase`). The email explains that the script’s best-case performance is O(1) (when no conflicts occur) and worst-case is O(N log N) (when every upstream commit introduces conflicts), rather than the O(log N) one might expect from a pure bisection. He acknowledges the name `bisect-rebase` is slightly misleading but defends it as reasonable given the script’s intent.

The clarification is substantive: it addresses a potential point of confusion about the script’s performance characteristics, which could influence the maintainers’ decision on whether to include it in `contrib/`. The O(N log N) worst-case (rather than O(log N)) is a notable trade-off, though Nico implies it is still preferable to the O(N) behavior of Colomar’s original `git-rebase-walk`. This detail may factor into the ongoing debate about whether the bisection-based approach is worth the added complexity, especially for users who rarely encounter conflicts in every upstream commit. The thread remains focused on `git-bisect-rebase` as the leading candidate, but this email adds nuance to the performance discussion.
