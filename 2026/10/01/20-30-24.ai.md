This is a **substantive review** of Karthik Nayak’s performance optimization for the packed-refs backend. Toon Claes raises several technical concerns about the patch’s approach, focusing on correctness and maintainability rather than performance.

The review identifies four side effects of bypassing `fprintf()` in favor of `fwrite()` for unchanged refs:
1. **Case sensitivity**: OIDs written in uppercase in the original file will remain uppercase (previously normalized by `oid_to_hex()`).
2. **Whitespace handling**: Tabs separating OIDs and refnames (instead of spaces) will be preserved, though `next_record()` uses `isspace()` for parsing.
3. **Invalid refnames**: Previously, malformed refnames triggered `oidclr()`, writing a zero OID; now the original OID is preserved.
4. **Peeled OIDs for broken refs**: Peelable refs with invalid names (e.g., `refs/tags/a-bad~tag`) previously omitted peeled OIDs; now they are written verbatim.

Toon suggests documenting these behavioral changes in the commit message, though they acknowledge none are likely to cause observable issues in practice.

On implementation, Toon proposes splitting `write_packed_entry()` into two functions—one for formatted writes (current behavior) and another for raw writes (new behavior)—to avoid mixing concerns. They also note minor style preferences (e.g., separating `if` conditions) but focus on the structural issue.

The review is **tested in spirit**: Toon clearly analyzed the code paths and edge cases, though they don’t claim to have run the patch locally. Their feedback is narrowly scoped to the packed-refs subsystem, with no broader implications for other Git components.

Key technical details for follow-up:
- The patch’s `record_start` field in `packed_ref_iterator` and the dual-mode `write_packed_entry()` function are central to the discussion.

- The side effects are subtle but could matter for interoperability with tools parsing packed-refs files (e.g., JGit or libgit2).
