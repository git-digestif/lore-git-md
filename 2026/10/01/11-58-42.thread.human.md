This thread is about whether Git’s `contrib/` area should include a helper script for incrementally resolving rebase conflicts, and if so, which one. Alejandro Colomar kicked it off with `git-rebase-walk`, a minimal Bash script that automates an "iterative rebase" workflow by walking commits in reverse topological order. The script is already used in real-world projects (shadow-utils and Linux man-pages), but Patrick Steinhardt noted that `git-imerge` (a third-party tool) already addresses the same problem, raising questions about whether another helper is needed.

The discussion took a turn when Nico Williams introduced `bisect-rebase.sh`, a more sophisticated alternative that uses a bisection-like strategy to reduce the number of conflict resolutions from O(n) to O(k). Nico critiqued `git-rebase-walk` for being slow on large histories and lacking conflict handling, though Alejandro later clarified that his script *does* handle conflicts—just manually. In his latest response, Alejandro concedes the performance issue but defends his script’s simplicity and robustness for his use case. He also expressed interest in trying `bisect-rebase.sh` and even floated the idea of writing his own bisection-based version, signaling openness to a more efficient approach.

The key unresolved questions are whether Git should include *any* helper for this workflow in `contrib/`, and if so, which one. Alejandro’s real-world usage and willingness to refine his script suggest he’s serious about upstreaming, but Nico’s alternative raises the bar for what might be acceptable. The thread remains open, with no clear consensus yet on whether minimalism (`git-rebase-walk`), sophistication (`bisect-rebase.sh`), or neither is the right path.
