This is a review follow-up to the second patch in Johannes Schindelin’s integer-overflow hardening series. The patch changes the `posn` variable in `http.c` from `int` to `size_t` to prevent a heap under-read when HTTP response bodies exceed `INT_MAX`.

Simon Richter suggests replacing `size_t` with `off_t` for the byte position, arguing that `off_t` is the conventional type for file offsets and that its signedness is safe because large-file support (`O_LARGEFILE`) guarantees a 64-bit width on platforms where it is available. His comment is substantive but brief, focusing on type choice rather than questioning the patch’s overall goal. The suggestion does not introduce new controversy—Junio and Patrick Steinhardt have already confirmed the type change is correct—but it does raise a minor design question about whether `off_t` would be more idiomatic than `size_t` for this use case. No other participants have weighed in yet, and the series remains otherwise uncontroversial and ready for integration.
