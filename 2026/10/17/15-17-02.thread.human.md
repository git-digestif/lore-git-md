This thread began as a bug report from first-time contributor Gingrspacecadet: `git apply --check` crashes with an assertion failure (`patch->is_new <= 0` in `check_preimage()` in `apply.c`) when fed a patch whose headers are malformed, a bug confirmed on released versions including 2.43 and 2.45.0. Tanga Joris has since submitted a three-part fix series (currently at v3, based on `maint`) that addresses the root cause -- `find_header()` leaving stale `old_name`/`new_name` state when `parse_git_diff_header()` returns early -- along with a memory-leak fix and clearer error messages, and the discussion has now converged on a proposed v4.

The maintainer, Junio C Hamano, has given substantive and largely confirming guidance in his latest reply. He endorses the stricter validation approach: `find_header()` should immediately diagnose a `diff --git` line with nothing after it as a corrupt patch rather than silently skipping it, which would make the memory-leak patch (1/3) unnecessary and simplify the rest of the series. He also drew three distinctions that will shape v4: malformed `diff --git` sections inside Git-generated patches should be rejected outright; non-patch commentary appearing between complete patches should continue to be ignored as before; and lines starting with `diff` that are not `diff --git` (third-party diff output) follow separate parsing paths and should be left alone. Phillip Wood had already endorsed rejecting corrupt headers, and Siddharth Asthana contributed the memory-leak finding and error-message refinements.

The series remains under review, not merged; the next step is Tanga Joris preparing v4, dropping patch 1/3 in favor of immediate rejection of empty `diff --git` sections in `find_header()`, now with the maintainer's explicit confirmation that this matches his intent. New tests in `t/t4133-apply-filenames.sh` cover the edge cases, including blank lines after `diff --git` and reversed header ordering.
