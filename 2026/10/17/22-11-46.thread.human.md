This thread is about fixing a severe performance regression in `git fetch` from shallow repositories over protocol v2, where fetches can take minutes instead of seconds due to inefficient history traversal. The issue was first reported by Harald Nordgren, who proposed a patch to align protocol v2 behavior with protocol v0 by directly advertising shallow grafts when no specific depth is requested.

The initial patch had a critical flaw: it misused the `packet_write_fmt` API, breaking sideband multiplexing. Junio C Hamano identified this issue and provided a failing test case to guard against the regression. Harald has since revised the patch, switching to the `packet_writer` API to preserve sideband functionality while maintaining the performance optimization. The updated patch also includes the failing test case and touches `shallow.c`, `upload-pack.c`, and the test suite (`t/t5537-fetch-shallow.sh`).

The revised patch appears to address all technical concerns raised in review. No substantive objections remain, and the underlying approach—directly advertising shallow grafts in protocol v2—is uncontroversial and well-supported by performance measurements (reducing fetch time from 7 minutes to 1 second in the reported case). The patch is now likely ready for further review or integration.
