This thread is about a performance regression in Git's `upload-pack` subsystem that causes slow fetches from shallow repositories over protocol v2. Harald Nordgren identified the issue: when fetching from a shallow repository with a long history (e.g., 900k commits), protocol v2 inefficiently traverses the entire commit history to determine which shallow commits to advertise, leading to fetch times of minutes instead of seconds. Protocol v0 avoids this by simply listing all shallow commits upfront.

Harald's patch aligns protocol v2's behavior with v0 when no specific depth is requested, advertising all shallow grafts directly rather than performing an expensive traversal. The fix also ensures `git clone --reject-shallow` works consistently across both protocols. For operations like `git fetch --unshallow` that still require history traversal, the patch optimizes the walk to visit each commit only once. The change is minimal (4 insertions, 4 deletions) and touches `shallow.c` and `upload-pack.c`.

The patch is marked as RFC, indicating the author is seeking feedback on the solution's correctness rather than its necessity. The performance improvement is dramatic (7 minutes → 1 second), and the approach appears technically sound. No reviews or maintainer decisions have been posted yet, so the thread is still in the early feedback stage.
