This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The thread has shifted from technical implementation to a fundamental debate about user intent and CLI design.

Harald Nordgren pushes back against Junio C Hamano’s proposal to split the feature into two distinct commands: the existing `--delete-merged` (strict, tip-reachability only) and a new `--delete-irrelevant` (looser, change-based detection). Harald argues that the distinction is unnecessary for his use case—once his work is integrated into the mainline, he no longer needs the branch, regardless of how it was merged (squash, rebase, or regular merge). He sees the current implementation as sufficient and questions the value of overcomplicating the CLI with a separate `--delete-irrelevant` option, especially since the integrated work remains easily retrievable from the mainline.

The exchange highlights a tension between safety and convenience. Junio’s concern is that `--delete-merged` should remain a conservative, predictable tool, while Harald prioritizes practicality for common workflows. The discussion now hinges on whether the project should prioritize strictness (and risk under-serving users who rely on squash/rebase workflows) or flexibility (and risk introducing ambiguity into a long-standing command). No technical changes have been proposed in this email—it’s purely a conceptual debate about the feature’s scope and design.
