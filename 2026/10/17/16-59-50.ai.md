This is a brief reply from Junio C Hamano in the ongoing discussion about Git’s inconsistent branch name validation. Junio acknowledges Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s observation that deprecating `@` as a synonym for `HEAD` would not inconvenience him personally, but he reiterates his skepticism that such a change would ever be implemented. He notes that Git contains many design flaws that are effectively unfixable due to "heavy inertia," implying that even well-motivated cleanups—like removing `@` as a shorthand for `HEAD`—are unlikely to gain traction.

The email does not advance the core issue (the usability trap of creating a branch named `@` via porcelain commands) or propose any technical solution. Instead, it reinforces Junio’s broader stance: while some inconsistencies in Git’s design are acknowledged, the project’s stability and backward compatibility often outweigh the benefits of fixing them. This reply signals that the thread’s focus remains on the narrower question of whether to reject bare `@` as a branch name in `--branch` validation, not on broader changes to `@`’s role in Git’s syntax.
