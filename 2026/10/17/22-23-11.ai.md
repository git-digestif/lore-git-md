This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The email captures a key disagreement between Harald Nordgren and Junio C Hamano over the **user intent** and **safety guarantees** of the feature.

Harald pushes back against Junio’s proposal to split the functionality into a strict `--delete-merged` (tip-reachability only) and a looser `--delete-irrelevant` (change-based detection). He argues that once his work is integrated into the mainline — regardless of whether it was squashed, rebased, or merged verbatim — the branch is no longer needed, and the mechanism of integration should not matter. His position is pragmatic: the branch’s purpose is fulfilled when its changes are upstream, and the user can always recover the work from the mainline if needed. This aligns with his earlier implementation, where squash and rebase detection are treated as a unified feature under `--delete-merged`.

The email does not engage with Junio’s architectural concerns about ambiguity (e.g., parallel AI-generated patches solving the same problem) or the scalability of the current CLI design (`--delete-merged` + `--no-squashed`). Instead, it reinforces Harald’s focus on the **primary use case**: local and forge-style workflows where a branch’s changes are integrated, even if the commit history is rewritten. He does not address Junio’s example of "irrelevant" branches (e.g., independently solved problems) or the broader question of whether `--delete-irrelevant` is a worthwhile addition.

This reply leaves the core tension unresolved: Junio’s framing prioritizes **safety and predictability** (strict vs. loose definitions of "merged"), while Harald’s prioritizes **practicality and workflow efficiency** (deleting branches once their changes are upstream, regardless of how). The discussion now hinges on whether the project will adopt Junio’s proposed CLI split or proceed with Harald’s unified approach. No new technical details or algorithmic proposals are introduced here.
