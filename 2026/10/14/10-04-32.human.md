This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Jun Wu’s series adding a configurable index lock timeout. Jeff King (Peff) identifies a **pre-existing race condition** in `git commit` that the patch inadvertently widens: the command reads the index *before* taking the lock, then later discards any updates made by other processes (like `git add`) while it was unlocked. The timeout mechanism doesn’t create the race, but it increases the window of opportunity for it to occur by allowing commands to wait for the lock.

Peff’s test case—adding a file while `git commit` sleeps after reading the index—demonstrates the problem: the commit will ignore the staged changes. He acknowledges that the patch *does* fix the issue by locking before reading, but argues the commit message should explicitly call out this race as a motivation, not just the lock-order history. He also notes the trade-off: locking earlier means holding the lock longer, which could increase contention for other commands.

The review is **tested** (Peff provides a reproduction script) and **substantive** (it engages with correctness, not just style). The race condition is a real concern, and Peff’s feedback suggests the patch may need a revised commit message to justify the lock-order change more clearly. This is now a blocker for the series’ progress until the author addresses it.
