This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series (v3) by Igor Wiedler that resolves a performance regression in Git's connectivity checks during deepening fetches. The issue caused multi-minute delays when fetching from large alternate repositories (e.g., 7M objects) by forcing full history walks of those alternates. The series addresses both the immediate regression and its root cause: alternate repositories that are shallow or grafted cannot be trusted as unconditional boundaries because their refs point to commits with missing history.

The first patch (already approved by Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler) fixes the immediate regression by treating alternate refs as a boundary in all connectivity checks, not just deepening fetches. The second patch, just posted, completes the solution by teaching `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to skip refs from shallow or grafted alternates. This is a minimal, surgical change with no on-disk format impact, and it’s backward-compatible—existing alternates without `shallow` or `info/grafts` files continue to work as before. The series is now code-complete, test-stable, and unblocked, with all prior design feedback from Junio and Peff fully incorporated. The only procedural hurdle—a Windows CI test failure—was resolved in v3.

The open edge case of partial clones (promisor remotes) is explicitly deferred, as detecting them would require reading the alternate’s config, which the series avoids for simplicity. Peff’s review endorsed this as a reasonable trade-off, though he noted the conceptual distinction from Git’s general insistence on full connectivity of original objects (e.g., `fsck` ignores replace refs). No objections remain, and the series appears ready for integration.
