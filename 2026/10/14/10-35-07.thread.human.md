This thread is about fixing a performance regression in Git's connectivity checks for deepening fetches, where `--alternate-refs` was forcing full history walks of large alternate repositories (e.g., 7M objects), causing multi-minute delays. Igor Wiedler posted a two-patch v2 series that both fixes the immediate regression and addresses the underlying design flaw by treating alternate refs as a boundary in *all* connectivity checks, not just deepening fetches. The series also teaches `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates, though it defers handling the edge case of partial clones (promisor remotes).

The v2 series retains approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler for the core regression fix (patch 1) while incorporating Junio Hamano’s design feedback in patch 2. Junio has paused the series procedurally, reminding Igor to engage in discussion before posting updated patchsets, but no technical concerns have been raised. Jeff King (Peff) has endorsed the two-step approach (immediate fix + root-cause solution) in principle, agreeing with Junio’s design direction, though he noted some uncertainty about the interaction with `opt->is_deepening_fetch` and clarified conceptual distinctions around replace refs and partial clones. His recent review of patch 2 is supportive and technically thorough, praising the low-level fix and test coverage, but stops short of an explicit "Reviewed-by."

Key technical details include changes to `connected.c` (negating `--alternate-refs` in connectivity checks), `odb.c` (ignoring shallow/grafted alternates), and expanded test coverage in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh`. The series is backward-compatible and resolves real-world CI performance issues, though the deferred partial-clone edge case may require future work.
