This thread is about fixing a performance regression in Git's connectivity check that causes multi-minute delays during shallow fetches from large alternate repositories (e.g., those with 7 million objects). Igor Wiedler posted a two-patch v2 series that first addresses the immediate regression (patch 1) and then tackles the underlying design flaw by treating alternate refs as a boundary in *all* connectivity checks (patch 2). The series supersedes an earlier v1 and incorporates feedback from Junio C Hamano, who had raised concerns about the broader trust boundary issue.

Key participants include Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler, who approved the core regression fix in v1, and Junio, who proposed the two-step approach now reflected in v2. The series is currently paused due to a procedural reminder from Junio about engaging in discussion before posting updates, though no technical objections remain. Jeff King (Peff) recently endorsed the two-step design, confirming that the immediate fix aligns with the original intent of the 2019 commit that introduced the bug. He also agreed with Junio’s broader concern but noted some uncertainty about the interaction with `opt->is_deepening_fetch`, reinforcing the case for the incremental approach.

The patches touch `connected.c` and `odb.c`, with expanded test coverage in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh` to verify the new behavior. The regression is resolved by negating `--alternate-refs` in `check_connected()` for deepening fetches, while the root-cause fix extends `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates. The series is backward-compatible, as existing alternates without `shallow` or `info/grafts` files continue to work unchanged. One loose end remains: the undetected edge case of partial clones (promisor remotes), which is explicitly deferred to future work. The next step is for Igor to respond to Junio’s procedural reminder before the series can proceed.
