This thread is about fixing a performance regression in Git’s connectivity checks during deepening fetches, where `--alternate-refs` forced a full history walk of large alternates (e.g., 7M objects), causing multi-minute delays. The issue was initially addressed in a single-patch v1 by Igor Wiedler, which moved the `--alternate-refs` flag to skip alternate refs during deepening fetches. While Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler approved the v1 patch, Junio C Hamano raised a broader design concern: the same trust model flaw (ignoring shallow boundaries in alternate refs) also affects non-deepening fetches.

Igor has now posted a two-patch v2 series that adopts Junio’s proposed two-step fix. The first patch negates `--alternate-refs` in *all* connectivity checks, not just deepening fetches, treating alternate refs as a boundary in both cases. The second patch extends this logic to `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()`, ensuring fetch-pack and receive-pack ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates entirely. The series includes expanded tests in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh` and touches `connected.c`, `odb.c`, and documentation. The only deferred edge case is partial clones (promisor remotes), which the commit message notes as future work.

The v2 series resolves the open question from v1—whether to merge a minimal fix or wait for a broader solution—by implementing both steps upfront. Patrick and Justin’s approvals still apply to the core regression fix, but the series now aligns with Junio’s design feedback. The changes are backward-compatible, and the thread appears ready for final review, with no unresolved technical concerns. The broader issue of shallow-boundary trust in alternate refs is now addressed, leaving only the partial-clone case as a loose end.
