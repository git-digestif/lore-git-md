This thread is about fixing a performance regression in Git’s connectivity checks during deepening fetches, where `--alternate-refs` was forcing full history walks of large alternate repositories (e.g., 7M objects), causing multi-minute delays. Igor Wiedler posted a two-patch v2 series that addresses both the immediate regression and the underlying design flaw by treating alternate refs as a boundary in all connectivity checks and ignoring refs from shallow or grafted alternates. The series retains approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler for the core regression fix (patch 1) and incorporates Junio Hamano’s design feedback in patch 2.

The series is currently paused due to a procedural reminder from Junio about workflow expectations, though no technical concerns have been raised. Jeff King (Peff) has endorsed the two-step approach (immediate fix + root-cause solution) and clarified design boundaries in earlier reviews, though he hasn’t given an explicit "Reviewed-by." His latest email addresses a Windows CI failure in the new test added by patch 2, proposing two fixes to normalize paths for cross-platform compatibility. This is a surface-level but practical fix—unrelated to the patch’s logic—and if incorporated, the series should be ready to proceed. The deferred edge case of partial clones (promisor remotes) remains unaddressed but is acknowledged as future work.
