This thread is about fixing a performance regression in Git’s connectivity checks during deepening fetches, where `--alternate-refs` could force multi-minute history walks of large alternate repositories (e.g., 7M objects). Igor Wiedler’s two-patch series (v3) addresses both the immediate regression and its root cause: patch 1 disables `--alternate-refs` in all connectivity checks, while patch 2 teaches Git to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates. The series is now technically complete, with all prior feedback incorporated, tests passing on all platforms, and no outstanding objections.

Key participants—Patrick Steinhardt, Justin Tobler, Jeff King (Peff), and Junio C Hamano—have reviewed the series. Patrick and Justin approved the core regression fix (patch 1) in earlier versions, and Patrick has now also approved patch 2, endorsing the root-cause solution. Peff’s review confirmed the technical soundness of both patches and clarified design boundaries, such as deferring support for partial clones (promisor remotes). Junio’s earlier design feedback was fully addressed in v3, and while he raised a procedural concern about email threading, Patrick has since closed that loop, stating no further reroll is needed for integration. The series is ready for Junio to pick up, with no remaining technical or procedural blockers.
