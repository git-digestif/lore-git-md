This thread is about a two-patch series from Igor Wiedler that fixes a performance regression in Git's connectivity checks during deepening fetches. The issue caused multi-minute delays when fetching from large alternate repositories (e.g., 7M objects) by incorrectly treating alternate refs as valid history boundaries. The v2 series, now posted, addresses both the immediate regression and the underlying design flaw raised by Junio Hamano in the v1 discussion.

The first patch (already approved by Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler) negates `--alternate-refs` in `check_connected()` to prevent unnecessary history walks. The second patch, just posted, extends `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates, ensuring these are no longer treated as valid "have"s in any connectivity check. This aligns with Junio's preference for a comprehensive solution rather than a stopgap fix. The series includes updated tests and documentation, and the changes are backward-compatible—existing alternates without shallow or grafted histories continue to work as before.

The only loose end is the deferred handling of partial clones (promisor remotes), which would require reading the alternate's config. No objections have been raised about this trade-off yet. While the core regression fix retains its approvals, the expanded scope of the second patch hasn't been reviewed by Patrick or Justin. The series is ready for further review, particularly on whether the root-cause fix adequately addresses Junio's design concerns.
