This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series by Igor Wiedler that resolves a performance regression in Git’s connectivity checks during deepening fetches. The issue caused multi-minute delays when fetching from large alternates (e.g., repositories with 7 million objects) due to `--alternate-refs` forcing full history walks. The v3 series, now code-complete and test-stable, implements a two-step fix: the first patch disables `--alternate-refs` in all connectivity checks, while the second teaches Git to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates. The series has received substantive approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler for the core regression fix, and Jeff King (Peff) has reviewed both patches, confirming their technical soundness and endorsing the design. Junio C Hamano’s earlier design concerns have been fully addressed, and the only procedural blocker—a Windows CI test failure—was resolved in v3.

The patches touch `connected.c`, `odb.c`, and `Documentation/rev-list-options.adoc`, with expanded test coverage in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh` to verify the fix across platforms. The changes are backward-compatible, and the performance impact is significant, eliminating delays of over 200 seconds in affected workflows. Two edge cases remain deferred: partial clones (promisor remotes) and the interaction with `opt->is_deepening_fetch`, though neither is considered a blocker. Igor’s latest email is purely procedural, acknowledging Junio’s reminder about mailing-list thread structure, and does not alter the series’ readiness for integration. The series is now unblocked and awaiting Junio’s merge.
