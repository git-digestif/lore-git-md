This thread is about a two-patch series by Igor Wiedler that fixes a performance regression in Git’s connectivity checks during deepening fetches. The issue causes multi-minute delays when fetching from large alternates (e.g., repositories with 7 million objects) because `--alternate-refs` forces full history walks. The v2 series, posted after feedback on v1, addresses both the immediate regression and the underlying design flaw by treating alternate refs as a boundary in *all* connectivity checks and ignoring refs from shallow or grafted alternates. The edge case of partial clones (promisor remotes) is explicitly deferred for future work.

The series has retained approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler for the core regression fix (patch 1), while patch 2 incorporates Junio Hamano’s design feedback. However, Junio has now paused the process with a procedural reminder: contributors are expected to engage in discussion *before* posting updated patchsets, rather than sending new versions unilaterally. This is not a technical objection—the v2 series still aligns with Junio’s earlier feedback—but the series cannot proceed until Igor responds to the reminder. The regression itself remains a real-world pain point for CI workflows, and the fix is well-motivated, but the next step is a simple acknowledgment from the author to unblock the process.
