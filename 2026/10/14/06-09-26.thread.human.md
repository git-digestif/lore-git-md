This thread is about a five-patch series from Johannes Schindelin that fixes integer overflows in Git’s codebase, flagged by Coverity. The goal is to harden several subsystems—archive-tar, http, wrapper, and date—against undefined behavior, crashes, or silent corruption when arithmetic exceeds type limits. These are defensive fixes targeting latent issues rather than observed bugs, and Junio has confirmed they only affect local user input (like `git commit --date` or `git log @{...}`), not stored commit timestamps, so there’s no risk of breaking history traversal.

The series is under review as v1, with no major objections to the technical approach. Key changes include new overflow-checking macros in `git-compat-util.h`, type adjustments in `http.c` (changing `posn` from `int` to `size_t` to prevent heap under-reads), and hardening in `wrapper.c` and `date.c` to reject implausibly large values upfront. Junio and Patrick Steinhardt have both confirmed the `http.c` type change is correct, and Junio recently rejected a suggestion to use `off_t` instead of `size_t` for `posn`, reinforcing the original choice on memory-safety grounds. Phillip Wood’s review raised platform-compatibility concerns about `SSIZE_MAX` vs. `size_t` width mismatches on NonStop and similar platforms, and proposed more portable alternatives for the GCC-only overflow macros in `date.c`. Randall S. Becker (NonStop maintainer) was cc’d to confirm whether this remains a live issue, but hasn’t responded yet.

The series is uncontroversial and technically sound, but two loose ends remain: the `SSIZE_MAX` platform question and Phillip’s macro feedback. If those are addressed in a v2, the patches are likely to be merged. No test coverage was added for the hardened edge cases, which might prompt review requests, but the fixes themselves are straightforward and defensive. The thread is otherwise quiet, with no new objections or design debates.
