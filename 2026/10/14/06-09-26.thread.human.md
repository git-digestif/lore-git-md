This thread is about a five-patch series from Johannes Schindelin that fixes integer overflows in Git’s codebase flagged by Coverity, hardening subsystems like archive-tar, http, wrapper, and date against undefined behavior or crashes. The series is under review, with v1 posted in early November 2026.

The patches introduce new overflow-checking macros in `git-compat-util.h` and apply defensive checks to functions handling user input, such as `approxidate_alpha()` in `date.c` and `read_in_full()`/`write_in_full()` in `wrapper.c`. Junio clarified that the hardening in `date.c` is limited to local user input (e.g., `git commit --date="..."`) and doesn’t affect stored commit timestamps, addressing concerns about breaking history traversal. However, Phillip Wood’s recent review of the fifth patch raises two key issues: the GCC-only limitation of the new `mult_overflows()` macro and the patch’s handling of unsigned `time_t` on some platforms. He proposes a more portable solution using `intmax_t` arithmetic, which would work across all compilers and `time_t` representations.

Phillip’s earlier review of the wrapper patch also questioned whether byte counts exceeding `SSIZE_MAX` could occur in practice on platforms like NonStop, where `ssize_t` may be narrower than `size_t`. Randall S. Becker, the NonStop port maintainer, was cc’d to confirm if this remains a live issue. So far, Johannes hasn’t responded to Phillip’s latest feedback, so a v2 may be needed to address the portability concerns. The series is otherwise uncontroversial, with Junio’s approval pending resolution of these technical points. No test coverage has been added for the hardened edge cases, which might prompt further review requests.
