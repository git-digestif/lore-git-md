Johannes Schindelin has posted the second iteration of a five-patch series addressing integer overflows flagged by Coverity across Git's archive, HTTP, wrapper, and date-parsing code. The series remains under review; Junio C Hamano's latest "What's cooking" report marks it as waiting for response, warning it could expire from the integration branches without further activity. Patches one and two -- fixing a latent `size_t` underflow in `archive-tar.c` and an `int` accumulator overflow in `http.c` -- are considered uncontroversial and ready to land, while patches three through five have been substantially revised to address reviewer concerns about portability and API design.

The wrapper-layer changes, now split across patches three and four, harden the I/O helpers against overflow by design. `write_in_full()` now returns `int` (0 or -1) instead of `ssize_t`, and the companion read side introduces `read_in_full_or_error()` for callers that do not need byte counts, while `read_in_full()` itself now traps requests larger than `SSIZE_MAX` with a `BUG()` rather than silently wrapping. Six call sites -- including `builtin/blame.c`, `combine-diff.c`, `dir.c`, `grep.c`, `refs/packed-backend.c`, and `strbuf.c` -- were migrated to the new error-only variant, and new clar unit tests in `t/unit-tests/u-wrapper.c` cover successful, empty, short, and error paths for both read and write.

Phillip Wood's earlier objections about GCC-specific overflow macros have been resolved by rewriting the `date.c` hardening to use portable `uintmax_t` arithmetic with explicit checks, and Junio confirmed the `http.c` switch to `size_t` is safe given `xwrite()`'s internal clamping. With the revised patches now addressing the API-migration and portability questions, the series awaits final acknowledgment from reviewers before Junio queues it.
