This thread is about a five-patch series from Johannes Schindelin that fixes integer overflows in Git’s codebase flagged by Coverity, hardening subsystems like archive-tar, http, wrapper, and date against undefined behavior or crashes. The series is under review in its first version, with most patches uncontroversial after clarifications from Junio and Patrick Steinhardt. The main open questions revolve around platform-specific risks—particularly whether byte counts exceeding `SSIZE_MAX` could still occur on platforms like NonStop—and the portability of the new overflow-checking macros, which Phillip Wood has flagged as GCC-only and potentially unsafe due to multiple argument evaluation.

Phillip Wood’s reviews have been substantive, raising concerns about the `SSIZE_MAX` check in `wrapper.c` and proposing more portable alternatives for the date-handling logic in patch 5. Randall S. Becker, the NonStop port maintainer, has been cc’d to weigh in on the platform-specific risk but hasn’t responded yet. Junio and Patrick Steinhardt have both given their approval for the uncontroversial parts, leaving the macro implementation and platform questions as the remaining hurdles before integration. Harald Nordgren’s recent comment suggests enabling Coverity as a GitHub CI check to prevent future regressions, but this is a process suggestion and doesn’t affect the current patches. The series is otherwise ready to advance once the technical feedback is addressed.
