This thread is a five-patch series from Johannes Schindelin, posted via GitGitGadget, that fixes integer overflows flagged by Coverity in several Git subsystems (archive-tar, http, wrapper, and date). The goal is to harden the codebase against undefined behavior, crashes, or silent corruption when arithmetic exceeds type limits, though these are latent issues rather than observed bugs.

The series is under review at v1, with most patches uncontroversial. Junio Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both confirmed the correctness of the `http.c` patch (changing `posn` from `int` to `size_t`), and Junio clarified that the date-handling hardening in `date.c` applies only to local user input (e.g., `--date` flags) and doesn’t affect stored commit timestamps. Phillip Wood’s review raised two key points: a platform-compatibility concern about `SSIZE_MAX` vs. `size_t` width mismatches on NonStop (cc’ing Randall Becker for confirmation), and a proposal to replace the GCC-only `mult_overflows` macro with portable `intmax_t` arithmetic for the date calculations. The wrapper patch’s defensive checks against implausibly large byte counts may address a real-world risk on some platforms, not just theoretical hardening.

The series touches no on-disk formats or CLI options, and no tests were added for the hardened edge cases—something reviewers may yet request. A low-weight suggestion to enable Coverity in GitHub CI was also floated. The patches are otherwise ready for integration once the macro portability and platform-specific questions are resolved.
