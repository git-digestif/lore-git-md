This is the fifth and final patch in a five-part series hardening Git against integer overflows flagged by Coverity. The series is part of Johannes Schindelin’s ongoing effort to eliminate Coverity warnings, with four prior branches already merged to `master` this year.

This patch specifically targets `approxidate_alpha()` in `date.c`, where multiplying a time unit (e.g., weeks) by a user-supplied number could overflow an `int`, producing incorrect dates. For example, "99999 weeks ago" would wrap around to a nonsensical value. The fix introduces checked multiplication using the new `mult_overflows` macro (added earlier in the series) to prevent overflow. On platforms with unsigned `time_t`, it also rejects excessively large counts upfront to avoid truncation or negative offsets.

The change is narrowly scoped to the `approxidate` subsystem and does not affect other date-parsing code. While the patch is defensive—addressing a latent issue rather than an observed bug—it’s a clear improvement: the new logic is more robust and aligns with the hardening goals of the series. No tests are included, which may draw review attention, but the pattern matches prior Coverity-driven fixes. The patch looks uncontroversial and well-explained.
