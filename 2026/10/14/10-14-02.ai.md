This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) in the ongoing thread about Jun Wu’s configurable index lock timeout series. Peff engages directly with the core problem and the proposed solution, offering both technical analysis and historical context.

The email centers on the trade-offs of `--no-optional-locks` and the rationale for introducing a lock timeout. Peff acknowledges that `--no-optional-locks` (introduced in 2017) was designed to mitigate IDE lock contention by making `git status` avoid taking the index lock, but notes that this mitigation is incomplete: users must eventually run an index-updating command to restore performance, and there is no guarantee they will. He agrees that a lock timeout is a reasonable complementary approach, though he suggests an IDE could theoretically alternate between locked and lock-free `status` calls based on elapsed time (a more complex solution that the series avoids).

Peff then turns to the broader question of race conditions. He observes that any race window the timeout might introduce was already present without it, but cautions that the timeout could make such races more likely — particularly for commands like `git add` that modify the index. He singles out `git commit` as having a long-standing (20+ year) race condition where concurrent index updates (e.g., from `git add`) can be silently discarded if `git commit` reads the index before acquiring the lock. This latent bug is fixed by Patch 2/2 in the series, which restores the 2007 lock-before-read order in `git commit`. Peff implies that once this race is closed, the timeout becomes a natural and uncontroversial extension.

The review is constructive and forward-looking. Peff does not oppose the series, but his comments reinforce the importance of clearly motivating the lock-order change in `git commit` as a correctness fix (not just a timeout enabler) and suggest that the timeout’s interaction with other commands (e.g., `git add`) may need further scrutiny. His reference to the 20+ year history of the `git commit` race provides useful context for reviewers assessing the urgency of the fix.
