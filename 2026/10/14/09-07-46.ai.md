Peff identifies a potential long-term issue: once Git 3.0 defaults to SHA-256, the test will effectively never run in CI, even though it remains valid for SHA-1 repositories. His suggested diff isolates the test in a subshell with its own repository, ensuring the SHA-1 objects are only used locally.

The review is **technically substantive** because it engages with the test’s behavior and long-term maintainability, not just its immediate correctness. Peff’s proposal would preserve test coverage without relying on a dedicated SHA-1 CI job, which may not always meet the other prerequisites (e.g., `UNZIP_ZIP64_SUPPORT`). The trade-off is slightly increased test complexity, but the benefit is broader coverage. This is a **likely point of discussion** in the thread, as it touches on test design philosophy (self-contained vs. environment-dependent) and the implications of Git’s SHA-256 transition. The patch’s current approach is correct for the immediate problem, but Peff’s feedback raises a valid concern about future-proofing.
