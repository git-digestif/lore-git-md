This thread is about a bug in Git’s files ref backend where root refs (like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`) are inconsistently handled in the loose ref cache. The issue arises because root refs are only included in the cache if the `REFS_FOR_EACH_INCLUDE_ROOT_REFS` flag is set during the *first* iteration that creates the cache, leading to unpredictable behavior in later calls. Andy Koppe proposed a fix that avoids storing root refs in the shared cache entirely, instead creating a temporary cache for them on demand when the flag is passed.

The discussion has expanded into a design debate. Junio C Hamano suggested an alternative approach: maintaining two persistent caches (one for refs under `refs/` and one for root refs) and combining them as needed. Andy defended his original solution, arguing it’s simpler and avoids unnecessary overhead, since the flag is only used in two places (`git for-each-ref --include-root-refs` and `git refs migrate`), both of which invoke it just once. He also considered but rejected modifying the cache to skip root refs during iteration when the flag is unset, as it would complicate the more common case of iterating over non-root refs.

The patch itself is small and well-scoped, touching only `files-backend.c` and adding a test in `t/t6302-for-each-ref-filter.sh` to verify `--include-root-refs` works correctly with `--merged`. However, the architectural direction remains unresolved. Andy floated a third idea—dropping the flag entirely and requiring callers to explicitly request refs under `refs/`—but acknowledged this would be a larger and riskier change. For now, the trade-offs between simplicity, performance, and maintainability are still being weighed, with no clear consensus yet.
