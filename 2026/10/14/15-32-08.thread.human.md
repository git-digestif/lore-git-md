This thread is about a bugfix for an inconsistency in how Git’s files ref backend handles root refs (like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`) in its loose ref cache. Andy Koppe identified the issue—root refs were inconsistently included or excluded depending on when the cache was first created—and posted a v2 patch that excludes them from the shared cache entirely, instead creating a temporary cache when the `REFS_FOR_EACH_INCLUDE_ROOT_REFS` flag is used. The fix is narrow, targeting only `files-backend.c`, and includes a new test in `t/t6302-for-each-ref-filter.sh`.

The patch is under review and technically unblocked. Patrick Steinhardt’s initial concerns about design and performance were resolved in earlier discussion, and his latest email is a surface-level review: he asks for a range-diff to ease comparison with v1 and suggests splitting the documentation corrections into a separate commit. The core fix remains unchanged. The main open question is architectural—whether to stick with Andy’s temporary cache approach (now implemented) or adopt Junio C Hamano’s proposed alternative of maintaining two persistent caches (one for the `refs/` hierarchy, one for root refs). Junio hasn’t endorsed either approach yet, and no one has implemented his alternative. For now, the patch is ready for further review, with only minor procedural tweaks requested.
