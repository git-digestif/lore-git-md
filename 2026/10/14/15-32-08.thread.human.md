This thread is about a bugfix for the files ref backend that addresses an inconsistency in how root refs (like `HEAD` or `ORIG_HEAD`) are handled in the loose ref cache. The issue arises because root refs are only included in the cache if the `REFS_FOR_EACH_INCLUDE_ROOT_REFS` flag is passed during the *first* iteration that creates the cache; subsequent iterations include them regardless of the flag, while earlier iterations omit them even if the flag is set.

Andy Koppe, the author, has posted a third iteration (v3) of the patch, which keeps root refs out of the shared loose ref cache entirely. Instead, when an iterator requests root refs, it creates a separate temporary ref cache for them, overlays its iterator on the loose ref iterator, and frees the cache when done. This approach avoids the inconsistency while minimizing overhead, as the flag is used in only two places and invoked just once per command. The core implementation is unchanged from v2, but the commit message has been expanded to discuss alternatives and justify the chosen design. The documentation corrections from v2 have been dropped and will be addressed in a separate patch, as requested by Patrick Steinhardt.

The patch touches `files-backend.c` and adds a test in `t/t6302-for-each-ref-filter.sh` to verify that `--include-root-refs` works correctly with `--merged`. The discussion remains focused on architectural trade-offs: Andy’s temporary cache approach (implemented in v3) versus Junio C Hamano’s proposed persistent dual-cache design. Patrick Steinhardt has accepted Andy’s explanation and no longer objects to the approach, leaving the patch technically unblocked. The patch is well-scoped and ready for further review or integration, pending resolution of the architectural question.
