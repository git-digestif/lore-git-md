This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Jun Wu’s configurable index lock timeout series. Jeff King (Peff) weighs in on the broader design trade-offs and the pre-existing race condition in `git commit`.

Peff agrees that the timeout approach is reasonable for mitigating IDE-induced lock contention, though he notes that `--no-optional-locks` was originally designed to be temporary, assuming users would eventually run an index-updating command. He suggests that IDEs could be smarter about occasionally running a full `git status` to refresh the index, but acknowledges that defining “a while” is tricky, making the timeout solution pragmatic.

On the race-avoidance logic, Peff clarifies that the timeout itself doesn’t create new races—it merely widens existing windows. He highlights the `git commit` race (where concurrent `git add` updates can be silently discarded) as a long-standing issue that the series coincidentally fixes by changing the lock order. While the race is rare in practice, closing it makes the timeout behavior safer. His earlier review of Patch 2 already identified this fix, but his tone here reinforces that the lock-order change is a correctness improvement, not just a refactor.

The summary leaves the series’ status unchanged: still under review, with Jun Wu needing to address Junio’s edge-case questions and clarify the race-fix motivation in Patch 2’s commit message. Peff’s comments don’t introduce new objections but underscore the importance of framing the lock-order change as a bugfix.
