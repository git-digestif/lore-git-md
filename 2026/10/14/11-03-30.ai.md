This is a substantive review of the `test-grep-lint` performance regression. Jeff King (Peff) confirms the issue, reporting that `make test-grep-lint` takes ~6.5 seconds on his machine—over 5% of the total test suite runtime—making it a noticeable bottleneck. He notes that while `chainlint` consumes similar CPU time, it runs in parallel, which mitigates its impact. Peff suggests two potential solutions: parallelizing `test-grep-lint` or amortizing its parsing cost by sharing effort with other lint checks (or even `chainlint`).

He also proposes a targeted optimization: a patch that skips further parsing if the test body doesn’t contain the string `grep`, reducing runtime by ~50%. The patch is simple but effective, though Peff acknowledges it conflicts with the broader idea of sharing parsing work. The trade-off here is between immediate performance gains and long-term maintainability. This email advances the thread by providing concrete data, a tested fix, and a clear technical trade-off for discussion. The next steps will likely involve weighing the patch’s simplicity against the architectural benefits of a more integrated linting system.
