This is a performance bugfix targeting the connectivity check in deepening fetches, now expanded into a two-patch series. The goal remains the same—restoring instant connectivity checks in shallow fetches—but the scope has grown to address the root cause Junio C Hamano identified: alternate refs are trusted without regard for their shallow boundaries, affecting both deepening and non-deepening fetches.

The first patch moves `--alternate-refs` into the boundary arguments of `check_connected()`, ensuring it is negated (like `--all`) in both deepening and non-deepening cases. This prevents the connectivity check from walking the alternate’s entire history, fixing the immediate regression. The second patch extends `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to ignore refs from shallow or grafted alternates, ensuring neither the connectivity check, `fetch-pack`, nor `receive-pack` treat history the alternate doesn’t have as present. Partial clones are not detected, as noted in the commit message.

The series touches `connected.c`, `odb.c`, and `Documentation/rev-list-options.adoc`, and adds new tests in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh`. The changes are minimal and targeted, avoiding unnecessary complexity. The commit messages are thorough, explaining the historical context, the performance impact, and the reasoning behind each change. This looks like a well-crafted, motivated fix for a real-world CI pain point, now addressing both the symptom and the underlying design flaw.

Junio’s earlier concern about the broader issue is now resolved by the second patch, which aligns with his proposed two-step fix. The series has already received approval from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler for the v1 patch, and the expanded scope should satisfy Junio’s design feedback. The only loose end is the lack of detection for partial clones, but this is explicitly noted as a limitation rather than an oversight. The series is likely to merge once Junio confirms the updated approach.
