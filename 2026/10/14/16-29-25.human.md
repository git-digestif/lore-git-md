This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing thread about adding a configurable index lock timeout (`core.indexLockTimeout`) to reduce contention between IDE background polling and user commands. D. Ben Knoble, after further reflection and clarification from Jun Wu, concedes that the proposed approach is reasonable and bows out of the debate.

Knoble acknowledges that his initial skepticism about the performance rationale for `--no-optional-locks` was addressed by Jun’s explanation: when the index lock is unavailable, `git status` falls back to full content hashing (not just stat refresh), leading to persistent slowdowns if users don’t run index-writing commands like `git add` to recover. He also accepts Jun’s framing of the timeout as a pragmatic middle ground, noting that other reviewers (notably Jeff King) support the approach. While Knoble still prefers root-cause fixes—such as improving `git status` performance, enabling lock-free writes, or adopting fsmonitor-style notifications—he recognizes these are longer-term solutions with their own trade-offs (e.g., major format changes for lock-free writes).

The reply includes a brief look at how Vim’s Fugitive plugin avoids lock contention by limiting background refreshes to specific events and caching results, though Knoble doesn’t propose this as a general solution for IDEs. His tone is conciliatory, and the discussion now shifts back to Junio C Hamano’s outstanding technical feedback on race-avoidance edge cases and the lock-order change in `git commit`. No new objections are raised here, but the broader debate over whether this is the right long-term solution remains unresolved.
