He cites a 2019 bugfix (`c14e5a1a50`) where `ssize_t` was narrower than `size_t` on certain platforms (e.g., NonStop), leading to similar overflow issues. Phillip has cc’d Randall S. Becker, the NonStop port maintainer, to confirm whether this remains a live issue on those platforms.

The review does not dispute the patch’s technical correctness or its defensive value—rejecting counts above `SSIZE_MAX` upfront is a clear hardening measure—but it challenges the commit message’s claim that such large counts "never" occur in practice. If `ssize_t` is narrower than `size_t` on some platforms, callers *could* inadvertently pass values exceeding `SSIZE_MAX`, making the overflow risk real rather than theoretical. The patch’s fallback definition of `SSIZE_MAX` (for platforms lacking it) and its `EINVAL` return on overflow are uncontroversial, but the broader assumption about caller behavior may need revisiting.

This exchange highlights a recurring tension in hardening patches: balancing defensive programming against the risk of over-engineering for edge cases that may not exist in practice.
