This is a performance discussion about the `test-grep-lint` check in Git’s test suite, which has become a noticeable bottleneck in the `make test` workflow. Jeff King (Peff) confirms the slowdown reported by Junio C Hamano, noting that `test-grep-lint` takes about 6.5 seconds on his machine—over 5% of the total test suite runtime—despite producing no output until it finishes. He suggests two potential optimizations: running the check in parallel (like `chainlint` does) or amortizing its parsing effort by combining it with other lint checks.

Peff also proposes a quick fix: a patch that skips further processing if the test script body doesn’t contain the word "grep," reducing runtime by about 50%. The patch is simple and pragmatic, though he acknowledges it might conflict with the broader goal of sharing parsing work across lint checks. The discussion is now focused on balancing immediate performance gains against longer-term architectural improvements. Contributors interested in test infrastructure or build performance may want to weigh in on the trade-offs.
