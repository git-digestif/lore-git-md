This is a **substantive review** of Jun Wu’s Patch 2/2 in the `core.indexLockTimeout` series. Jeff King (Peff) identifies a **pre-existing race condition** in `git commit`’s index-handling logic that the patch inadvertently widens, though it does not introduce the issue. The race occurs when `git commit` reads the index *before* taking the lock, then later discards updates made by other processes (e.g., `git add`) during the unlocked window. Peff’s instrumentation (`sleep(5)`) demonstrates the race: a concurrent `git add` can be silently clobbered if `git commit` is slow to acquire the lock.

The patch’s lock-order change (locking before reading) fixes this race, but Peff argues the commit message should **explicitly motivate the change as a correctness fix**, not just a timeout-enabling refactor. He also notes the trade-off: holding the lock longer may increase contention, though the timeout mechanism mitigates this. The review does not oppose the patch but calls for clearer documentation of the race and its resolution. No alternative implementation is proposed, but Peff hints at a possible "optimistic read" pattern (read without lock, then verify post-lock) that could reduce lock duration—though this would require deeper changes.

This feedback does not block the patch but raises the stakes: the series’ second patch is now tied to a latent bug, not just a feature enhancement. The thread’s unresolved questions (race-avoidance edge cases in Patch 1, default timeout value) remain, but this review adds a new dimension—**whether the lock-order change in `commit` is the right fix for the race, or if a more nuanced approach is needed**. Future discussion may pivot to this correctness issue rather than the timeout feature itself.
