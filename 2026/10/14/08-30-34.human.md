This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s eight-part CI housekeeping series, now in its third iteration. It repurposes the `linux-reftable` job to test reftable with SHA-256 (`GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`), addressing a gap in test coverage while preserving foreign-SCM interoperability (CVS, SVN, Perforce) in the renamed `linux-reftable-leaks` job. The change is purely mechanical—two one-line renames in `.github/workflows/main.yml` and `.gitlab-ci.yml`, plus a four-line addition to `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` to set the new environment variables—and has Junio C Hamano’s explicit endorsement.

The patch resolves a review concern from Junio about the v1 series, which proposed dropping the `linux-reftable` job entirely. By splitting coverage—SHA-256 + reftable in the new `linux-reftable-sha256` job, foreign-SCM interoperability in `linux-reftable-leaks`—the series avoids reducing test surface area. The trade-off is well-motivated: SHA-256 support is a long-term project, and reftable is now the default ref backend in Git 3.0, making their combination a logical focus for CI. The patch is uncontroversial and unlikely to draw further objections, though contributors invested in reftable (Patrick Steinhardt, Karthik Nayak) or SHA-256 (brian m. carlson) may weigh in on the coverage balance.

This is a CI/build system patch with no user-visible impact. The series as a whole reduces complexity (39 deletions, 20 insertions) while maintaining or improving coverage, and all changes pass linked CI runs. The only follow-up discussion likely to reference this patch will involve the renamed job (`linux-reftable-sha256`) or the reftable/SHA-256 coverage split.
