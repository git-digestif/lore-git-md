This is a cover letter for a five-patch series addressing integer overflows and unsafe arithmetic identified by Coverity in Git's codebase. The patches are part of Johannes Schindelin's ongoing effort to eliminate Coverity warnings in Git for Windows, though the fixes themselves are not Windows-specific and apply to upstream Git.

The series introduces new overflow-checking macros (`signed_mult_overflows` and `mult_overflows`) in `git-compat-util.h` and then uses them to harden several subsystems: `archive-tar` (against zero-length input), `http` (using `size_t` for byte positions), `wrapper` (rejecting implausibly large byte counts), and `date` (preventing overflow in `approxidate` relative-time calculations). The changes are small and targeted, touching only the specific code paths flagged by Coverity.

The motivation is defensive programming: preventing undefined behavior, crashes, or silent corruption when integer arithmetic exceeds type limits. The commit messages do not describe user-visible symptoms, suggesting these are latent issues rather than bugs that have been observed in the wild. The series builds on four previous Coverity-fix branches already merged to `master` between July and October 2026.

Files modified include `archive-tar.c`, `date.c`, `git-compat-util.h`, `http.c`, and `wrapper.c`. The new macros in `git-compat-util.h` are likely to be reused in future overflow-hardening efforts elsewhere in the codebase. No tests are included in this series, consistent with the pattern of prior Coverity-driven fixes. The patches are mechanical and appear uncontroversial, though the absence of test coverage for the new edge cases may draw review attention.
