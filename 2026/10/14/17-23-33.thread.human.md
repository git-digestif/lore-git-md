This thread is about a bugfix for Git’s pretty-printing subsystem, specifically the `%w()` format specifier used in `git log` and related commands. The issue is a long-standing regression where the first whitespace character after a hard line break (like `%n`) is incorrectly skipped, causing misaligned output in real-world scenarios such as wrapped commit messages or indented lists. The bug, introduced 18 years ago, was likely unintentional and undocumented, making it fair game for correction.

Andy Koppe submitted a v2 patch that surgically fixes the logic in `utf8.c` to distinguish between "line too long" (where skipping a space is correct) and "hard line break" (where it’s not). The patch also updates the documentation to clarify the corrected behavior and includes a new regression test. René Scharfe and Junio C Hamano both endorsed the fix, with Scharfe noting the backward-compatibility risk but ultimately supporting the change given the undocumented nature of the old behavior. Junio agreed, signaling the patch is ready for `next`.

The fix is narrowly scoped, well-tested, and addresses a practical readability issue without breaking on-disk formats. No open questions remain, and the thread appears converged. The patch is likely to proceed to integration soon.
