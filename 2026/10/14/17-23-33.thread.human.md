Andy Koppe's v2 patch fixes a long-standing regression in Git's `%w()` pretty-format specifier where the first whitespace character after a hard line break (`%n`) was incorrectly consumed, causing misaligned output in commands like `git log --format=%w(72)%B`. The fix distinguishes between automatic wrapping (where skipping a space is correct) and explicit hard breaks (where indentation must be preserved), and now includes the previously missing documentation update in `Documentation/pretty-formats.adoc`. Junio C Hamano and René Scharfe have both endorsed the approach, noting that the 18-year-old behavior was undocumented and likely unintentional, making the change acceptable now that the documentation gap is addressed.

Tanga Joris has since confirmed the fix resolves their specific case involving list indentation, but identified an edge case where consecutive hard breaks (`%w(20)aa%n%nbb`) could produce an unwanted leading space (" bb" instead of "bb") if the space-skipping logic is applied after a paragraph break. They propose adding a regression test comparing raw input against the formatted output to catch this, and suggest tightening the documentation wording to clarify that newlines are converted to spaces specifically "when wrapping," since width-zero formats like `%w(0,2,2)` preserve newlines verbatim. Apart from these two refinements, the reviewer considers the patch sound.

The series remains under review pending incorporation of Joris's edge-case test and documentation clarification.
