This is a **substantive review** of the `remote.<name>.refmap` fetch-mode series, now queued in `next`. Phillip Wood raises a fundamental design objection: he argues that overloading `remote.<name>.refmap` to control *both* ref-namespace mapping *and* which refs are fetched repeats the same inflexibility that the series aims to fix. Instead, he proposes a new `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` config key that would explicitly specify which refs to fetch, decoupling the "what" from the "where".

The review is technical and constructive. Phillip sketches a concrete alternative: `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` would accept patterns like `*@{upstream}` (fetch only the upstream branches of local branches on this remote) and support `+`/`-` prefixes for forced updates and negation. He also points out that Git’s current refspec-matching logic uses the *first* matching refspec rather than the *last*, which contradicts the usual "last-one-wins" config behavior and makes per-branch overrides impossible. His proposal would fix that inconsistency.

This is not a surface-level nit; it challenges the series’ core design. The objection is well-motivated and directly addresses the problem the series sets out to solve (shallow-repo fetch performance). Phillip’s alternative would preserve backward compatibility while offering more flexibility, and his critique of the refspec-matching order is a real technical wart.

The series is currently queued in `next` and has Junio’s blessing, but this review introduces a credible alternative that could require a redesign. The thread is now at a crossroads: either the current design proceeds (with Phillip’s objection noted but not addressed), or the series is revised to adopt `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` and the refspec-matching order fix. Either way, the discussion is now about the right long-term architecture, not just implementation details.
