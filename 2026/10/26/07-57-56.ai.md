This is a governance discussion about the Git project's proposed community voting process, specifically the eligibility criteria for voters. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King's empirical data on contributor commit counts by emphasizing the need to balance inclusivity with the risk of skewing votes toward either long-term contributors or newcomers.

Patrick agrees with Peff’s approach of using data to inform the threshold but argues that the decision should also consider the *shape* of the voting population, not just the raw number of voters. He notes that long-term contributors (often employed to work on Git) might dominate if the threshold is too low, while newcomers could skew the vote if the bar is set too low. He suggests a middle ground—perhaps 25 or 30 commits with a 2- or 3-year activity window—as a compromise, though he acknowledges his own bias as a long-term contributor and admits there is no "correct" value.

The email advances the discussion by framing the threshold as a trade-off between oldtimers and newcomers, adding a qualitative dimension to Peff’s quantitative data. It does not propose a specific number but reinforces the need for a balanced approach. Key technical details for future discussion include the commit-counting methodology (e.g., whether to count patch series or individual commits) and the definition of "recent activity" (e.g., any commit in the last N years).
