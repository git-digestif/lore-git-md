This is the author's reply to the maintainer's review of the final patch in a seven-patch series refactoring Git's `rerere` subsystem to remove its dependency on the global `the_repository` variable. The series is part of the ongoing `the_repository` removal effort and is now cooking in Junio's integration branch.

Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano's minor design curiosity about whether the `rerere_cache_enabled` field added to `struct repository` in patch 4/7 could have been replaced by the new `rerere_cache` struct. Patrick explains his rationale for caching the value in the repository itself rather than the cache struct, noting that checking for the existence of `.git/rr-cache` and reading the `rerere.enabled` config on every call to `rerere_cache_setup()` would introduce unnecessary overhead. He acknowledges this may be premature optimization and proposes a patch that removes the caching entirely, deferring the decision to the config lookup and filesystem check on each call.

The proposed patch removes the `rerere_cache_enabled` field from `struct repository`, initializes a local `enabled` variable in `is_rerere_enabled()`, and returns its value directly. This simplifies the code by eliminating the cached state but reintroduces the redundant config and filesystem checks. Patrick leaves the final decision to Junio, framing it as a trade-off between code cleanliness and perceived performance.

This exchange is a low-stakes design discussion about API hygiene and state management. The proposed change is small and mechanical, and the series as a whole remains on track for integration. The patch does not introduce new behavior or regressions; it is a refactoring detail that affects internal state organization. No other reviewers have weighed in, and the discussion is unlikely to derail the series. The key takeaway for future discussion is whether the caching of `rerere.enabled` is justified or whether the simpler, stateless approach should be preferred.
