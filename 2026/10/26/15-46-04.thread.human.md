This thread is about a performance optimization for `git pack-objects` introduced by Marc Strapetz. The series adds a new `--inspect-have <commit>` option that allows the sender to inspect root trees of commits already known to the receiver, identifying shared objects without walking their commit histories. This avoids packing objects the receiver already has, even when those commits are excluded from the revision walk as boundary commits.

The second patch in the three-part series implements this by comparing root trees of outgoing tips with those of receiver-known commits using Git's existing sparse tree marking machinery. If a subtree is identical or exists only on the receiver side, it is skipped. The patch updates `builtin/pack-objects.c` to parse the new option, resolve the commits, and mark their trees as uninteresting before the main revision walk. It also extends the ODB pack-generation pipeline to carry these commits and updates the source-files backend to emit the new option when generating pack input. The implementation handles edge cases carefully, disabling the optimization in partial clones and when bitmaps are used, and includes thorough test coverage.

So far, the patch has been well-received as technically sound and well-motivated, with clear explanations in the commit message and comprehensive handling of edge cases. The series is at its first iteration (`v1`), and no reviews or objections have been recorded yet. The optimization appears to offer a genuine performance improvement for clients pushing to servers that already know some of the history.
