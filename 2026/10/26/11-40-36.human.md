This is the third patch in Miklos Vajna’s four-part series improving `git log --follow` performance for rename tracking in merge-heavy histories. The patch refactors the rename-following logic in `log-tree.c` to eliminate redundant diff calculations and fix two bugs: one where `--follow` with `-m` lost history on the second parent of a merge, and another where the path was not properly restored between parent diffs. The changes are preparatory for the performance optimization in patch 4, but this patch itself does not yet claim a speedup.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is substantive but brief. He identifies the patch’s dual purpose—bugfix and refactoring—and notes that the commit message could better separate and explain the two bugs and the performance regression being addressed. His feedback suggests the patch might be easier to review if split into smaller, issue-specific changes, though he acknowledges this may not be practical. The only surface-level nit is a style inconsistency in brace usage. No benchmark data or deeper technical objections are raised, but the review implies the patch’s structure and motivation could be clearer. The series remains under active review, with this patch central to the overall goal.
