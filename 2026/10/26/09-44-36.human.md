This is a **substantive design review** of the first patch in Harald Nordgren’s v10 series introducing `remote.<name>.refmap`. Phillip Wood raises a **fundamental objection** to the current design, arguing that overloading `remote.<name>.refmap` to control both ref-namespace mapping *and* which refs are fetched repeats the same inflexibility the series aims to fix.

Phillip proposes renaming the configuration key to `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` to clarify its scope and allow future extension to `remote.<name>.pushRefmap`. More importantly, he questions whether the current design adequately separates the "what to fetch" logic from the "where to store it" logic. His critique centers on the interaction between `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap`: the documentation for `--refmap` states that when `--refmap` is given, the refspec config is ignored, but the patch’s behavior is ambiguous about whether `remote.<name>.refmap` similarly overrides `remote.<name>.fetch`. The commit message’s explanation of this interaction is unclear, and Phillip finds the paragraph describing the fallback logic ("A refspec-less fetch with a refmap configured...") unintelligible.

The review also points out minor documentation issues: redundant links to `git-fetch(1)` and a sentence that adds no new information ("the same way `remote.<name>.fetch` provides the default refspecs to fetch"). These are surface-level but reinforce the broader concern about clarity and precision in the design.

This is **not a nitpick review**—Phillip’s objection goes to the heart of whether the series solves the problem it sets out to address. The thread is now at a crossroads: the current design could proceed (with Phillip’s objection noted but not addressed), or the series could be revised to adopt a cleaner separation of concerns, possibly using a new `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` key as Phillip suggested in earlier feedback. The maintainer’s "What's cooking" report still lists the series as queued in `next`, but this review may prompt a re-evaluation before graduation to `master`.
