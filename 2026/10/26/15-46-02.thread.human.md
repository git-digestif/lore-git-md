This thread is about a patch series from Marc Strapetz that optimizes `git send-pack` to avoid re-uploading large unchanged blobs when force-pushing rebased feature branches. The series introduces a new `--inspect-have` option in `pack-objects` to mark the old branch tip's root tree as "have" without walking its history, aiming to reduce redundant transfers for small rewrites.

The initial submission (v1, posted July 10, 2025) faced a fundamental question from Junio Hamano: whether the series reinvents Git's existing push negotiation mechanism rather than building on it. Marc has now responded, clarifying that the series targets a narrower optimization within Git's *default* packing behavior for forced non-fast-forward updates, avoiding the latency of negotiation rounds or the performance trade-offs of `--objects-edge-aggressive`. He argues that the new approach improves the default path without protocol changes or commit history walks, citing historical context (a 2014 regression with `--objects-edge-aggressive`) to justify bypassing negotiation.

The series remains under review, with no maintainer decision yet. Key technical details include the `--inspect-have` option, a new `odb_has_object_file()` helper, and conditional logic restricting the optimization to forced updates of existing `refs/heads/` branches. The author has provided micro-benchmarks showing substantial pack-size and wall-clock reductions for small rewrites, but the worst-case 8% packing-time overhead for widespread changes remains a point of discussion. No other reviewers have weighed in yet. The next step is likely Junio's assessment of whether Marc's rationale addresses the original concern.
