This thread is about a patch series from Marc Strapetz that optimizes `git send-pack` to avoid re-uploading large unchanged blobs when force-pushing rebased feature branches. The series introduces a new `--inspect-have` option in `pack-objects` that lets `send-pack` mark the old branch tip’s root tree as "have" without walking its history, reducing redundant transfers for forced updates. The optimization is opt-in and narrowly scoped to existing branches where the old tip is locally available, avoiding regressions in shallow repositories or partial clones.

Marc argues this is a lighter-weight alternative to existing push negotiation mechanisms, which either add latency or were disabled by default due to performance trade-offs. Micro-benchmarks show substantial improvements for small rewrites (e.g., a single-file reword drops from 2.776 ms to 1.338 ms), but there’s an acknowledged 8% worst-case packing-time overhead for widespread changes. The series includes thorough test coverage, with 62 new tests across two scripts.

Junio C Hamano initially questioned whether the series is redundant with existing push negotiation, and that question remains unresolved. Marc has clarified the series’ narrower scope and invited feedback from Taylor Blau and Derrick Stolee on pack generation and revision traversal. The thread is still under review, with no maintainer decision or additional reviewer feedback yet. The key open question is whether the optimization’s benefits outweigh its overhead and whether it’s distinct enough from existing mechanisms to justify inclusion.
