This is a governance discussion about the proposed community voting process for Git, specifically the eligibility threshold for voters. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Tuomas Ahola’s observation that the original threshold suggested at the Contributor Summit (50 merged patches) was significantly higher than the thresholds currently under discussion (e.g., Junio’s proposal of five commits).

Peff clarifies that the number 50 was an off-the-cuff suggestion and not a carefully considered recommendation. He provides data on the distribution of commit counts among contributors, showing how many people would qualify under different thresholds (200, 50, 10, or 1 commits) and time windows (1, 2, 3, or 5 years of recent activity). The table illustrates the trade-off between inclusivity and practicality: lower thresholds or shorter time windows include more contributors but may also increase administrative overhead or the risk of "drive-by" participation. Peff suggests that the project could use this data to pick a threshold that balances these concerns, though he does not endorse a specific number or time window.

The email advances the discussion by grounding it in empirical data, but it does not resolve the core tension between ensuring voters have sufficient context (e.g., brian m. carlson’s concern about "serious contributors") and avoiding overly restrictive criteria (e.g., Junio’s preference for simplicity). The thread remains open, with no consensus on the threshold or whether to include time-based cutoffs. Key technical details for future discussion include the commit-counting methodology (e.g., whether to count patch series or individual commits) and the definition of "recent activity" (e.g., any commit in the last N years).
