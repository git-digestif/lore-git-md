This is the author's response to Junio's observation that the series may be reinventing the existing push negotiation mechanism. Marc Strapetz clarifies the scope and intent of the patch series, positioning it as an optimization of Git's *default* packing behavior for forced non-fast-forward updates, rather than an alternative to negotiation.

The response distinguishes the series from two existing optimizations: `push.negotiate` (which adds protocol round trips) and `--objects-edge-aggressive` (which recursively marks trees and was deemed too slow for non-shallow repositories). The new `--inspect-have` option avoids both the latency of negotiation and the overhead of recursive tree marking by limiting inspection to the old tip of the branch being force-pushed and using sparse tree comparison to prune unchanged subtrees. The optimization is narrowly scoped to forced updates of existing branches with locally available old tips, leaving fast-forward pushes and shallow repositories unchanged.

The explanation is well-supported with references to the 2014 series that introduced `--objects-edge-aggressive` and the performance trade-offs that led to its limited adoption. The author argues that the new approach avoids the protocol cost of negotiation and the performance regression of aggressive tree marking, while still addressing the specific case of large blobs retained across a rebase. The response does not yet address whether reviewers find this trade-off acceptable, but it provides a clearer rationale for why the series is not redundant with existing mechanisms. The thread remains open, with the next step likely being further review or maintainer feedback on the revised scope.
