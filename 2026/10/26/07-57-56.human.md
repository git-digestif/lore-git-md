This is a governance discussion about the Git project’s proposed community voting process, specifically the eligibility criteria for voters. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King’s empirical data on contributor commit counts, weighing the trade-offs between inclusivity and ensuring voters have meaningful experience with the project.

Patrick agrees with Peff’s approach of using data to inform the threshold but argues that the decision should also consider the *composition* of the voting population, not just the raw number of voters. He notes that a low commit threshold (e.g., 10 commits) risks skewing the vote toward newcomers, while a high threshold (e.g., 50+ commits) could disproportionately favor long-term contributors—many of whom are employed to work on Git. His concern reflects a broader tension in open-source governance: balancing fresh perspectives against institutional knowledge. He suggests a middle ground (e.g., 25–50 commits with a 2–3 year activity window) but acknowledges there’s no objectively "correct" threshold.

The email advances the discussion by framing the threshold as a deliberate balance of interests rather than a purely mechanical decision. It doesn’t resolve the debate but clarifies the stakes: the project must decide whether to prioritize inclusivity (lower bar) or experience (higher bar). Patrick’s bias as a "oldtimer" is transparent, which helps contextualize his preference for a higher threshold. The thread remains open, with no consensus yet on the final criteria.
