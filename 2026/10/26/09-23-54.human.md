This is a governance discussion about the Git project's proposed community voting process, specifically refining the eligibility criteria for voters. Tuomas Ahola joins the thread to propose a data-driven middle ground for the commit-count threshold, suggesting a range of 25 to 30 commits based on the average number of commits per contributor (approximately 25.7).

Tuomas’s contribution is brief but substantive. He calculates the average number of commits per contributor (excluding merges) using `git shortlog -s --no-merges` and finds it to be around 25.7. This aligns closely with Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier suggestion of 25 or 30 commits as a middle ground between oldtimers and newcomers. Tuomas frames this as a "nice" number, though he acknowledges his argument isn’t particularly strong. His proposal doesn’t resolve the broader debate but adds a concrete, empirical anchor to the discussion, making it easier for the thread to converge on a specific threshold.

The thread remains focused on balancing inclusivity and practicality.
