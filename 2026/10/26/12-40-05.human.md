This is the final patch in Julia Evans’s six-part documentation series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s merge-conflict resolution guidance. The series is now fully approved, with Junio’s `Reviewed-by:` covering all patches, and has been merged to `next` for cooking.

This patch updates `git-pull.adoc` to replace a single redundant line of conflict-resolution advice with a cross-reference to the new `gitmergeconflicts(7)` guide. The change is minimal: it removes the generic instruction to use `--abort` and instead points users to the comprehensive guide, where they will find detailed explanations of conflict markers, resolution workflows, and command-specific options. The patch preserves the existing mention of `--abort` for both merge and rebase, ensuring users still have immediate access to the escape hatch without cluttering the `git-pull` man page with duplicated advice.

The series as a whole is a well-crafted, user-focused improvement. It surgically removes scattered and often inconsistent conflict-resolution sections from five existing man pages (`git-merge`, `git-rebase`, `git-revert`, `git-cherry-pick`, and now `git-pull`), replacing them with a single, example-driven guide that clarifies concepts like "ours" vs. "theirs," demonstrates `diff3` conflict markers, and provides practical resolution workflows. The new guide is purely additive—no behavior changes—and has been thoroughly vetted across five iterations, incorporating feedback from Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, and D. Ben Knoble, among others.

For readers following Git’s documentation improvements, this series is a clear win: it reduces duplication, improves clarity, and makes conflict resolution guidance more discoverable. The only loose ends are minor follow-up ideas (e.g., enhancing conflict markers with commit details, revisiting `zdiff3` documentation), but these are explicitly deferred and do not detract from the series’ readiness. If you care about Git’s usability, this is worth noting as a model for how to consolidate and improve scattered documentation.
