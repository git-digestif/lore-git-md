This is a **design objection** in the ongoing `remote.<name>.refmap` thread, now cooking in `next`. Phillip Wood explicitly rejects Junio C Hamano’s example configuration that combines `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap`, arguing that the series’ current design repeats the same inflexibility it aims to fix. His objection is fundamental: the new `refmap` configuration overloading both *which refs to fetch* and *where to store them* conflates two distinct concerns, making it impossible to cleanly separate them or support per-branch overrides.

Phillip’s position is not new—he previously proposed `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` as a dedicated configuration for *what to fetch*, leaving `refmap` to handle only *where to store*. His reply here is brief but pointed: he sees Junio’s example as evidence that the current design is flawed, not as a valid use case. The objection is substantive and directly targets the series’ core goal of improving shallow-repository fetch performance by dynamically selecting branches.
