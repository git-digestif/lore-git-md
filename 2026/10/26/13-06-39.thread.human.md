This thread is about a documentation cleanup proposed by Julia Evans to address an inconsistency in Git's glossary. The current glossary defines "object name" as the canonical term for an object's hash identifier (SHA-1/SHA-256), but in practice, the term is used ambiguously across the documentation—sometimes referring to the hash, other times to human-readable names like branch names or refs. Meanwhile, "object ID" is often used specifically for the hash. Julia's patch updates the glossary to make "object ID" the canonical term for the hash and clarifies that "object name" is context-dependent and may refer to either the hash or a human-readable name.

The change is small and focused, touching only `Documentation/glossary-content.adoc` to update definitions for "hash," "object identifier," "object name," "ref," and "SHA-1." The commit message references a 2020 suggestion from Junio Hamano to make a similar change, indicating this has been a long-standing issue. So far, there’s no controversy in the thread, though future discussion might explore whether "object ID" is the best term or if a broader documentation audit is needed to ensure consistency across all man pages. The patch itself is well-motivated and should help reduce confusion for readers.
