This is a governance discussion about the proposed community voting process for Git, specifically the eligibility threshold for voters. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Tuomas Ahola’s observation that the original threshold suggested at the Contributor Summit (50 merged patches) was significantly higher than the five-commit proposal now under discussion.

Peff clarifies that the number 50 was an off-the-cuff suggestion and not a carefully considered recommendation. He provides data on the distribution of commit counts among contributors, showing how many people would qualify under different thresholds (200, 50, 10, or 1 commit) and time windows (1, 2, 3, or 5 years of recent activity). For example, 133 people have at least 50 commits, while 439 have at least 10. He also introduces a hybrid approach: requiring both a minimum total commit count *and* at least one commit in the last N years, which narrows the pool further (e.g., 47 people with 50+ commits and activity in the last 2 years).

The email does not advocate for a specific threshold but offers the data as a tool for tuning the parameters. It advances the discussion by grounding the debate in concrete numbers, making it easier for the community to weigh trade-offs between inclusivity and practicality. The hybrid approach (total commits + recent activity) is a new idea that could address concerns about "drive-by" contributions while still allowing long-term but less frequent contributors to participate. No decisions are made here, but the data will likely inform future proposals.
