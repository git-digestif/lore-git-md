This is a **maintainer response** in the `remote.<name>.refmap` series, now cooking in `next`. Junio C Hamano acknowledges Phillip Wood’s design objection and **endorses the need for a dedicated `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` configuration key** to separate "what to fetch" from "where to store it". The email clarifies that the current v11 implementation (which overloads `remote.<name>.refmap` for both purposes) is **not inherently broken**—it correctly handles edge cases like configuring both `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap`—but **repeats the same inflexibility the series aims to fix**. Junio’s example (`remote.<name>.fetch = master next seen` + `remote.<name>.refmap = +refs/heads/*:refs/remotes/cabal/*`) demonstrates the problem: users must duplicate logic across two keys to achieve fine-grained control, and the design cannot support per-branch overrides or clean refspec-matching semantics.

The response **resolves the design direction** for v12: the series will introduce `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` to explicitly specify which refs to fetch (e.g., `*@{upstream}`), while `remote.<name>.refmap` reverts to its original role of namespace mapping only. Junio emphasizes that the **precedence rule** (`remote.<name>.fetch` > `remote.<name>.fetchRefs` > dynamic branch enumeration) will remain, and forced updates (`+`) will belong on the refmap side (the "where"). The email also **validates the v11 implementation’s correctness** for edge cases, but the series is **blocked from graduating to `master`** until the redesign is implemented. Key technical details for v12: `remote.c`/`remote.h` will add `fetchRefs` parsing, adjust precedence rules, and rework `branches_tracking_remote()` to integrate with the new key; documentation and tests will be updated accordingly. No timeline for v12 is given, but the maintainer’s endorsement suggests it will be prioritized. Harald Nordgren has not yet responded to the design discussion.
