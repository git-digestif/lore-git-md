## 2026/10/27

- [2026/10/27/06-36-58 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt agreed with merging the compound-literals topic ("Yup, agreed"), asked which two C99 constructs remain unusable, and surveyed newer C standards: he finds C11's `_static_assert()` interesting (current static asserts are "extremely hacky" with poor error messages), calls C17 uninteresting and C23 too new to target. He also raised the idea that compound literals could let `STRBUF_INIT` be defined as `((struct strbuf) { ... })` so `buf = STRBUF_INIT;` compiles at a later point, making `strbuf_init()` essentially obsolete — while explicitly not proposing a crusade to replace every site, and asking whether that is a direction worth moving toward.
- [2026/10/27/10-49-28 by Phillip Wood <phillip.wood123@gmail.com>] Phillip Wood answered that the two remaining C99 constructs are the "%z"/"%zu" format specifiers (MinGW) and ".a.b = c" struct initializers (IBM xlc), noting he cc'd Johannes to check whether UCRT64 changes affect format-specifier support. He agreed `static_assert()` would be nice, suggested C11 atomics might reduce undefined behavior in signal handling but felt they are optional, and asked whether `_Generic` macros would be useful anywhere. On the `STRBUF_INIT` idea he said converting it to a compound literal would match what the reftable tempfile test balloon did and is probably worth doing for new code, while noting `strbuf_init()` takes a pre-allocation size that 79 of its callers pass as 0 (with a grep tally of the other hardcoded values), and agreed a uniform initialization method for stack- and heap-allocated structs would be nice.
