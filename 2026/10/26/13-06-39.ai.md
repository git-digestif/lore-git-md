This is a documentation patch that proposes a terminology cleanup in Git's glossary. Julia Evans identifies an inconsistency in the current documentation: the glossary defines "object name" as the canonical term for an object's SHA-1/SHA-256 identifier, but many parts of the documentation use "object name" to mean *any* name for an object (e.g., branch names, refs, or even human-readable labels), while using "object ID" to refer specifically to the hash. The patch updates the glossary to make "object ID" the canonical term for the hash identifier and adds a warning that "object name" is context-dependent and not always synonymous.

The change touches only `Documentation/glossary-content.adoc`, updating the definitions of "hash", "object identifier", "object name", "ref", and "SHA-1" to consistently point to "object ID" for the hash. The new definition of "object name" explicitly acknowledges its ambiguity and warns readers that the term may refer to either an object ID or a human-readable name, depending on context. The commit message references a 2020 suggestion from Junio Hamano to make a similar change, indicating this is not a new concern.

The patch is well-motivated and addresses a real source of confusion for readers. The diff is small and focused, and the commit message clearly explains the problem and the rationale for the chosen solution. No controversy is evident from the patch itself, though follow-up discussion might explore whether "object ID" is the best term or whether a broader documentation audit is needed to align all man pages with the new glossary definition.
