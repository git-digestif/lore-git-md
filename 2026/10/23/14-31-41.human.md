This is a governance discussion refining the proposed community voting process for Git. Kristoffer Haugsbakk replies to Junio C Hamano’s pushback on Phillip Wood’s suggestion to limit voting eligibility to contributors active in the past two years. The exchange focuses on whether inactive but historically significant contributors should have a voice in contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy.

Kristoffer argues that the practical risk of "dogpiling" — outsiders flooding a vote to sway the outcome — is negligible when eligibility is restricted to past contributors. He points out that orchestrating a coordinated effort among former contributors (e.g., those who fixed a bug or typo years ago) would be logistically difficult and unlikely to succeed. His response reinforces Phillip’s original concern: while inactive contributors might care about the project, their engagement in a vote could be superficial or manipulated, and the administrative burden of verifying their eligibility (e.g., handling outdated email addresses) outweighs the benefit. He frames the two-year activity window as a pragmatic safeguard rather than an exclusionary measure, noting that it filters out low-effort participation without disenfranchising genuinely invested contributors.

The discussion remains unresolved but clarifies the trade-offs: Junio prioritizes inclusivity and respect for past contributions, while Kristoffer and Phillip emphasize practicality and the risk of noise in high-stakes votes. No new technical details or changes to the patch are proposed; this is purely a conceptual debate about governance design. The thread’s next steps will likely hinge on whether Junio’s preference for a low, flat eligibility threshold (e.g., five commits) or Phillip’s more nuanced criteria (e.g., substantive reviews, two-year window) gains broader support.
