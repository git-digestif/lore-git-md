This is the second patch in Phillip Wood’s seven-patch refactoring series `pw/stash-optimize-check_changes-calls/v4`, which is now cooking in `next` and on track for `master`. The series targets redundant index operations in `git stash push` and `git stash create`, eliminating unnecessary `lstat()` calls and untracked-file walks.

This patch removes a duplicate change-detection call in `create_stash()`. Before this change, `git stash create` checked for unstaged or uncommitted changes twice: once in `create_stash()` (before refreshing the index) and again in `do_create_stash()` (after refreshing). The earlier check was unreliable because it ran before the index was refreshed, and its removal simplifies the logic without affecting behavior. The patch also removes an unnecessary `repo_read_index()` call from `check_changes_tracked_files()`, since all remaining callers now refresh the index before invoking it.

The change is a straightforward cleanup—no user-visible behavior changes, just fewer redundant operations. The patch is well-motivated in the commit message, which explains why the duplicate check was both unnecessary and unreliable. This is a small but clear step toward the series’ goal of reducing overhead in stash operations. If you care about performance or code hygiene in the stash subsystem, this patch (and the series as a whole) is worth noting.
