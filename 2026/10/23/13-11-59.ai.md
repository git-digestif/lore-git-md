This is the **final patch (6/6) in a six-patch performance feature series** introducing an **auto mode** for Git's connectivity check during fetches and pushes. The patch implements a **three-mode config design** (`full`/`incremental`/`auto[=<n>]`) with a **configurable 1,000-commit cutoff**, dynamically switching between the incremental and full connectivity checks based on the size of the incoming history.

The auto mode addresses the trade-off between the incremental check's per-commit overhead and the full check's fixed cost. For small incoming histories (≤1,000 commits by default), it uses the incremental check, which is significantly faster for small pushes to large repositories (up to 190x speedup, with memory usage reduced from 3.4 GB to 14 MB for a single commit). For larger incoming histories, it falls back to the full check, avoiding the incremental mode's regression (1.5x slower for 10,000+ commits). The cutoff is conservative but effective across repository sizes, including large monorepos, and can be customized with `auto=<n>`.

The implementation is minimal and self-contained, touching only the necessary files: `builtin/rev-list.c` (adds auto-mode logic, counts incoming commits, and switches modes), `connected.c` (plumbs the auto mode and configurable cutoff through the config system), `Documentation/config/transfer.adoc` (documents the new `auto` and `auto=<cutoff>` values), and `Documentation/rev-list-options.adoc` (updates the internal flag documentation). The test suite (`t/t5413-connectivity-check.sh`) is extended to verify the auto mode's fallback behavior, including edge cases like malformed cutoffs and pushes into empty repositories.

The patch is **well-motivated and well-executed**, with clear benchmarks (included in the commit message) showing the auto mode's effectiveness across real-world repositories. The design is conservative (opt-in, fallback to full check for edge cases) and thoroughly tested. The series as a whole is **ready for integration**, with all prior feedback addressed and no new blockers introduced. The auto mode completes the feature, making the incremental connectivity check practical for users who occasionally receive large pushes.
