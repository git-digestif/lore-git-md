This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-part refactoring series that removes the `rerere` subsystem’s implicit dependency on the global `the_repository`. The series is part of the ongoing `the_repository` removal effort.

Patch 7/7 eliminates the last vestige of global state by replacing the global `rerere_cache` variable with an explicit struct that callers must set up and tear down. The patch introduces two new public functions—`rerere_cache_setup()` and `rerere_cache_release()`—and updates every caller in `builtin/rerere.c` and `rerere.c` to manage the cache’s lifetime manually. The lock file (`write_lock`) is now owned by the cache struct and rolled back in `rerere_cache_release()`, ensuring no stale locks remain. The only exception is `rerere_gc_needed()`, which still initializes a minimal cache on the stack because it only needs the `dirs` strmap and does not touch the lock or autoupdate fields.

The change is purely mechanical and does not alter user-visible behavior. It completes the series’ goal of making `rerere` fully repository-agnostic, enabling it to be used concurrently across multiple repositories without leaking state. The patch is well-scoped and fits cleanly into the broader `the_repository` removal work. Reviewers will likely focus on verifying that the new lifetime management does not introduce leaks or regressions in `rerere`’s locking and caching logic.
