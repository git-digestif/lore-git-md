This is a documentation and terminology cleanup series that aligns Git’s glossary, man pages, code comments, and tests with the 2024 redefinition of "pseudoref" (now limited to FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD) and the introduction of "root ref" for refs like HEAD and ORIG_HEAD. The series is under review and has received generally positive feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt, though some glossary phrasing and man page details remain under discussion.

Julia Evans, the author of `gitdatamodel.adoc`, suggests expanding its "References" section to cover pseudorefs and root refs more explicitly. Currently, the document only briefly mentions ORIG_HEAD as an example of a reference outside the `refs/` hierarchy, which may leave readers unclear about the distinction between pseudorefs and root refs. This is a reasonable follow-up to Andy Koppe’s series, as `gitdatamodel.adoc` is a key pedagogical resource for understanding Git’s internals. The suggestion doesn’t challenge the series’ direction but highlights an opportunity to reinforce the terminology in another part of the documentation.

The series itself is well-scoped and uncontroversial, focusing on consistency rather than behavioral changes. It touches 21 files, including the glossary, man pages (`git-update-ref`, `git-worktree`, etc.), built-in commands (`for-each-ref`), refs internals (`refs.h`, `revision.c`), and test scripts. The only deliberate omission is leaving `__git_pseudoref_exists()` in the completion script unchanged (except for its comment), due to ambiguity about what term would cover both pseudorefs and root refs. Junio’s feedback on the glossary’s "pseudoref" definition (omitting "different semantics") and Patrick’s suggestions for improving the "per-worktree ref" entry and man page details are the main open points, but these are editorial rather than technical.

For a busy reader, this series is worth tracking if you care about Git’s documentation clarity or work with refs internals. The changes are mechanical but thorough, and the feedback so far suggests it’s on track for eventual merging. Julia’s suggestion about `gitdatamodel.adoc` is a natural extension of the effort and could be addressed in a follow-up patch.
