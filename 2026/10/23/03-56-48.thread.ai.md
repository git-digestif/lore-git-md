Core problem or goal
Replace manual integer arithmetic in `diff-delta.c:create_delta_index()` with the safe integer helpers `st_mult()`, `st_add()`, and `st_add3()` to prevent silent overflows during allocation size calculations. The patch is now justified solely as a precautionary measure against hypothetical future changes in struct layout or bounds, after Pablo Sabater’s repeated analyses refuted the theoretical overflow risk under current conditions. The commit message’s arithmetic has been corrected in response to Junio’s and Pablo’s feedback, but Pablo’s latest reply identifies three remaining factual errors: (1) `struct unpacked_index_entry` is 12 bytes on 32-bit systems (not 16), as `inner entry` is not a separate field but a combination of `ptr` and `val`; (2) `entries` is capped by `0xfffffffeU / RABIN_WINDOW` (not `0xffffffu`), a stricter bound; and (3) `hsize` is explicitly capped at `2²⁶` (not vaguely "appropriately"). These corrections further tighten the bounds analysis, making overflow even more implausible under current conditions.

- Bounds: `entries` capped at `0xfffffffeU / RABIN_WINDOW` (≈268,435,455), `hsize` at `2²⁶` (67,108,864), ensuring `hsize + 1` cannot overflow. Pablo’s latest corrections tighten these bounds further.
- Struct layout: `struct unpacked_index_entry` is **12 bytes** on 32-bit systems (4 bytes each for `ptr`, `val`, and `next` pointer; no padding), per Pablo Sabater’s repeated analyses. The commit message’s claim of 16 bytes is incorrect.
- Allocation: `4 * hsize + 12 * entries` cannot exceed 2³² under actual bounds, per Pablo’s counter-analysis. The patch’s justification now rests entirely on hypothetical future changes.
- Justification: precautionary future-proofing against hypothetical changes in struct layout or bounds, with no demonstrated exploit under current conditions.
