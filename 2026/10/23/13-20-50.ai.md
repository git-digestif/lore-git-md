This is the **v2 cover letter** for a five-patch series improving `git stash` shell completion, with **refactoring**, a **Zsh-specific bugfix**, and the **removal of deprecated `git stash save` completion and documentation**. The series is from D. Ben Knoble and is now in its second iteration after feedback from Patrick Steinhardt.

The v2 updates are minor but clarify key points. The commit messages have been reworded for precision, particularly around the behavior of `git stash save` completion. The author now explicitly documents that while `save` is removed from the list of completable subcommands (so `git stash <TAB>` no longer shows it), option autocompletion for manually typed `save` (e.g., `git stash save --<TAB>`) still works, and this behavior is independent of the `GIT_COMPLETION_SHOW_ALL` setting. This addresses Patrick’s earlier design question and removes ambiguity about what the patch actually changes.

The series remains focused on three goals: (1) **refactoring** the stash subcommand completion logic to reduce duplication and add the previously omitted `store` subcommand to completion candidates (patch 1), (2) **fixing a Zsh-specific bug** where stash names like `stash@{0}` were not handled correctly due to Zsh’s brace expansion behavior (patch 3), and (3) **removing support for the deprecated `git stash save`** from both documentation (patch 4) and completion (patch 5). The latter two patches are still framed as conversation starters rather than demands, and the author is open to dropping them if they prove too controversial.

The Zsh bugfix (patch 3) is the most technically substantive change. It removes the `-Q` flag in `compadd` to ensure metacharacters like braces are quoted (e.g., `stash@\{0\}` instead of `stash@{0}`), which is a real usability improvement for Zsh users. The author confirms Bash is unaffected by the bug and that no portable solution exists, so the Zsh-specific override is the only viable fix for now. The change to the displayed format (e.g., `stash@\{0\}`) is a side effect of the fix, not the goal.

The series touches four files: `contrib/completion/git-completion.bash`, `contrib/completion/git-completion.zsh`, `Documentation/git-stash.adoc`, and `builtin/stash.c`. No tests are added or modified, and there are no on-disk format changes. The patches are small, focused, and unlikely to break existing workflows, though the removal of `save` completion may affect users who still rely on the deprecated subcommand.

The author also includes a note about their patch-generation workflow, leaving the exact `git format-patch` command in the cover letter as a potential aid for new contributors. This is a minor but practical detail that could be helpful for those learning the mailing list workflow.
