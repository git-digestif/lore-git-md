This is a governance process discussion, part of the ongoing thread about Emily Shaffer’s proposal to define a structured voting process for the Git project. Junio C Hamano responds directly to Justin Tobler’s concerns about eligibility criteria, pushing back against the idea of introducing complexity or temporal filters.

Junio argues that the project should keep the voting rules simple and egalitarian: one person, one vote, with a straightforward eligibility rule. He warns that attempts to weight influence by recency or volume of contributions—such as excluding dormant contributors or granting more votes to recent or prolific participants—would lead to an overly elaborate system that invites accusations of unfairness. His position is pragmatic: simplicity reduces ambiguity and makes the process harder to challenge on procedural grounds.

The email does not propose a specific eligibility threshold, but it reinforces the direction established earlier in the thread: a binary, easy-to-explain standard (e.g., "two or more topics in a released version of Git") rather than a sliding scale. Junio’s stance shifts the focus away from Justin’s concerns about corporate bias or dormant contributors, framing the discussion instead around process clarity and perceived fairness. This reply may help narrow the debate, but it leaves the core tension unresolved: whether a simple, one-vote-per-eligible-contributor rule can adequately address the community’s concerns about representation and influence.
