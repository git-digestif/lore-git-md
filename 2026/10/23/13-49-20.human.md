He also acknowledges the administrative burden of managing email changes for inactive contributors but sees this as a necessary trade-off for security.

Junio’s earlier pushback on the activity window—arguing that past contributors who still care about the project should have a voice—remains unresolved. Phillip counters that the proposed voting system would automatically notify eligible voters, making it easy for even inactive contributors to participate if they choose. He also emphasizes the risk of outsiders derailing the process, a concern that Junio does not directly address in this reply.

On the counting mechanism, Phillip is flexible between commit count and patch series, as long as the threshold isn’t too low. He agrees that the exact duration of the activity window (e.g., two years vs. one) is less important than the principle of limiting eligibility to recent participants. The discussion now centers on balancing inclusivity (allowing engaged but inactive contributors) with practicality (simplifying administration and preventing abuse).
