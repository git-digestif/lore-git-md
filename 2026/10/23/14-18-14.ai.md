This is a governance discussion refining the proposed community voting process for Git, specifically the eligibility criteria for voters. Kristoffer Haugsbakk joins the thread to reinforce Phillip Wood’s argument for a two-year activity window, framing it as a safeguard against "drive-by" participation in contentious votes like the AI contribution policy. He draws a parallel to the 2020 default branch name debate, where outsiders with no stake in the project flooded the list with opinions, and notes that AI policy is similarly prone to broad, low-effort engagement from non-contributors. His point is pragmatic: limiting eligibility to recent contributors reduces noise and administrative overhead without disenfranchising those who are genuinely invested in the project’s future.

The email does not introduce new technical details or propose changes to the patch itself. Instead, it strengthens the case for Phillip’s earlier refinements—automated voting links and a time-based cutoff—by highlighting real-world risks of open participation. The discussion remains focused on balancing inclusivity with practicality, and Kristoffer’s contribution adds weight to the argument that Git’s voting process should prioritize active, engaged contributors over casual observers or bad actors. No resolution is reached, but the thread continues to narrow the design space for governance.
