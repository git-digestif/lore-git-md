This is a **feature patch** in the `git status` area, part of an ongoing debate about default behavior in triangular workflows. The email is Harald Nordgren’s reply to Junio C Hamano’s substantive review of v2, which challenged the patch’s core justification for changing the default comparison branches.

Harald pushes back against Junio’s argument that users who configure a separate push destination are equally capable of discovering the `status.compareBranches` setting. He counters with real-world experience: most users set their push branch to match upstream (via `git push -u`), leaving them unaware of upstream changes. When they later use `--set-upstream-to` to switch to a triangular workflow, the sudden appearance of upstream status can feel disruptive, making the push branch seem "lost." The patch, he argues, is not about catering to experts but about easing less experienced users into triangular thinking by showing both comparisons by default.

The exchange remains focused on the *motivation* for the default change, not the implementation. Junio’s objection—that `@{push}` is less likely to introduce surprises—is met with a practical counter: users often don’t realize they’re missing upstream information until they explicitly reconfigure their workflow. Harald’s framing suggests the patch is a usability improvement for a broad audience, not just a niche feature for power users. The thread is now at a crossroads: either Harald provides additional evidence (e.g., user surveys, anecdotes from other maintainers) to strengthen the case for the default change, or the discussion may stall unless another contributor weighs in with a tie-breaking perspective.
