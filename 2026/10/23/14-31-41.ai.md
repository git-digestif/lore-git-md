This is a governance discussion refining the proposed community voting process, specifically the eligibility criteria for voting on contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Junio C Hamano’s pushback on Phillip Wood’s two-year activity window, reinforcing the pragmatic case for limiting eligibility to recent contributors.

Kristoffer argues that the risk of "dogpiling" (a flood of low-effort participation from outsiders) is overstated when applied to former contributors. He points out that even if past contributors were mobilized via social media, the effort required to organize such a campaign would be impractical, especially given the filtering effect of the eligibility criteria (e.g., requiring prior contributions). His framing shifts the debate from abstract inclusivity to practical safeguards: the two-year window is not about disenfranchising engaged contributors but about reducing noise and administrative overhead in contentious votes. He cites the 2020 default branch name debate as a cautionary example where outsiders with no stake in the project flooded the list with opinions, and implies that a time-based cutoff would mitigate similar risks in future votes.

The email does not introduce new technical proposals or resolve the eligibility debate, but it strengthens the case for Phillip Wood’s refinements (automated voting links, time-based cutoff) by grounding them in real-world concerns about vote integrity. The discussion remains focused on balancing inclusivity (respecting past contributors) with practicality (preventing abuse and simplifying administration), with no clear consensus yet. Key unresolved questions include whether the two-year window will be adopted, how to define "active contributor," and how to script eligibility checks for non-code contributions.
