This is a governance discussion refining the proposed community voting process for Git, specifically the eligibility criteria for voters. Phillip Wood responds to Junio C Hamano’s pushback on two key points: the two-year activity window and the simplicity of commit counting.

Phillip clarifies that the voting system under discussion would automatically send voting links to eligible participants, making inactive contributors aware of the vote without requiring manual effort. He argues that a time limit (e.g., two years) reduces administrative overhead—such as handling claims from past contributors with changed email addresses—and mitigates the risk of bad actors derailing the vote, citing the 2020 default branch name debate as a cautionary example. While he acknowledges Junio’s concern about excluding engaged but inactive contributors, he frames the time limit as a pragmatic safeguard rather than a rejection of their input.

On the counting mechanism, Phillip remains flexible, accepting Junio’s proposal of a flat commit count (e.g., five commits) over tracking patch series, provided the threshold isn’t set too low. He notes that most reviewers who would qualify via reviews likely have enough authored patches to meet the commit threshold anyway, making the distinction less critical. The discussion now centers on balancing inclusivity (respecting past contributors) with practicality (preventing abuse and simplifying administration), with no clear resolution yet. The thread remains focused on governance design, with no new technical details or files introduced.
