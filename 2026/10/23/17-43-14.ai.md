This is Junio C Hamano's reply to Harald Nordgren's v2 patch, which proposes changing the default behavior of `git status` to compare against both `@{upstream}` and `@{push}` by default. The email is a **substantive review** that questions the underlying assumption of the patch and challenges its justification.

Junio argues that the patch's rationale—that users in triangular workflows benefit from seeing both comparisons—is unconvincing. He points out that `@{push}` is typically under the user's control, unlike `@{upstream}`, which may receive unexpected changes from others. This makes `@{push}` less likely to introduce "surprises" that warrant default monitoring, undermining the claim that comparing against both branches is universally useful. He also questions the assertion that users new to triangular workflows are less likely to discover the `status.compareBranches` setting than those who have already configured it, calling the logic "unclear."

The review does not engage with the patch's implementation or test coverage (both of which were praised in v1 feedback) but instead focuses on the *motivation* for the change. Junio's tone suggests skepticism about whether the benefit outweighs the cost of surprising existing users, and he explicitly states that the patch fails to answer the original question posed in 2026: *"if people like the option."* The presence of an escape hatch (restoring the old behavior via config) is dismissed as insufficient justification for flipping the default.

This reply reopens the discussion on the patch's core premise, which had appeared settled after v2 addressed earlier feedback. No new technical objections are raised, but the patch's readiness for merging is now in doubt unless the author can provide a stronger argument for the default change. The thread is likely to require a v3 or further discussion to resolve Junio's concerns.
