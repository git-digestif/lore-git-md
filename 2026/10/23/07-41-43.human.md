This is a **documentation patch** in the ongoing effort to clean up Git’s terminology around "pseudorefs" and "root refs." It is the first patch in Andy Koppe’s five-part series (v1) that updates the glossary, man pages, and code comments to align with the 2024 redefinition of these terms.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review here is **substantive but narrowly focused**. He agrees with the structural improvements—splitting "root ref" into its own glossary entry and adding clarity about how pseudorefs behave when they contain multiple object IDs—but pushes back on two points. First, he wants the glossary to retain the original phrasing about pseudorefs having "different semantics" rather than just describing what they *aren’t* (not stored like refs, not writable by `git update-ref`). Second, he argues the glossary should explicitly state what operations *are* supported for pseudorefs (direct reading) instead of only listing what isn’t. These are not nitpicks; they go to the heart of whether the glossary will be clear to users who don’t already know the history.

The review does not address the rest of the series, nor does it signal approval or rejection of the patch as a whole. It is a targeted critique of the glossary wording, leaving the door open for Andy to revise or defend the current phrasing. The tone is constructive and matter-of-fact, suggesting this is a detail to resolve rather than a fundamental disagreement. If Andy incorporates these suggestions, the glossary will likely be more precise without changing the series’ overall goal.
