This is a follow-up in the ongoing governance discussion about Git’s proposed community voting process. Kristoffer Haugsbakk clarifies his earlier point about why a two-year activity window for voting eligibility is a pragmatic safeguard, not an attempt to exclude engaged contributors. He explains that some users—even those who don’t contribute code—care deeply about whether projects use AI tools, and this sentiment can lead to low-effort, high-volume participation in contentious votes (like the AI contribution policy). His reply reinforces the argument that strict eligibility criteria are needed to prevent "drive-by" voting from outsiders who lack a long-term stake in the project, while still allowing inactive but invested contributors to participate if they choose to re-engage.

The email doesn’t propose new technical refinements or resolve the core tension—Junio’s preference for inclusivity versus Kristoffer’s focus on practical safeguards—but it sharpens the rationale for time-based cutoffs. The thread remains unresolved, with the eligibility criteria still open to debate. For readers tracking Git’s governance evolution, this exchange highlights the challenge of balancing openness with the need to protect contentious votes from noise.
