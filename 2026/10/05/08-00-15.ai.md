This is a **substantive review** of the implementation patch (2/2) in Kristofer Karlsson’s incremental connectivity check series. Patrick Steinhardt engages deeply with the core algorithm, its trust model, and integration points, raising technical questions about boundary detection, performance trade-offs, and error handling.

The review focuses on three key areas. First, Patrick clarifies the trust boundary mechanism: how the verifier distinguishes between "already-connected" parent trees (trusted) and incoming trees (untrusted). He asks how the algorithm identifies trusted trees without parsing all refs—a potential bottleneck the current design does not address. Second, he questions the error-handling strategy for cycles in the incoming commit graph, suggesting that `die()` is too harsh for server-side operations like `git-receive-pack` and may disrupt client communication. Third, he proposes splitting the patch into smaller, focused commits (option introduction, benchmark, `rev-list` flag, and strategy implementation) to improve reviewability, though this is more a process suggestion than a technical objection.

Patrick also notes the performance trade-off: while the incremental mode scales with change size rather than repository size, it may verify more objects in scenarios with many refs or large boundary trees. His tone is constructive, and the questions are precise (e.g., "Where do you establish the trust boundary?"), indicating a thorough understanding of the code. No new bugs are identified, but the review surfaces design-level considerations that could shape a v3. The series remains under active review, with no merge status.
