## 2026/10/07

- [2026/10/07/15-33-09 by René Scharfe <l.s.r@web.de>] René Scharfe explained that Python 3.14's ZipInfo.date_time reporting 2100 for a 1972 ZIP makes sense because Python's zipfile module decodes Zip64 and Info-ZIP Unicode Path extra fields but lacks support for the UNIX (0x000d) extra field.
- [2026/10/07/15-33-09 by René Scharfe <l.s.r@web.de>] He stated he is not aware of any ZIP extension that allows storing arbitrary dates and listed three options: emitting only valid DOS and UNIX timestamps and refusing out-of-range ones, wrapping consistently, or clamping earlier timestamps to the epoch and later ones to the maximum value.
- [2026/10/07/15-33-09 by René Scharfe <l.s.r@web.de>] He recommended rejecting non-representable timestamps when creating ZIP files as the most honest option, suggesting this might motivate users to adopt the tar format instead, and noted that Info-ZIP zip(1) clamps at the low end and rolls over at the high end.
- [2026/10/07/15-33-09 by René Scharfe <l.s.r@web.de>] He argued against rejecting negative timestamps in fast-import, noting that Git commit objects store timestamps as decimal numeric strings supporting arbitrarily high and low values, making imports of ancient records such as 1960s mainframe files or centuries-old legal documents reasonable, and observed that timestamp_t is unsigned only for historical reasons.

## 2026/10/11
