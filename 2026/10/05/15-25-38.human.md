This is a maintainer response to the RFC for adding porcelain commands to share Git stashes through remotes. Junio C Hamano aligns with **Johannes Sixt (Hannes)**'s opposition, stating that branches already provide robust support for discovery, transfer, and cleanup of shared work. He sees no compelling reason to duplicate this functionality for stashes, reinforcing the view that stashes should remain personal and ephemeral. The message is brief but decisive, signaling that the proposal—even in its narrowed form—faces a high bar for justification. No technical counterproposal or compromise is offered; the stance is that the existing branch-based workflow is sufficient. This reply effectively shifts the burden back to the author to demonstrate why branches are inadequate for the proposed use case.
