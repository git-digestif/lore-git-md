This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing RFC thread about adding porcelain commands for sharing Git stashes through remotes. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) directly addresses Hanan Arshad’s narrowed proposal, rejecting the idea of even a minimal convenience command (`git stash publish`).

Hannes’s position remains firm: stashes are inherently personal and ephemeral, and the existing workflow—using `git stash export` followed by `git push`, or simply pushing a branch—is already sufficient. He argues that the proposed convenience command adds no new functionality and that if users need to share unfinished work frequently, they should use branches instead. His tone is uncompromising, framing the feature as unnecessary rather than merely over-engineered.

The reply advances the discussion by clarifying that Hannes’s opposition is not just to the original design but to the core premise of making stash sharing more convenient. He implies that the use case (sharing unfinished work ten times a day) is itself flawed and that branches are the correct tool for the job. This leaves little room for compromise on the porcelain interface, though the thread remains open for others to weigh in.

For a busy reader, this email signals that the proposal faces strong philosophical resistance from a respected contributor. The debate is now less about implementation details and more about whether the feature aligns with Git’s design principles. If no other participants step in to support the narrowed scope, the RFC may stall.
