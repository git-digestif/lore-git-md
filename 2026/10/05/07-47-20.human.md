This is a substantive review of Hanan Arshad’s RFC proposing porcelain commands to share Git stashes through remotes. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) pushes back sharply against the entire premise of the feature, arguing that stashes are inherently personal, short-lived, and not meant for sharing. His critique is rooted in Git’s design philosophy: if a change is worth sharing, it should be committed to a branch, not stashed. He sees no need for dedicated porcelain commands when the existing plumbing (`git stash export` + `git push`) already suffices, and suggests users who want to share stashes should simply use `git push origin stash@{0}:<ref>`.

Hannes’s feedback is not just surface-level; it questions the fundamental motivation behind the RFC. He implies that the use case itself—sharing stashes—is a misuse of Git’s stash mechanism, which is intended for temporary, local work-in-progress. His alternative (`git push origin stash@{0}:...`) is presented as a simpler, more transparent way to achieve the same result without adding new commands. This review may shift the thread’s focus from *how* to implement stash sharing to *whether* it should be implemented at all. The tone is direct but not dismissive, leaving room for Hanan to defend the use case if they can articulate scenarios where stash sharing is genuinely valuable.
