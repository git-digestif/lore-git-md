This email is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically demonstrating why a **multi-shot workflow** (manual control over rebase order) is essential for complex dependency graphs. Alejandro Colomar provides a detailed, 150-line shell session showing how his `git-bisect-rebase` script (the leading candidate for `contrib/` inclusion) is used to rebase a *tree of branches* (A, B, and C) in a specific, non-linear order. The example proves that Phillip Wood’s proposed **single-shot workflow**—where `git rebase --continue` automatically advances to the next conflicting commit—would break real-world use cases involving interdependent branches.

The session walks through a concrete scenario:
1. Rebasing branch A onto `main` (stops at first conflict).
2. Aborting that rebase to switch to branch B, rebasing it onto A (resolving conflicts).
3. Rebasing branch C onto A (succeeds without conflicts).
4. Returning to branch A to complete its rebase.
5. Rebasing B and C onto the updated A (handling new conflicts).

Colomar’s key argument is that this process requires *manual control* over the order of operations, which a single-shot workflow cannot accommodate. He also notes that even for a single branch, he prefers the multi-shot approach to run `git range-diff` after each step for verification. The demonstration reinforces the practical utility of `git-bisect-rebase` for projects like `shadow-utils`, Linux man-pages, and PostgreSQL, and counters Phillip’s usability concern by showing how the current design enables flexibility.

The email does not introduce new code or proposals but clarifies the *requirements* for any future integration into `git rebase` itself. Colomar’s position is now aligned with Patrick Steinhardt’s preference for direct integration, but he emphasizes that the multi-shot workflow must be preserved. The thread’s focus has shifted from "whether" to "how" to implement incremental rebase, with this exchange ruling out single-shot implementations for complex cases. The unresolved questions remain: whether the project will accept `git-bisect-rebase` in `contrib/`, pursue a built-in option, or rely on third-party tools like `git-imerge`.
