This is a substantive review of Hanan Arshad’s RFC proposing porcelain commands for sharing Git stashes through remotes. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) pushes back strongly against the entire premise of the feature, arguing that stashes are inherently personal, short-lived, and not meant for sharing. His position is that if a stash is worth preserving or sharing, users should already be using branches instead, and the existing plumbing (`git stash export` + `git push`) is sufficient for any edge case where sharing might be justified.

Hannes’s critique is technical and philosophical: he sees the proposed porcelain as unnecessary magic that encourages misuse of stashes. He explicitly rejects the idea that stashes should be treated as shareable project states, framing the feature as a solution in search of a problem. His alternative is blunt: users who want to share a stash should manually export it and push the resulting ref, with no porcelain shortcuts. The review is substantive—it engages with the core motivation of the proposal rather than surface-level details—and suggests the feature may face significant resistance if it proceeds to implementation. No counterarguments or compromises are offered; the tone is firm.
