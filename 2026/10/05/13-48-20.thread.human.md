This thread is a bug report from Matthew E. Luallen about three date-handling inconsistencies in Git’s ZIP archive generation and `git fast-import` logic. The issues involve overflows and wraps in ZIP timestamp fields (legacy DOS dates and extended Unix timestamps) and a validation gap in `fast-import` that accepts negative timestamps later rejected by `git fsck --strict`.

The discussion has narrowed to policy questions: whether to clamp or reject out-of-range timestamps in ZIP archives, and how strictly to validate dates in `fast-import`. René Scharfe has suggested clamping ZIP legacy DOS dates to 1980-01-01 (matching `zip(1)`) and extended timestamps to 2106-02-07 (aligning with TAR), while Matthew leans toward rejection to avoid silent misinterpretation by downstream tools. The `fast-import` issue—where negative timestamps slip through validation—is seen as the most actionable near-term fix, with both participants agreeing stricter checks are needed.

Matthew’s latest email provides real-world examples of how current behavior affects tools like Python’s `ZipInfo.date_time`, UnZip, and bsdtar. Notably, UnZip’s update mode retains outdated content when processing archives with post-2106 timestamps due to the wrapped Unix timestamp, creating subtle data inconsistencies. These observations strengthen the argument for rejection over clamping in the 2106 case, though no decisions have been made yet. The thread remains under review, with no patches proposed or maintainer feedback on merge status.
