This thread began as a bug report by Matthew E. Luallen detailing three date-handling inconsistencies in Git’s ZIP archive generation and `git fast-import` logic. The most pressing issue—ZIP extended timestamp wrap, where commits after 2106-02-07 produced silent 1970 timestamps in the UT field—has now been resolved. René Scharfe’s v2 patch omits the UT field entirely for out-of-range timestamps (`> 0x7fffffff`), preventing tools like UnZip from misinterpreting archives in update mode. The patch also includes a test infrastructure fix (using `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` instead of `TIME_IS_64BIT`) to ensure compatibility on 32-bit platforms, which Junio C Hamano has confirmed as sound. This fix is ready for integration, with no remaining technical blockers.

The thread also uncovered two unresolved issues. First, the ZIP legacy DOS date field overflows for pre-1980 commits (e.g., 1972 → 2100), and a newly identified bug in `dos_time()` produces invalid timestamps for values beyond 32-bit range. René advocates rejecting out-of-range timestamps outright, arguing this is the "most honest" approach, while clamping remains a prior suggestion. Second, `git fast-import`’s `--date-format=raw` accepts negative timestamps that `git fsck --strict` later rejects, creating a validation gap. René opposes stricter validation, citing Git’s existing flexibility with decimal strings, while Matthew proposes rejection to align with `NEEDSWORK` comments. Junio’s `#leftoverbits` note suggests a broader audit of `timestamp_t` coercion in external formats, though this is not urgent.

Key participants include Matthew, who provided real-world tool behavior examples and the test infrastructure fix, and René, who implemented the UT field omission patch and advocates rejection for the legacy DOS date issue. Junio’s review has been limited to surface-level confirmation of the test fix. The thread remains open for the two unresolved issues, with no consensus yet on whether to address them incrementally or as part of a broader solution.
