This thread reports three date-handling bugs in Git’s ZIP archive generation and `git fast-import` logic, originally raised by Matthew E. Luallen. The issues involve timestamp overflows and validation gaps that cause tool compatibility problems and silent data inconsistencies, particularly with UnZip and Python’s `ZipInfo`.

The discussion has narrowed significantly since the report. René Scharfe, the most active reviewer, now advocates rejecting out-of-range timestamps outright when generating ZIP archives rather than clamping or wrapping them. This approach prioritizes correctness over tool compatibility and could encourage users to adopt more robust formats like TAR. For `fast-import`, however, René opposes rejecting negative timestamps, arguing that Git’s commit objects should remain flexible for importing historical records. Matthew had previously leaned toward rejection for ZIP extended timestamps to avoid silent misinterpretation, and both agree that `fast-import`’s validation needs tightening, though the specifics remain unresolved.

The thread is now focused on policy decisions: whether to reject or clamp timestamps in ZIP exports, and how strictly to validate negative timestamps in `fast-import`. No patches have been proposed yet, but the conversation is converging toward a solution that balances backward compatibility with long-term robustness. The most actionable near-term change appears to be addressing `fast-import`’s acceptance of negative timestamps, which `git fsck --strict` later rejects.
