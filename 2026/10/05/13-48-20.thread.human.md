This thread is a bug report from Matthew E. Luallen about three date-handling inconsistencies in Git’s ZIP archive generation and `git fast-import` logic. The issues involve overflows and wraps in ZIP timestamp fields (affecting tools like UnZip and Python’s `ZipInfo`) and a validation gap in `fast-import` that accepts negative timestamps later rejected by `git fsck --strict`.

Key participants are Matthew, who provided detailed reproduction steps and real-world impact examples, and René Scharfe, who engaged with the technical trade-offs in a substantive review. René suggested clamping ZIP legacy DOS dates to 1980-01-01 (matching `zip(1)`) and extended timestamps to 4294967295 (aligning with TAR), while advocating for stricter validation in `fast-import`. Matthew’s latest follow-up adds new data on tool behavior—such as Python’s `ZipInfo` misreporting 1972 commits as 2100—and leans toward *rejecting* out-of-range timestamps, especially for the ZIP extended field. He proposes starting with a smaller fix: rejecting negative timestamps in `fast-import`’s `--date-format=raw` mode, which would address the strict validation gap without broader changes.

The thread remains in discussion, with no patches posted yet. The open questions revolve around whether to prioritize tool compatibility (clamping) or strict correctness (rejection), and whether to tackle the issues incrementally or in a single series. The `fast-import` negative timestamp fix appears to be the most actionable near-term change, while the ZIP timestamp policies may require further debate. No maintainer decisions have been made, but the real-world examples Matthew provided could help shape the eventual resolution.
