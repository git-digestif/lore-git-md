This thread is a bug report from Matthew E. Luallen about three date-handling inconsistencies in Git’s ZIP archive generation and `git fast-import` logic. The most pressing issue—ZIP extended timestamp wrap—has now been addressed by René Scharfe with a patch that omits the Extended Timestamp (UT) extra field when the commit time exceeds the 32-bit signed integer range. This prevents silent timestamp wraps that cause tools like UnZip to misbehave in update mode, aligning with René’s updated stance favoring rejection (omission) of out-of-range timestamps over clamping for correctness.

The patch is narrowly scoped to the ZIP backend (`archive-zip.c`) and includes tests to verify the UT field is included for valid timestamps and omitted for out-of-range values. While this resolves one of the three reported bugs, two remain open: the ZIP legacy DOS date overflow (where 1972 commits produce 2100 timestamps) and the `fast-import` validation gap (where negative timestamps are accepted but later rejected by `git fsck --strict`). René continues to oppose stricter validation for `fast-import`, arguing that Git’s commit objects allow arbitrary decimal strings and that `timestamp_t`’s unsigned nature is not immutable.

The discussion has converged toward rejection as the preferred policy for ZIP export, but no consensus has been reached on the DOS date field or `fast-import`. The patch advances the thread but leaves broader questions about tool compatibility and incremental fixes unresolved. Key participants remain Matthew (advocating for rejection and incremental fixes) and René (supporting rejection for ZIP but opposing stricter `fast-import` validation). No maintainer decisions have been made yet.
