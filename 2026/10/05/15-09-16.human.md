This is a small but clarifying reply in the ongoing discussion about Phillip Wood’s patch series to preserve original conflict labels in `git checkout -m`. Phillip acknowledges Johannes Sixt’s concern that preserving the original conflict style (e.g., `diff3`) could interfere with users who occasionally override the style for specific conflicts. He clarifies that his proposal only affects the default behavior of `git checkout -m` when no explicit style is provided—it would use the same style as the original merge, but users could still override it with `--conflict=<style>` or `merge.conflictStyle`. The distinction is subtle but important: the series would not lock users into the original style, only make the default more consistent with what they initially saw. This reply narrows the scope of the open question and should help reviewers evaluate whether the change would be convenient or confusing in practice. The series itself remains ready for integration, with this conflict-style detail as the sole unresolved point.
