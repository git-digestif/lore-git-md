This is a bug report about Git’s date-handling edge cases in ZIP archives and `git fast-import`.

Matthew E. Luallen describes three reproducible bugs:

1. A 1972-dated commit exported to ZIP shows a legacy DOS date of 2100 while the extended Unix timestamp remains 1972. The mismatch can confuse archive tools.
2. A commit timestamped at 2106-02-07 06:28:16 UTC (epoch 4294967296) wraps the ZIP’s four-byte extended timestamp back to 1970, whereas the same commit exported as TAR keeps the correct value.
3. `git fast-import --date-format=raw` accepts the negative timestamp -32184000 +0000, but `git fsck --strict` later flags it as a badDate and ISO rendering produces literal placeholders.

The report includes simple reproduction steps and notes that the ZIP overflow affects real-world behavior (macOS UnZip retained different content based on the apparent age of the archive). Matthew asks what range-handling policy ZIP should adopt and whether strict raw import should reject timestamps that `fsck` already considers invalid.
