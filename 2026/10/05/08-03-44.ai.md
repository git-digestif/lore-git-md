This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread proposing porcelain commands for sharing Git stashes through remotes. The author, Hanan Arshad, responds to Johannes Sixt’s (Hannes) strong opposition by narrowing the scope and clarifying the intended use case.

Hanan acknowledges Hannes’s core objection—that stashes are inherently personal and ephemeral—and agrees the feature should not encourage treating them as a replacement for branches. Instead, he frames the use case as a lightweight, temporary handoff of unfinished work, analogous to Perforce shelves: a way to share a working state without committing to a branch workflow. The key distinction is that the work remains temporary and unfinished, but another developer may need to inspect, reproduce, or continue it.

The revised proposal emphasizes that the feature would not introduce new object formats, server-side storage, or synchronization mechanisms. It would merely wrap existing plumbing (`git stash export`, `git push`, `git fetch`, `git stash import`) into a porcelain interface to make an already-possible workflow more discoverable and less error-prone. Hanan explicitly asks whether a narrowly scoped convenience command—limited to publishing, with listing, fetching, and deletion left to existing Git commands—would still face opposition.

The email advances the discussion by shifting focus from the philosophical question ("should stashes be shareable?") to a practical one: whether a minimal porcelain wrapper around an existing workflow has value. This reframing may help clarify whether Hannes’s objection is to the concept of shared stashes entirely or to the perceived complexity of the original proposal. The tone remains collaborative, and the author is open to further narrowing the scope if it addresses concerns. No new technical details or implementation plans are introduced.
