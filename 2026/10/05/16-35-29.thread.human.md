This thread is about a small refactoring series by Phillip Wood that removes redundant change-detection logic in `git stash push` and `git stash create`. The goal is to consolidate duplicate calls to `check_changes()` into a single check for cleaner code, with no intended behavior change. The series is currently at its first iteration (`v1`) and consists of two patches touching `builtin/stash.c`.

Phillip’s patches are under review, and Junio C Hamano has raised two points so far. The first was a minor but blocking issue: three instances in the first patch’s commit message incorrectly referenced `stash store` instead of `stash create`, which must be fixed before the patch can proceed. The second, more substantive observation came in the review of the second patch: while the patch removes one redundant `check_changes()` call, it introduces a new inefficiency by causing `do_push_stash()` to refresh the index twice in succession—once before calling `do_create_stash()` and again inside it—even though nothing has changed in between. Junio did not object to the patch’s direction but noted that the optimization is incomplete and may need a follow-up to avoid the double refresh.

No other reviewers have weighed in yet, and there are no open questions beyond the commit-message fix and the index-refresh concern. The series is purely mechanical, touching only `builtin/stash.c` with no new functions, options, or on-disk format changes. If the commit-message typos are corrected and the index-refresh issue is addressed, the series should be ready to proceed.
