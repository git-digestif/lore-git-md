This thread is about a refactoring series by Phillip Wood to optimize redundant change-detection logic in `git stash push` and `git stash create`. The series, now in its third iteration (`v3`), has been queued for integration testing in Junio C Hamano’s `next` branch and is likely to graduate to `master` in the next cycle unless issues arise during testing.

The goal is to eliminate duplicate `check_changes()` calls, index loads, and refreshes, reducing `lstat()` overhead and untracked-file walks. The series introduces an `enum create_result` to clarify the return values of `do_create_stash()`, distinguishing between success, errors, and cases where no changes were stashed. It also corrects a minor behavioral inconsistency in `create_stash()`’s exit status. All changes are confined to `builtin/stash.c` and focus on mechanical performance improvements without altering user-visible behavior.

Junio’s latest review of the first patch in the series raised minor stylistic concerns—such as making enum values more explicit and preferring `switch/case` for handling return values—but these are not blocking issues and do not require a reroll. The series has already addressed all prior feedback, including technical refinements and commit message improvements, and Junio’s overall assessment remains positive. Phillip Wood, the author, is a well-established contributor with deep expertise in the stash subsystem, and the series is uncontroversial. No open questions or loose ends remain.
