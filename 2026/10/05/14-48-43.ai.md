This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing thread about Phillip Wood’s `git checkout -m` conflict label preservation series. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) responds to Phillip’s open question about whether `git checkout -m` should also remember and restore the original conflict style (e.g., `diff3`). Hannes argues against preserving the conflict style, citing a concrete scenario: users who normally prefer regular conflict markers might occasionally want to switch to `diff3` for a specific conflict, and preserving the original style would prevent them from doing so with `git checkout --conflict=diff3 -m <unmerged-path>`. His position is clear and practical, grounded in workflow considerations rather than implementation details. The feedback is narrowly scoped to the conflict style question and does not revisit the architectural debate about index extensions or other aspects of the series. This reply advances the thread by providing a reasoned objection to the proposed extension, giving Phillip and other reviewers a concrete trade-off to evaluate.
