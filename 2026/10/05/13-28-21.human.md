This is a discussion about workflow preferences for incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically whether the tool should operate in a multi-shot or single-shot mode. Alejandro Colomar responds to Phillip Wood’s earlier suggestion that `git rebase --continue` should automatically advance to the next conflicting commit (single-shot), arguing that a multi-shot approach is necessary for rebasing complex branch trees.

Colomar demonstrates his use case with a detailed shell session showing how he rebases a tree of branches in a specific order, stopping mid-rebase to work on descendant branches before returning to the parent. This workflow, he explains, would be impossible with a single-shot approach that completes the entire rebase in one go. He also notes that even for single branches, he prefers stopping after each conflict resolution to verify the result with `git range-diff`.

The email reinforces the earlier consensus that the functionality belongs in `git rebase` itself rather than as a standalone script, but it introduces a key design question: should the built-in option (`--first-conflict`, `--incremental`, or similar) support multi-shot operation, or should it enforce a single-shot workflow? Colomar’s example makes a strong case for multi-shot flexibility, especially for advanced users managing interdependent branches. The discussion remains open, with no clear resolution yet on which approach the project will prefer.
