This is the latest exchange in the long-running series to introduce a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories. The series aims to solve a real usability problem: `git fetch` in a shallow clone (`--depth`) can hang or become very slow when the remote has many branches, because a refspec-less fetch negotiates history for all remote branches. The proposed solution uses a new `remote.<name>.refmap` configuration to define namespace mappings separately from fetch selection logic, and dynamically fetches only the branches tracked by local branches at the remote.

The series is technically complete and has been through six iterations, but remains blocked on a design decision: what should happen when both `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap` are configured? Harald previously proposed that `remote.<name>.fetch` should take precedence, resolving a fatal error by ignoring the `refmap` setting. Junio has not yet responded to this proposal, instead noting a textual conflict with Collin’s recently merged `followRemoteHAD` topic and asking Harald to review the resolution in the `seen` branch.

In this email, Harald reports that he has range-diffed the conflict resolution in `seen` and found it acceptable. He asks whether Junio wants him to hold off sending a new version. The question is procedural rather than technical: the design ambiguity remains unresolved, and the conflict resolution does not address it. The series is still under active review, not queued to any integration branch, and Junio’s earlier "Will replace" statement remains on hold.

For a busy reader, the key takeaway is that the series is stuck on a design point, not a technical flaw. The conflict with `followRemoteHAD` is resolved, but the core question about precedence between `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap` is still open. If Junio accepts Harald’s proposal, the series could move forward quickly; if not, another iteration will be needed.
