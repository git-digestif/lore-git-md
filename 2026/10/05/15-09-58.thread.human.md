This thread introduces a new warning in `git commit` when the commit's date is earlier than any of its parents, which could break date-based history traversal. The patch, submitted by Devi Srinivas Vasamsetti, adds the warning via Git's advice system, controlled by the `advice.clockSkew` configuration setting, and includes tests to verify its behavior.

Junio C Hamano has raised significant concerns about the feature's usefulness. He argues that the warning doesn't solve the underlying issue—clocks on other machines may already be incorrect—and questions what action a user should take if warned (e.g., wait, request a force-push). He also notes that the warning only applies to the current commit, missing skew in existing history, and that date-based traversal (`git log --since`) remains unreliable regardless. His feedback suggests that users should avoid date-based options entirely if they need dependable results, implying the warning may not justify the added complexity. The patch is still under review, with no other participants having weighed in yet.
