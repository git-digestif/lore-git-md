This is a documentation review in response to Julia Evans’s patch rewriting the explanation of detached HEAD state. The patch is part of an ongoing effort to improve Git’s user-facing documentation, specifically by creating a new standalone guide (`gitdetachedhead(7)`) and updating advice messages to reference it.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s review is substantive, focusing on both structural and textual improvements. He identifies three build-system omissions (missing meson/Makefile changes to generate the new man page) and suggests a hyphen in the man page name (`gitdetached-head(7)` instead of `gitdetachedhead(7)`), though the latter is already consistent with existing guides like `gitdatamodel(7)`. His most detailed feedback concerns the opening definition of HEAD, where he argues the current phrasing ("`HEAD` is where Git stores your current branch") could mislead users into thinking HEAD is empty when no branch exists. He proposes an alternative framing that emphasizes HEAD always points to a commit, with branches as an additional layer of indirection.

The review also critiques the patch’s explanation of *why* detached HEAD is useful, suggesting it could better highlight practical use cases (e.g., testing commits, browsing history, or working directly on remote-tracking branches). Haugsbakk advocates for mentioning `git reflog` earlier to reassure users that "garbage" commits aren’t immediately lost, and for replacing "main ways" with "typical ways" to describe accidental detachment scenarios. While he praises the clarity of the tag/checkout explanation, he notes that `git log` might be a more intuitive recovery tool than `git reflog` for users who’ve just created commits in detached state.

The feedback is constructive and technically grounded, though some suggestions (like the HEAD definition rewrite) may require careful wording to avoid introducing new confusion. The build-system omissions are the most critical issue, as they would prevent the new guide from being generated. No objections are raised to the patch’s core goals—creating a standalone guide and updating advice messages—but the review highlights opportunities to refine the text for better usability. The discussion remains documentation-only, with no impact on Git’s on-disk format or behavior.
