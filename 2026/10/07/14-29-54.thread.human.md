This thread is about a proposed policy change to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions to Git, replacing the current cautious stance with a more permissive framework. Scott Chacon, the author, has submitted an RFC patch updating the `SubmittingPatches` document to set clear expectations for human responsibility, disclosure, and quality in AI-assisted work. The new policy requires contributors to review, understand, and test their AI-assisted contributions; comply with the DCO; disclose substantial AI assistance via an `Assisted-by:` trailer; and maintain records of prompts and outputs. The patch cites alignment with peer GPL projects like the Linux kernel and is framed as a modern, legally reviewed approach.

The proposal is likely to spark significant discussion, as it touches on a sensitive topic where the project has historically been wary. Key points of contention may include how "substantial assistance" is defined, whether the policy can be practically enforced, and whether it adequately addresses risks of low-quality or legally problematic submissions. The patch does not address the potential increased burden on reviewers assessing AI-assisted work. So far, this is the first email in the thread, and no responses or decisions have been made. The discussion is just beginning.
