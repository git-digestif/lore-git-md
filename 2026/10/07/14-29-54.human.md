This is a documentation patch proposing a policy change to Git's `SubmittingPatches` guide. It replaces the current blanket prohibition on AI-generated contributions with a more nuanced framework that allows responsible AI assistance while maintaining the project's quality and licensing standards.

The patch argues that the existing policy—rejecting anything that "looks AI-generated"—creates uncertainty for contributors and discourages transparency about tool use. Instead, it proposes requiring human understanding, proper testing, and disclosure of substantial AI assistance via an `Assisted-by:` trailer. The Developer's Certificate of Origin (DCO) remains unchanged, and reviewers retain the right to request additional evidence or reject submissions they cannot confidently assess.

The commit message cites the Linux kernel's approach to AI assistance as a reference and includes an `Assisted-by:` trailer for the AI tool used to draft the policy language. The diff itself rewrites the entire AI section of `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, replacing vague warnings about "AI slop" with concrete expectations around review, disclosure, and provenance.

This is likely to be a controversial change, as it directly addresses a long-standing tension in open-source projects: how to balance the practical benefits of AI tools with concerns about code quality, licensing, and contributor accountability. The patch is well-motivated and clearly written, but the topic itself may generate significant discussion.
