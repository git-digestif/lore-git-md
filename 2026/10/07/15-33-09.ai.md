This is a substantive review reply in the ongoing ZIP timestamp and `git fast-import` date validation bug thread. René Scharfe responds to Matthew E. Luallen’s tool observations and policy questions with a clear preference for strict correctness over tool compatibility, while acknowledging the trade-offs.

René’s key position is to **reject out-of-range timestamps** when generating ZIP archives, rather than clamping or wrapping them. He argues this is the "most honest option" and could motivate users to adopt a more robust format (like TAR) or push for a proper ZIP extension. He notes that `zip(1)`’s behavior (clamping at the low end, rolling over at the high end) is inconsistent and not a good model to follow. This aligns with Matthew’s earlier lean toward rejection but contrasts with René’s prior suggestion to clamp ZIP extended timestamps to match TAR’s behavior.

On `fast-import`, René pushes back against rejecting negative timestamps, calling it "bad for people who want to import ancient records." He points out that Git commit objects store timestamps as decimal strings, allowing arbitrary values, and that `timestamp_t`’s unsigned nature is not immutable. This suggests he sees the `fast-import` strict validation gap as a separate issue from the ZIP export bugs, with different constraints.

The email advances the thread by narrowing the policy debate: rejection for ZIP export is now the leading proposal, while `fast-import`’s handling of negative timestamps remains open. René’s framing—prioritizing correctness over compatibility and questioning the rigidity of `timestamp_t`—may shape how the project weighs backward compatibility against long-term robustness. No patches have been proposed yet, but the discussion is converging on a direction.
