This is a follow-up in the ongoing bugfix series targeting packfile corruption from stale delta base cache entries. The email is a brief exchange between Patrick Steinhardt (author) and Junio C Hamano (maintainer) about the platform dependency of the regression test introduced in patch 2/2.

Patrick clarifies that the test’s reliability is not limited to glibc: it also reproduces the bug on Alpine Linux with musl libc, broadening its coverage beyond the original commit message’s claim. The discussion reaffirms the thread’s consensus that the test’s value—exercising submodule merges across many submodules—outweighs its platform dependency, and that a more complex deterministic alternative (e.g., custom allocator simulation) would be disproportionate. No new objections or action items emerge; the series remains on track for `next`.

Key technical detail: the test’s behavior is now confirmed to work on at least two libc implementations (glibc and musl), though it may still fail on others (e.g., macOS, Windows, or systems with ASan). The fix itself and its core rationale (evicting stale delta base cache entries on pack close) are uncontested. The only lingering question—whether to document the test’s limitations more prominently—remains unresolved but is unlikely to block the series.
