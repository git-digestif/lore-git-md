This is a review of a bugfix patch that makes `git diff --relative` work correctly with combined diffs. The patch itself is well-crafted and addresses a real usability issue, but Junio's review raises two technical concerns.

First, he points out a coding style violation: the `strip_relative_prefix()` helper has an overly long line, and the project's coding guidelines should be followed. This is a surface-level nit, not a functional problem.

The more substantive issue is about rename handling. When a combined diff involves renamed files (e.g., one parent has `here/file` while another has `there/file`), the current patch would strip the `--relative` prefix from `here/file` but leave `there/file` unchanged. Junio questions whether this inconsistency could mislead users, suggesting that either always stripping the prefix (even for renamed paths) or always leaving paths unchanged might be clearer. This is a design-level concern about how `--relative` should behave in edge cases, not just a bug in the implementation.

The patch is otherwise solid: it's minimal, well-tested, and fixes a long-standing inconsistency. The rename question is the only open issue, and it's not clear yet whether the author will need to revise the approach or just clarify the intended behavior. For now, this remains under review.
