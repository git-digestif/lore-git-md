This is a policy RFC that proposes amending `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. The email is Junio C Hamano’s reply to Scott Chacon’s initial patch, raising pointed questions about enforceability, reviewer bandwidth, and the practicality of certifying AI-generated output under the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO).

Junio plays devil’s advocate throughout, questioning whether the proposed text—requiring contributors to review, test, and sign off on AI-assisted work—can realistically be enforced. He notes that the current policy (“do not send in AI-generated slop, period”) is more direct and easier to apply, whereas the new language risks overwhelming reviewers with plausible-sounding but low-quality submissions. He also highlights discrepancies with the Linux kernel’s guidelines, which avoid naming specific AI tools (to prevent free advertising) and give maintainers broader discretion to reject contributions outright.

Key technical details remain unchanged from the initial patch: the change is confined to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, introduces an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI assistance, and does not alter code, tests, or build systems. However, Junio’s critique shifts the focus from the mechanics of the policy to its practical implications—whether reviewers can meaningfully assess AI-assisted work, whether the DCO’s requirements can be met, and whether the project can handle the potential influx of AI-generated patches without sacrificing review quality. His questions echo brian m. carlson’s earlier legal concerns but frame them as operational challenges rather than ethical or licensing ones. The thread is now at a crossroads: the patch’s viability hinges on addressing these enforcement and scalability issues.
