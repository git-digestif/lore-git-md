This is a policy discussion about whether Git should explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions in its `SubmittingPatches` documentation. The email is a substantive review from brian m. carlson opposing the proposed change, raising legal and ethical concerns about the provenance of AI-generated code.

brian argues that current large language models (LLMs) are trained on code from sources with unclear or restrictive licensing, including material that does not permit redistribution. Because LLMs can reproduce or closely mimic their training data, contributors cannot honestly assert the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) without knowing all the sources involved. This creates legal risk for distributors of Git, who could face copyright infringement claims if AI-generated code turns out to violate licensing terms—such as omitting required copyright notices from MIT- or BSD-licensed code. brian emphasizes that neither they nor the Git project have the resources to defend against such claims and that the project should not adopt policies that could expose contributors to legal or reputational harm.

The review cites Gentoo and NetBSD as examples of projects that have taken a cautious stance on AI contributions, contrasting them with the Linux kernel, Xen, and Debian, which Scott Chacon referenced in support of the proposed change. brian also alludes to discussions at the Contributor Summit where this issue was debated, suggesting that the topic remains unresolved and contentious. The tone is firm and principled, framing the question as one of legal and ethical responsibility rather than technical merit or contributor convenience.

This email does not engage with the mechanics of the patch itself (which only updates documentation) but challenges its underlying premise. It is likely to spark further debate, particularly around whether the benefits of permitting AI-assisted contributions outweigh the risks of unclear provenance and potential licensing violations. For readers following the thread, this response signals that the proposal faces significant opposition from a respected contributor with strong legal concerns.
