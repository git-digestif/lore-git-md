This is the **third patch in a four-patch bugfix series** (v3) that integrates branch copy and rename operations into Git's ordinary reference transaction system. The goal is to ensure the `reference-transaction` hook sees both the deletion of the old ref and the creation of the new one in a single transaction, fixing a long-standing inconsistency where hooks missed the new ref entirely.

This patch does the heavy lifting: it replaces the backend-specific `rename_ref` and `copy_ref` callbacks with ordinary transactions that queue both the source and destination as updates. The destination update carries a pointer to its source, allowing the backend to replay the source's reflog history. The patch handles edge cases like D/F conflicts (where the source and destination form a directory/file pair), case-only renames on case-insensitive filesystems, and symlink reflogs. It also preserves the distinct final reflog records of each backend: reftable now replaces destination history instead of retaining unrelated entries, and the files backend stages logs in temporary files to avoid exposing partial changes to hooks.

Performance regressions are acknowledged: files-based rename with 10,000 reflog entries slows from ~5 ms to ~17 ms, and reftable from ~44 ms to ~54 ms. The patch includes a new perf script (`t/perf/p1424-ref-copy-rename.sh`) and extensive test coverage (`t/t1424-ref-copy-transaction.sh`) for mixed transactions, hook vetoes, source races, and failure paths. The test suite verifies that hooks see the correct old/new values, that reflog history is preserved or replaced as expected, and that aborts leave no partial state.

Key technical details: the patch introduces `REF_COPY_SOURCE` and `REF_NEEDS_PACK` flags, a `copy_from` field in `struct ref_update`, and helper functions like `ref_transaction_prepare_copy` and `ref_update_record_copy_source`. It also centralizes hook suppression for internal child transactions (e.g., when a D/F rename must lock the destination in packed-refs). The files backend now stages source reflogs in temporary files to support rollback if the destination log installation fails.

The patch is **ready for substantive review**, with Patrick Steinhardt's earlier blocking objections addressed by the per-ref-update state and centralized hook suppression. The series is independent of the batched-deletion old-OID fix and targets the ref backend and hook infrastructure subsystems. Expect discussion to focus on the performance trade-offs, the idiomaticity of the new flags and helpers, and the correctness of the reflog staging logic.
