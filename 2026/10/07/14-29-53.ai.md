This is an RFC cover letter proposing a policy change to Git’s `SubmittingPatches` documentation. The patch would explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under certain conditions, aligning Git’s policy with Linux, Xen, and Debian.

The email argues that Git’s current policy—encouraging careful AI use but threatening to reject anything that looks AI-generated—creates uncertainty for contributors who produce valuable, reviewed patches. The proposed update would require contributors to review and understand their submissions, test them, meet the existing DCO and licensing requirements, and add an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI assistance, briefly explaining what the tool did and how they verified it. The author cites recent shifts in the Software Freedom Conservancy’s public guidance (which originally informed Git’s policy) and examples from other major projects that now permit responsible AI use without changing their contributor agreements.

The patch touches only the `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` file, replacing the current cautionary language with a clear, permissive framework. No code or test changes are involved. The motivation is to provide a predictable path for high-quality AI-assisted work while preserving the review standards that make patches worth merging. The email includes extensive footnotes linking to the Contributor’s Summit discussion, SFC’s updated recommendations, and parallel policies in Linux, Xen, and Debian.
