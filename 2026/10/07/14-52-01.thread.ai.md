Key technical details
No new technical changes or revisions to the merged patch. The discussion continues to reference:
- Julia Evans’s March 2024 user research, which revealed that even experienced Git users often operate with imprecise mental models (e.g., confusion around HEAD, detached HEAD, and the distinction between HEAD and lowercase "head"). The research informed her phrasing choices in the merged patch, prioritizing alignment with user language over technical precision.
- Julia’s clarification that the "wrong" user quotes came from experienced users, not novices, underscoring that imprecise mental models are widespread even among proficient practitioners. The XKCD comic (2501) is cited as a humorous but apt illustration of the broader challenge: users may succeed in practice despite gaps in their understanding.
- Junio’s latest feedback on phrasing, which distinguishes between `HEAD` *recording* a reference (branch or commit) and *being* a branch or commit. His proposed rewrite avoids anthropomorphizing `HEAD` and instead describes its role in terms of direct vs. indirect references to commits, structured as a bulleted list:
  - `HEAD` records the name of the current branch (e.g., `main`), which in turn refers to a commit. New commits advance the branch, and `HEAD` indirectly refers to the new commit.
  - `HEAD` can also record a commit object name (e.g., `abc123`). New commits are created without advancing a branch, and `HEAD` directly refers to the new commit.
  This framing explicitly ties `HEAD`’s behavior to the creation of new commits and reinforces the distinction between branch-based and detached states.
- Julia’s earlier proposed rewrite: "HEAD always refers to the current commit. Usually, it does this by pointing to a branch name (like `main`), which in turn points to a commit. But in detached HEAD state, HEAD points directly to a commit (like `abc123`)." This phrasing avoids the term "commit ID" but retains the user-friendly framing of detached HEAD as a direct reference to a commit.
- A persistent difference in assumptions between Julia and Junio about documentation goals: Julia advocates for meeting users where they are, while Junio probes whether Git’s documentation should aim to educate users toward more accurate terminology. The thread does not resolve this tension but frames it as a recurring challenge, now concluded.
