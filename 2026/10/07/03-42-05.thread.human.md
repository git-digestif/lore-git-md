This thread is a focused cleanup of Git’s test infrastructure, converting the mergesort tests from the old shell-based framework to the modern Clar unit-test system. Muhammed Dilshad A is the author, and the work responds directly to a suggestion from Patrick Steinhardt to retire unused subcommands and obsolete benchmarks. The series is now in its third version, with all prior feedback addressed—most notably simplifying the test design by replacing a sprawling 1,680-case grid with direct tests for key scenarios (sorted, reversed, equal-value, and repeatable random inputs).

Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt have both endorsed the cleanup direction, and the current patch set reflects their guidance: the leak-fix patch was dropped (since the helper is removed entirely), the Clar migration is split into three focused commits, and the final patch deletes the obsolete benchmark and helper. The net result is a smaller, more maintainable codebase—170 lines added, 477 removed—with no user-facing behavior changes. The only remaining question is whether the simplified test design fully preserves the original coverage goals, but the author’s approach aligns with Patrick’s feedback and appears uncontroversial. The series looks ready for acceptance.
