This thread is a policy discussion about whether Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` should explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. Scott Chacon proposed a patch to replace the current ambiguous prohibition with a permissive framework requiring contributors to review, test, and sign off on AI-assisted submissions (DCO), and to add an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help. The goal is to align Git’s policy with those of Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidance.

The discussion has shifted from the mechanics of the policy to its practical viability. Junio C Hamano raised concerns about enforceability, reviewer bandwidth, and whether the DCO can realistically cover AI-generated code. Brian m. carlson opposes the change on legal and ethical grounds, arguing that AI tools may reproduce training-set material from non-permissive sources without proper attribution, making DCO compliance impossible. In the latest email, Chacon counters that the risk of distributing infringing code is not unique to AI—human contributors also reuse ideas without exhaustive provenance tracking—and cites the SFC’s guidance and Linux’s precedent (where AI-assisted contributions are accepted under the same DCO) to argue that Git’s current ambiguity is overly restrictive. The debate now hinges on whether the proposed policy strikes the right balance between openness and risk, with no clear consensus yet on how to address enforceability or legal concerns. The patch remains under review, with no updates to its text.
