This thread is about a proposed update to Git’s `SubmittingPatches` documentation to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. Scott Chacon, the author, argues that Git’s current policy—while encouraging careful AI use—creates uncertainty by threatening to reject patches that appear AI-generated, even if they are well-reviewed and valuable. The proposed change would align Git’s policy with those of Linux, Xen, and Debian by requiring contributors to review, test, and understand their submissions, comply with the existing Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) and licensing rules, and add an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI assistance, briefly explaining the tool’s role and how the contributor verified the work.

The patch itself is a straightforward documentation update, replacing the current cautionary language with a permissive framework that maintains Git’s review standards while providing a predictable path for high-quality AI-assisted contributions. No code or test changes are involved. The email includes references to recent discussions at the Contributor’s Summit, updated guidance from the Software Freedom Conservancy (which originally informed Git’s policy), and examples of how other major projects have adopted similar policies without altering their contributor agreements. The thread is still in its early stages, with no responses or reviews yet.
