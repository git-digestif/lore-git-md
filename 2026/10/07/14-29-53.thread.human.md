This thread is a policy discussion about whether Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` should explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under defined conditions. Scott Chacon proposed replacing the current ambiguous language with a permissive framework requiring contributors to review, test, and sign off on AI-assisted work (DCO), and to add an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help. The patch cites updated guidance from the Software Freedom Conservancy, Linux, Xen, and Debian as precedents.

Junio C Hamano has raised sharp questions about the proposal’s practicality, arguing that the current policy—rejecting AI-generated submissions outright—is simpler and more enforceable. He doubts whether reviewers can realistically verify AI-assisted work, whether the DCO’s requirements can be met for AI-generated output, and whether the project can handle an influx of such patches without overwhelming reviewers. His concerns align with brian m. carlson’s earlier legal and ethical objections, which warned that AI-generated code may violate licensing terms or omit required attribution, creating legal risks for distributors. The thread now hinges on whether the proposed policy can address these enforcement and scalability challenges. No decisions have been made, and the patch remains under review.
