This thread is about a proposed change to Git’s `SubmittingPatches` documentation to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. The patch, submitted by Scott Chacon, would replace Git’s current ambiguous stance on AI-generated work with a permissive framework requiring human review, testing, DCO sign-off, and an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI assistance. The goal is to align Git’s policy with those of Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy, which have updated their guidelines to permit AI-assisted contributions under similar conditions.

The proposal has sparked a substantive policy debate. Scott argues that the current policy creates uncertainty and that responsible AI use should be allowed under the same review standards as human-written patches. However, brian m. carlson has raised significant legal and ethical objections, arguing that LLMs trained on code from sources without explicit permission for copying, modification, or distribution make it impossible for contributors to honestly assert the DCO. They also highlight that AI-generated output often omits required copyright and permission notices, creating potential license violations. Their concern is practical: as a Git distributor, they do not want to risk distributing code they lack the right to distribute or spend resources defending against copyright claims. They cite Gentoo’s and NetBSD’s more restrictive policies and reference prior discussions at the Contributor Summit.

The thread is still in the early review phase, with no prior versions of the patch or decisions made. Key open questions include whether the `Assisted-by:` trailer should be mandatory for any AI assistance or only for "substantial" help, whether the trailer’s explanation should follow a prescribed format, and whether reviewers should treat AI-assisted patches with extra scrutiny. The legal and ethical concerns raised by brian m. carlson are likely to be a major point of contention, as they challenge the foundational premise of the proposal. No other participant positions have been recorded yet.
