This thread is a policy discussion about whether Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` should explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. The proposal, authored by Scott Chacon, would replace the current ambiguous prohibition with a framework requiring contributors to review, test, and sign off on AI-generated code (DCO), and add an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help. The goal is to align Git with more permissive policies adopted by Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidance.

The discussion remains contentious, with no consensus in sight. Key objections include legal and ethical concerns about DCO compliance and training-data provenance (brian m. carlson), philosophical skepticism about AI’s role in open-source contributions (Luca Milanesio and D. Ben Knoble), and operational worries about reviewer bandwidth and enforceability (Junio C Hamano). Scott Chacon argues that the current policy is equally unenforceable and that responsible AI use won’t worsen the signal-to-noise ratio, but brian counters that the SFC’s guidance isn’t an endorsement and that several member projects (like QEMU) maintain restrictive policies.

Patrick Steinhardt’s recent reply shifts the focus to the practical impact on reviewers. He reports a surge in low-quality AI-generated patches over the past few months, which has already strained review capacity. The proposed policy, he warns, would remove the current social friction that discourages contributors from submitting code they don’t fully understand, leading to even more unreviewable noise. His stance is pragmatic: Git’s bottleneck is reviewer bandwidth, not code production, and the change risks overwhelming an already stretched system. This adds a new dimension to the debate—whether the project should prioritize openness or reviewer sanity—and underscores the challenge of crafting a policy that balances both. The thread now hinges on whether the proposed text can realistically enforce responsible AI use without becoming a dead letter.
