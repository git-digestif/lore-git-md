The Git mailing list is debating a proposed update to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` that would explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions—review, testing, DCO sign-off, and an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help. The patch, introduced by Scott Chacon, aims to replace Git’s current ambiguous stance with a permissive framework modeled after Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidance. However, the discussion has quickly shifted from policy mechanics to deeper questions about legal, ethical, and practical viability.

Key voices have emerged with starkly opposing views. Scott Chacon argues that responsible AI use should be permitted under the same review standards as human-written code, citing precedents in other major projects and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s guidance. Junio C Hamano has raised operational concerns, questioning whether reviewers can realistically enforce the proposed requirements and warning of potential reviewer burnout from low-quality submissions. Brian m. carlson and Luca Milanesio have both opposed the change on legal and ethical grounds, with carlson highlighting the risk of reproducing training-set material from non-permissive sources and Milanesio questioning who bears responsibility for malicious code or copyright violations. The latest reply from Kristoffer Haugsbakk frames the debate as overstated, dismissing the analogy between human and AI contributions while treating the decision to accept legal risks as a personal choice rather than a project-wide policy matter.

The patch itself is narrowly scoped, modifying only `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to introduce the `Assisted-by:` trailer and outline requirements for AI-assisted contributions. No code or technical changes are involved, but the open questions remain significant: whether the trailer should be mandatory for any AI assistance or only substantial help, how reviewers should handle AI-assisted patches, and whether the DCO text should explicitly address AI. The thread is still under review, with no clear consensus in sight.
