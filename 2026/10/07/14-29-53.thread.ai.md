Thread identity
Subject: [RFC PATCH] SubmittingPatches: allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions
Series version: standalone patch (no versioning yet)
Originating author: Scott Chacon <scott@gitbutter.net>
Thread type: policy RFC (documentation change)

Core problem or goal
Amend Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions provided the contributor reviews, understands, tests, and signs off on the submission (DCO), and adds an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help, briefly describing the tool’s role and the contributor’s verification steps. The goal is to replace the current ambiguous language (“we may reject anything that looks AI-generated”) with a predictable, permissive framework that aligns with Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidance.

Current status
Under review; no prior versions. The patch touches only `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, replacing the cautionary paragraph with a new section that lists the requirements (review, testing, DCO, `Assisted-by:` trailer) and cites the SFC’s revised stance and other projects’ policies.

Key technical details
- File modified: `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`
- New trailer: `Assisted-by:` (analogous to `Helped-by:`)
- No code, build system, or test changes
- No on-disk format or CLI changes
- No new symbols or renamed functions

Open questions and loose ends
- Whether the `Assisted-by:` trailer should be mandatory for *any* AI assistance or only for “substantial” help (the patch currently says “substantial”).
- Whether the trailer’s explanation field should be free-form or follow a prescribed format (e.g., “Tool: X; Verification: Y”).
- Whether the policy should require reviewers to treat AI-assisted patches differently (e.g., extra scrutiny, additional test coverage).
- Whether the patch should also update the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) text to mention AI assistance explicitly.
- **New**: Legal and ethical feasibility of AI-assisted contributions under the DCO, given that LLMs may reproduce training-set material from sources that do not permit copying, modification, or distribution, and that AI-generated output typically omits required copyright and permission notices, creating potential license violations.

Participant positions
- Scott Chacon (author): proposes the change, arguing that the current policy creates uncertainty and that responsible AI use should be allowed under the same review standards as human-written patches. Cites SFC’s updated guidance and parallel policies in Linux, Xen, and Debian.
- **New**: brian m. carlson: opposes the change on legal and ethical grounds. Argues that LLMs are trained on code from sources that do not permit copying, modification, or distribution, and that AI-generated output may reproduce training-set material without proper attribution or license compliance, making it impossible for contributors to honestly assert the DCO. Cites Gentoo’s and NetBSD’s more restrictive policies and references the Contributor Summit discussion where this issue was debated. Their concern is practical: as a Git distributor, they do not want to risk distributing code they lack the right to distribute or spend resources defending against copyright-infringement claims.

Related work
- Linux kernel’s `Documentation/process/submitting-patches.rst` (updated in 2024 to allow AI assistance with similar requirements).
- Xen Project’s contributor policy (permits AI assistance with disclosure).
- Debian’s AI assistance guidelines (published in 2024).
- Software Freedom Conservancy’s 2025 update to its public guidance on AI and FOSS contributions.
- Gentoo’s and NetBSD’s policies on AI-assisted contributions (more restrictive).
- Contributor Summit discussion on AI and FOSS contributions.
