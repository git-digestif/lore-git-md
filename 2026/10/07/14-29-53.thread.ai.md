Thread identity
Subject: [RFC PATCH] SubmittingPatches: allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions
Series version: standalone patch (no versioning yet)
Originating author: Scott Chacon <scott@gitbutter.net>
Thread type: policy RFC (documentation change)

Core problem or goal
Amend Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions provided the contributor reviews, understands, tests, and signs off on the submission (DCO), and adds an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help, briefly describing the tool’s role and the contributor’s verification steps. The goal is to replace the current ambiguous prohibition with a predictable, permissive framework aligned with Linux, Xen, Debian, and the Software Freedom Conservancy’s updated guidance.

Current status
Under review; no prior versions. The patch modifies only `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, replacing the cautionary paragraph with a new section that lists requirements (review, testing, DCO, `Assisted-by:` trailer) and cites other projects’ policies. Junio C Hamano’s reply raises operational concerns about enforceability, reviewer bandwidth, and DCO compliance, shifting the discussion from policy mechanics to practical viability. Scott Chacon’s latest reply frames the debate as a choice between Git’s current conservative stance and the more permissive policies adopted by larger projects with comparable legal exposure, positioning the patch as a reasoned middle ground.

Key technical details
- File modified: `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`
- New trailer: `Assisted-by:` (analogous to `Helped-by:`), required for “substantial” AI assistance
- No code, build system, test, CLI, or on-disk format changes
- No new symbols or renamed functions

Open questions and loose ends
- Whether the `Assisted-by:` trailer should be mandatory for *any* AI assistance or only for “substantial” help (patch currently says “substantial”).
- Whether the trailer’s explanation field should be free-form or follow a prescribed format (e.g., “Tool: X; Verification: Y”).
- Whether reviewers should treat AI-assisted patches differently (e.g., extra scrutiny, additional test coverage).
- Whether the DCO text should explicitly mention AI assistance.
- Legal and ethical feasibility of AI-assisted contributions under the DCO, given potential reproduction of training-set material from non-permissive sources and omission of required copyright/permission notices (brian m. carlson’s concern).
- Enforceability of the proposed requirements—whether reviewers can realistically verify that contributors have reviewed, understood, and tested AI-generated code, and whether the policy risks overwhelming reviewers with low-quality submissions (Junio’s concern).
- Discrepancy with Linux kernel’s guidelines, which avoid naming specific AI tools (to prevent free advertising) and grant maintainers broader discretion to reject contributions outright.
- Whether the rarity of complex code reproduction from training sets justifies the policy change, given the precedent set by Linux and other projects (Scott Chacon’s argument).

Participant positions
- Scott Chacon (author): proposes the change, arguing the current policy creates uncertainty and that responsible AI use should be allowed under the same review standards as human-written patches. Cites SFC’s updated guidance and parallel policies in Linux, Xen, and Debian. Frames the debate as a choice between Git’s current conservative stance and the more permissive policies adopted by larger projects, positioning the patch as a reasoned middle ground. References Linus Torvalds’ acceptance of AI-assisted contributions under the same DCO and license.
- brian m. carlson: opposes the change on legal and ethical grounds. Argues LLMs may reproduce training-set material from non-permissive sources without proper attribution or license compliance, making it impossible for contributors to honestly assert the DCO. Cites Gentoo’s and NetBSD’s more restrictive policies and references the Contributor Summit discussion.
- Junio C Hamano: raises operational concerns about enforceability, reviewer bandwidth, and DCO compliance. Questions whether the proposed text can be realistically enforced and whether it risks overwhelming reviewers with plausible-sounding but low-quality submissions. Notes the Linux kernel’s approach avoids naming specific tools and gives maintainers broader discretion to reject contributions.

Related work
- Linux kernel’s `Documentation/process/submitting-patches.rst` (updated in 2024 to allow AI assistance with similar requirements but avoids naming tools and grants maintainers broader rejection discretion).
- Xen Project’s contributor policy (permits AI assistance with disclosure).
- Debian’s AI assistance guidelines (published in 2024).
- Software Freedom Conservancy’s 2025 update to its public guidance on AI and FOSS contributions.
- Gentoo’s and NetBSD’s policies on AI-assisted contributions (more restrictive).
- Contributor Summit discussion on AI and FOSS contributions.
