This thread began as a documentation improvement to rewrite Git’s explanation of detached HEAD state, which Julia Evans successfully landed in `master` on July 10, 2024. The patch replaced the existing section in `git-checkout.adoc` with a concise, practical guide (`gitdetached-head(7)`) that frames detached HEAD as useful for experimentation—such as testing commits or browsing history—while clarifying how users might end up in this state accidentally. Junio C Hamano accepted the final version (v4) after all prior feedback was addressed, though a follow-up bugfix was needed to fix a test failure caused by backtick-quoted commands in the new advice message.

The thread has since shifted to a broader discussion about documentation methodology. In this latest exchange, Julia Evans responds to Junio’s request for a clearer explanation of her phrasing choices by sharing insights from user research she conducted in March 2024. She argues that effective documentation should build on the language and mental models users already hold, even if those models are technically imprecise, rather than introducing new terminology that may not resonate. Her email includes quotes from experienced Git users illustrating common (if sometimes incorrect) understandings of HEAD, such as viewing it as a pointer to the current commit or conflating it with lowercase "head." While this discussion doesn’t propose immediate changes, it highlights a tension between technical precision and usability in Git’s documentation—a theme that may inform future improvements. The merged patch itself remains stable, with no further revisions planned.
