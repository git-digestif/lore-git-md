This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Scott Chacon’s `sha1dc-accel` performance series, focusing on the vectorized unavoidable-bit-conditions (UBC) check. Johannes Schindelin engages deeply with the implementation, offering both a **practical tool** and **technical feedback** on the NEON backend.

Schindelin provides a **Perl script** that regenerates the 1,789-line `ubc_check.c` tables from the original Rust source (`srijs/sha1dc`). The script is offered as a way to validate the translation and ease future updates if the Rust solver improves. While he notes the script was AI-assisted, he asserts it meets his personal quality bar and suggests including it in the series (either in this patch or a follow-up) to make the tables maintainable.

On the NEON implementation, Schindelin identifies a **readability nit**: the current code uses `vqsubq_u32` or `vminq_u32` to mask lanes based on `g->want`, but this obscures the intent. He suggests replacing it with `vbicq_u32` (bitwise clear) or `vandq_u32` (bitwise and) to make the masking logic clearer. The change is purely stylistic—no performance or correctness impact—but aligns better with NEON idioms. He defers to Scott’s judgment on whether to apply this or similar cleanups elsewhere in the vectorized code.

The review is **tested** (Schindelin verified the script’s output) and **substantive**, addressing both infrastructure (the Perl script) and low-level implementation details. It does not challenge the series’ overall design or performance claims but improves maintainability and clarity. The NEON suggestion is minor, but the script is a significant contribution to the series’ long-term viability.
