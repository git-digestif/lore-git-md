This is a documentation patch proposing a policy change for the use of AI tools in Git contributions. It updates the `SubmittingPatches` document to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions while setting clear expectations for human responsibility, disclosure, and quality.

The patch replaces the current AI section, which warns against AI-generated content and threatens rejection based on appearance, with a more permissive framework. The new policy requires contributors to review, understand, and test AI-assisted work; comply with the Developer's Certificate of Origin (DCO); disclose substantial AI assistance via an `Assisted-by:` trailer; and maintain records of prompts and outputs for review purposes. It also reserves maintainers' right to request further evidence or decline submissions they cannot confidently assess.

The commit message frames this as aligning Git with modern, legally reviewed approaches taken by peer GPL projects like the Linux kernel, citing a specific reference. The author discloses their own use of an OpenAI model to craft the policy language. The patch touches only the `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` file, adding 49 lines and removing 24.

This is an RFC patch, signaling it is intended to spark discussion rather than being ready for merging. The proposed policy is likely to be controversial, as it directly addresses a sensitive topic where the project has historically taken a cautious stance. Key points of contention may include the definition of "substantial assistance," the practicality of enforcing provenance requirements, and whether the policy sufficiently mitigates risks of low-quality or legally problematic submissions. The patch does not address how reviewers should handle the increased burden of assessing AI-assisted work.
