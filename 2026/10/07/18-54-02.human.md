This is a **substantive review** of the `--non-intrusive` clone proposal, aimed at clarifying the threat model.

D. Ben Knoble pushes back on the premise that `git clone` itself is the attack vector. He points out that a fresh clone cannot automatically install or enable hooks—hooks must be explicitly placed in `.git/hooks/` and made executable by the user or another tool (e.g., npm’s `post-install` scripts). The real risk, he argues, comes from archives that include a pre-populated `.git/` directory, which *can* contain malicious hooks. His review shifts the discussion from “how do we make `git clone` safer?” to “what is the actual threat we are trying to mitigate?” and suggests that user education (“be careful with untrusted archives”) is the current best defense. The tone is constructive, but the implication is that the original proposal may be solving the wrong problem.
