Subject: [RFC PATCH] SubmittingPatches: allow AI-assisted contributions under clear guidelines
Series version: RFC (single patch)
Originating author: Scott Chacon <scott@gitbutler.net>
Thread type: policy RFC

Core problem or goal
Propose a policy change to explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions to Git, replacing the current blanket warning with a structured framework that requires human responsibility, disclosure, and quality control. The goal is to modernize Git’s stance while mitigating risks of low-quality or legally problematic submissions.

Current status
Under discussion (RFC stage). No prior versions. No maintainer decisions or merge status recorded. The patch touches only `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, adding 49 lines and removing 24 to replace the existing AI section.

Key technical details
- File modified: `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`
- New trailer: `Assisted-by:` for disclosing substantial AI assistance
- Policy requirements: human review, DCO compliance, prompt/output record-keeping, maintainer discretion
- No new symbols, CLI options, or on-disk changes
- Cites Linux kernel’s approach as precedent

Open questions and loose ends
- Definition of "substantial assistance" (threshold for `Assisted-by:` trailer)
- Practicality of enforcing provenance requirements (prompt/output records)
- Reviewer burden for assessing AI-assisted work (not addressed in patch)
- Legal/quality risks of submissions that cannot be confidently assessed
- Community consensus on permissiveness vs. caution

Participant positions

Related work
