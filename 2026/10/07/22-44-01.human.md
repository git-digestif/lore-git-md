This is a policy RFC that proposes to update Git’s `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. The patch itself is a straightforward documentation change, replacing the current ambiguous caution with a permissive framework that requires contributors to review, test, and sign off on AI-assisted work, and to disclose substantial AI help via an `Assisted-by:` trailer.

Junio’s reply is a skeptical but measured review from the maintainer’s perspective. He plays devil’s advocate, questioning whether the proposed rules are enforceable in practice and whether they adequately address the legal and practical risks of AI-generated content. His concerns center on three main points: (1) the difficulty of contributors honestly certifying that AI output complies with licensing and attribution requirements, (2) the risk of reviewers being overwhelmed by plausible-sounding but low-quality AI-generated patches, and (3) whether the policy gives maintainers enough flexibility to reject submissions they cannot confidently assess. He also notes that the Linux kernel’s guidelines differ in key details—such as avoiding naming specific AI tools to prevent free advertising—and suggests the proposed text could be more aligned with those nuances.

The discussion is now squarely about policy trade-offs: whether Git should adopt a permissive stance like Linux and Debian, or maintain a more restrictive approach like Gentoo and NetBSD. Junio’s questions are not rhetorical; they highlight real gaps in the current proposal and signal that the patch will need revision to address enforceability and maintainer discretion.
