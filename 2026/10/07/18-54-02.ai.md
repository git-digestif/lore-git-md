Ben Knoble replies to Luca Di Carlo’s RFC with a key technical correction: Git’s `clone` operation itself does not execute hooks, so the immediate threat model (malicious hooks shipped in a repository) is not directly exploitable via `clone`. He clarifies that the real risk comes from archives containing a `.git/` directory with pre-installed hooks, which Git *will* execute when operations like `commit` or `push` are run. This distinction shifts the discussion from "how do we make `clone` safer?" to "how do we protect users from malicious `.git/` directories in archives?"

The review is concise but substantive, as it identifies a gap in the original threat model and implicitly questions whether `--non-intrusive` is the right solution. It also hints at existing mitigations (`safe.directory`, `ignore-scripts` in npm) and suggests that user education ("be careful") is insufficient but may be the current best option. The tone is constructive, leaving room for Luca or others to refine the proposal or pivot to addressing archive-based threats instead. No alternative implementation is proposed, but the feedback is likely to shape future iterations of the idea.
