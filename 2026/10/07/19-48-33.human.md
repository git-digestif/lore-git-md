This is the second patch in a **documentation** series replacing the outdated `gittutorial` with a beginner-focused guide. The patch itself is the full rewrite of `Documentation/gittutorial.adoc`, covering local repository setup, basic commands (`git init`, `git add`, `git commit`, `git diff`, `git status`, `git restore`), and pushing to a remote host (GitHub/GitLab) via SSH.

D. Ben Knoble’s review is **surface-level**, focusing on wording, platform assumptions, and minor UX details. He raises questions about whether to clarify UNIX-like environment requirements, suggest alternatives to `git commit -m`, or adjust explanations of `git diff` output for colorblind users. His feedback is constructive but avoids engaging with the tutorial’s core structure or pedagogy. Notably, he endorses the current approach to SSH setup and repository creation (via forge UI) despite its fragility, deferring to external guides for advanced topics like per-host SSH keys.

The patch is well-crafted and addresses a clear need: the existing tutorial is outdated, and Julia Evans’ user-tested replacement reflects modern workflows. The open questions from the cover letter (repository creation and authentication) remain unresolved, but Knoble’s review does not challenge the series’ direction. This looks like a straightforward improvement for beginners, though the forge-dependent steps may require periodic updates. Worth watching for broader feedback on the structural choices.
