This is a policy proposal to update Git's `SubmittingPatches` documentation. The patch relaxes the project's current stance on AI-assisted contributions, aligning it with practices adopted by Linux, Xen, and Debian. Instead of discouraging or implicitly rejecting AI-generated work, the new text would explicitly allow it under three conditions: contributors must review and understand the entire submission, meet existing DCO and licensing requirements, and add an `Assisted-by` trailer for substantial AI assistance, briefly explaining what the tool did and how they verified it.

The motivation is practical: the current policy creates uncertainty for contributors who use AI tools responsibly but fear their patches will be rejected if they disclose the assistance. The author argues that the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) already covers this scenario, as it requires the contributor to have the right to submit the work, regardless of how it was produced. The patch cites recent guidance from the Software Freedom Conservancy (SFC), which now recommends disclosure and record-keeping over outright bans, and points to Linux and Xen as precedents for using `Assisted-by` trailers.

This is a cover letter for a single-patch series, so the actual change is straightforward: a rewrite of the relevant section in `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`. The tone is collaborative, framing the update as a way to encourage valuable contributions while maintaining the project's quality standards. The patch is marked as an RFC, signaling that the author expects discussion rather than immediate acceptance.

The proposal is likely to be controversial.
