This is a **bugfix patch series** (v3) targeting the ref backend and hook infrastructure, addressing the long-standing issue where `reference-transaction` hooks miss the creation of new refs during branch rename (`git branch -m`) and copy (`git branch -c`) operations. The series now consists of four focused patches, splitting the work into logical steps: internal hook suppression, reflog replacement support, copy/rename integration, and removal of backend-specific callbacks.

The redesign follows Patrick Steinhardt’s review feedback from v2, abandoning the transaction-wide copy/rename state in favor of per-ref-update tracking. This preserves the genericity of the transaction API and enables multi-ref operations (e.g., renaming A→X, B→Y, and C→Z in one transaction). The series introduces `ref_transaction_replace_reflog()` to discard destination reflog history before installing queued entries, replacing the old backend-specific callbacks with a unified transaction-based approach. Hook suppression is now centralized in `run_transaction_hook()`, and the pre-lock revalidation step (criticized in v2) has been dropped, aligning with the documented expectation that hooks may modify refs during the "preparing" phase.

Performance regressions are acknowledged but deemed acceptable: files-based rename operations slow by ~137% for large reflogs (10,000 entries), while reftable sees smaller overhead (~20-25%). The series includes a new perf script (`t/perf/p1424-ref-copy-rename.sh`) and thorough test coverage for mixed transactions, hook vetoes, source races, and failure paths. Backend-specific work remains necessary (e.g., files staging logs in temporary files, reftable writing tombstones), but the logical ref updates and reflog history are now handled through ordinary transactions.

The series is **ready for substantive review**, with Steinhardt’s earlier blocking objections addressed by the split and refactoring. Key files touched include `refs.c`, `refs/files-backend.c`, `refs/reftable-backend.c`, and new test scripts. The base is `0f8e75abeb`, and the series is independent of the batched-deletion old-OID fix discussed elsewhere. Expect this to advance to `next` if reviews confirm the architectural improvements.
