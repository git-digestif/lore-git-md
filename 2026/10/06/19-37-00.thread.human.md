This thread is about replacing Git’s outdated `gittutorial` man page with a modern, beginner-focused two-part guide tested on 112 users. The new tutorial, authored by Julia Evans, covers local repository creation and commits in Part 1 and pushing to a remote host (GitHub/GitLab) in Part 2. The series is now in its third version (v3), mechanically complete, and ready for merging after addressing all feedback—both substantive and surface-level.

Key participants include Julia Evans, who drove the effort with user testing and incorporated feedback on platform assumptions, editor warnings, and SSH key handling; D. Ben Knoble, who provided surface-level reviews focusing on wording and UX refinements; and Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, who has yet to weigh in on v3 but previously raised minor AsciiDoc formatting and SSH key generation nits. The series is uncontroversial, with no unresolved technical objections, though a few open questions remain, such as whether to start with `git init` or `git clone` and how to handle SSH/HTTPS setup generically.

The latest email from Richard Kerry, a Windows-based developer, highlights platform-specific concerns: the tutorial’s reliance on `vim` and `ssh` may alienate Windows users, who often prefer GUI clients, HTTPS authentication, and editors like Notepad++ or Visual Studio. While his feedback doesn’t demand changes or block merging, it underscores a tension between the tutorial’s Linux-centric tooling and Windows usability, which could inform future iterations. The series remains on track for merging, pending Junio’s final review.
