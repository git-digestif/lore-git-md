Julia Evans’s patch series replacing the outdated `gittutorial` man page with a modern, user-tested beginner guide has reached its final stage. The series, now at version 5, was merged to Junio C Hamano’s `next` branch for integration testing after addressing all substantive and surface-level feedback. The new guide—split into two parts covering local repository creation and remote pushes—was tested with 112 users and reflects current workflows while avoiding confusion about Git’s decentralized model.

The only remaining loose ends are minor: a broken cross-reference in `Documentation/git.adoc` that fails `make check-docs` (acknowledged by Junio, who plans a follow-up fix) and a few unresolved pedagogical questions, such as whether to start with `git init` or `git clone` and whether to specify `ssh-keygen -t ed25519` for key generation. Julia defends the current pragmatic choices (e.g., `git commit -m`, local `git init`, and platform-specific authentication instructions) but remains open to post-merge iteration based on user feedback. The series is uncontroversial, with all reviewers—including Junio and D. Ben Knoble—focusing on surface-level refinements rather than substantive objections. The final hurdle was a mechanical build issue caused by dangling references after `gittutorial.adoc`’s removal; Junio provided a fixup commit to comment out these references, ensuring the build stays functional at every step. The series is now in `next` and on track for graduation to `master`.
