This is a **performance feature** patch reply in the ongoing incremental connectivity check series. Kristofer Karlsson responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s substantive review of the implementation (2/2), clarifying the trust model, boundary detection, and error handling while agreeing to split the patch for better reviewability.

The key technical clarification is how the incremental verifier establishes its trust boundary: it relies on `git rev-list --not --all` to implicitly identify already-connected commits (the "old tips"), then processes incoming commits in reverse topological order. Each commit’s tree is verified only against its parents’ trusted trees, with memoization ensuring objects seen in trusted trees are skipped. This avoids parsing all refs upfront, though Kristofer acknowledges that ref-parsing overhead remains a separate scalability challenge (out of scope for this series).

On error handling, Kristofer defends the use of `die()` for cycles in the incoming commit graph, noting that the existing `rev-list`-based implementation already uses this pattern and that the subprocess architecture ensures clean error propagation to the parent process (e.g., `git-receive-pack`). However, he concedes that a single-process implementation with non-fatal variants would be a worthwhile long-term improvement.

The reply also resolves Patrick’s question about "parents" in the context of force pushes or new references: the verifier always compares a commit to its direct parents (e.g., `T vs T^1, T^2`), regardless of whether those parents are preexisting or incoming. This inductive approach ensures correctness as long as ancestors are processed before descendants.

Kristofer accepts Patrick’s suggestion to split the patch into smaller commits (option introduction, benchmark, `rev-list` flag, and strategy implementation), which should improve reviewability. He also agrees to minor documentation tweaks, such as replacing "Worked example" with "Example" and clarifying the inductive trust model in the commit message.

No new technical objections are raised, but the discussion leaves open questions about the long-term viability of the subprocess architecture and the scalability of ref-parsing overhead. The series remains under active review, with a v3 likely to incorporate these changes. Key files touched include `tree-verify.c`, `builtin/rev-list.c`, and `connected.c`; the new config key `transfer.connectivityCheck=incremental` and `rev-list` flag `--verify-trees-incremental` are central to the implementation.
