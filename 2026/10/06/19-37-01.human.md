This is a documentation patch removing the old `gittutorial` man page. It is the first of two patches: the next one will introduce a replacement tutorial. The author (Julia Evans) explains that splitting the removal and addition into separate commits makes the changes easier to review on the mailing list, since the new content is entirely different.

The deleted file (`Documentation/gittutorial.adoc`) is a 676-line AsciiDoc document that walks new users through basic Git concepts: initializing a repository, making commits, branching, merging, collaborating with others, and exploring history. It has been part of Git’s documentation for many years but is now being retired in favor of a fresh tutorial.

The commit message is minimal but clear about the intent. There is no controversy here—this is a straightforward cleanup step before introducing updated material. The patch itself is mechanical (a single file deletion) and unlikely to generate much discussion on its own. Readers who care about Git’s documentation or onboarding experience may want to watch for the follow-up patch that introduces the replacement.
