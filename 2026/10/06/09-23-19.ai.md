This is a substantive review of Domen Kožar’s v3 1/2 patch introducing the unified `post-worktree` hook. Phillip Wood engages deeply with the design and implementation, raising several technical concerns and suggesting alternatives. The review is thorough, referencing specific code paths, test cases, and prior discussion, and it surfaces edge cases that could affect the hook’s usability and correctness.

Phillip’s core critique centers on the hook’s interface and execution model. He questions the usefulness of the worktree identifier (the administrative directory name) as an argument, arguing that it is not a user-facing concept and that different repositories can have worktrees with the same ID, making it unreliable as a lookup key. Instead, he proposes passing the worktree’s absolute git-dir, which is both unique and directly usable by tools. He also challenges the four-argument interface (`event`, `id`, `old-path`, `new-path`), suggesting that padding with empty strings for `add` and `remove` events is unnecessarily complex and that the hook should pass only the relevant paths for each event. This would simplify the interface and reduce the risk of tools misinterpreting empty strings.

The review also raises timing concerns for the `remove` event. Phillip points out that notifying tools *after* the worktree is removed may be too late for them to clean up associated resources (e.g., container environments, IDE state). He suggests a `worktree-event` hook that runs *before* removal (but after addition or movement) to give tools a chance to act before the worktree is gone. This is a significant design question: whether the hook’s purpose is observability (after the fact) or coordination (before the fact), and whether the current design adequately serves the latter.

Phillip questions the necessity of running the hook serially, noting that parallel execution could improve performance for tools that do not need strict ordering. He also flags minor test redundancy, suggesting that separate tests for paths with spaces or orphan worktrees may not be necessary given the existing coverage. These are surface-level but practical concerns that could streamline the patch without affecting functionality.

The review is constructive and forward-looking. Phillip explicitly endorses the goal of adding worktree lifecycle hooks, calling wrapper scripts "a pain" due to their fragility and the difficulty of ensuring all callers use them. His feedback is aimed at refining the design rather than rejecting it, and he engages with Domen’s technical rationale (e.g., the hook’s execution environment, the order of `post-checkout` and `post-worktree`) while pushing for clearer justification and simpler alternatives.

Key technical details for follow-up discussion:
- The worktree identifier (`id`) vs. absolute git-dir as an argument.
- The four-argument interface vs. event-specific argument lists.
- Timing of the `remove` event (after vs. before worktree deletion).
- Serial vs. parallel hook execution.
- Test coverage for edge cases (spaces, orphan worktrees).

The review does not address Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s `--id=<string>` counterproposal or Domen’s framing of hooks and IDs as complementary, focusing instead on the internal design of the hook itself. Junio C Hamano’s fundamental objection to hooks remains unresolved, but Phillip’s feedback is orthogonal to that debate, targeting the implementation rather than the necessity of the feature. The patch is not yet in `next`; this review is likely to shape the next iteration.
