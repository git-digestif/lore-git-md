This is a small but user-visible documentation patch that aligns Git's merge conflict advice with the rest of its conflict-resolution workflows. Julia Evans's patch updates the advice message shown during a merge conflict to suggest `git merge --continue` instead of `git commit`, matching the advice already given for rebase, revert, and cherry-pick. The change is uncontroversial and well-motivated, addressing a minor inconsistency in Git's user interface without altering behavior—`git merge --continue` and `git commit` are already documented as equivalent.

Phillip Wood's review is surface-level, focusing on a minor grammatical tweak to the advice text. He suggests adjusting the phrasing from *"to conclude merge"* to *"to conclude the merge"* for better readability, but otherwise endorses the change as sensible. The patch itself touches `wt-status.c` and updates two test scripts (`t7060-wtstatus.sh` and `t7512-status-help.sh`) to reflect the new wording. Julia notes that translations will need updating, but the change is minimal—just a command name substitution—so the process should be straightforward.

The patch is part of a broader effort to improve Git's user experience by ensuring consistency across its advice messages. Since `git merge --continue` was introduced in 2016 as a synonym for `git commit`, this update brings the merge workflow in line with other conflict-resolution commands. No objections or deeper technical concerns have been raised, and the change is unlikely to be controversial. If the grammatical tweak is incorporated, this patch is likely to be merged soon.
