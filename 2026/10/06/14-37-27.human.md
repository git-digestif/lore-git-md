This is a review of a feature patch that proposes a new warning for clock skew during commit creation. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, raises fundamental concerns about the value and practicality of the warning. He points out that clock skew is often outside the user's control—such as when cloning a repository where the tip commit was made on a machine with a misconfigured clock—and asks what the user is supposed to do in response to the warning. His examples illustrate that even if the warning were emitted, it would not reliably solve the underlying problem: date-based history traversal (e.g., `git log --since`) remains inherently unreliable when clocks are not synchronized across contributors.

Junio also notes that the warning would only catch skew in the immediate parent-child relationship of the commit being created, not deeper or broader skew in the history. This means the warning could miss cases where skew still breaks date-based traversal, giving users a false sense of security. His broader point is that time-based queries in Git are fundamentally fragile and should not be treated as authoritative; users who need reliable results should rely on topology-based queries instead.

The review is substantive and challenges the core motivation of the patch. It does not reject the patch outright but suggests that the benefit may not justify the added complexity or user friction. No alternative implementation is proposed, but the tone implies skepticism about whether this is a problem worth solving in Git itself. The author will likely need to address these concerns—either by refining the scope of the warning, demonstrating a clearer use case, or reconsidering whether the feature belongs in Git at all.
