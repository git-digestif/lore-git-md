This is the second patch in Phillip Wood’s two-patch refactoring series `pw/stash-optimize-check_changes-calls/v1`, which consolidates redundant change-detection logic in `git stash push` and `git stash create`. The patch itself is a straightforward cleanup: it removes the duplicate `check_changes()` call in `do_push_stash()` and adjusts the return-value convention of `do_create_stash()` so that 2 now signals “no changes to stash” (previously conflated with “nothing was stashed”).

Junio C Hamano’s review raises a new technical concern: after the patch, `do_push_stash()` calls `repo_refresh_and_write_index()` unconditionally, then invokes `do_create_stash()`, which refreshes the index again. This means the index is refreshed twice in succession when running `git stash push`, even though nothing has changed in between. The patch does eliminate one redundant `check_changes()` call, but at the cost of introducing a redundant index refresh. Junio does not object to the patch’s direction, but the observation suggests the optimization is incomplete and may need a follow-up to avoid the double refresh. No other issues are raised; the earlier commit-message typos noted in the first patch are not repeated here.
