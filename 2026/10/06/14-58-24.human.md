This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically addressing Phillip Wood’s proposal to extend `git rebase --update-refs` to automate rebasing interdependent branches. Nico Williams clarifies that the problem his `bisect-rebase` script solves—rebasing quickly across thousands of upstream commits—is orthogonal to the stacked-branch workflow Phillip described. While `--update-refs` could theoretically be combined with a bisection-based approach, Nico argues they serve different purposes: one automates branch dependency management, while the other minimizes conflict resolution steps in large rebases.

Nico also responds to Phillip’s suggestion of inserting "break" commands into the rebase sequence to allow inspection via `git range-diff`. He notes that `bisect-rebase` already stops at conflicts requiring user intervention, and the bisection noise (progress output) is mostly cosmetic. He questions whether stopping at every bisection midpoint would be useful, as it could make the process tedious if conflicts aren’t trivially resolvable at each step. His counterproposal—allowing users to optionally skip the rest of the bisection if conflicts are obvious—hints at a potential feature but acknowledges it might not be practical.

The email is a substantive review, engaging with the technical feasibility of Phillip’s proposal while defending the bisection-based approach’s efficiency for large-scale rebases. Nico’s perspective carries weight given his experience using `bisect-rebase` on real-world repositories (e.g., rebasing 10,000+ PostgreSQL commits). The thread remains unresolved on whether Git should prioritize built-in support for stacked branches (`--update-refs`) or incremental conflict resolution (bisection scripts), but Nico’s input reinforces the value of the latter for performance-critical use cases.
