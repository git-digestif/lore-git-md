Christian frames the questions as potentially naive but necessary to assess whether the interoperability work could be upstreamed in time to smooth the Git 3.0 transition. He acknowledges the project’s prior stance that the interoperability work should not block Git 3.0 but suggests that merging at least part of it soon could still improve the transition experience. The questions cover five key areas: public availability of the current state, completeness (especially submodule support), estimated effort, collaboration opportunities, and realistic testing strategies.

The tone is constructive and forward-looking, focusing on how to move the interoperability work forward rather than re-litigating the strategic merits of the tree-digest proposal or the Git 3.0 plan. Christian’s offer to help and his inquiry about open collaboration with the unnamed company signal a willingness to treat the interoperability work as a community effort. The questions about testing (real-world repositories, mixed clients/servers, interoperability with other implementations) underscore the complexity of the transition and the need for thorough validation.

This email is likely to prompt brian m. carlson to clarify the status and roadmap of the interoperability work, which could in turn help the community decide whether to prioritize it for inclusion before Git 3.0. If the work is close to ready and the company involved is willing to collaborate openly, it might reduce the perceived urgency of the tree-digest proposal as a stopgap.
