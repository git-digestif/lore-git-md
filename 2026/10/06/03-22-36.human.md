This is a follow-up in the ongoing bug report about Git’s ZIP timestamp handling and `fast-import` date validation. Matthew E. Luallen provides concrete examples of how real-world tools (Python’s `ZipInfo.date_time`, UnZip, and bsdtar) behave when encountering the problematic timestamps described in the original report.

The key new information here is the **tool compatibility impact**: Python’s `ZipInfo.date_time` misinterprets a 1972 commit’s DOS timestamp as 2100, even though the correct Unix timestamp is present in the archive. Meanwhile, UnZip and bsdtar correctly restore the 1972 date, but UnZip’s *update mode* behaves unexpectedly with post-2106 timestamps—retaining outdated content instead of updating it. These observations strengthen the case for addressing the issues, as they demonstrate that the bugs aren’t just theoretical but affect downstream workflows.

Matthew’s reply doesn’t propose a specific fix, but it reinforces the need for clarity in how Git should handle edge cases. The distinction between the 1972 (DOS overflow) and 2106 (Unix timestamp wrap) cases remains important, as they affect different tools in different ways. The thread is still focused on gathering data and understanding trade-offs, but this update makes the real-world stakes clearer for anyone considering a patch.
