This is a performance optimization patch targeting the packed-refs backend. Karthik Nayak posts v3 of a standalone patch that speeds up operations rewriting the packed-refs file (like deleting references) by avoiding redundant formatting of unchanged refs. Instead of using `fprintf()` for every entry, the patch now tracks raw byte positions in the snapshot iterator and writes them directly with `fwrite()`, delivering a consistent ~20% speedup in benchmarks.

The change is confined to `refs/packed-backend.c` and is now split into two clear functions: `write_packed_entry()` for formatted writes and `write_packed_entry_raw()` for raw writes. This addresses earlier feedback from Toon Claes, who was satisfied with v2 after minor wording corrections. The commit message now explicitly documents the side effects of bypassing `fprintf()`—preserving original formatting, including case, whitespace, and even malformed refnames—which could affect tools parsing packed-refs files.

The patch looks well-crafted: it includes performance measurements, a clean diff, and clear documentation of trade-offs. The risk of regression appears low given the narrow scope, and the optimization is likely uncontroversial since it preserves on-disk format compatibility.
