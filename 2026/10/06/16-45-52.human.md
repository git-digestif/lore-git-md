This is a discussion reply in the ongoing RFC thread about incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically the proposed `git-bisect-rebase` script for `contrib/`. Alejandro Colomar responds to Nico Williams and Phillip Wood, reinforcing the need for a **multi-shot workflow** (manual control over rebase order) for handling complex dependency graphs, such as trees of interdependent branches. He argues that automating this process via `git rebase --update-refs` would be overly complex and inflexible, as it would require recursive rebasing and could force an undesirable order of conflict resolution.

Colomar acknowledges that forgetting to abort a rebase before resolving conflicts (to align child branches first) is a minor inconvenience—at worst, it requires resolving the same conflict twice—but insists that this is preferable to the rigidity of an automated solution. He suggests that integrating the functionality into `git rebase` itself should wait until users are thoroughly familiar with the standalone script, and invites others to experiment with it to identify improvements. His position is substantive, engaging with the technical challenges of recursive rebasing and the trade-offs between automation and manual control.

The email also touches on Phillip Wood’s idea of inserting "break" commands to allow inspection via `git range-diff`. Colomar is skeptical, noting that breaks would only be useful at the end of conflicting rebases and could complicate the workflow. This reply advances the thread by clarifying that the multi-shot approach is non-negotiable for complex use cases, but leaves open whether the script’s current design (or a future `git rebase` integration) can accommodate both simplicity and flexibility. The broader question of whether Git should include any helper for this workflow remains unresolved.
