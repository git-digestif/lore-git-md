This is the third patch in Harald Nordgren’s six-patch feature series enhancing `git status` and `git push` to better handle rebased push branches. It builds directly on the infrastructure introduced in earlier patches (notably the `same_changes` flag and upstream-aware branch comparison logic in `remote.c`) to add a targeted hint when `git status` detects a clean rebase.

The change is small but user-facing: when `git status` reports that the local branch and its push target have diverged *and* the divergence is due to a clean rebase (i.e., the non-upstream commits are identical on both sides), it now appends a one-line suggestion to use `git push --force-with-lease`. The lease mechanism is explicitly called out as a safety measure, addressing the concern that a plain `--force` could overwrite others’ work. The hint only appears when `status.compareBranches` includes `@{push}` and the `statusHints` advice is enabled, so it is opt-in and consistent with Git’s existing advice patterns.

The test update in `t/t6040-tracking-info.sh` verifies the new message format in a rebase scenario, confirming that the hint appears exactly as intended. No new edge cases are introduced in this patch; it simply leverages the three-way commit comparison logic already in place.

This patch is uncontroversial in isolation—it is a natural extension of the series’ core idea and follows Git’s established advice conventions. The real discussion points for the series (edge cases around what constitutes a "clean rebase," performance of the `rev-list --cherry-mark` approach, and whether `--force-with-lease` is always the right advice) are already surfaced in the cover letter and earlier patches, so this one is unlikely to generate new debate. It is a clean, well-scoped addition that delivers on the series’ promise of smarter divergence reporting.
