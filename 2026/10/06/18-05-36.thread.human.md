The Git Contributor's Summit 2026 thread has grown into a sprawling discussion covering seven major topics and now eleven follow-ups focused on governance—specifically, a proposed community voting process to resolve contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. All seven original follow-ups have been delivered, and the community voting process patch is under active, substantive review by Phillip Wood, brian m. carlson, Junio C Hamano, and others.

The core of the current debate is the eligibility criteria for voting. The patch initially proposed a low threshold (e.g., five commits), but this has drawn criticism from contributors like Weijie Yuan, who worry that less-experienced participants could sway high-stakes decisions. Toon Claes’s recent email clarifies that the voting system would include an "abstain" option, allowing eligible voters to opt out if they feel unqualified. This addresses some of the discomfort without changing the threshold itself, but the broader question remains: should the bar be higher to ensure voters have a meaningful track record? Peff’s data shows that under a 50-commit threshold and a two-year window, 42 contributors would qualify, while a five-commit threshold would include 120. The project is now weighing inclusivity against practicality, with proposals ranging from counting patch series (Phillip Wood) to using the average commit count (26, per Tuomas Ahola) or a hybrid model requiring both a track record and recent activity.

The voting process is designed to be a fallback for decisions where consensus isn’t reached through normal discussion, but its specifics—eligibility, mechanism, and tooling—are still unresolved. Brian m. carlson opposes GitHub-based voting on principle, while Emily Shaffer prefers email-based systems to align with Git’s culture. Meanwhile, the AI contribution policy vote, which this process is meant to resolve, remains on hold until the governance details are finalized. Other topics, like Git 3.0 planning (consensus on SHA-256 and Rust inclusion) and pluggable ODB (repository extension planned for Git 2.57), are actionable but overshadowed by the governance debate. The thread is still in discussion phase, with no merges or decisions yet.
