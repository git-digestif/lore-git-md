The Git Contributor’s Summit 2026 thread has evolved into a detailed discussion about project governance, particularly a proposed community voting process to resolve contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. The original summit notes covered seven topics—security, Git 3.0 planning, documentation, Outreachy sponsorship, pluggable ODB, AI policy, and Protocol v2 for pushes—but the focus has now narrowed to refining the voting process’s eligibility criteria. A patch was posted to formalize this process, and it’s under active review by Phillip Wood, brian m. carlson, Junio Hamano, and Kristoffer Haugsbakk.

The key sticking point is who should be allowed to vote. The patch initially proposed contributors with at least 50 merged patches or multiple code reviews, but reviewers have raised concerns about "drive-by" contributions and whether the threshold is too low or too high. Phillip Wood suggested counting patch series instead of individual patches and limiting eligibility to contributors active in the past two years to prevent last-minute "dogpiling." Junio Hamano pushed back on the two-year window, arguing that even inactive contributors who care about the project’s future should have a voice. Kristoffer Haugsbakk countered that the window is a pragmatic safeguard to reduce noise and administrative overhead, citing the 2020 default branch name debate as a cautionary example. The latest exchange between Junio and Kristoffer highlights a disconnect: Junio questions the relevance of filtering out non-contributors, while Kristoffer emphasizes the need to protect contentious votes from low-effort engagement. No consensus has emerged, and the thread remains unresolved.

Other topics from the summit are actionable but less contentious. The security process will be documented publicly, Git 3.0’s two-stage rollout (2.99 → 3.0) is confirmed, and the pluggable ODB effort is moving forward with a repository extension planned for Git 2.57. Documentation tooling is close to standardizing on Asciidoctor, and Outreachy sponsorship outreach is underway. The AI policy vote is now dependent on the community voting process, and Protocol v2 for pushes is likely to see a patch series soon.
