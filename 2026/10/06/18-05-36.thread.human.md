The Git Contributor's Summit 2026 thread continues to refine the proposed community voting process, with the latest discussion focusing on voter eligibility. The original plan, inspired by Peff's suggestion at the summit, aimed for a threshold of 50 merged patches to ensure voters were experienced contributors. However, recent proposals—such as Junio's flat five-commit threshold—have sparked debate, with Tuomas Ahola highlighting the gap between the summit's intent and current proposals.

Weijie Yuan's email adds a new perspective, expressing discomfort with a low threshold that might allow less-experienced contributors to influence high-stakes decisions. While Weijie doesn't propose a specific number, their hesitation underscores the tension between inclusivity and ensuring voters have the context to make informed choices. This aligns with earlier concerns from brian m. carlson and Kristoffer Haugsbakk about "drive-by" contributions and administrative overhead, though Junio has pushed back on strict time-based cutoffs, arguing that even inactive contributors who engage thoughtfully should have a voice.

The discussion remains unresolved, with key questions still open: Should the threshold be raised to match the summit's original intent? How can the project balance inclusivity with practical safeguards? The thread is now centered on these governance trade-offs, with no technical changes or new files introduced yet. The outcome will shape how contentious decisions—like the AI contribution policy—are resolved in the future.
