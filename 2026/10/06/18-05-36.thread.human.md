The Git Contributor's Summit 2026 thread has been a sprawling discussion covering seven major topics and, more recently, a deep dive into governance—specifically, how to formalize a community voting process for contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. The latest development comes from Stéphane Glondu, a Debian developer, who introduces **Belenios** as a third voting platform option alongside the previously debated CIVS and devotee. This addition could break the current deadlock over tooling.

The voting process itself remains the most active subthread. After Emily Shaffer posted a patch proposing a system modeled on the 2019 Code of Conduct adoption, the discussion narrowed to six open questions, with the voting platform and voter eligibility formula at the top. The platform debate had stalled between CIVS (hosted, easy to use but requires voter sign-up) and devotee (self-hosted, aligns with Debian but requires maintenance). Stéphane’s email adds Belenios to the mix: it’s Condorcet-compatible (like the other two), used by Debian (`vote.debian.net`), and can be either hosted or self-hosted. This flexibility directly addresses concerns about third-party dependencies (CIVS) and self-hosting overhead (devotee). Belenios also sidesteps neutrality concerns, as it’s not tied to a major tech company or forge.

On eligibility, the thread is coalescing around a hybrid model proposed by Jeff King: an algorithmic threshold (e.g., "X commits in the last Y years") for most contributors, supplemented by a human-curated list for edge cases like former maintainers or deeply involved but inactive contributors. The algorithmic threshold remains contentious—Peff’s data shows 42 contributors would qualify under a 50-commit/2-year window, while 120 would qualify under a 5-commit/2-year window—but Patrick Steinhardt’s latest email shifts focus to the human-curated list’s governance. He raises two critical questions: whether it’s fair to exclude long-inactive contributors (even if historically significant) and how to ensure transparency in the list’s curation. Junio Hamano opposes strict time-based cutoffs, arguing that engaged but inactive contributors should retain a voice, while Kristoffer Haugsbakk supports them as a pragmatic safeguard against "drive-by" participation.

The other topics from the summit are mostly actionable but lack momentum. The security process is waiting on Peff’s draft proposal, Git 3.0 planning has consensus on a two-stage rollout (2.99 → 3.0) but unresolved questions about LTS status and Rust mandates, and the documentation tooling debate is now unblocked (Fedora’s Asciidoctor compatibility was resolved). Outreachy sponsorship, pluggable ODB, and Protocol v2 for pushes are all moving forward but need owners or funding. The AI contribution policy vote is now gated on the community voting process, which won’t be finalized until at least mid-November.

Key participants include Peff (driving the hybrid eligibility model and security proposal), Junio (advocating for inclusivity in eligibility), Patrick Steinhardt (refining the human-curated list’s governance), and Emily Shaffer (coordinating the patch and vote logistics). Stéphane Glondu’s Belenios proposal is the most concrete step forward in weeks, offering a middle ground that could satisfy both hosted and self-hosted preferences. The next steps will likely involve evaluating Belenios’s usability and auditability compared to CIVS and devotee, while the eligibility debate continues to balance inclusivity with practicality.
