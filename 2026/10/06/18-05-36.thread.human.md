The Git Contributor's Summit 2026 thread continues to focus on governance, particularly the proposed community voting process to resolve contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. The latest exchange between Kristoffer Haugsbakk and Junio C Hamano clarifies why a two-year activity window for voter eligibility is being considered: to prevent outsiders—even those with strong opinions on issues like GenAI—from derailing votes with low-effort participation. This reinforces the earlier argument that such safeguards are pragmatic, not exclusionary, though the tension between inclusivity and practicality remains unresolved.

The voting process patch, posted by Emily Shaffer, is under active review by Phillip Wood, brian m. carlson, Junio, and Kristoffer. Key sticking points include how to define "active contributor" (e.g., commit count thresholds, review quality, or time-based cutoffs), whether to include non-code contributions, and how to prevent last-minute "dogpiling." Junio favors a simple five-commit threshold, while others advocate for stricter criteria to ensure voters have a long-term stake in the project. The AI policy vote, which this process is designed to resolve, is now blocked on these refinements.

Other topics from the summit remain actionable but are progressing independently. Peff is drafting a public security process proposal, Git 3.0 planning is firming up (with SHA-256 and Rust support confirmed but no mandate yet), and documentation tooling is poised to standardize on Asciidoctor. The pluggable ODB and Protocol v2 for pushes efforts are moving forward, though the latter lacks a concrete timeline. Outreachy sponsorship outreach is underway, but no funding is yet secured. The thread’s next steps hinge on resolving the voting process details, which will unlock the AI policy vote and set a precedent for future governance decisions.
