The Git Contributor’s Summit 2026 thread has evolved into a multi-topic discussion covering seven major areas (security, Git 3.0, documentation, Outreachy, pluggable ODBs, AI policy, and Protocol v2 pushes) and now a formal proposal for a **community voting process** to resolve contentious decisions like the AI contribution policy. The voting process patch, posted by Emily Shaffer, is under active review, with the biggest unresolved question being **who gets to vote**.

The original idea at the summit was to limit voting to "active developers with a track record"—Jeff King (Peff) suggested 50 merged patches as a threshold, but the current patch proposes a much lower bar (e.g., five commits or substantive reviews). This has sparked debate: some (like brian m. carlson) worry a low threshold could let "drive-by" contributors sway high-stakes votes, while others (like Junio Hamano) argue even inactive contributors who care about the project should have a voice. Peff later clarified his 50-patch suggestion was off-the-cuff and provided data showing how many contributors would qualify under different thresholds (e.g., 42 under 50 commits/2 years, 120 under 5 commits/2 years). Patrick Steinhardt added a new dimension, warning that too low a bar might skew votes toward newcomers, while too high a bar could favor long-term contributors (often employed to work on Git). He suggested a middle ground (e.g., 25–30 commits with a 2–3 year window) but acknowledged no perfect answer exists.

Other key topics remain actionable but unresolved:
- **Git 3.0**: Consensus on a two-stage rollout (2.99 → 3.0) and SHA-256 support, but questions linger about LTS status and whether Rust will be mandatory.
- **Documentation**: The last infrastructure blocker to standardizing on Asciidoctor (better maintained than AsciiDoc) was resolved—Junio confirmed pre-built docs have used it since mid-2024—but funding and structural improvements (e.g., Diataxis framework) are still needed.
- **AI policy**: The vote hinges on the community voting process. Legal review of competing proposals is pending, and the `Assisted-by` trailer (from Johannes Schindelin) is being considered for attribution.
- **Protocol v2 pushes**: Optimizations (prefix filtering, compression) are planned to avoid multi-hundred-megabyte ref advertisements in large repos, but no timeline or owner yet.

The thread is still in discussion phase, with no merges or final decisions. The voting process is the most immediate blocker for the AI policy, while other topics await follow-up (e.g., Peff drafting security process docs, Outreachy sponsorship outreach).
