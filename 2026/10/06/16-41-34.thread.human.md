This thread is about a performance regression in Git’s `test-lint` target, specifically the `test-grep-lint` check, which became a workflow bottleneck after its introduction. The issue was reported by Junio C Hamano and confirmed by Jeff King (Peff), who also proposed an initial optimization. Tyler Cipriani authored a four-patch series to address the problem, reducing the total `test-lint` runtime from ~25 seconds to ~6 seconds for five consecutive runs on a 16-core machine.

The series is now at patch 3/4, which refactors the core greplint logic into a new shared library, `lib-greplint.pl`. This patch doesn’t improve performance on its own but is a necessary step for the next patch, which will integrate greplint checks into `chainlint.pl`’s tokenization pass, eliminating redundant parsing overhead. The refactoring preserves standalone functionality for `greplint.pl` while enabling the performance gains. Patch 2/4, which implemented Peff’s optimization (an early return in `greplint.pl` to skip files without "grep"), was already merged into the series and halved the standalone `test-grep-lint` runtime.

The series touches `t/Makefile`, `t/chainlint.pl`, `t/greplint.pl`, the new `lib-greplint.pl`, and one interop test script. No objections or requests for changes have been raised, and the patches appear uncontroversial. The final patch (4/4) will deliver the full performance improvement by consolidating parsing effort between `chainlint` and `greplint`. The series is ready for review and addresses the maintainer’s workflow concerns without changing Git’s functionality.
