This thread is about a performance regression in Git’s test suite linting, specifically the `test-grep-lint` check introduced in commit `c9a92e239f`. The check, which ensures `grep` invocations in tests use `--perl-regexp` where needed, now takes ~50 seconds to run five times—up from ~9 seconds when reverted—creating a noticeable delay during `make test`. Junio C Hamano reported the issue, framing it as a workflow bottleneck for developers.

Jeff King (Peff) confirmed the problem, measuring `test-grep-lint` at ~6.5 seconds on his machine—over 5% of the total test suite runtime. He proposed two solutions: parallelizing the check or sharing its parsing effort with other lint tools like `chainlint`. Peff also provided a simple patch that skips parsing if the test body lacks the string `grep`, cutting runtime by ~50%. The trade-off is between this quick fix and a more maintainable but complex integrated linting system. No decisions have been made yet, but Peff’s data and patch give the discussion a clear technical direction. The next step is likely deciding whether to adopt the patch or pursue a broader optimization.
