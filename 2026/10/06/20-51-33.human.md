This is patch 12/13 of Patrick Steinhardt’s 13-patch series refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) to move alternates handling into the "files" backend. The series is a major architectural cleanup that eliminates a long-standing conceptual mismatch where alternates were managed at the ODB source level, causing performance regressions and blocking future ODB work like pluggable backends.

Karthik Nayak’s review of this patch is **surface-level**. He raises a single technical question about the removal of an `if (r->objects)` check in `midx.c`, asking whether it is safe to assume `r->objects->source` is always non-NULL. The rest of his review is a mechanical confirmation that the code moves "looked okay" after a full read. He does not engage with the patch’s core architectural changes—such as the alternates infrastructure relocation, the linked-list traversal updates, or edge cases like promisor remotes or partial clones. His earlier conceptual question about the series’ design (whether multiple sources per ODB could use different strategies) was resolved in a prior email after discovering Patrick’s explanation in patch 12/13, and he does not revisit it here.

The patch itself is the linchpin of the series, moving alternates handling into the files backend and dropping the `read_alternates` callback. It touches 35 files, with the bulk of the changes in `odb.c` (354 lines removed) and `odb/source-files.c` (260 lines added). The changes are well-motivated and align with the series’ goal of simplifying the ODB architecture, but the patch is large and invasive, and the lack of substantive review so far suggests it may need closer scrutiny from other ODB experts. The series is under active review, and a reroll may be required to resolve merge conflicts with `seen`. If you care about Git’s ODB internals or future pluggable backends, this patch is worth your time.
