This is the second patch in Phillip Wood’s two-patch refactoring series that eliminates redundant change-detection logic in `git stash push` and `git stash create`. The series is a clean refactoring with no intended behavior change, motivated by code hygiene and a recent patch by Kazumasa Shigeta.

Junio C Hamano’s review of this patch raises a performance concern: after the refactoring, `git stash push` now calls `repo_refresh_and_write_index()` twice in succession when there are no changes to stash. The first call happens in `do_push_stash()` before `do_create_stash()` is invoked, and the second happens inside `do_create_stash()` itself. Since nothing has changed between the two calls, the second refresh is redundant and could slow down the command on large repositories. The patch does save one `check_changes()` call, but the trade-off may not be favorable if the index refresh is expensive.

The patch itself is otherwise straightforward: it removes the duplicate `check_changes()` call in `do_push_stash()`, shifts the remaining check into `do_create_stash()`, and adjusts the return-value convention so that 2 now signals “no changes to stash” while 1 continues to mean “nothing was stashed” (e.g., `--patch` with no hunks selected). The change is mechanical and touches only `builtin/stash.c`.

Phillip Wood is the author; he is an established contributor with deep expertise in the stash and sequencer subsystems. Junio’s feedback is substantive but narrowly focused on the double-refresh issue; no other technical objections have been raised. The series is still in its first iteration (`v1`) and is under active review.
