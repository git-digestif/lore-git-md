This is a feature series that improves `git status` and `git push` to better handle cases where a push branch has been rebased onto a newer upstream. The six-patch series from Harald Nordgren teaches Git to detect when your local work was cleanly rebased on top of an updated upstream, and adjusts the advice it gives accordingly.

The core idea is to split the divergence between your branch and its push target (`@{push}`) into two parts: commits that came from the upstream (`@{upstream}`), and everything else. When the "everything else" is identical on both sides — meaning your work was rebased cleanly — Git now tells you so explicitly in `git status` and suggests a force push with `--force-with-lease`. If the push is rejected because the remote branch has moved, the error message now distinguishes between someone else updating the upstream (suggesting `git pull`) and you rewriting commits you already pushed (suggesting `--force-with-lease`).

The patches touch `builtin/push.c`, `remote.c`, and `transport.c`, adding logic to detect clean rebases and adjust the hints. The test coverage in `t/t6040-tracking-info.sh` is extensive, covering the new scenarios with 163 new lines. The commit messages are clear and the rationale is well-explained in the cover letter.

This looks like a thoughtful, user-facing improvement that addresses a real pain point: after rebasing your work on a newer upstream, Git currently gives generic advice that doesn’t account for the fact that the divergence is harmless. The series is self-contained, well-tested, and unlikely to be controversial. Worth a look for anyone who works with push branches and rebases.
