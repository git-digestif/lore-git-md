Harald Nordgren's six-patch series improves how `git status` and `git push` handle branches that have been rebased onto a newer upstream, distinguishing commits that come from `@{upstream}` from local-only commits and suggesting `--force-with-lease` when a clean rebase is detected. The series is at its initial v1 posting and remains under review. The main technical concern raised so far was a performance regression in `git status`: Aniket Gupta measured a 5-8x slowdown when `status.compareBranches` includes `@{push}` and branches carry thousands of diverged commits, because `stat_outside_upstream()` computes patch-ids for every commit outside the upstream. Gupta proposed a two-phase optimization to avoid that cost for large divergences, but Nordgren worried that making the "rebased cleanly" message appear only for small divergences would confuse users.

In his latest reply, Gupta concedes the UX point and effectively withdraws the objection. He notes that the expensive `--cherry-mark` pass only runs on commits outside the upstream, so a typical rebased branch with a handful of local commits stays fast; the slowdown only appears with thousands of diverged commits on each side. He concludes the cost is "probably acceptable as is," leaving the series without the proposed optimization and easing the immediate pressure to resolve the performance question. No other reviews have arrived yet, and the series has not been picked up by the maintainer.
