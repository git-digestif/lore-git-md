This thread is a six-patch series from Harald Nordgren that improves how `git status` and `git push` handle rebased branches. The goal is to make Git smarter about detecting when a local branch has been cleanly rebased onto a newer upstream, and to give users more precise advice—like suggesting `--force-with-lease` instead of a generic "diverged branches" warning.

The series is still in its initial version (v1) and under review. The first two patches laid the groundwork: they added logic in `remote.c` to compare branches against both `@{upstream}` and `@{push}`, and introduced a helper function (`stat_outside_upstream()`) that uses `git rev-list --cherry-mark` to detect when non-upstream commits are identical on both sides (indicating a clean rebase). The third patch, just posted, builds on this by adding a targeted hint in `git status` when a clean rebase is detected, suggesting `--force-with-lease` as a safer alternative to `--force`.

The changes are opt-in, requiring users to configure `status.compareBranches` to include `@{push}`, and the new advice only appears when `statusHints` is enabled. The test suite (`t/t6040-tracking-info.sh`) has been expanded to verify the new behavior. So far, no reviews have been posted, but the open questions from the cover letter remain: whether the "clean rebase" detection is robust enough (e.g., handling merge commits or conflict resolutions), whether `--force-with-lease` is always the right advice, and how the new logic performs with large divergence counts or deep histories. The third patch itself is uncontroversial—it’s a straightforward extension of the series’ core idea—but the broader discussion about edge cases and safety will likely continue as reviews come in.
