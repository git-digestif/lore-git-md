Harald Nordgren's initial six-patch series (v1) improves `git status` and `git push` when a local branch has been rebased onto a newer upstream: `git status` can now detect and report a "clean rebase" by comparing non-upstream commits on both sides, suggesting `--force-with-lease`, and push rejection messages name the specific remote branch to pull from and detect when the remote carries only rewritten commits. The series touches `remote.c`, `builtin/push.c`, `transport.c`, and adds `t/t6040-tracking-info.sh`. It remains under review with no maintainer merge decision reported.

The first substantive review has arrived from Aniket Gupta, targeting the second patch. He identifies a real performance problem in `stat_outside_upstream()` in `remote.c`: the three-way `git rev-list --cherry-mark` call that powers clean-rebase detection computes patch-ids for every commit on both sides, making `git status` roughly 5-8x slower when `@{push}` is in `status.compareBranches` and the branches have diverged (measured 25ms to 150ms with 3000 diverged commits, 60ms to 500ms with 10000). He proposes a two-phase optimization -- count diverged commits cheaply first, and only run the expensive patch-id comparison when the counts are small enough to justify the cost.

The performance concern is the main open item blocking the series. No other reviews, objections, or maintainer positions have been recorded yet, and the series remains at v1 with no integration status beyond Harald's own note that it is in use via `seen`.
