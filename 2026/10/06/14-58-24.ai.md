This is a substantive review in the ongoing discussion about incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically clarifying the scope and purpose of the proposed `bisect-rebase` script. Nico Williams responds to Phillip Wood’s suggestion that `git rebase --update-refs` could automate rebasing interdependent branches, emphasizing that the two features address distinct problems: `bisect-rebase` is designed for efficiently rebasing a single branch across a large number of upstream commits (e.g., 10,000+), while `--update-refs` handles stacked branches. He notes that the two could be combined but are fundamentally orthogonal.

Nico also addresses Phillip’s idea of inserting "break" commands to allow inspection via `git range-diff`. He explains that `bisect-rebase` already stops at the first conflict requiring user resolution, and the intermediate bisection steps are primarily a progress indicator. While he acknowledges the potential value of pausing at each bisection step to check for trivial conflicts, he warns that this could make the process cumbersome if the user mostly elects to continue. He suggests this could be an optional feature but does not explore its implementation further.

The review is substantive, engaging with the technical trade-offs of workflow design and clarifying the intended use case for `bisect-rebase`. Nico’s distinction between rebasing stacked branches and rebasing across large histories is a key insight for the thread, reinforcing that the proposed script is not a replacement for `--update-refs` but a complementary tool. His feedback carries weight as it comes from the original author of the bisection-based approach, though it leaves open the question of whether the optional "break" feature would be a net benefit or a distraction. The thread’s broader question—whether Git should include any helper for this workflow—remains unresolved, but Nico’s input narrows the focus to the single-branch, large-history use case.
