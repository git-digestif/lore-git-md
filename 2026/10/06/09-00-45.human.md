This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread about Scott Chacon’s SHA-256 tree-digest signing mechanism and the broader Git 3.0 SHA-256 transition plan. Christian Couder asks a series of practical, forward-looking questions about the interoperability work that brian m. carlson previously identified as the real solution to the ecosystem disruption concerns raised by Chacon.

The email is not a review of the RFC patches themselves, nor does it take a position on the strategic debate between Chacon and Carlson. Instead, it focuses on the logistics of upstreaming the interoperability code — the missing piece that would allow repositories to be automatically rewritten between hash algorithms during clone and fetch. Couder’s questions are concrete: where the code lives, what’s missing (submodules), how big the patch series is, how long it would take to upstream, how others can help, which company is involved, and whether realistic testing is possible. The tone is collaborative and solution-oriented, aiming to clarify what it would take to make the interoperability work a reality rather than leaving it as a theoretical alternative.

This is a high-signal intervention for anyone following the Git 3.0 transition. It shifts the thread from abstract debate to actionable next steps, and the answers — if provided — could determine whether the interoperability work becomes a viable path forward or remains a footnote. The questions about testing and real-world scenarios suggest Couder is thinking about the operational challenges of a transition, not just the technical ones. If the interoperability work is close to upstreamable, it could address many of the ecosystem disruption concerns that Chacon’s RFC was designed to mitigate, potentially reshaping the strategic discussion.
