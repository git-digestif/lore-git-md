This is the **wrap-up email** from Derrick Stolee for his seven-patch series hardening the trace2 subsystem against crashes caused by `die()`-triggering helpers. The series introduced `banned-die.h` to block functions like `xsnprintf()`, `xstrdup()`, and `ALLOC_ARRAY()` in trace2, replacing them with defensive fallbacks that prioritize process stability over telemetry completeness.

Derrick acknowledges that the protections are **incomplete**—the compile-time bans do not address indirect `die()` calls via helpers like `strbuf` or `json-writer.c`, a limitation Jeff King highlighted in his review. While the series fixes the immediate crash (a "recursion detected in die handler" error on Git for Windows), it leaves some crash vectors open. Derrick’s tone is pragmatic: the patches provide *some* protection and serve as a template for future hardening, but a complete solution would require significant architectural changes (e.g., a ground-up rewrite of trace2 or a "safe strbuf" library).

The email links to an earlier RFC for making `strbuf` and related helpers more robust, which also stalled due to similar concerns. Derrick does not propose a v4 or further iterations, suggesting the series may remain as-is for now. The MinGW-specific fix (disabling `libintl_vsnprintf()` redirection for `xsnprintf()`) remains a planned follow-up, but this email does not mention it.

**Assessment:** The series is a **well-executed incremental improvement** that addresses a real stability issue, but its limitations are now clearly framed. The patches are technically sound (v3 fixed all prior feedback) and the trade-offs (degraded telemetry under failure) are intentional. However, the broader architectural debate—whether trace2 can ever be truly `die()`-free without a rewrite—remains unresolved. **Worth attention** for those tracking Git’s stability or telemetry subsystems, but unlikely to see further development soon unless a contributor picks up the rewrite idea.
