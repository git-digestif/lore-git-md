This is a bugfix patch that has now been accepted and merged. The thread is effectively closed.

The patch fixes a long-standing bug in `git ls-files` and `git add` where negative (exclusion) pathspecs were incorrectly stripped of their common prefix alongside positive pathspecs. This could cause negative pathspecs to degenerate into empty strings—matching everything—or trigger out-of-bounds reads when the negative pathspec was shorter than the prefix. The fix, now merged to `master`, ensures negative pathspecs are preserved in full, aligning with their intended semantics as absolute patterns relative to the working directory.

The patch itself is minimal (11 lines in `dir.c`) and well-tested, with three new tests in `t/t6132-pathspec-exclude.sh` covering edge cases. The maintainer, Junio C Hamano, initially raised a design question about potential future optimizations for cases with many negative patterns but ultimately accepted the original patch as the correct and sufficient fix. The discussion about further optimizations is explicitly deferred as a separate topic.

For anyone tracking pathspec behavior or working with negative patterns, this is a notable correctness fix with no expected performance regressions. The fix preserves the performance benefit of the positive-path prefix optimization while ensuring negative pathspecs behave as intended. The thread is now resolved, and the patch is part of the upcoming Git release.
