This is a performance optimization patch for the packed-refs backend, now in its second version. The patch speeds up operations that rewrite the packed-refs file (like deleting references) by avoiding redundant formatting of unchanged refs. Instead of using `fprintf()` for every entry, it tracks raw byte positions of unchanged refs and writes them directly with `fwrite()`, delivering a consistent ~20% performance improvement in benchmarks.

The change is well-motivated and narrowly scoped to `refs/packed-backend.c`. Version 2 incorporates feedback from Toon Claes by splitting the write logic into two functions—`write_packed_entry()` for formatted writes and `write_packed_entry_raw()` for raw writes—improving clarity and documenting the side effects of preserving original formatting (case, whitespace, malformed refnames, peeled OIDs for broken refs). The commit message now explicitly notes these trade-offs, addressing earlier concerns about interoperability with tools parsing packed-refs files.

The implementation is clean: it adds a `record_start` field to `struct packed_ref_iterator`, updates `next_record()` to set it, and modifies `write_with_updates()` to pass raw data for unchanged refs. Benchmarks show a clear win—deleting 100,000 packed refs drops from ~28.7 ms to ~23.8 ms—without altering the on-disk format or breaking backward compatibility. The patch is part of the packed-refs subsystem maintained by Karthik Nayak and Patrick Steinhardt, and the risk of regression appears low given the focused scope.

No new objections have been raised, and the structural feedback from the first version has been addressed. The remaining open question is whether the subtle formatting changes (e.g., uppercase OIDs, tabs) could affect external tools like JGit or libgit2, though no specific compatibility issues have surfaced yet. The patch looks ready for further review or integration.
