This is a discussion follow-up in the ongoing thread about modernizing Git’s tutorial documentation. Junio C Hamano responds to Julia Evans’s earlier email, clarifying his position on the future of `gittutorial` and the sequencing of the replacement effort.

Junio rejects Julia’s characterization of his suggestion as "option 1" (referring users to the current `gittutorial` for branching and collaboration). Instead, he frames the decision as a diagnostic one: if the existing `gittutorial` is inadequate, the project should determine *why* it fails (e.g., outdated assumptions about users’ prior knowledge) and then decide whether to salvage or replace it. He implies that a from-scratch rewrite is likely necessary but does not rule out interim use of `gittutorial` if it can serve as a temporary stopgap without causing harm. His stance is pragmatic: if the old tutorial is actively misleading, it should not be recommended at all, even temporarily; if it is merely outdated but still useful, it could bridge the gap until a better replacement is ready.

The email does not propose new patches or technical changes but sharpens the debate over sequencing. Junio’s position leaves room for incremental progress (e.g., shipping Julia’s draft now and adding branching/collaboration later) but insists on evaluating the old tutorial’s utility rather than dismissing it outright. The thread’s next steps will likely hinge on whether the project accepts Julia’s incremental approach or demands a more comprehensive replacement before removing `gittutorial`. Key context: Julia’s draft has been tested with 22 beginners, and branching/collaboration are identified as critical gaps, but no consensus exists on how to address them.
