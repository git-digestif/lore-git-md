This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s 13-patch series refactoring Git’s object database (ODB) subsystem to move alternates handling into the "files" backend. The goal is to eliminate a long-standing mismatch where alternates were managed at the ODB source level, causing performance regressions and complicating future work like pluggable backends. The series is under initial review, with no substantive objections yet, but Karthik Nayak has raised two medium-weight concerns about earlier patches: one about a commit message lacking motivation (patch 1/13) and another about a test helper’s hard-coded object directory path (patch 2/13).

The series touches core ODB code, the files backend, quarantine logic, and commit-graph handling, introducing new structures like `struct odb_files_dir` to manage multiple object directories. Alternates are now a backend-specific detail, with the `read_alternates` callback removed entirely in the final patch. Karthik’s latest review of patch 4/13 is surface-level, merely acknowledging the mechanical change from `struct odb_source` to `struct odb_files_dir` without engaging with deeper implications like traversal order or canonicalization. The series is still early in review, and while the architectural direction seems uncontroversial, the size and complexity may lead to further feedback on edge cases like promisor remotes or partial clones. A reroll is likely due to minor merge conflicts with the `seen` branch.
