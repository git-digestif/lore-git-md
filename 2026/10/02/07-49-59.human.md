This is a small feature patch that adds `@{p}` as a shorthand for `@{push}` in Git's revision syntax, mirroring the existing `@{u}` shorthand for `@{upstream}`. The v2 update incorporates the historical rationale Junio Hamano requested: `@{upstream}` and its `@{u}` shorthand were introduced together in 2009, while `@{push}` (added in 2015) lacked a shorthand due to concerns about potential future `@{publish}` or `@{pull}` notations—neither of which materialized.

The patch itself is minimal and well-scoped. It modifies `object-name.c` to recognize `@{p}` alongside `@{push}`, updates `Documentation/revisions.adoc` to document the new shorthand, and adds test coverage in `t/t1514-rev-parse-push.sh` to verify that both `@{p}` and `@{P}` resolve correctly. The implementation follows Git's existing case-insensitive handling of revision suffixes, so no additional complexity is introduced.

The motivation is purely ergonomic: users who type `@{p}` by analogy with `@{u}` currently encounter an error, which feels inconsistent. The change aligns the push shorthand with the upstream one without altering behavior or on-disk formats. The updated commit message now clearly frames this as a symmetry fix rather than a new feature, which should help reviewers assess its merit.

No technical objections remain, and the patch looks ready for integration. If you use `@{push}` frequently, this is a small but welcome quality-of-life improvement.
