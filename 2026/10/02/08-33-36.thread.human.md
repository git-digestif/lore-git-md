This thread is about a two-patch series that optimizes Git's commit-graph write strategy during fetches for large repositories. The goal is to avoid the costly full scan of all reachable refs after every fetch by writing only newly fetched commits incrementally when possible. The series, now at version 3, was authored by Kristofer Karlsson and has been under review since its first iteration.

The technical approach is uncontested after Patrick Steinhardt's thorough review of the initial version, which addressed edge cases like additive seeds, split-mode behavior, and shallow ref handling. Version 2 resolved all open questions, and version 3 made only surface-level improvements—refining commit messages, simplifying test scripts, and adding a small test helper to verify commit-graph contents. The series touches the commit-graph subsystem, introducing a new enum for write strategies, an `oidset` to track updated refs, and a few new APIs for testing and fetch logic. Performance benchmarks show a significant speedup: incremental writes reduce fetch time from ~357ms to ~9.3ms in a synthetic setup with 200K refs.
