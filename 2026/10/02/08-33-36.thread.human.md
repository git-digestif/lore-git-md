This thread is about a patch series by Kristofer Karlsson that optimizes Git's `fetch.writeCommitGraph` behavior for large repositories. The goal is to avoid the current inefficiency of rebuilding the commit-graph from all reachable refs after every fetch by introducing an incremental write strategy that only processes newly fetched commits when possible.

The series is now technically uncontested after Kristofer addressed all of Patrick Steinhardt's substantive review questions about design assumptions, edge cases, and benchmarking. Patrick's latest reply is a surface-level acknowledgment, accepting Kristofer's clarifications and agreeing that benchmark numbers should be included in the commit messages. Kristofer plans to post a reroll with updated commit messages, extended test coverage (ensuring no coverage loss after incremental writes), and benchmark results. The optimization touches the commit-graph subsystem, specifically `builtin/fetch.c` and `commit-graph.c`, and relies on split-mode commit-graph writes to ensure additive updates. Performance improvements are significant: in a synthetic test with 200K packed refs, incremental writes reduced fetch time from ~360ms to ~9ms for a single ref update. The series remains under review with no merge status reported.
