This thread is about a two-patch series from Kristofer Karlsson that optimizes Git’s `fetch.writeCommitGraph` behavior for large repositories. The goal is to avoid the current inefficiency of rebuilding the commit-graph from all reachable refs after every fetch by introducing an incremental write strategy that only processes newly fetched commits when possible.

The series is under active review, with Patrick Steinhardt raising substantive questions about correctness and edge cases in the initial version. Kristofer has now responded with clarifications that resolve most of Patrick’s concerns. The core design relies on additive seeds—extending the existing graph rather than replacing it—and assumes fetch always operates in split mode, which ensures new commits are layered on top of the existing graph. Kristofer also provided benchmark numbers showing significant performance improvements: in a synthetic test with 200K refs, the incremental write reduced fetch time from ~360ms to ~9ms for a single ref update, and no-op fetches are now skipped entirely. He plans to include these numbers in the next version of the series.

The discussion has also clarified implementation details, such as why the code uses `rm->old_oid` (the remote’s advertised value) instead of `rm->new_oid` (which isn’t set during fetch) and why the explicit `GRAPH_WRITE_SKIP` mode is necessary as a safeguard. Kristofer’s reroll will include updated commit messages, extended test coverage to verify no commits are lost, and the benchmark results. The series remains under review, but the technical objections have been addressed, and the path forward is clear.
