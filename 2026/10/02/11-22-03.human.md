This is a substantive review of the second patch in Kristofer Karlsson’s two-patch series that optimizes commit-graph writes after fetches. The series aims to replace the current full-reachability scan with an incremental write that uses only the newly updated refs as seeds, reducing overhead in large repositories.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review engages deeply with the technical design. His main concern is whether the new seeds are *additive* (extending the existing graph) or *exclusive* (replacing it). Kristofer’s implementation is additive: in split mode, `close_reachable()` walks from the updated tips and stops at commits already present in the graph, preserving coverage of unrelated refs. Patrick’s follow-up question about non-split mode remains unanswered in this exchange, leaving a small but important edge case open.

Patrick also questions the choice of `rm->old_oid` in the ref-collection logic, asking why the old OID is used instead of the new one. This touches on a subtle correctness issue: the patch currently tracks the pre-fetch state of refs, which may not match the intended incremental behavior if the fetch updates multiple refs pointing to the same commit. Additionally, he points out that the ref-collection loop should filter out more `REF_STATUS_*` values (e.g., rejected or failed refs) to avoid including invalid tips.

The review notes the absence of performance benchmarks, which would help quantify the benefit of the optimization. While the patch is well-structured and includes thorough test coverage, the lack of empirical data makes it harder to assess the real-world impact.

Overall, the patch is a promising optimization with a clean design, but Patrick’s feedback highlights areas needing clarification—particularly around non-split mode behavior and ref-collection correctness. The series is under active review, and the next revision will likely address these points. If you care about commit-graph performance in large repos, this thread is worth following.
