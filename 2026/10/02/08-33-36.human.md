This is a performance optimization for the commit-graph feature in `git fetch`. The series introduces a smarter way to update the commit-graph when `fetch.writeCommitGraph` is enabled, avoiding the current expensive full scan of all refs after every fetch.

The core idea is to use only the newly updated refs as seeds for the commit-graph write, rather than rescanning all reachable refs. The patch introduces a three-mode policy (REACHABLE/TIPS/SKIP) that handles different scenarios: skipping the write entirely for no-op fetches, writing incrementally from updated tips when possible, and falling back to a full scan when there's no existing graph or when fetching from multiple remotes.

Patch 1 adds a test helper (`test-tool read-graph commit-info`) to verify graph contents in tests, while Patch 2 implements the optimization in `builtin/fetch.c` with four comprehensive tests covering incremental updates, unrelated commits, no-op fetches, and fallback cases. The implementation looks well-motivated and comes with solid test coverage, suggesting it's ready for serious review. This appears to be a meaningful performance win for repositories with many refs.
