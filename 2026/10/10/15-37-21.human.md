This is a licensing policy follow-up in the `sha1dc-accel` thread. Todd Zullinger expands on his earlier point about Fedora’s approach to Git’s mixed-license components, explaining how the Fedora Git package arrives at its license list (`BSD-3-Clause AND GPL-2.0-only AND GPL-2.0-or-later AND LGPL-2.1-or-later AND MIT`).

He details the specific subsystems that contribute each license: `reftable/` (BSD-3-Clause), `xdiff/xhistogram.c` (BSD-3-Clause and/or EDL-1.0), `ewah/` (GPL-2.0-or-later), and `compat/obstack.[ch]`/`compat/regex/` (LGPL-2.1-or-later). The tooling (`licensecheck`) and manual review process he describes show that Fedora’s license list is not a guess but a deliberate enumeration of every license that applies to any part of the shipped binary. Zullinger also notes that Fedora once allowed license simplification (e.g., listing only GPL-2) but now explicitly rejects that practice, reinforcing the transparency of the current approach.

The exchange is purely informational and does not introduce new objections or policy concerns. It confirms that the `sha1dc-accel` series, which adds MIT-licensed code to Git’s GPL-2 codebase, is handled consistently by downstream distributions. For a busy reader, this email is a footnote: the licensing question is settled, and the series remains ready for integration.
