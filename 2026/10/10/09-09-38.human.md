This is a reply in the ongoing bugfix series for a `rerere` locking race condition. The author, Thomas Bachem, agrees to drop the third patch (v6 3/3) from the series in response to Patrick Steinhardt’s objection. That patch would have allowed conflict-stopping commands like `git rebase` to proceed with a warning if the `MERGE_RR.lock` remained held after the timeout, but Patrick argued it was premature without evidence of real-world user impact.

The decision leaves the series as two patches: one introducing a configurable lock timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`) and another adding a `--skip-locked` mode for `git rerere gc` (used by background maintenance). Both are uncontroversial and ready for integration. Thomas notes that the earlier patches already reduce the likelihood of the race—rebases no longer trigger auto-maintenance during their own commits, and the timeout gives other processes a chance to release the lock. If users still encounter the issue, he plans to reintroduce the dropped behavior later.

The thread’s architectural question—whether the warning-and-proceed behavior is a pragmatic stopgap or an overreach—is now settled for this iteration, but the door remains open for future discussion if the problem resurfaces. No other participants have weighed in on Patrick’s objection, so the series can proceed without further debate.
