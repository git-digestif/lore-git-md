This is a CI/build system follow-up in the ongoing thread about updating GitHub Actions macOS runners. Brian M. Carlson responds to Todd Zullinger’s procedural question about whether to use `macos-latest` (a floating tag) or `macos-26` (an explicit version) for the `osx-reftable` and `osx-gcc` jobs in Junio’s v2 patch.

Carlson notes that macOS releases annually, so GitHub’s runner images likely update once per year with one version being removed. While `macos-latest` would simplify maintenance by automatically tracking the current default, it would also mean Git’s CI no longer tests older macOS versions. He observes that macOS has historically adhered closely to POSIX standards (until recently being UNIX-certified), so compatibility risks are low. His preference leans slightly toward `macos-latest` for convenience, but he emphasizes that Linux environments exhibit far more divergence and would benefit more from cross-version testing. The tone is neutral, framing this as a low-stakes trade-off rather than a technical necessity. No new objections or alternatives are introduced; the thread remains converged on the v2 job-splitting structure, with this single open question about tag choice.
