This is a follow-up in the now-merged documentation thread about Git’s detached HEAD explanation. Julia Evans responds to Junio C Hamano’s questions about her March 2024 user research, clarifying the purpose of the curated user quotes and defending her methodological approach.

Julia explains that the quotes were not meant to argue that Git should avoid explaining HEAD, but rather to illustrate the variety of mental models and language that real Git users employ. She wanted to show why she rejected Junio’s suggested wordings (e.g., “records”) in favor of phrasing that aligns with how users already think and talk about Git. The examples also highlight the gap between her assumptions about what users know and the reality revealed by the research, which informs her preference for user-aligned language over technically precise but unfamiliar terminology.

The exchange remains a meta-discussion about documentation philosophy. Julia emphasizes that her role is to synthesize user research into practical documentation decisions, and she asks for trust in her judgment on wording choices. She acknowledges that some user comments are technically incorrect but argues they reflect the mental models of experienced users who are nonetheless more knowledgeable than the average Git user. No new patches or technical changes are proposed; the focus is on how user research should shape future documentation efforts. Junio’s questions about the implications of the research remain open, but Julia’s reply provides clearer context for her approach.
