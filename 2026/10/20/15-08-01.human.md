This is a **process discussion** in the reftable backend bugfix thread. Junio C Hamano replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s critique of AI-generated commit messages, offering a more nuanced take.

Junio agrees that AI-generated output often falls short but suggests the issue lies more in poor prompting than in the technology itself. He argues that a well-guided AI could produce usable results, but only if the user already understands the problem and solution well enough to steer the tool effectively. His preferred workflow is manual drafting followed by AI-assisted polishing for grammar and clarity, rather than relying on AI to generate the message from scratch. He also notes that reviewers are left frustrated when they have to correct AI-generated nonsense, especially when the author lacks domain knowledge.

The email closes with a pointed question: whatever happened to the AI policy discussion promised at the Contributor’s Summit? Junio implies that the project still lacks clear guidelines for how (or whether) to use AI in contributions, leaving reviewers to navigate inconsistent quality on a case-by-case basis. The thread remains focused on the reftable bugfix, but this exchange highlights a broader, unresolved tension about maintaining standards in an era of AI-assisted development.
