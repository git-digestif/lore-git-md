This is a follow-up in the now-merged documentation thread about Git’s detached HEAD explanation. Julia Evans responds to Junio C Hamano’s questions about her March 2024 user research, clarifying the purpose and implications of the curated user quotes she presented earlier.

Julia explains that the quotes were not intended to argue that Git should avoid explaining HEAD, but rather to illustrate the variety of mental models and language that experienced Git users employ—some technically accurate, others vague or incorrect. She emphasizes that these examples justify her resistance to Junio’s suggested wordings (e.g., replacing “records” with a more precise but less user-aligned term) and highlight why she prioritizes phrasing that aligns with how users *actually* think and talk about Git, even if it sacrifices some technical precision. The goal, she argues, is to make documentation accessible to users who may not grasp (or need to grasp) the full technical details, while still providing accurate information.

The exchange also reveals a difference in assumptions between Julia and Junio about what Git users know and what they need from documentation. Julia acknowledges that some of the user quotes are “wrong” by technical standards but insists they reflect the reality of how many users operate. She frames her role as sorting through these perspectives to craft documentation that meets users where they are, rather than expecting them to adopt Git’s terminology wholesale. While she does not directly answer Junio’s question about whether Git should avoid explaining HEAD, she implies that the focus should be on bridging the gap between user understanding and technical accuracy, rather than either avoiding the topic or insisting on strict precision.

No new patches or technical changes are proposed; this remains a meta-discussion about documentation philosophy. The thread is now effectively a dialogue about how to balance user accessibility with technical correctness in Git’s documentation, with Julia advocating for a user-centric approach grounded in research. Junio’s questions remain open, but Julia’s response provides clearer context for her methodology and goals.
