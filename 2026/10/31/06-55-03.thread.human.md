This thread is about a bugfix from Linus Torvalds for `git describe`, which was miscomputing commit distances when commit timestamps were non-monotonic (a real issue that surfaced during the Linux kernel v7.4 merge window). The fix, now queued for integration, replaces the date-based comparison with one that prioritizes commit generation numbers from the commit graph, ensuring topological order is respected.

The patch itself is straightforward—a one-line change in `builtin/describe.c`—and has no maintainer objections. The test debate (whether to use hard-coded dates or `test_tick`) was resolved in favor of Linus’s original version, as Junio C Hamano argued the explicit dates were clearer for this case. However, Patrick Steinhardt recently raised a substantive concern: the fix’s reliance on commit generation numbers means it silently falls back to the old (buggy) behavior if the commit graph isn’t available or up-to-date. He proposes calling `ensure_generations_valid()` to guarantee correctness, even though it’s computationally expensive, highlighting a trade-off between performance and consistency.

The thread is otherwise settled—Junio has signaled intent to integrate, and minor test hygiene issues (like redundant `core.commitGraph` settings) are acknowledged but deferred. The open question now is whether to accept the performance cost of Patrick’s suggestion or document the dependency on commit graph freshness. Linus, Junio, and SZEDER Gábor have all weighed in with high- or medium-weight contributions, while Tuomas Ahola’s test rewrite attempt was deemed flawed and abandoned. No merge date has been set, but the patch is likely cooking in `next`.
