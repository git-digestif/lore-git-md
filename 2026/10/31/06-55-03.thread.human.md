The `git describe` regression fix from Linus Torvalds—correcting miscomputed commit distances when commit dates are skewed—is now queued for integration, but a critical edge case has emerged. The fix relies on commit generation numbers from the commit graph, which means it silently reverts to the old (buggy) behavior if the graph is stale or missing. Patrick Steinhardt first flagged this dependency, and Derrick Stolee has now endorsed the proposed mitigation: calling `ensure_generations_valid()` to guarantee fresh generation numbers, even though it may trigger a full commit graph walk. Both agree this is a necessary trade-off for correctness, though the performance cost could be significant for large repositories.

The patch itself is a one-line change in `builtin/describe.c`, replacing the date-based queue comparison with one that prioritizes generation numbers. Junio Hamano has signaled intent to integrate it, and the test debate—whether to use hard-coded dates or `test_tick`—was resolved in favor of Linus’s original version, which Junio deemed clearer despite minor redundancy (like explicitly setting `core.commitGraph=true`). The fix maintains backwards compatibility and touches no on-disk formats, but the new concern about commit graph freshness introduces a potential inconsistency: users might see different `git describe` results depending on when `git maintenance` last updated the graph. No alternative solutions have been proposed yet, leaving the performance-vs-correctness question open as the patch awaits Junio’s next integration cycle.
