This thread began as a bugfix from Linus Torvalds to correct `git describe`'s miscalculation of commit distances when commit timestamps are non-monotonic—a regression that caused real-world discrepancies in the Linux kernel's version reporting. Linus's original one-line patch, which prioritizes commit generation numbers over unreliable dates, was queued for integration in `next` and appeared headed for merger with an opt-out `--exact` flag and warning mechanism. However, the discussion took a turn when Kristofer Karlsson proposed an alternative algorithm that delivers near-exact distances without relying on generation numbers, potentially eliminating the need for the flag and warning entirely.

The thread is now at a crossroads between two competing solutions. Linus's patch is simple and leverages existing commit-graph infrastructure but introduces a performance cost when `--exact` is enabled (amortized via a proposed commit-graph write-back mechanism). Kristofer's prototype, by contrast, avoids generation numbers entirely, using a two-counter algorithm and epsilon-based heuristic to achieve near-exact results with the same performance profile as the current implementation. Junio Hamano has called the alternative "interesting" but has not weighed in on its technical merits, leaving the decision to Linus, Patrick Steinhardt, and Derrick Stolee, who have been the primary voices in the debate. No maintainer or key participant has yet endorsed or rejected Kristofer's approach, and the thread remains in limbo pending further feedback.

The core technical details remain unchanged: Linus's fix modifies a single line in `builtin/describe.c` to use generation numbers, while Kristofer's prototype rewrites the priority queue logic with roughly 120 lines of new code. Both approaches maintain backwards compatibility, but Kristofer's avoids the performance cost and warning mechanism of Linus's patch. The unresolved questions—whether to adopt Kristofer's algorithm, stick with Linus's fix, or pursue a hybrid approach—will determine not only the final shape of the fix but also whether the proposed commit-graph write-back mechanism (a complementary optimization for Linus's patch) is still needed. For now, the patch remains queued in `next`, but the design debate is very much alive.
