This is a review comment on Elia Pinto’s bugfix series addressing object-size truncation on 32-bit platforms.

Simon Richter raises a technical clarification about the choice of `size_t` versus `off_t`. He points out that `size_t` is 32-bit on 32-bit architectures, so it cannot represent sizes above 4 GiB either. The correct type for on-disk object sizes, he argues, is `off_t` (which is 64-bit when large-file support is enabled), not `size_t`. He suggests that `size_t` should be reserved for in-memory data, while `off_t` should track the total bytes processed when reading from disk.

The comment is substantive: it questions a core design decision in the series and could lead to a rework if the maintainers agree. No counter-argument or resolution is present in this email; the thread is still open.
