This thread is about a four-patch series from Ravi Mistry that improves Git’s HTTP transport error handling—fixing an off-by-one retry bug, adding exponential backoff, making retryable status codes configurable, and enabling retries for POST requests. The series is now at v2, addressing all of Junio C Hamano’s feedback on the first version, and is under review awaiting a maintainer decision on whether to include 5xx errors in the default retry set.

The patches touch `http.c`, `remote-curl.c`, and related documentation, introducing a new `http.retryStatusCodes` config key (defaulting to 429) and refactoring retry logic to handle POST requests and exponential backoff. Tests cover retry counts, backoff timing, and POST retries for 429 and 5xx errors. Junio’s reviews have been constructive and approving, focusing on implementation and documentation clarity, while Ravi has incorporated all feedback.

The main open question—whether 5xx errors should be retried by default—remains unresolved. Now, brian m. carlson has raised a new concern: the current default of `http.retryAfter = 0` (immediate retry when no Retry-After header is present) risks overwhelming servers, even for 429 responses. He argues for a non-zero default (e.g., 30 seconds) to better respect rate-limiting. This challenges the series’ design premise and may require broader discussion before the patches can move forward.
