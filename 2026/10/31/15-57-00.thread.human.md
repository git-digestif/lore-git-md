This thread proposes a five-patch overhaul of Git's HTTP transport retry logic, authored by Ravi Mistry, addressing an off-by-one retry counter bug, adding exponential backoff when servers omit Retry-After headers, making retryable status codes configurable, and extending retries to POST requests used by fetch and clone. The series is at v3 and under review, with the most contentious issue -- the default initial backoff delay -- resolved at a compromise of 10 seconds, which balances server protection against interactive usability. Ravi has committed to several changes for a forthcoming v4: restricting POST retries to read-only services like git-upload-pack while excluding push operations, honoring explicit Retry-After: 0 headers over the 10-second default, and changing the attempt counter to an unsigned type.

Junio C Hamano has raised a procedural question about whether the 10-second default should be folded into patch 2 rather than introduced separately in patch 5, and is awaiting input before the series proceeds. Patrick Steinhardt has provided the most substantive technical review across all patches; while he maintains an architectural objection to making retry status codes configurable, he and Ravi have now converged on the documentation framing: Ravi has agreed that v4 will explicitly state service providers should return 429 when overloaded and that configuring other codes like 502 or 503 should be presented as a temporary workaround for buggy provider infrastructure rather than a permanent feature. This concedes the documentation aspect of Patrick's convergence argument, though the underlying question of whether Git should support non-429 retries at all remains unresolved. The series awaits Junio's final decision on patch organization and integration.
