Ravi Mistry has posted v5 of his five-patch series hardening Git's HTTP transport retry logic, addressing four gaps: an off-by-one retry count bug from the original 429 handling, missing exponential backoff when Retry-After headers are absent, no retries for transient 5xx errors, and no retries for POST requests during fetch/clone and partial-clone transfers. This latest email resends patch 1/5 unchanged from v4 -- it fixes the pre-decrement retry counter that made `http.maxRetries=N` perform only N-1 retries and wrapped to a truthy -1 when zero, and moves `CURLINFO_RETRY_AFTER` extraction into `finish_active_slot()` so Trace2 logs the real value.

The series is now complete and under review. Patches 3/5 and 4/5 were updated in v5 to resolve Junio C Hamano's two remaining items: patch 3 now documents that an empty `http.retryStatusCodes` disables all retries and that 401/404 can never be retried due to check ordering, with a new test; patch 4 now memsets the `slot_results` struct before each retry attempt in both `post_rpc()` and `probe_rpc()` to prevent stale values. Junio has already approved patches 1 through 4, and the series awaits his final look at patch 5/5, which changes the default `http.retryAfter` from 0 to 10 seconds.

Still open are two higher-level questions: whether configurable retry status codes should exist at all versus standardizing on 429 (the docs now frame non-429 codes as temporary workarounds for buggy providers), and Junio's procedural concern about introducing the 10-second default in a separate fifth patch rather than upfront. Patrick Steinhardt's substantive review points were incorporated in earlier versions, including restricting POST retries to read-only services like `git-upload-pack` while excluding push.
