This thread concerns Ravi Mistry's five-patch v3 series improving Git's HTTP transport resilience, addressing an off-by-one retry counter bug, adding exponential backoff when Retry-After headers are absent, making retryable status codes configurable via `http.retryStatusCodes`, extending retries to POST requests used in fetch and partial-clone operations, and formalizing a 10-second default for `http.retryAfter`. The series has resolved the earlier debate over the default retry delay, settling on 10 seconds as a compromise between brian m. carlson's push for longer delays to protect rate-limited servers and concerns about interactive usability, and it incorporates extensive documentation and implementation feedback from Junio C Hamano.

Patrick Steinhardt has provided substantive reviews of patches 1, 2, and 3, endorsing the exponential backoff approach and the off-by-one fix while raising organizational questions -- such as whether the two bug fixes in patch 1 belong in separate commits, whether the zero-retry case should return `HTTP_RATE_LIMITED` rather than `HTTP_ERROR`, and whether the backoff calculation could be expressed more directly. His latest review of patch 3 challenges the architectural decision to make retryable HTTP status codes user-configurable. Rather than accepting the opt-in design for 5xx errors, Patrick argues that Git should encourage providers to standardize on 429, warning that configurable codes perpetuate ecosystem fragmentation where users are told they are "holding it wrong" and must adjust client settings to match diverse server behaviors.

The series remains under review and has not been queued in any integration branch. Beyond Patrick's objections to the configurable status codes and his unaddressed points on the earlier patches, Junio has raised a procedural concern about whether the 10-second default should remain in a separate fifth patch or be folded into patch 2, with Ravi offering both options for the maintainer to decide.
