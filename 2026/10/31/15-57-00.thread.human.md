Ravi Mistry's five-patch series improving Git's HTTP transport resilience has completed review and is now heading into integration. The series addresses four long-standing gaps: an off-by-one bug in the 429 retry counter, the lack of exponential backoff when servers send no Retry-After header, the inability to retry transient 5xx errors, and the fact that POST requests (fetch, clone, and partial-clone blob fetches) were never retried at all. Over five iterations, the series grew configurable retry status codes via `http.retryStatusCodes`, exponential backoff with a new 10-second default for `http.retryAfter`, safe retries for read-only POST services while push stays fail-fast, and handling for explicit `Retry-After: 0` headers.

Junio C Hamano reviewed each patch and approved all of them, working through the details with the author: the retry counter fix, the overflow-safe backoff doubling, the documentation of edge cases like empty config values and 401/404 never being retryable, and the cleanup of stale slot results and gzip headers on POST retries. The one design-level question -- whether non-429 retry codes should be configurable at all -- was set aside in favor of documenting them as temporary workarounds for misbehaving provider infrastructure. In his latest message on the v5 cover letter, Junio accepted the final documentation clarifications in patch 3/5 and announced he will replace the topic with v5 and mark it ready for the `next` integration branch, pending no objections in the coming days.

In short: the series has cleared review after five versions, with all code-level concerns from Junio and Patrick Steinhardt resolved, and is now cooking in `next` -- not yet merged to `master`. The main remaining open item is simply the few-day objection window before graduation.
