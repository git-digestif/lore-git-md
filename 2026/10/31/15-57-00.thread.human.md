The HTTP transport resilience series from Ravi Mistry is now in its third version and addresses four key gaps in Git’s error handling: an off-by-one retry counter, missing exponential backoff, inability to retry transient 5xx errors, and lack of retries for POST requests. The series has converged on a compromise default of 10 seconds for `http.retryAfter`, resolving the main point of contention between Ravi and brian m. carlson, who had argued for a longer initial delay to protect servers. Junio’s feedback on earlier versions has been fully incorporated, including documentation refinements and implementation clarifications.

The latest addition, patch 4/5, extends retry logic to HTTP POST requests—critical for fetch, clone, and partial-clone operations—by refactoring retry timing into a shared helper and ensuring compressed requests retain their headers on retry. Tests now cover POST retries for 429 and 502 responses, as well as edge cases like gzipped payloads. The opt-in design for 5xx retries remains unchanged, avoiding controversy while allowing users to configure it. With all substantive feedback addressed and the default value settled, the series is technically complete and awaits Junio’s final review before integration.
