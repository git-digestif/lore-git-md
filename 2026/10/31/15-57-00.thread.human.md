This thread covers Ravi Mistry's five-patch v3 series improving Git's HTTP transport resilience: fixing an off-by-one retry counter and a Retry-After parsing race, adding exponential backoff when servers omit Retry-After headers, making retryable status codes configurable via `http.retryStatusCodes`, extending retries to POST requests used by fetch/clone and partial clone, and formalizing a 10-second default for `http.retryAfter`. The 10-second default resolved the main earlier debate between brian m. carlsson's push for stronger server protection and interactive usability concerns, and all of Junio C Hamano's substantive v1 feedback has been incorporated.

Patrick Steinhardt has now completed a substantive review of all five patches, and Ravi has begun responding. On patch 1, Ravi defended keeping the two small bug fixes in one commit and explained the error-code choice for the zero-retry case. On patch 2, Ravi just agreed to switch the new `attempt` parameter to an unsigned `size_t` in a future v4 and defended the iterative doubling loop over a direct bit-shift expression, citing undefined-behavior and overflow risks if a user sets a very large `http.maxRetries`. Patrick's optional suggestion to add jitter remains unaddressed.

The significant open items are Patrick's architectural objections on patches 3 and 4: he argues the configurable `http.retryStatusCodes` design may perpetuate provider fragmentation rather than driving convergence on 429, and he has flagged a correctness risk in patch 4 where retrying POST requests could duplicate or corrupt data during push operations, since `push_git()` also uses `post_rpc()`. He also questioned whether patch 5 properly honors an explicit `Retry-After: 0` header over the new 10-second default. Junio has additionally raised a procedural question about whether the default value should stay in a separate fifth patch or be folded into patch 2 in a v4, and Ravi has offered both options for Junio and brian to weigh in on.
