This thread proposes a four-patch v2 series from Elia Pinto fixing truncation bugs when Git handles objects of 4 GiB or larger on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32-bit, such as 64-bit Windows. The series converts the pack-receiving and verification pipeline -- `unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck` -- from `unsigned long` to `64-bit `size_t`, addressing inflate failures, crashes, spurious SHA-1 collision reports, and false corruption errors. Patrick Steinhardt has provided substantive review across the patches, and the author has agreed to a resubmission plan: patches 2/4 and 3/4 will be resent standalone as v3 with added overflow guards and an expensive test, while patches 1/4 and 4/4 will be deferred until two dependent `size_t` conversion series land.

The most recent exchange resolves an open question about patch 4/4. Patrick had asked whether `fsck` silently validates only a truncated prefix of oversized objects; Elia Pinto clarified that on current master the verify callback is never reached for such objects because `check_object_signature()` -- which also takes `unsigned long` -- hashes only the prefix and reports a spurious "packed <oid> is corrupt" error on an intact object. He concluded that 4/4 therefore belongs on top of the in-flight series converting `parse_object_buffer()` and `fsck_object()` to `size_t`, and will be resent there with a corrected commit message. He also noted that blobs are unaffected because `core.bigFileThreshold` is capped below 4 GiB, routing very large blobs through `stream_object_signature()` instead.

No maintainer decision has been recorded, and the series remains under review. No Reviewed-by or Acked-by has been given; the immediate next step is the v3 resubmission of patches 2/4 and 3/4, with 1/4 and 4/4 waiting on the dependent conversion work.
