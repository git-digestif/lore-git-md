This thread is a **bugfix series** from Elia Pinto addressing critical truncation issues when Git handles large objects (4 GiB or more) on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32-bit, such as Windows. The series converts key components in the pack-receiving and verification pipeline (`unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck`) from `unsigned long` to `size_t` to match the underlying pack code, fixing crashes, false SHA-1 collision reports, and partial object checks. Each patch targets a specific failure mode: crashes in `unpack-objects`, inflate inconsistencies in `index-pack --fix-thin`, false collision errors in `index-pack`, and incomplete object checks in `fsck`. The failures are all fatal or correctness-related, with no risk of corrupt data being written.

The series is self-contained, merges cleanly with other in-flight work, and touches files like `builtin/fsck.c`, `builtin/index-pack.c`, and `pack.h`. While no tests are included (due to the impracticality of generating 4 GiB objects or testing on 32-bit platforms), the commit messages provide detailed reproduction scenarios. The changes are mechanical but critical for Windows compatibility, making this a high-priority fix. No controversy is evident, and the series appears ready for review. The author notes that full correctness for non-streamed objects will require follow-up patches from related series, but this effort explicitly closes a gap left by earlier work on `fsck_object()` and object parsing.
