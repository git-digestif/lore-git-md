Elia Pinto has posted version 2 of a four-patch series fixing truncation bugs that occur when handling objects larger than 4 GiB on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32 bits (notably 64-bit Windows) while `size_t` is 64 bits. The series converts key parts of the pack-receiving and verification pipeline--`unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck`--from `unsigned long` to `size_t`, addressing inflate failures, crashes, false SHA-1 collision reports, and partial object checks. Version 2 drops a now-redundant hunk after `stream_blob()` was converted to `size_t` in master, adds a `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` guard in `write_object()` so oversized objects die with a clear error rather than being written truncated, and clarifies commit messages. The series remains under review with no merge status indicated.

Patrick Steinhardt has provided substantive reviews on patches 1, 2, and 4. On patch 1 he questioned whether the `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` guards are worth the maintainability cost given that a separate series is already converting `odb_write_object()` and `parse_object_buffer()` to `size_t`, suggesting the work might be deferred to avoid a redundant cleanup pass; he also flagged the commit message as overly verbose. On patch 2 he raised an overflow concern about adding the new `size_t` return value of `write_compressed()` to the signed `off_t` field `obj[1].idx.offset` in `append_obj_to_pack()`, and asked whether an `EXPENSIVE` test could cover the 4 GiB+ scenario. On patch 4 he now asks whether `parse_object_buffer()` and `fsck_object()`--which still accept `unsigned long`--actually detect that they received a truncated buffer and report an error, or whether they silently validate only the prefix of large objects and miss corruption in the unexamined tail, which would itself be a bug. None of these questions have been resolved.

Full correctness for non-streamed objects and `git fsck` depends on follow-up work converting `odb_write_object()`, `parse_object_buffer()`, and `fsck_object()` to `size_t`, which Pinto links to in the cover letter but which has not yet landed. No Reviewed-by, Acked-by, or formal objection has been recorded; the series is waiting on responses to Patrick Steinhardt's sequencing, overflow, test-coverage, and silent-truncation-detection questions.
