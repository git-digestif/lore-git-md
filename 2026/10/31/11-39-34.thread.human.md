This thread is a v2 four-patch bugfix series from Elia Pinto addressing how Git truncates or mishandles objects of 4 GiB or larger on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32 bits but `size_t` is 64 bits, most notably 64-bit Windows. The series converts the pack-receiving and verification pipeline -- `unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck` -- from `unsigned long` to `size_t` so that large objects are inflated, written, indexed, and verified correctly instead of producing "inflate returned -5" errors, crashes on large delta bases, false SHA-1 collision reports, or silently truncated writes. The series is based on master and is under review; no merge status has been indicated, and v1 drew no review comments.

The cover letter explains that v2 was rebased onto current master, where `stream_blob()` was already converted to `size_t` by a prior commit, so patch 1/4 now focuses on the remaining paths: non-streamed objects, `unpack-objects -n`, and delta handling. Patch 1/4 itself, which has now arrived, reworks `builtin/unpack-objects.c` across roughly a dozen functions and structures, and adds a `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` guard in `write_object()` so that objects too large for the still-`unsigned long` downstream functions (`odb_write_object()`, `parse_object_buffer()`) fail with a clear error rather than being written truncated. No tests accompany the patches, since reproducing the bugs requires 4 GiB objects or a 32-bit platform; the author describes simulating the truncation at 16 bits instead.

Nothing is decided or contested yet -- no reviewer has weighed in, and there are no objections. The main open item is that full correctness for non-streamed objects depends on follow-up work converting `odb_write_object()`, `parse_object_buffer()`, and `fsck_object()` to `size_t`, which the author links to in the cover letter but which is not part of this series. A related `pack-objects` fix for streamed large blobs was already merged separately.
