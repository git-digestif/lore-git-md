This thread is Elia Pinto's v2 series of four patches fixing truncation and correctness bugs when Git handles objects of 4 GiB or larger on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit (notably 64-bit Windows). The patches convert `unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck` from `unsigned long` to `size_t`, addressing inflate failures, crashes, false SHA-1 collision reports, and partial object verification. Version 2 was rebased onto master after a related `stream_blob()` conversion landed, and reframes patch 1 around a `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` guard that makes oversized objects die cleanly instead of being written truncated.

Patrick Steinhardt has provided substantive reviews that keep the series open on several fronts: whether the series should be deferred until the in-flight downstream conversions of `odb_write_object()` and `parse_object_buffer()` land (making the guards redundant), whether the `off_t` addition in `append_obj_to_pack()` needs an overflow check now that `write_compressed()` returns `size_t`, whether an EXPENSIVE test could cover the 4 GiB scenario, and whether `fsck`'s downstream path silently validates only a truncated prefix of large objects rather than failing loudly. None of these questions is resolved, no Reviewed-by or Acked-by has been given, and the series remains under review with no merge status indicated.

Junio C Hamano has now weighed in on the presentation side of the thread, agreeing with Patrick that the commit message on patch 1 is excessively verbose and generalizing the point: he suggests contributors consider using an LLM to condense commit messages by roughly 70% while preserving substance. His email adds nothing technical about the fixes themselves and does not move any of the open review questions toward resolution.
