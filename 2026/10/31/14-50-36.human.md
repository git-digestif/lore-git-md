This is a follow-up in the ongoing design discussion about fixing a race condition in Git’s `sigchain` subsystem. The thread is refining the proposed signal-handling redesign for threaded Git code, specifically whether to block SIGPIPE (and other thread-directed signals) during thread creation.

Phillip Wood acknowledges Oswald Buddenhagen’s clarification that SIGPIPE is inherently thread-directed, unlike process-directed signals such as SIGINT or SIGHUP. Phillip now realizes that SIGBUS, SIGFPE, SIGILL, and SIGSEGV are also thread-directed, meaning they should *not* be blocked during thread creation if the goal is to ensure the process is killed when they occur. He notes that `sigchain_push_common()` installs a handler for SIGPIPE, raising the theoretical possibility of concurrent unwinding in multiple threads, though he is unsure how likely this is in practice. As a potential mitigation, he suggests using an atomic flag in the signal handler to prevent races, though he acknowledges the complexity of doing this safely.

The discussion remains in the design phase, with no objections to the core proposal but a growing awareness that thread-directed signals require separate reasoning. The thread is still awaiting feedback from Jeff King and Junio before moving to a v2 patch series. If you are following Git’s signal-handling internals or threaded code safety, this is a detail-oriented but important refinement to the earlier proposal.
