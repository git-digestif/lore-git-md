This is a **bugfix series** targeting the pack-receiving and verification pipeline, addressing truncation issues when handling large objects (4 GiB or more) on platforms where `unsigned long` is 32-bit (notably Windows). The series converts several key components from `unsigned long` to `size_t` to match the underlying pack code, fixing crashes, false SHA-1 collision reports, and partial object checks in `unpack-objects`, `index-pack`, and `git fsck`.

The cover letter clearly motivates each patch with specific failure modes: patch 1/4 fixes a crash in `unpack-objects` on Windows when receiving objects ≥4 GiB; patch 2/4 resolves a "serious inflate inconsistency" in `index-pack --fix-thin` when local bases exceed 4 GiB; patch 3/4 prevents false "SHA1 COLLISION FOUND" errors in `index-pack` on Windows; and patch 4/4 ensures `git fsck` checks full objects rather than prefixes in packs. The failures are all fatal (`die`) or correctness issues, with no risk of corrupt data being written. The series is self-contained and merges cleanly with other in-flight work, though full correctness for non-streamed objects requires follow-up patches from related series (object parsing and ODB write sizes).

Files touched include `builtin/fsck.c`, `builtin/index-pack.c`, `builtin/unpack-objects.c`, `odb.h`, and `pack.h`, with changes primarily involving type conversions and minor signature adjustments. No tests are included due to the impracticality of generating 4 GiB objects or testing on 32-bit platforms, but the commit messages provide detailed reproduction scenarios (e.g., simulating 16-bit systems for patch 2/4). The series explicitly closes a gap left by earlier work on `fsck_object()` and object parsing, while deferring `pack-objects` delta search fixes to a future effort.

The patches appear well-scoped and technically sound, with clear explanations of the underlying issues. The lack of tests is justified by the constraints, and the series addresses a real-world problem (Windows compatibility with large objects) that has likely affected users. The changes are mechanical but critical for correctness, making this a high-priority fix for platforms with 32-bit `unsigned long`. No controversy is evident, and the series looks ready for review.
