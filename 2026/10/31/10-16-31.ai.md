This is the **cover letter for v3 of a four-patch bugfix series** targeting 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms) and general integer-overflow safety. The series converts several key length and counter variables from 32-bit types (`unsigned long`, `long`, `unsigned`, `int`) to `size_t` to prevent silent data corruption, crashes, or incorrect metadata handling when Git processes files larger than 4 GiB.

The v3 iteration addresses the last two procedural issues raised by Patrick Steinhardt in the v2 review. The first patch’s test script (`t1007-hash-object.sh`) now avoids redundant file I/O by dropping the size comparison and using `git cat-file -e` to verify object existence, eliminating a race-condition risk. The fourth patch’s commit message has been simplified to avoid overstating the overflow risk and to remove overly detailed references to `st_add()`/`st_mult()`. No technical objections remain, and the series appears ready for integration.

The patches touch 11 files across the object database (`odb.c`, `odb.h`, and related source files), diff (`diff.c`), and file conversion (`convert.c`, `attr.c`) subsystems. The changes are mechanical type conversions with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux, but they prevent real-world issues on Windows: truncated blobs, crashes in word-diff buffer allocation, and incorrect binary detection or CRLF/keyword expansion. The on-disk format remains unchanged. The revised test in `t1007` is marked `EXPENSIVE` and `SIZE_T_IS_64BIT`, matching the existing test for hashing large files.

The series builds on prior work by Johannes Schindelin (read-side ODB, commit c6a4629e32) and Jeff King (xdiff, commit 3c7a31204f), and the author notes that a scan identified 237 Windows-specific truncations to `long`/`unsigned long`—this series fixes four of them. The fourth patch preemptively addresses a theoretical overflow risk in `convert.c` counters (used for CRLF/keyword decisions) by converting them to `size_t`, even though the risk is not Windows-specific. The author has rebased the series on current `master` with no conflicts.

No other participants have reviewed the series beyond Patrick’s feedback, which was narrowly focused on test hygiene and commit-message style. The patches are well-scoped, thoroughly motivated, and address clear correctness issues on a supported platform. The addition of test coverage and the preemptive fix for overflow risks in `convert.c` strengthen the series, and it appears to be on track for merging.
