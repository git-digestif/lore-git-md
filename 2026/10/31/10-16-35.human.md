This is the fourth and final patch in Elia Pinto’s four-part bugfix series targeting 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). It converts the text statistics and ident counters in `convert.c` from `unsigned`/`int` to `size_t` to prevent theoretical overflow when processing files larger than 4 GiB. While the overflow risk is not Windows-specific, the change aligns with the series’ broader goal of eliminating truncation and overflow bugs in file-size handling.

The patch touches only `convert.c`, changing the `struct text_stat` counters (`nul`, `lonecr`, `lonelf`, `crlf`, `printable`, `nonprintable`) and the `count_ident()` helper from 32-bit types to `size_t`. The commit message gives a concrete example: a file with exactly 2³² lone LFs would wrap the `lonelf` counter to zero, causing `will_convert_lf_to_crlf()` to miss the conversion. The fix also updates `strbuf_grow()` to use `st_add()` and `st_mult()` for overflow-safe arithmetic, though the commit message now avoids overstating the risk after Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback on the v2 phrasing.

No test is added because triggering the overflow would require impractical input sizes (>4 GiB), but the change is a sensible preemptive measure. The patch is mechanical and low-risk, with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux. Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the v2 version and explicitly approved it ("The patch itself looks good to me"), and the v3 iteration resolves the last procedural feedback by simplifying the commit message. The series as a whole is now ready for integration.
