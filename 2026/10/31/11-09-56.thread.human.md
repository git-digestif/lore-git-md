This thread is Elia Pinto's patch series fixing object-size truncation on 32-bit platforms (Windows/LLP64), where object sizes above 4 GiB were silently truncated because in-memory buffer sizes were passed around as `unsigned long`. The series converts the object-parsing and fsck paths -- `parse_object_buffer()`, `parse_commit_buffer()`, `parse_tag_buffer()`, `check_object_signature()`, the fsck callbacks, `copy_subject()`, and related functions across about two dozen files -- to use `size_t` for in-memory sizes, while leaving on-disk sizes to a separate companion patch using `off_t`. The user-visible symptoms being fixed include `git fsck` only inspecting a prefix of very large objects and `git tag -v` failing to locate signatures.

The series is still at v1 and under review, with no code-level feedback so far; the only substantive critique came from Simon Richter, who pointed out that `size_t` remains 32 bits on these platforms and is therefore wrong for on-disk sizes -- a point Elia addressed by splitting the on-disk `off_t` work into a separate patch. In his latest message Elia pings the list again, noting the series still applies cleanly to master and asking explicitly whether reviewers want the `EXPENSIVE` tests guarded by `!LONG_IS_64BIT` that the cover letter offered. He also flags that the series has become a blocking dependency for Patrick Steinhardt's pack-receive work: two patches there are being held back because they need `parse_object_buffer()` to accept `size_t` without a cast, and because `git fsck` currently cannot reach its verify callbacks for objects of 4 GiB or more. Whether the series advances toward `next` or needs a v2 now hinges on that test question and on any substantive review arriving.
