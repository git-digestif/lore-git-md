This is a documentation patch discussion, specifically about the ongoing effort to modernize cross-references in Git’s man pages. Jean-Noël Avila chimes in on the technical rationale for using the two-part AsciiDoc link syntax (`<<target,text>>`) rather than the single form (`<<target>>`). His point is straightforward: translated man pages may need to adjust the displayed text independently of the anchor target, so the two-part form is more future-proof for localization. This reinforces the design choice already made in the approved patch (queued in `next` since July 2024) and adds a new dimension—i18n—to the earlier arguments about rendering consistency and link stability. The email does not challenge the patch’s readiness or propose changes; it simply provides another reason to prefer the current approach. For a reader scanning the digest, this is a small but useful clarification: the cross-reference modernization is not just about HTML navigation, it also prepares the documentation for better translation support.
