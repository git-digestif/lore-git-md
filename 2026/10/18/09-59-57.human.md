This is a **substantive review** in the ongoing discussion about `branch.autoSetupMerge=inherit` behavior. Phillip Wood weighs in on the design question raised by Junio C Hamano: whether `inherit` should set up tracking for *all* starting points (including tags, commit hashes, or local branches without tracking) or only for remote-tracking branches.

Phillip’s position is pragmatic and user-focused. He describes two common workflows: when branching from a pushed local branch (e.g., `origin/master`), he prefers tracking the remote branch rather than the starting point, but when branching from a remote-tracking branch (e.g., `origin/main`), he *does* want to track the starting point. This suggests the current behavior (doing nothing for non-tracking starting points) is not inherently worse than the proposed change, but neither is it clearly better. His feedback implies that the real issue may be the lack of flexibility in tracking configuration, rather than a flaw in `inherit` itself.

He also notes that the existing tracking options for `git switch -c` are confusing enough that he often manually runs `git branch --set-upstream-to` afterward to set the desired upstream. This underscores a broader usability problem: users may not trust Git’s automatic tracking setup, regardless of the mode. While he doesn’t outright reject the patch, his comments suggest that a more nuanced solution—such as Junio’s proposed `inherit-or-fail` or `inherit-or-direct` modes—might better serve real-world workflows.
