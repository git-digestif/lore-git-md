This is a **substantive review** in the ongoing design discussion about `branch.autoSetupMerge=inherit` behavior. Phillip Wood weighs in on the core question: whether `inherit` should set up tracking for starting points that lack existing tracking configuration (e.g., tags, commit hashes, or local branches without tracking).

Phillip’s position is pragmatic and user-centric. He describes two common workflows: (1) when creating a local branch from a remote-tracking branch (e.g., `origin/main`), he *does* want the new branch to track the starting point, but (2) when creating a local branch from a pushed-but-not-tracked branch (e.g., `my-feature` pushed to `origin/my-feature`), he *does not* want the new branch to track the starting point—instead, he prefers to track something like `origin/main`. This real-world example undermines the patch’s proposed behavior (tracking the starting point directly when no tracking exists) and suggests that the current behavior (doing nothing for non-tracking starting points) may actually be more intuitive for some users.

Phillip stops short of endorsing either the patch or the status quo, calling them "not better or worse" than each other. His feedback shifts the discussion from a technical bugfix to a usability question: what do users *expect* `inherit` to do when the starting point lacks tracking? His closing remark—about the confusion around `git switch -c` tracking options—hints that the entire tracking setup mechanism may need broader rethinking, though he does not propose a specific solution here.

The review is substantive but not tested; Phillip does not report building or running the code, nor does he suggest an alternative implementation. His contribution is primarily conceptual, providing a concrete user scenario that challenges the patch’s assumptions. This input will likely influence whether the patch proceeds as-is, expands to include Junio’s proposed modes (`inherit-or-fail`, `inherit-or-direct`), or stalls pending further clarification from the original `inherit` author.
