This is a **substantive reply** in the ongoing discussion about Harald Nordgren’s patch series to extend `` `git branch --delete-merged` `` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The email responds directly to Junio C Hamano’s proposal to reuse `` `git range-diff` ``’s commit-matching logic for rebase detection, reporting on a **local experiment** that tested this approach.

Harald implemented a version of the feature that uses `` `git range-diff` `` to pair commits for rebase detection (while keeping squash detection behind an opt-in flag). His findings are **technical and performance-focused**: the range-diff approach correctly identifies the same 14 branches to delete as the v2 implementation, but it is **20× slower** (10 seconds vs. 0.5 seconds for 65 branches). He also clarifies that `` `git range-diff` `` pairs commits based on diff content alone, with log messages and authorship appearing only in the output — meaning these metadata do not influence the matching process itself. This contradicts Junio’s assumption that range-diff’s commit-matching logic could be reused to improve rebase detection by incorporating log-message similarity and authorship.

The email advances the thread by **providing concrete data** on the trade-offs between accuracy and performance. Harald’s experiment suggests that while `` `git range-diff` `` may offer more robust matching for rebases, its overhead makes it impractical for the `` `--delete-merged` `` use case, where speed is critical for usability. This leaves the rebase-detection algorithm at an impasse: the current tree-based diffing approach is fast but may miss edge cases (e.g., edited commit messages), while range-diff is more thorough but too slow. The discussion now hinges on whether a middle ground exists — such as using patch IDs (as proposed by D. Ben Knoble) or selectively applying range-diff’s logic as a fallback — or whether the project should accept the limitations of the faster approach.

The email does not address Junio’s broader **CLI redesign proposal** (decoupling `` `--delete-merged` `` and `` `--delete-irrelevant` ``) or Phillip Wood’s call for clearer documentation of the algorithm’s limitations. These remain open questions, but Harald’s performance data will likely shape the next iteration of the series.
