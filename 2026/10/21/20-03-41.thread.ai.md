Subject: [PATCH] Documentation: define the voting process
Series version: standalone patch (no series)
Originating author: Emily Shaffer
Thread type: governance proposal (process documentation)

Core problem or goal
Define a structured voting process for the Git project to resolve contentious decisions when traditional consensus-based discussion fails. The immediate motivation is the unresolved AI contributions debate, but the patch is process-only: it specifies *how* to vote, not *what* policy to adopt. The proposal adds a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file, updates `Documentation/DecisionMaking.adoc` to reference it, and wires the new document into the build system.

Current status
Under review; no prior versions. Junio C Hamano (maintainer) has refined the proposal’s framing by rejecting time-bound or proportional eligibility rules as overly complex, reinforcing a binary standard (one person, one vote). Emily’s latest reply clarifies that the process is designed for **multi-option ballots** (e.g., ranked-choice voting among competing proposals), addressing Junio’s question about plurality vs. proportional outcomes, but the document itself still leaves the exact mechanism unspecified (`<mechanism TBD>`). The patch remains self-contained with no external dependencies. The thread has now reached consensus that **secret ballots** are a necessary safeguard to protect contributors from external pressure (employers or community), with Emily committing to update the proposal to include this. Eligibility criteria remain contested: Junio prefers a stricter binary standard (e.g., "two or more topics in released versions"), while Emily leans toward inclusivity (citing her own reduced contributions due to role changes). Justin Tobler’s warning about corporate bias in stricter criteria persists, as does Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s ideological critique that voting inherently favors corporate interests. Logistics for secret ballots (e.g., third-party tools like CIVS, trusted intermediaries) are unaddressed.

Open questions and loose ends
- **Voting mechanism**: The document still leaves the mechanism unspecified (`<mechanism TBD>`), though Emily’s reply clarifies the intent is for **multi-option ballots** (e.g., ranked-choice voting). Tools like CIVS and devotee are cited as potential models, but no decision has been made.
- **Eligibility criteria**: Junio’s preference for a stricter binary standard (e.g., "two or more topics in released versions") remains in tension with Emily’s inclusivity stance. Jeff King’s alternative (filtering by total commit count and recency) is also under consideration. Justin Tobler’s warning about corporate bias in stricter criteria is unresolved.
- **Versioning**: Junio’s suggestion to use Git OIDs to uniquely identify proposal texts remains unresolved (e.g., how voters reference these OIDs).
- **Secret ballots**: Now agreed as necessary (Emily, Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, brian m. carlson), but logistics (e.g., third-party tools, trusted intermediaries) are unaddressed. Kristoffer’s ideological critique (that voting inherently favors corporate interests) persists, though the secret ballot consensus may mitigate some concerns.
- **Adoption of formal voting**: The central open question—whether the project should adopt any formal voting process at all—remains unanswered. Kristoffer’s critique (that voting inherently favors corporate interests, particularly on AI-related policies) is unresolved, though the secret ballot proposal may address some of these concerns.

Participant positions
- **Emily Shaffer (author)**: Proposes the process as a last-resort tool, explicitly framing it as a response to the AI debate stalemate. Confirms the intent is for **multi-option ballots** (e.g., ranked-choice voting) to determine broadly acceptable outcomes. Leans toward inclusivity in eligibility criteria but defers to community consensus. Now agrees that **secret ballots** are necessary to protect contributors from external pressure, committing to update the proposal. Suggests using the same approval mechanism (general consensus + `Acked-by:` trailers) that was used for the 2019 Code of Conduct for this document itself.
- **Junio C Hamano (maintainer)**: Constructive and matter-of-fact; accepts the patch’s premise and engages with technical refinements. Rejects time-bound or proportional eligibility rules as overly complex, reinforcing a binary standard (one person, one vote). Prefers a stricter but still binary eligibility criterion (e.g., "two or more topics in released versions") to exclude drive-by contributors but acknowledges the trade-offs raised by Justin Tobler. Explicitly endorses **secret ballots** as a necessary safeguard, aligning with Patrick Steinhardt and brian m. carlson.
- **Kristoffer Haugsbakk (documentation contributor)**: Skeptical of the proposal’s premise, arguing that formal voting would inherently favor corporate interests. His latest critique narrows the focus to *paid* contributors (not all corporate-affiliated contributors), asserting that their votes may in aggregate align with employer interests, particularly on AI-related policies. His critique is ideological, questioning whether voting is the right solution for Git’s governance, and does not engage with the secret ballot proposal or propose alternatives.
- **Patrick Steinhardt (GitLab engineer)**: Pragmatic supporter of the proposal; endorses the *idea* of formal voting but advocates for **secret ballots** to address concerns about corporate influence. His intervention shifts the debate from "whether voting is appropriate" to "how to design voting fairly."
- **Justin Tobler (contributor)**: Aligns with Junio’s concerns about eligibility but cautions against over-correction that could skew representation toward corporate-affiliated contributors. Proposes a time-bound filter (e.g., contributions within the last *X* releases) to exclude dormant contributors while avoiding bias, but Junio’s latest reply rejects this as overly complex. Reinforces Junio’s "topics in released versions" metric over tracking commits in `next`.
- **brian m. carlson (long-time contributor)**: Rebuts Kristoffer’s critique by asserting that corporate-affiliated contributors are capable of independent judgment. Emphasizes personal integrity and endorses **secret ballots** as a safeguard against external pressure (from employers or the community). Their reply shifts the debate toward individual agency and process design, but Kristoffer’s latest reply dismisses this as irrelevant to the structural issue.
- **Jeff King (Peff) (long-time contributor)**: Proposes an alternative eligibility criterion based on total commit count and recency, gaining traction in the thread as a middle ground between inclusivity and perceived legitimacy.
