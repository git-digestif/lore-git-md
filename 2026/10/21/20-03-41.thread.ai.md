Originating author: Emily Shaffer
Thread type: governance proposal (process documentation)

Core problem or goal
Define a structured voting process for the Git project to resolve contentious decisions when traditional consensus-based discussion fails. The immediate motivation is the unresolved AI contributions debate, but the patch is process-only: it specifies *how* to vote, not *what* policy to adopt. The proposal adds a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file, updates `Documentation/DecisionMaking.adoc` to reference it, and wires the new document into the build system.

Current status
Under review; no prior versions. Junio C Hamano (maintainer) has suggested the final document title be "define the voting process" (rather than "propose a voting process") to reflect its authoritative role once merged. The patch is self-contained and does not depend on external work. Junio’s feedback has advanced the proposal by clarifying versioning (using Git OIDs to identify proposal texts), vote-counting mechanics (single-option vs. proportional outcomes), and eligibility criteria (raising the bar to two or more topics in a released version of Git). Patrick Steinhardt and Junio now agree that **secret ballots** are a necessary safeguard to address concerns about corporate influence and external pressure, reframing the debate from "whether voting is appropriate" to "how to design voting fairly." Justin Tobler’s reply surfaces a tension in eligibility design: stricter criteria risk skewing representation toward corporate-affiliated contributors, while looser criteria may admit inactive or drive-by participants. brian m. carlson’s reply challenges Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s assumption that corporate-affiliated contributors would inevitably vote in alignment with their employers’ interests, arguing that personal integrity and secret ballots can mitigate such concerns.

Open questions and loose ends
- **Voting mechanism**: Still unspecified in the document (flagged as TBD); Junio’s feedback surfaces the need to clarify whether outcomes are determined by plurality (single-option) or proportional support (multi-option).
- **Eligibility criteria**: Junio proposes a stricter standard (two or more topics in a released version of Git) to exclude drive-by contributors, but Justin Tobler warns this could disproportionately favor corporate-affiliated contributors. Justin also questions whether dormant contributors (e.g., inactive for 15 years) should retain voting rights, suggesting a time-bound filter (e.g., contributions within the last *X* releases). The community must balance recency, impact, and inclusivity to ensure the criteria’s perceived legitimacy. brian’s reply does not directly address eligibility but reinforces the need for secret ballots to protect individual autonomy.
- **Versioning**: Junio suggests using Git OIDs to uniquely identify proposal texts, but implementation details (e.g., how voters reference these OIDs) remain unresolved.
- **Secret ballots**: Patrick Steinhardt, brian m. carlson, and Junio now agree that secret ballots are necessary to mitigate concerns about corporate influence and external pressure. However, the logistics of anonymity (e.g., third-party tools, trusted intermediaries) are unaddressed. This introduces a new open question about how to reconcile secrecy with the project’s transparency norms, particularly given brian’s argument that anonymity would protect contributors from both employer and community pressure.
- **Adoption of formal voting**: The central open question—whether the project should adopt any formal voting process at all—remains unanswered. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s critique (that voting inherently favors corporate interests) persists, though brian’s reply and the emerging consensus on secret ballots may address some of these concerns. The ideological debate about voting’s neutrality is still unresolved, with brian’s intervention shifting the focus toward individual agency and process safeguards.
- Emily plans to self-review the patch to flag these open questions explicitly.

Participant positions
- **Emily Shaffer (author)**: Proposes the process as a last-resort tool, explicitly framing it as a response to the AI debate stalemate. Suggests using the same approval mechanism (general consensus + `Acked-by:` trailers) that was used for the 2019 Code of Conduct for this document itself.
- **Junio C Hamano (maintainer)**: Constructive and matter-of-fact; accepts the patch’s premise and engages with technical refinements (versioning, vote counting, eligibility). Implicitly endorses **secret ballots** as a necessary safeguard, aligning with Patrick Steinhardt and brian m. carlson. Proposes stricter eligibility criteria (two or more topics in a released version of Git) to exclude drive-by contributors but acknowledges the trade-offs raised by Justin Tobler.
- **Kristoffer Haugsbakk (documentation contributor)**: Skeptical of the proposal’s premise, arguing that formal voting would inherently favor corporate interests (e.g., AI adoption) due to the composition of active contributors. His critique is ideological rather than procedural, questioning whether voting is the right solution for Git’s governance. Does not propose alternatives or revisions to the patch.
- **Patrick Steinhardt (GitLab engineer)**: Pragmatic supporter of the proposal; endorses the *idea* of formal voting but advocates for **secret ballots** to address concerns about corporate influence. His intervention shifts the debate from "whether voting is appropriate" to "how to design voting fairly."
- **Justin Tobler (contributor)**: Aligns with Junio’s concerns about eligibility but cautions against over-correction that could skew representation toward corporate-affiliated contributors. Proposes a time-bound filter (e.g., contributions within the last *X* releases) to exclude dormant contributors while avoiding bias. Reinforces Junio’s "topics in released versions" metric over tracking commits in `next`.
- **brian m. carlson (long-time contributor)**: Rebuts Kristoffer’s critique by asserting that corporate-affiliated contributors are capable of independent judgment. Emphasizes personal integrity and endorses **secret ballots** as a safeguard against external pressure (from employers or the community). Their reply shifts the debate toward individual agency and process design.
