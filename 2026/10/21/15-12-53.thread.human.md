This thread is about a bug in `git rev-list` where options like `--grep`, `--author`, or `--since` misparse their arguments if the next argument starts with `--`. Azuama Odinaka Maximus posted a v4 patch that fixes the issue for 18 options by introducing a helper function to recognize them during an early argument scan, but Junio C Hamano pointed out a critical flaw: the helper hardcodes option names, creating a maintenance hazard. The author then proposed a v5 direction to refactor the early scan to reuse the main parser’s option-recognition logic, avoiding hardcoded lists.

Junio’s latest response challenges the entire premise of the early scan, suggesting it might be unnecessary. Instead of sharing parser logic, he proposes eliminating the early scan altogether and solving the underlying problem (`.fetch_if_missing` and related defaults) by recording option settings during normal parsing and synthesizing the final configuration at the end. This would avoid duplicating parser logic entirely, but it’s a more invasive change that would require reevaluating the original 2017 commit that introduced the early scan. The thread is now at a crossroads: the v4 patch is uncontroversial on its core logic but architecturally flawed, while the proposed v5 refactor and Junio’s alternative both remain unresolved. No decision has been made on which direction to pursue, and the patch is not ready for merging.
