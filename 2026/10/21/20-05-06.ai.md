This is a documentation patch that formalizes a community voting process for the Git project. It establishes a structured three-phase approach—proposal collection, voting window, and implementation—for resolving contentious decisions when consensus cannot be reached through normal discussion. The patch adds two new files: `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` (the detailed process) and a brief reference in `Documentation/DecisionMaking.adoc`. It is explicitly *not* a proposal for the AI contribution policy itself, but rather the mechanism by which such a policy (or any other divisive issue) would be decided.

The proposed process is modeled after the 2019 Code of Conduct adoption, aiming for broad agreement on the voting framework before applying it to specific issues. Eligibility to vote is restricted to contributors with merged commits or multiple code reviews, with a cutoff date to prevent last-minute "dogpiling" by outsiders. The patch leaves the voting mechanism unspecified (noted as "TBD"), but Emily Shaffer’s follow-up email explores options like Debian’s *devotee*, the Linux Foundation’s *CIVS*, or GitHub polls, with a preference for email-based systems to align with Git’s mailing-list culture.

Key open questions remain, particularly around the voting mechanism and eligibility criteria. Emily acknowledges trade-offs: balancing inclusivity (e.g., non-code contributors like moderators) against practicality (scriptable eligibility checks) and resistance to manipulation. The patch is well-motivated—it directly addresses the AI policy stalemate discussed at the Contributor Summit—but its success hinges on whether the community accepts the proposed guardrails. If adopted, this process could become a template for future contentious decisions, making it a foundational governance document. The patch itself is uncontroversial in structure, but the unresolved details (e.g., voting system, eligibility tweaks) may spark debate.
