Here’s the human-readable summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread proposes a four-patch series to improve `git worktree list` filtering and safety. The core idea is to extend `%(worktreepath)` in `git for-each-ref` to show worktree paths for branches being rebased or bisected, while also making `git worktree remove` refuse to delete in-progress worktrees (rebase, bisect, cherry-pick, revert, or `git am`) unless `--force` is given. The series is now at **v4**, with patches 1 (safety fix) and 2 (documentation) deemed ready for integration, while patches 3 (extending `%(worktreepath)`) and 4 (cleanup) await further review.

The main debate centers on whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering worktrees. Phillip Wood and Ben Knoble have raised concerns about its limitations—no NUL-terminated output, inability to distinguish main/linked worktrees, and risks of surfacing mid-rebase worktrees in filtered output. The series mitigates the latter by making `git worktree remove` safer, but the broader question of whether `for-each-ref` is fit for this purpose remains unresolved. Eric Sunshine also flagged a broken invariant: filtering worktrees disrupts the guarantee that the main worktree appears first in output, which could break scripts.

The latest twist comes from D. Ben Knoble, who challenges the documentation’s claim that a branch can be checked out in multiple worktrees. He argues Git’s safety mechanisms should prevent this entirely, making the warning misleading. If he’s right, the documentation needs revision; if not, it should clarify how such a scenario could occur. This ambiguity must be resolved before the documentation patch can merge. The author, Jasper Mayone, has addressed most prior feedback (including Phillip Wood’s edge cases for `worktree_is_in_progress()`), but this new question adds a critical blocker. Junio has so far only weighed in procedurally, leaving the technical discussion to the community.
