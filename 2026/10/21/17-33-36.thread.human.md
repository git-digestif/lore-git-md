This thread proposes and refines a two-patch series to improve Git's worktree safety and usability. The first patch, already merged, makes `git worktree remove` refuse to delete worktrees in the middle of a rebase, bisect, or other in-progress operation unless `--force` is given, protecting uncommitted work. The second patch, now in its fourth iteration, extends `git for-each-ref`'s `%(worktreepath)` atom to report the worktree path for branches being rebased or bisected, closing a gap where these branches appeared "detached" in earlier versions.

Jasper Mayone is the author, and the series has drawn substantive review from Phillip Wood, Eric Sunshine, and Ben Knoble. The latest version addresses Phillip's feedback by eliminating code duplication (exposing a shared helper in `branch.c` instead of copying logic), removing a redundant `+` marker in `git branch` output, and documenting a breaking change: `%(worktreepath)` no longer prints the `git_dir` of bare worktrees. The patch also adds a warning about parsing paths with newlines, recommending NUL-terminated output. While these changes resolve immediate technical and usability concerns, broader questions remain about whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering worktrees. Its limitations—no NUL-terminated output, inability to distinguish main from linked worktrees, and risk of surfacing mid-rebase worktrees—are now documented but unaddressed. The series is technically sound and backward-incompatible only for scripts relying on the old behavior for bare worktrees or the `+` marker, both of which are clearly documented. No integration branch has been assigned, and Junio's feedback has been procedural rather than technical.
