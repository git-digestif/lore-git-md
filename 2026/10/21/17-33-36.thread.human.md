This thread proposes adding filtering capabilities to `git worktree list` by extending the `%(worktreepath)` atom in `ref-filter.c` to report worktree paths for branches in rebase or bisect state, while also making `git worktree remove` safer by refusing to delete in-progress worktrees unless `--force` is given. The series is now at **v4** (relabeled from v3 per Junio’s request), but the core design questions remain unresolved.

The two patches address real gaps: the `%(worktreepath)` change aligns behavior with `git worktree add` and `git branch -d`, and the safety fix prevents accidental deletion of worktrees mid-rebase or bisect. However, reviewers have raised significant concerns about the approach. Eric Sunshine and Phillip Wood argue that filtering worktrees via `for-each-ref` breaks the invariant that the main worktree appears first in output, complicating scripting. Phillip and Ben Knoble also highlight that `for-each-ref` lacks NUL-terminated output, cannot distinguish main from linked worktrees, and risks surfacing mid-rebase worktrees in ways that could lead to data loss. The series mitigates the latter risk by making `git worktree remove` refuse deletion of in-progress worktrees unless forced, but the broader question of whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering remains open.

The thread has not advanced beyond these critiques. Jasper Mayone has not responded to the design objections, and Junio’s feedback was purely procedural. Key open questions include whether the main worktree should be filtered out by default, how to handle branches checked out in multiple worktrees, and whether a `foreach`-style mechanism would be a better fit for this use case. Until these are addressed, the series is unlikely to move forward.
