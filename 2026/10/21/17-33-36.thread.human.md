This thread is about a five-patch series by Jasper Mayone that adds filtering capabilities to `git worktree list` and improves safety around worktree operations. The series has reached its fifth version, addressing nearly all prior feedback, but Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has raised concerns about the final two patches—particularly whether the new `%(currentworktree)` atom and the cleanup of `git branch` output are necessary or desirable.

The first patch, which adds a safety check to `git worktree remove` to prevent deletion of worktrees in the middle of operations like rebase or bisect, is uncontroversial and ready for integration. Junio’s latest feedback on this patch is forward-looking, suggesting future refactoring to unify safety checks across commands but not blocking its acceptance. The rest of the series introduces two new ref-filter atoms (`%(worktreepath)` and `%(currentworktree)`), improves performance with O(1) lookups, and makes backward-incompatible changes to `git branch` output to reduce redundancy. Junio has questioned whether the final patch, which suppresses `+` markers and paths for rebasing/bisecting branches in the current worktree, is a regression for users who rely on that output. He also wonders if `%(currentworktree)` has broader utility beyond this cleanup, implying the series might be better off stopping at the third patch.

Key participants include Phillip Wood, who proposed the `%(currentworktree)` atom and hashmap refactor (both now adopted), and Ben Knoble, who raised safety concerns that were addressed in the series. Eric Sunshine and Kristoffer Haugsbakk also contributed feedback on invariants and gaps in the original implementation. The series is fully tested and includes documentation updates, but Junio’s objections to the final patches remain unresolved. The next steps will likely involve Jasper revising or defending the contested changes, particularly around `git branch` output and the `%(currentworktree)` atom’s purpose.
