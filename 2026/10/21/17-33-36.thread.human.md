This thread is about adding filtering capabilities to `git worktree list` to help developers identify worktrees tied to specific branches, while also fixing two safety issues: preventing accidental deletion of worktrees mid-operation (like rebase or bisect) and ensuring `git for-each-ref`'s `%(worktreepath)` atom correctly reports worktree paths for branches in those states. The series, now at v4, consists of two patches—one already merged that makes `git worktree remove` refuse deletion of in-progress worktrees without `--force`, and another that extends `%(worktreepath)` to cover rebasing/bisecting branches.

The discussion has revealed significant concerns about the design. Phillip Wood and Eric Sunshine have highlighted that using `for-each-ref` for filtering breaks key invariants, like the main worktree always appearing first in output, and lacks safety features such as NUL-terminated output or distinguishing between main and linked worktrees. There’s also debate over whether `for-each-ref --merged` should exclude mid-rebase worktrees by default to prevent data loss, and whether the `--force` behavior in `git worktree remove` should align with this. Phillip’s latest review supports the safety fix but notes a subtle edge case: the patch doesn’t check for `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` or `REVERT_HEAD` after conflict-free cherry-picks or reverts, leaving those operations technically "in progress" and thus protected, which he argues is correct but could be clearer in the documentation.

The series is backward-incompatible for scripts relying on the old behavior of `%(worktreepath)` or the ability to delete in-progress worktrees without `--force`. While the safety fix is uncontroversial, the broader design questions—like whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering worktrees—remain unresolved. The thread is still open, with no clear consensus on how to address the limitations of the current approach.
