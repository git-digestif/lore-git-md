Here’s the human-readable summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread proposes a four-patch series to add filtering capabilities to `git worktree list` by extending the `%(worktreepath)` atom in `git for-each-ref`. The goal is to help developers identify worktrees tied to specific branches while fixing two safety issues: (1) `git worktree remove` currently allows deletion of worktrees mid-rebase, bisect, or other operations, risking data loss, and (2) `%(worktreepath)` doesn’t report paths for branches being rebased or bisected, creating inconsistency with other Git commands.

The series is now at **v4** (a procedural relabeling of v3) and consists of:
1. A **safety fix** making `git worktree remove` refuse deletion of in-progress worktrees unless `--force` is given. This patch is **ready for integration** and addresses a long-standing bug.
2. A **documentation update** (the latest email) warning users that `%(worktreepath)` paths can contain newlines and recommending safe parsing methods like `--shell` or NUL-terminated output (`%00`).
3. An **enhancement** to `%(worktreepath)` to report paths for rebasing/bisecting branches, closing the gap noted by Kristoffer Haugsbakk.
4. A **cleanup** removing the redundant `+` marker in `git branch` output for worktrees in rebase/bisect state.

Key participants include **Jasper Mayone** (author), **Phillip Wood** (substantive review, including edge cases for `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`/`REVERT_HEAD`), **Eric Sunshine** (flagged ordering risks when filtering worktrees), and **Ben Knoble** (raised safety concerns about mid-rebase worktrees). The series is **backward-incompatible** for scripts relying on the old behavior of `%(worktreepath)` or `git worktree remove`, but the changes are well-tested and address real usability gaps. Open questions remain about filtering the main worktree, handling branches checked out in multiple worktrees, and whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for this job. The latest patch (documentation) is uncontroversial and ready to merge.
