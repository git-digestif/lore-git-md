This thread is about a four-patch series by Jasper Mayone that adds filtering capabilities to `git worktree list` and addresses safety issues around worktree deletion. The series has evolved through several iterations, with the latest (v4) incorporating substantive review feedback and expanding to four patches.

The core goal is to help developers identify worktrees associated with specific branches while preventing data loss. The series achieves this by extending the `%(worktreepath)` atom in `ref-filter.c` to report worktree paths for branches being rebased or bisected, and by making `git worktree remove` refuse to delete worktrees in the middle of operations like rebase, bisect, cherry-pick, revert, or `git am` unless `--force` is given. The implementation avoids introducing new CLI options by leveraging existing mechanisms, though this approach has limitations—particularly around parsing safety and the inability to distinguish between main and linked worktrees.

Key participants include Jasper Mayone (the author), Phillip Wood, Eric Sunshine, Ben Knoble, and Junio C Hamano. Phillip Wood’s reviews have been particularly influential, identifying edge cases (such as single-commit cherry-picks or reverts without conflicts) and proposing architectural improvements (like a `%(currentworktree)` atom for better composability). Eric Sunshine raised concerns about the broken ordering invariant when filtering worktrees, while Ben Knoble highlighted safety risks around surfacing mid-rebase worktrees in filtered output. Junio provided procedural feedback and clarified the documentation’s accuracy regarding branches checked out in multiple worktrees.

The series is now ready for review, with all major technical concerns addressed. The patches are well-tested and include documentation updates to warn about parsing `%(worktreepath)` safely and to clarify backward-incompatible changes (such as dropping support for printing the `git_dir` of bare worktrees). Open questions remain about broader design choices—like whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering worktrees—but these are largely deferred for future discussion. The latest feedback from Phillip Wood is purely editorial, focusing on minor documentation formatting improvements, and is unlikely to require significant changes.
