Here’s the human-mode summary for a developer catching up on the thread:

This thread proposes and implements a set of improvements to Git’s worktree handling, focusing on two main goals: making `git worktree remove` safer by preventing accidental deletion of in-progress worktrees, and fixing inconsistencies in how `git for-each-ref` and `git branch` report worktree paths for branches being rebased or bisected. The series is now at version 4, with the first two patches (a safety fix and documentation update) ready for integration, and the remaining two (an enhancement and cleanup) updated to address review feedback.

The key changes are: (1) `git worktree remove` now refuses to delete worktrees in the middle of a rebase, bisect, cherry-pick, revert, or `git am` operation unless `--force` is given, closing a long-standing safety gap; (2) `git for-each-ref`'s `%(worktreepath)` atom now correctly reports the worktree path for rebasing or bisecting branches, aligning with how `git worktree add` and `git branch -d` already treat those branches; and (3) `git branch` no longer shows a redundant `+` marker for worktrees rebasing or bisecting their own branch. The series also includes a documentation warning about safely parsing `%(worktreepath)` output, which can contain newlines.

The implementation is clean and well-tested, with particular attention to edge cases like single-commit cherry-picks or reverts that have completed without conflicts. Reviewers Phillip Wood and Ben Knoble raised concerns about the safety implications of surfacing mid-rebase worktrees in filtered output, which the series addresses by making `git worktree remove` refuse deletion of in-progress worktrees. Phillip also identified a breaking change—`%(worktreepath)` no longer prints the `git_dir` of bare repositories—which the author has documented as intentional. The main open questions revolve around whether scripts rely on the old behavior (empty `%(worktreepath)` for rebasing branches, the `+` marker in `git branch` output, or the ability to delete in-progress worktrees without `--force`), and whether `for-each-ref` is the right tool for filtering worktrees given its limitations (no NUL-terminated output, no distinction between main and linked worktrees). The series is ready for review, with patches 1 and 2 marked as ready for integration.
