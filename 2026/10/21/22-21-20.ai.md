This is a follow-up in the SHA-256 interoperability discussion, specifically about how `convert_commit_object` should handle unknown commit headers like `mergetag` and `predecessor`. Brian M. Carlson responds to Adam Majer’s RFC with a nuanced position: while he agrees that strict 1:1 OID mapping for every referenced object is impractical (citing real-world blockers like the Linux kernel), he raises concerns about the security and correctness implications of copying opaque headers verbatim.

Brian’s key point is that if Git cannot validate or rewrite the OIDs in these headers (because the objects are missing or the header format is unknown), the headers themselves become meaningless. He suggests that rather than preserving them as opaque text—which would result in different commit IDs depending on the source OID type—Git should consider dropping such headers entirely during conversion. This would avoid the inconsistency while acknowledging that the headers are no longer serving their original purpose (e.g., `mergetag` signatures would be invalidated anyway).

The email does not propose a patch or a specific implementation, but it shifts the discussion toward a trade-off: whether to prioritize *compatibility* (by preserving headers as text) or *correctness* (by dropping them to avoid misleading data). Brian leans toward the latter, framing the issue as a design flaw in the headers themselves rather than a limitation of the conversion tool. This stance is likely to resonate with contributors focused on security and long-term maintainability, though it may clash with Adam’s pragmatic goal of enabling full-history conversions for large repositories.

No decisions have been made yet, but the thread now has a clear tension between two approaches: relax the conversion rules to unblock real-world use cases, or enforce stricter handling to avoid propagating invalid data. The outcome will affect the SHA-256 transition effort, particularly for repositories with complex histories. Key technical details to watch include the handling of `mergetag` and `predecessor` headers, the potential for opt-in flags, and whether the solution will involve changes to the conversion tool or broader commit-header policies.
