# Thread deltas

## 2026/10/21

- [2026/10/21/20-03-41 by Emily Shaffer <emilyshaffer@google.com>] Emily Shaffer posted a patch adding Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc (plus links from DecisionMaking.adoc and the Makefile) outlining a formal three-phase community voting process (proposal collection, two-week voting window, implementation), motivated by interest from the September Contributor Summit in voting on whether/how to accept AI contributions; she stressed the patch covers only the vote-collection process, not an AI policy, proposed approving the process itself by general consensus with Acked-by: trailers as done for the 2019 Code of Conduct, and flagged that a self-review with open questions would follow.
- [2026/10/21/21-22-13 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano judged the approach sensible and asked that the subject use a verb stronger than "propose" (once merged the doc defines the process rather than proposing it), and thanked her for starting the process.
- [2026/10/21/22-06-58 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano endorsed reserving voting strictly for policy decisions rather than technical ones, and suggested uniquely identifying each ballot proposal by its commit or blob object name (retrievable via `git cat-file blob`) so voters see the exact text, noting this also supports multiple-choice votes.
- [2026/10/21/22-06-58 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano questioned the implementation-phase wording "% in favor out of total number of proposals," asking whether "number of votes" was meant or whether N competing proposals were envisioned.
- [2026/10/21/22-06-58 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano objected to the voting-eligibility criteria: the "2 or more code reviews" bar is hard to define and enforce, and "merged to next" is hard to track across release cycles; he suggested instead requiring two or more topics appearing in any released versions (counting a multi-patch topic as one), which he noted would also make the dogpiling cutoff based on pre-kickoff contributions a non-issue.

## 2026/10/22

- [2026/10/22/07-29-26 by Kristoffer Haugsbakk <kristofferhaugsbakk@fastmail.com>] Kristoffer Haugsbakk argued that the proposed AI-contributions vote will pass because contributors who work on Git at least monthly are employed by tech companies that are now AI companies, so votes will drift toward agreement with management; he also dismissed the idea of revisiting the AI policy later ("Even the Himalayas will flatten one day") and said acceptance of non-GenAI users is inevitable.
- [2026/10/22/12-44-26 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt disputed that observation, saying he works for such a company yet remains highly skeptical of the technology, but agreed with the sentiment that the vote should be secret so voters can voice honest opinions differing from their employer's stance without fear of retribution.
- [2026/10/22/15-35-31 by Justin Tobler <jltobler@gmail.com>] Justin Tobler agreed the proposed voting eligibility (a commit merged to next or 2+ code reviews) is too low a bar, suggesting eligible voters should have a vested and current interest, possibly requiring contributions within the last X releases, while warning that overly restrictive requirements would bias the voter pool toward active contributors at tech companies; he also agreed with an earlier suggestion that counting topics in released versions is better than counting commits in next.
- [2026/10/22/22-51-04 by brian m. carlson <sandals@crustytoothpaste.net>] brian m. carlson rebutted the claim that tech-company employees parrot their employer's line, stating his own vote will not be influenced by his employer, and endorsed secret votes because they prevent people from having to prove their votes to others.
- [2026/10/22/23-17-19 by Junio C Hamano <gitster@pobox.com>] Junio C Hamano thanked brian m. carlson for raising the secret-vote point, which he had not seen in the proposal, and noted he likewise does not parrot his employer's company line.
