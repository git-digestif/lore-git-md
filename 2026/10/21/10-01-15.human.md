This is a **bugfix patch discussion** targeting the shallow-fetch regression introduced by batched reference updates. Karthik Nayak replies to Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal to supplement the backfill fetch’s "haves" with pending, uncommitted reference updates from the transaction. The thread is now evaluating whether this alternative design is feasible or if the current two-transaction approach should proceed.

Karthik expresses conceptual concerns about exposing uncommitted references as "haves" during negotiation, arguing it could mislead the server into assuming objects are durably referenced when they are only locked by a `.keep` file. This could create a race condition where the server’s garbage collection removes objects still needed by the client. However, he notes that `negotiation_include_tips` in the transport structure might be the right mechanism to implement Patrick’s idea, suggesting he is open to exploring it further.

The discussion remains focused on design trade-offs: the two-transaction approach is proven but introduces atomicity limitations for `--atomic` fetches, while the pending-transaction proposal could restore atomicity but risks exposing unstable state to the server. The patch’s readiness for `next` now depends on resolving this feasibility question. If the pending-transaction approach is deemed unsafe or overly complex, the two-transaction design will likely proceed with Justin Tobler’s error-handling feedback addressed. The tone is collaborative, with Karthik engaging thoughtfully on the technical merits of both options.
