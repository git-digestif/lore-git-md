Subject: [PATCH v4] rev-list: fix `--grep` and related option parsing when followed by another option
Series version: standalone patch, v4 (v5 direction proposed but now contested)
Originating author: Azuama Odinaka Maximus
Thread type: bugfix patch

Core problem or goal
The `git rev-list` command misparses any of the 18 `parse_long_opt()`-style options (`--grep`, `--author`, `--committer`, `--since`, `--until`, etc.) when the option is immediately followed by another argument that begins with `--`. The parser treats the next argument as a separate flag rather than as the option's value, causing the command to fail or behave unpredictably. The patch aims to ensure these options are handled early in the argument scan so that their following arguments are always treated as values, even if they begin with `--`. The author initially proposed a targeted fix (v4) and later a more robust architectural direction (v5): refactoring the early option scan to reuse the same option-recognition logic as the main parser, eliminating the maintenance hazard of hardcoded option lists. The maintainer has now challenged the necessity of the early scan itself, suggesting an alternative approach that would avoid duplicating parser logic altogether.

Current status
Under active review; fourth version posted, with a proposed fifth version direction now contested. The v4 patch is a targeted refactor in `builtin/rev-list.c` that introduced a helper function, `rev_list_option_takes_argument()`, to recognize all 18 affected options during the early option scan and skip their arguments. The test suite (`t/t6022-rev-list-missing.sh`) includes five new tests verifying that `--grep` and `--author` patterns beginning with `--` are correctly applied. **Maintainer review (Junio C Hamano) identified two critical issues**: (1) the hardcoded list of 19 option strings in the helper function duplicates parser logic and is already incomplete (missing `--diff-merges`, `--src-prefix`, `--dst-prefix`, etc.), creating a maintenance hazard; (2) the necessity of the early option scan itself is now in question. The maintainer suggests eliminating the early scan entirely and instead solving the underlying problem (`.fetch_if_missing` and related defaults) by recording explicit option settings during normal parsing and synthesizing the final configuration at the end. The architectural direction remains unresolved; the patch is not ready for merging.

Open questions and loose ends
- **Architectural direction**: The maintainer has proposed an alternative to the author’s v5 refactor: eliminate the early option scan entirely and solve the underlying problem (`.fetch_if_missing` and related defaults) by recording explicit option settings during normal parsing and synthesizing the final configuration at the end. This would avoid duplicating parser logic and the maintenance hazard of hardcoded lists. The author has not yet responded to this suggestion.
- **Scope of the fix**: The maintainer’s example (`git rev-list --src-prefix --missing-only HEAD`) suggests the problem may extend beyond the 18 options currently addressed. The maintainer’s proposed approach would naturally address this by eliminating the early scan’s limitations.
- **Implementation details**: Neither the author’s v5 proposal nor the maintainer’s alternative has been concretely implemented. Key challenges include accommodating both table-driven (`add_diff_options`) and hand-written (`handle_revision_opt`) parsers, and ensuring that the final configuration synthesis correctly handles all edge cases.
- **Test coverage**: The architectural shift may necessitate revisiting test coverage to ensure the revised approach handles all cases correctly, including options not previously addressed (e.g., `--diff-merges`, `--src-prefix`).
- **Backwards compatibility**: The maintainer’s alternative must preserve the existing behavior of `rev-list` for all valid invocations, including those that rely on the early scan’s current defaults.
