This is a **governance process discussion** in reply to Emily Shaffer’s proposal to formalize a voting process for the Git project. Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) engages constructively with the patch, offering specific technical and procedural feedback while accepting the overall premise. The email advances the thread by clarifying key details and surfacing potential implementation challenges.

Junio’s feedback focuses on three areas: versioning of proposals, vote counting, and eligibility criteria. He suggests using Git commit or blob OIDs to uniquely identify proposal versions, ensuring voters can reference the exact text they’re voting on—a practical improvement that leverages Git’s own tooling. On vote counting, he asks for clarification: is the outcome determined by the number of votes for a single proposal, or by the proportion of support across competing options? This distinction matters for multi-choice ballots, which the proposal explicitly allows.

The most substantive critique concerns eligibility. Junio argues the current bar (one commit in `next` or two code reviews) is too low, as it could be gamed or include contributors with minimal long-term investment. He proposes a stricter standard: two or more *topics* (not individual commits) that have appeared in any released version of Git. This would exclude drive-by contributors while still capturing those with sustained impact. He also notes that tracking contributions in `next` is unreliable, as topics may be dropped or rewritten before release.

The tone remains collaborative, with Junio framing his suggestions as refinements rather than objections. His feedback implies the proposal is likely to move forward if these details are addressed, though the broader question of whether Git should adopt formal voting at all remains unresolved. The discussion now hinges on whether the community can agree on eligibility thresholds and vote-counting mechanics—both of which could become points of contention in future iterations.
