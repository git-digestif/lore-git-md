This thread is about a proposal by Emily Shaffer to introduce a formal voting process for the Git project, documented in a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file. The patch is a response to the stalled AI contributions debate, offering a structured way to resolve contentious decisions when consensus fails. Junio C Hamano, the project maintainer, has reviewed the patch and suggested a minor but significant title change—from "propose a voting process" to "define the voting process"—indicating his acceptance of the proposal’s direction and his expectation that it will eventually be merged.

The patch itself is self-contained, touching only documentation and build-system files, with no code or on-disk changes. Key open questions remain, including the specifics of the voting mechanism and eligibility criteria, which Emily plans to address in a follow-up self-review. Junio’s response does not engage with these details but treats the proposal as a serious effort to address a real governance gap. The broader community has yet to weigh in on whether a formal voting process is the right tool for Git, but Junio’s constructive tone suggests the proposal is on track for adoption once the unresolved details are settled.
