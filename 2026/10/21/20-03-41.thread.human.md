This thread is about Emily Shaffer’s proposal to define a structured voting process for the Git project, intended as a last-resort mechanism for resolving contentious decisions like the ongoing AI contributions debate. The patch adds a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file and updates existing governance documentation, with Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) suggesting minor refinements, such as clarifying the document’s authoritative title and using Git OIDs to version proposal texts.

The discussion has converged on key design choices, particularly the need for **secret ballots** to mitigate concerns about corporate influence and external pressure, a point now endorsed by Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, and brian m. carlson. However, the eligibility criteria remain contested. Junio advocates for a simple, binary standard—such as requiring contributors to have authored two or more topics in a released version of Git—to exclude drive-by participants, while Justin Tobler warns this could disproportionately favor corporate-affiliated contributors. Junio’s latest reply rejects time-bound or proportional voting rules as overly complex, reinforcing the principle of "one person, one vote" and narrowing the debate to a choice between a low bar or a stricter but still binary standard. Kristoffer Haugsbakk remains skeptical of the proposal’s premise, arguing that formal voting inherently favors corporate interests, particularly on AI-related policies, though his critique does not engage with the secret ballot safeguard.

The patch is still under review, with open questions including the final eligibility criteria, the logistics of secret ballots, and whether the project should adopt formal voting at all. Emily plans to update the patch to flag these unresolved issues explicitly. The thread’s ideological divide—between those who see voting as a necessary tool for stalemates and those who view it as a risk to the project’s consensus-based culture—remains unresolved, though the emerging consensus on secret ballots may address some concerns about fairness.
