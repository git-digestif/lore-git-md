This thread is about Emily Shaffer’s proposal to define a formal voting process for the Git project, aimed at resolving contentious decisions like the ongoing AI contributions debate. The patch adds a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file and updates existing governance documentation to outline *how* to vote, not *what* policies to adopt. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has engaged constructively, refining details like versioning (using Git OIDs) and eligibility criteria (requiring contributors to have worked on two or more topics in a released version of Git). However, key questions remain unresolved, including the voting mechanism (plurality vs. proportional outcomes) and whether the project should adopt formal voting at all.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s recent reply introduces a skeptical perspective, questioning the premise of democratic decision-making in Git’s traditionally consensus-driven culture. He argues that corporate interests—particularly around AI adoption—could dominate any vote due to the composition of active contributors, framing the proposal as a potential shift toward institutionalizing corporate priorities. While his concerns don’t directly address the patch’s technical details, they highlight a broader ideological tension: whether structured voting is a necessary tool for breaking deadlocks or a risky departure from Git’s collaborative roots. The thread’s trajectory will likely hinge on whether other participants echo these concerns or focus on refining the procedural aspects of the proposal. For now, the patch remains under review, with no clear consensus on its adoption.
