This is a **follow-up discussion** in the ongoing thread about Harald Nordgren’s patch series to extend `git branch --delete-merged` with squash-merge and rebase-merge detection. The email exchanges views on the **false-positive issue** (a branch being deleted because another author independently made the same change) and **CLI design trade-offs** between Harald’s unified approach and Jasper Mayone’s proposal for a granular `--landed=<kind>` option.

Harald acknowledges Jasper’s false-positive report but downplays its practical significance, calling it an "extremely rare edge case." He argues that even if such a branch were incorrectly deleted, its changes are already upstream, so the branch itself is redundant. This reflects his **pragmatic stance**: the feature’s primary goal is to delete branches once their changes are upstream, regardless of how those changes landed. However, he does not address Jasper’s core concern—that the false positive undermines the feature’s safety and predictability, especially for users who rely on `--delete-merged` as a conservative operation.

On CLI design, Harald **rejects Jasper’s `--landed=<kind>` proposal**, calling it "pushing it in terms of UX mental load." He prefers a simple, opinionated tool where users opt into looser detection via `--squashed` (now opt-in in v3) rather than configuring multiple levels of strictness. His argument centers on **simplicity**: the command should "just delete merged branches" without requiring users to understand or configure detection methods. This aligns with his earlier rejection of Junio’s `--delete-irrelevant` split, but it leaves the false-positive issue unresolved.

Harald also notes he will review Jasper’s RFC for `git worktree list --merged`, which proposes reusing the "has this branch landed?" logic. This suggests the feature’s utility may extend beyond branch deletion, but the discussion here remains focused on the immediate CLI design conflict.

**Key takeaways for the thread’s direction:**
- The **false-positive issue** remains a sticking point. Jasper sees it as a fundamental flaw requiring a CLI redesign (e.g., `--landed` or `--delete-irrelevant`), while Harald treats it as a rare edge case that doesn’t justify added complexity.
- **No consensus on CLI design**: Harald’s unified approach prioritizes simplicity, while Jasper (and Junio) advocate for clearer separation of strict vs. loose detection. The `--landed` proposal offers a middle ground but is unlikely to be adopted without broader support.
- **Performance vs. correctness**: Harald’s tree-based diffing (fast, no blob fetches) is the only viable approach for `--delete-merged`, but it sacrifices precision. Jasper’s `--landed=merge` (three-way merge) would be slower but more accurate, but Harald sees this as over-engineering for a "delete branches" command.
- **Next steps**: The discussion is stalled on philosophical differences. Junio’s `--delete-irrelevant` proposal remains the most likely path forward if the project prioritizes safety, but Harald’s resistance suggests a compromise (e.g., `--landed` as a config-backed option) may be needed. The first patch (refactoring) is uncontroversial and ready for integration, but the rebase/squash detection patches are blocked on this design debate.
