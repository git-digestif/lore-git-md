This is the third and final patch in Elia Pinto's **bugfix and platform-compatibility series** addressing 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). It fixes silent misbehavior in Git's file conversion subsystem when handling files larger than 4 GiB.

The patch converts four helper functions (`gather_stats`, `gather_convert_stats`, `gather_convert_stats_ascii`, and `count_ident`) and the `read_blob_data_from_index` API from `unsigned long` to `size_t` for buffer sizes. On Windows, where `long` is 32-bit, these functions previously truncated file sizes above 4 GiB, leading to incorrect binary detection, CRLF conversion decisions, and keyword expansion. The change is mechanical and does not alter behavior on 64-bit Linux.

The patch touches four files (`attr.c`, `convert.c`, `read-cache-ll.h`, and `read-cache.c`) and removes the `cast_size_t_to_ulong` wrapper in `read_blob_data_from_index`, which was a known truncation point. No new tests are included, as reproducing the issue requires multi-gigabyte files and a 32-bit `long` environment. The commit message explains the impact clearly: files over 4 GiB could be misclassified as binary or non-binary, and CRLF conversion or keyword expansion might be applied incorrectly.

This patch completes the series, which was identified via clang-tidy in a MinGW cross-compilation setup. The fixes align with prior work by Johannes Schindelin (read-side ODB) and Jeff King (xdiff) and address a clear correctness issue on a supported platform. The series is narrowly scoped and uncontroversial.
