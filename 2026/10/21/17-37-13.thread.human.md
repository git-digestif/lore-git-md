This thread discusses a practical blocker in converting repositories from SHA-1 to SHA-256: the `convert_commit_object` function's strict handling of commit headers like `mergetag` and `predecessor`. Adam Majer reports that commits in real-world repositories (such as the Linux kernel) contain references to objects that no longer exist, causing the conversion to fail. He proposes a more flexible approach—treating unknown headers as opaque text and copying them verbatim—arguing that Git doesn’t currently validate these references anyway, making the trade-off of non-identical commit IDs acceptable for interoperability.

The discussion centers on a tension in the SHA-256 transition: whether to enforce strict OID consistency or prioritize practical compatibility. Adam’s examples (commits `ab5af2903baa` and `3a413ece2504` with `predecessor` headers, and "dozens" of unfetchable `mergetag` references in the Linux tree) highlight the real-world impact of this issue. While no patch has been proposed yet, the thread is likely to draw attention from contributors like brian m. carlson, who have worked on hash transition efforts. The outcome could shape how Git handles edge cases in repository conversion tooling.
