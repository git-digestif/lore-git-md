This thread is a design discussion about how Git’s SHA-256 interoperability layer should handle unknown commit headers (like `mergetag` or `predecessor`) when converting repositories. The issue was raised by Adam Majer, who proposed relaxing the current strict requirement that all referenced objects must exist, arguing that this blocks full-history conversions of repositories like the Linux kernel. Instead, he suggests treating unknown headers as opaque text and copying them verbatim during conversion.

Brian M. Carlson, a key contributor to Git’s SHA-256 transition effort, has now weighed in with a contrasting perspective. While he agrees that strict 1:1 OID mapping is impractical, he argues that preserving opaque headers as text would introduce inconsistencies (different commit IDs for SHA-1 vs. SHA-256 conversions) and propagate invalid data. Instead, he proposes dropping such headers entirely during conversion, as they become meaningless when their referenced objects are missing or their format is unknown. This approach prioritizes correctness over compatibility, framing the issue as a design flaw in the headers themselves rather than a limitation of the conversion tool.

The discussion is now centered on a trade-off: whether to relax conversion rules to unblock real-world use cases or enforce stricter handling to avoid misleading data. No decisions have been made yet, but the thread highlights the tension between pragmatic compatibility and long-term maintainability. Key technical details to watch include how `mergetag` and `predecessor` headers will be handled, whether the solution will be opt-in, and whether broader commit-header policies might change as a result. The outcome will directly impact the SHA-256 transition effort, particularly for repositories with complex histories.
