This is a **bugfix** targeting the `git maintenance` subsystem. The patch reports and demonstrates a regression introduced in Git 2.55 (commit `6e95b07e5f`) where a stale `maintenance.lock` file can silently disable auto-maintenance if the detached child process is killed (e.g., via `SIGKILL` or power loss). The issue is more likely to occur now because the lock is held for the entire duration of background tasks, not just the foreground phase.

The author, Masaya Kazama, provides a clear reproduction case using a FIFO trick to simulate a long-running background task, along with real-world evidence: they found the lock persisting for four months in a large partial clone, leading to excessive packfile accumulation and degraded performance. The commit message is thorough, explaining the regression’s cause, its impact (silent failure of auto-maintenance), and why existing mechanisms like `core.lockfilePid` cannot solve it.

The patch does not include a proposed fix but outlines three potential solutions, ranging from adding user-visible traces (e.g., a `gc.log`-style file) to adopting OS-level locks. The issue is well-motivated and affects users who rely on auto-maintenance, particularly in environments where background processes might be terminated abruptly. Given the regression’s severity and the author’s detailed analysis, this looks like a serious problem worth attention from maintainers familiar with the maintenance subsystem.
