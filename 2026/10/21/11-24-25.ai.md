This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Elia Pinto’s three-patch series fixing 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). The reviewer, Patrick Steinhardt, engages directly with the technical issue and the patch’s approach.

The patch itself converts the object-database write path from `unsigned long` to `size_t` to prevent silent truncation of blobs larger than 4 GiB on Windows. Patrick’s feedback is brief but pointed: he acknowledges the correctness of the type conversion and the seriousness of the bug (data corruption on Windows when `git add` or `git hash-object -w` processes a file over 4 GiB that requires filtering). However, he challenges the commit message’s justification for omitting a test, arguing that the existing test suite already includes expensive tests (marked `EXPENSIVE`) and that a bug of this severity warrants one. He does not object to the patch itself, but his comment raises a procedural question about test coverage that the author may need to address in a follow-up.

No other concerns are raised; the review is narrowly focused on the testability of the fix rather than its implementation. The patch remains uncontroversial in scope and is part of a larger, well-motivated effort to address platform-specific truncation issues.
