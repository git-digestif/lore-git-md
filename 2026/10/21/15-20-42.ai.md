This is a **substantive review** of Nakul V S's bugfix patch that adds a safeguard against reverting or cherry-picking shallow boundary commits. Phillip Wood engages with the patch's design and implementation, raising two technical points and one design question.

The review suggests two concrete improvements: (1) extracting the shallow-boundary detection logic into a helper function to avoid code duplication if the check is reused elsewhere, and (2) omitting the rarely-used `--allow-shallow-boundary` option from the command synopsis to keep usage messages concise. Neither suggestion questions the core premise of the patch—default refusal with an opt-in override—but both aim to improve maintainability and user experience.

Phillip also asks a design question: whether the safeguard should extend to `git rebase` as well, given that rebasing a shallow boundary commit could similarly lead to unintended data loss. He notes that the new `--allow-shallow-boundary` flag was added out of caution rather than a known use case, implying that the flag might be unnecessary if no legitimate use exists. This question invites discussion about the scope of the safeguard and whether the escape hatch should be retained.
