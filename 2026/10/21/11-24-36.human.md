This is a review of the second patch in Elia Pinto’s three-patch series fixing 64-to-32 truncation bugs on Windows. The patch itself is a straightforward type conversion in the word-diff subsystem, changing two allocation counters from `unsigned long`/`long` to `size_t` to prevent overflow crashes on LLP64 platforms. Patrick Steinhardt’s review is brief but substantive: he confirms the correctness of the change and highlights the specific risk (a negative allocation size triggering `die()` via `ALLOC_GROW()`). His comment underscores that the bug is real and the fix is minimal and safe.

The patch is uncontroversial and well-motivated. It touches only `diff.c` and aligns with the series’ goal of eliminating truncation risks without altering behavior on 64-bit Linux. The absence of test coverage remains the sole open question, but Patrick’s review does not revisit that concern here—his feedback is narrowly focused on the code change itself. This patch looks ready to merge once the series as a whole addresses the testability discussion.
