This is a **bugfix patch follow-up** in the ongoing thread addressing a shallow-fetch regression. Karthik Nayak replies to Justin Tobler’s substantive review of v3, clarifying the error-handling logic in the two-transaction approach.

Karthik explains why the code logs an error from the first transaction but continues to the second: batched updates allow partial success, so even if some refs fail, others may have been applied, and the rest of the fetch (including tag backfilling) should proceed. This aligns with Git’s general behavior for non-atomic fetches, where failures on individual refs don’t halt the entire operation. The reply also justifies why post-fetch operations (upstream config, `FETCH_HEAD` writes) execute even if the initial transaction failed to initialize—these steps are independent of the transaction’s success and are necessary for correct repository state.

The exchange leaves Justin’s questions technically resolved but doesn’t address whether the two-transaction approach will be retained or replaced by Patrick Steinhardt’s pending-transaction "haves" proposal. Karthik’s reply defers to the ongoing design discussion in Patrick’s thread, signaling that the error-path concerns may become moot if the new proposal is adopted. The patch remains in review limbo: the v3 logic is now better explained, but its future depends on the outcome of the alternative design exploration. No code changes are proposed here—this is purely a clarification of intent.
