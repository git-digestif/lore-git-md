This is a **substantive review** of the third patch in Elia Pinto’s **bugfix and platform-compatibility series** addressing 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). The series fixes silent misbehavior in Git’s file conversion subsystem when handling files larger than 4 GiB.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review engages with the patch’s technical details, raising two specific points about potential overflow risks in the `convert.c` code. First, he notes that `struct text_stat` still uses `unsigned` counters (for `printable` and `nonprintable` fields), which could theoretically overflow when processing multi-gigabyte files, even though the buffer size itself is now correctly typed as `size_t`. Second, he acknowledges that the `count_ident` function (which counts `$Id$` markers) is unlikely to overflow in practice but flags the theoretical possibility. Neither point disputes the patch’s core correctness—both are forward-looking observations about edge cases rather than objections to the current change.

The review does not request changes or block the patch, but it surfaces a subtle design question: whether the series should go beyond the immediate truncation fixes and address secondary overflow risks in related data structures. This question is narrowly scoped to the `convert.c` helpers and does not affect the rest of the series. No test coverage or procedural concerns are raised here (unlike in the first patch’s review), and the tone is confirmatory rather than adversarial. The patch remains uncontroversial, but Patrick’s comments may prompt follow-up discussion about whether to extend the type conversions to adjacent code.
