This is a **substantive review** of the ephemeral branch reaping patch (v2 2/2) in Jason Newton’s worktree improvements series. Eric Sunshine argues that the entire feature is unnecessary and potentially disruptive, pointing out that `git worktree add --detach` already serves the use case of throwaway worktrees without leaving behind disposable branches. He contends that the problem the patch aims to solve—manual cleanup of auto-created branches—is better addressed by teaching tooling or agents to use `--detach` rather than adding complexity to Git’s worktree implementation.

The review raises two key objections. First, it questions the motivation: if `--detach` already exists, why introduce a new mechanism that risks breaking existing workflows? Second, it highlights the unconditional nature of the branch reaping as a design flaw, arguing that even with the `--keep-branch` escape hatch, the change is likely to disrupt tooling that relies on the current behavior. Eric suggests that if the feature were to proceed, it should be opt-in (e.g., via a new flag or config) rather than opt-out, but he implies that even this would be redundant given `--detach`.

This feedback directly challenges the patch’s core premise and could lead to its rejection unless the author can demonstrate a compelling advantage over `--detach` or propose a less intrusive design. The thread’s next steps will likely hinge on whether Jason can justify the added complexity or if the discussion shifts toward modifying the approach to be opt-in. Key technical details for follow-up include the `--detach` flag’s current behavior, the scope of existing tooling that might rely on auto-created branches, and the feasibility of making the reaping feature configurable.
