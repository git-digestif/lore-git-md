This is a **documentation proposal** introducing a formal voting process for the Git project. Emily Shaffer presents a new `Documentation/CommunityVoting.adoc` file outlining how the project would conduct rare, contentious decisions when consensus fails—specifically motivated by the unresolved debate over AI contributions.

The patch defines a three-phase process: (1) a four-week period for collecting and refining proposals (submitted as patches, seconded by another voter), (2) a two-week voting window on the finalized ballot, and (3) immediate implementation of the winning proposal. Eligibility to vote is restricted to contributors with at least one merged commit in `next` or two code reviews, with a cutoff date to prevent last-minute "dogpiling" by new participants. The mechanism for tallying votes is left unspecified ("TBD").

The proposal is explicitly *not* about the AI policy itself—only the process for deciding it. Emily suggests adopting this framework via consensus and `Acked-by:` trailers, mirroring the 2019 Code of Conduct adoption. She acknowledges open questions (e.g., vote-counting method) and plans to self-review them in a follow-up. The patch also updates `DecisionMaking.adoc` to reference the new voting document.

This looks like a serious, well-considered effort to address a long-standing gap in Git’s governance. The eligibility criteria and phased timeline are pragmatic, though the lack of a defined voting mechanism leaves a key detail unresolved. The proposal’s immediate relevance to the AI debate ensures it will attract attention, but its framing as a neutral process document may help keep discussion focused.
