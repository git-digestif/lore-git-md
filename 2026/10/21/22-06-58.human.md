This is a **governance documentation update** refining the proposed voting process for the Git project. Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) replies to Emily Shaffer’s patch with constructive feedback on three key aspects of the proposal.

First, Junio suggests using Git’s own object identifiers (commit or blob OIDs) to uniquely pin the exact version of a proposal being voted on. This would let voters fetch the precise text from Junio’s tree with a simple `git cat-file blob` command, avoiding ambiguity about which version of a proposal is on the ballot. He notes this approach scales naturally to multi-choice votes or ballots with multiple independent questions.

Second, Junio flags a wording ambiguity in the "Implementation" phase: the commit message refers to “% in favor out of total number of proposals,” but it should probably say “number of votes” instead. He also asks whether the vote is selecting among competing proposals or simply measuring support for a single option.

Finally, Junio questions the eligibility criteria. He finds the current bar (one commit in `next` or two code reviews) too low, especially since “code review” is hard to define rigorously and `next` is a moving target that disadvantages contributors whose patches were in earlier cycles but never graduated. Instead, he proposes counting “two or more topics that appear in any released version,” treating a multi-patch topic as a single contribution. This change would also reduce the risk of last-minute “dogpiling” by making eligibility depend on past, already-merged work rather than activity during the vote itself.

The tone is matter-of-fact and collaborative; Junio accepts the premise of the patch and focuses on tightening the mechanics rather than re-litigating the need for a voting process. The open questions he raises are likely to become the next round of discussion points.
