This is a **bugfix and platform-compatibility patch series** targeting Windows (specifically LLP64 platforms where `long` is 32-bit). The series addresses three remaining areas where Git silently truncates or misbehaves with files larger than 4 GiB due to `size_t` values being narrowed to `unsigned long` or `long`. The patches are mechanical type conversions with no behavior changes on 64-bit Linux, but they prevent data corruption (truncated blobs in the object database), crashes (allocation overflow in word-diff), and incorrect metadata handling (binary detection, CRLF conversion) on Windows.

The author, Elia Pinto, used a MinGW cross-compilation setup with clang-tidy to systematically identify all 64-to-32 truncations in the codebase. The scan revealed 237 Windows-specific truncations to `long`/`unsigned long`, of which this series fixes three: the object database write API, word-diff buffer allocation, and file conversion helpers. The commit messages explain how to reproduce each issue and note that the fixes align with prior work by Johannes Schindelin (read-side ODB) and Jeff King (xdiff).

The series touches 10 files across `odb.c`, `diff.c`, `convert.c`, and their headers. No new tests are included, as reproducing the issues requires multi-gigabyte files and a 32-bit `long` environment. The patches are narrowly scoped and appear to be well-motivated; the author explicitly states they are following up on known problem areas rather than introducing new functionality.

This is the first iteration (`[PATCH 0/3]`), and the author acknowledges Johannes Schindelin’s prior work in the area, suggesting awareness of potential overlap. The patches are likely to be uncontroversial, as they address clear correctness issues on a supported platform.
