This is the third and final patch in Elia Pinto’s three-part series fixing 64-to-32-bit truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). It targets the file conversion subsystem, where Git currently misclassifies files larger than 4 GiB as binary or non-binary, mishandles CRLF line endings, and fails to expand `$Id$` keywords beyond the first 4 GiB. The patch mechanically changes buffer-size parameters from `unsigned long` to `size_t` in four helper functions (`gather_stats`, `gather_convert_stats`, `gather_convert_stats_ascii`, and `count_ident`) and in `read_blob_data_from_index`, eliminating silent truncation when the staged blob exceeds 4 GiB.

The change is narrow and well-motivated: the commit message explains exactly how the truncation leads to incorrect behavior (e.g., converting line endings in a file that is binary past 4 GiB) and why `size_t` is the correct type. No new tests are added, as reproducing the issue requires a 32-bit `long` environment and multi-gigabyte files, but the fix aligns with prior work by Johannes Schindelin and Jeff King on 64-bit file handling. The patch touches four files (`attr.c`, `convert.c`, `read-cache-ll.h`, and `read-cache.c`) and is uncontroversial—it simply plugs a correctness hole on a supported platform.
