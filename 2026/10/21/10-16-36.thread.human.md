This thread is a bugfix series from Elia Pinto addressing silent 64-to-32-bit truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms), where Git misbehaves with files larger than 4 GiB. The series, now in its second iteration (`v2`), fixes four specific truncation or overflow sites in the object database, word-diff buffer allocation, file conversion helpers, and text statistics counters. The patches mechanically widen integer types to `size_t` or `uintmax_t` to prevent narrowing, with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux.

Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the first version (`v1`) and raised two concerns—lack of test coverage for the 4 GiB+ truncation bug and theoretical overflow risks in `convert.c`—both of which were addressed in `v2`. The latest email is a surface-level review of the first patch in `v2`, focusing on minor test script improvements in `t/t1007-hash-object.sh`. Steinhardt suggests two small changes to avoid redundant file I/O and potential race conditions, but these are stylistic tweaks rather than substantive objections. The series appears ready for integration, with no unresolved technical concerns. No other reviewers have weighed in yet.
