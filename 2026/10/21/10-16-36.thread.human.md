This thread is a patch series from Elia Pinto fixing silent 64-to-32-bit truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms) that cause data corruption, crashes, and incorrect metadata handling when Git processes files larger than 4 GiB. The series targets three specific truncation sites: object database writes, word-diff buffer allocation, and file conversion helpers. All three patches have now been posted and reviewed by Patrick Steinhardt, who has confirmed their correctness but raised two unresolved questions.

The first question—whether the series should include an `EXPENSIVE` test for the 4 GiB+ truncation bug—was raised in Patrick’s review of the first patch. He argued that the severity of the bug (data corruption on Windows) justifies the effort despite the impractical test conditions. The second question, surfaced in his review of the third patch, is whether the series should also address secondary overflow risks in related data structures (e.g., `struct text_stat` counters in `convert.c`). Neither question blocks the patches, but they remain open for discussion.

The series is narrowly scoped to the three high-impact truncation sites identified via clang-tidy in a MinGW cross-compilation setup. It mechanically converts `size_t` values to `uintmax_t` or `size_t` to avoid narrowing to `long` or `unsigned long`, with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux. No new symbols are introduced, and the on-disk format is unchanged. The patches touch 12 files, including `odb.c`, `diff.c`, and `convert.c`. While the fixes are uncontroversial, the lack of test coverage and the potential for follow-up work on adjacent overflow risks may shape the series’ final form.
