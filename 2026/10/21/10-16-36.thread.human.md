This thread is about a four-patch series from Elia Pinto that fixes 64-to-32-bit truncation bugs on Windows (LLP64 platforms), where `long` is 32-bit. The series targets silent data corruption, crashes, and incorrect metadata handling for files larger than 4 GiB. The second patch, just summarized, fixes a crash in the word-diff subsystem by converting buffer allocation variables from `unsigned long`/`long` to `size_t`, preventing overflow when buffers exceed ~700 MiB on Windows.

The series is now in its third iteration (`v3`) and has addressed all feedback from earlier versions. Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the patches and raised minor procedural concerns—test-script hygiene and commit-message phrasing—which were resolved in `v3`. No technical objections remain, and the series appears ready for integration. The fixes are mechanical type conversions with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux, and the revised test in the first patch (`t1007-hash-object.sh`) now avoids redundant file I/O by using `git cat-file -e` to verify object existence for 4 GiB+ files. The fourth patch preemptively fixes a theoretical overflow risk in CRLF/keyword counters, while the second and third patches build on prior work by Jeff King and Johannes Schindelin. The series is uncontroversial and on track for merging.
