This thread began as a technical patch series to fix 64-to-32-bit truncation bugs on Windows, where Git silently corrupts or crashes when handling files larger than 4 GiB. Elia Pinto’s v3 series addresses four specific truncation sites in the object database, word-diff buffer allocation, file conversion helpers, and text statistics counters. All technical feedback from earlier versions has been resolved—tests were strengthened, overflow risks preemptively fixed, and the series rebased on current master—leaving the code ready for integration.

The thread has since shifted to a meta-discussion about AI-assisted contributions, sparked by Patrick Steinhardt’s frustration with Elia’s commit-message phrasing, which Patrick perceives as AI-generated rather than human-authored. Patrick has made it clear that his objection isn’t to AI tooling itself but to submissions where the human author hasn’t meaningfully engaged with the work. He expects commit messages and mailing-list prose to reflect genuine understanding, not just mechanical compliance with feedback. Junio C Hamano has broadened the conversation to explore how the project should evaluate AI-assisted contributions, and Patrick’s latest email lays out his criteria: human authorship must be evident in the prose, and the author must be able to discuss the changes substantively. Until Elia revises the fourth patch’s commit message to Patrick’s satisfaction, the series remains in limbo, with Patrick threatening to ignore future patches from Elia if the feedback is treated as a checkbox exercise. The technical fixes are sound, but the procedural dispute now determines whether they’ll be merged.
