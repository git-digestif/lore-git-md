This thread is a three-patch series from Elia Pinto fixing silent 64-to-32-bit truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms) that cause data corruption, crashes, and incorrect file handling for files larger than 4 GiB. The series targets three specific areas: object database writes, word-diff buffer allocation, and file conversion helpers. The fixes are mechanical type conversions from `unsigned long` to `size_t` or `uintmax_t`, with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux.

The series is under review and uncontroversial, addressing clear correctness issues on a supported platform. No prior version exists, and no tests are included due to the impracticality of reproducing the issue (requiring multi-gigabyte files and a 32-bit `long` environment). The fixes build on prior work by Johannes Schindelin and Jeff King. The third and final patch, just posted, fixes truncations in Git’s file conversion subsystem, preventing misclassification of large files as binary or non-binary and ensuring correct CRLF conversion and keyword expansion. The series touches 14 files in total, with no new symbols or on-disk format changes. No objections or open questions have been raised yet.
