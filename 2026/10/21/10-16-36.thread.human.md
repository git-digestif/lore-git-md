Elia Pinto's four-patch v3 series fixing 64-to-32-bit truncations on Windows (LLP64) remains technically complete but procedurally stalled. In a recent follow-up, Pinto noted that v3 has been on the list since October 31st, still applies cleanly to current `master`, and that two other patch series of theirs are blocked waiting for this one to land. The series converts `unsigned long`/`long` length parameters and counters to `size_t` across the object database, diff, and convert subsystems to prevent silent data corruption with files over 4 GiB on Windows, with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux.

The technical feedback from earlier iterations has been fully addressed, including a revised `EXPENSIVE` test in `t1007-hash-object.sh` and preemptive overflow fixes in `convert.c`. However, the series cannot advance because of an unresolved dispute over commit-message phrasing in the fourth patch. Patrick Steinhardt has indicated that the message reads as AI-generated rather than demonstrating human authorship, and he has set a precedent that contributors using AI tooling must show evident human understanding in their prose and engage substantively in discussion. Junio C Hamano has broadened the conversation to explore how the project should evaluate AI-assisted contributions generally.

The thread now sits at an impasse between technical readiness and procedural uncertainty. Until Pinto rewrites the commit message to satisfy Steinhardt's criteria or the maintainers establish clearer norms for AI-assisted submissions, the series--and the two dependent series behind it--remain in limbo.
