This is a **substantive review reply** on the first patch of Harald Nordgren’s v10 series introducing `remote.<name>.refmap` for matching-inspired fetch mode. The series is part of a long-running effort to improve `git fetch` performance in shallow repositories by dynamically fetching only branches tracked by local branches.

D. Ben Knoble’s reply engages with the technical correctness of the fallback logic in `remote_find_tracking()`, a helper function that resolves remote-tracking refs. The patch under discussion modifies this function to fall back to `remote->refmap` only when `remote->fetch` is either empty or contains no matching refspec for the query. Knoble’s original concern was whether this fallback could have unintended consequences (e.g., for `--delete-merged`), and while he acknowledges the updated logic now falls back to `refmap`, he remains uncertain about what the "correct" behavior should be. He suggests the fallback might be necessary for the `--limited-fetch` feature introduced later in the series but leaves the question open for further discussion.

This reply is **not a blocker** for the series, which is otherwise ready for merging to `next`. The technical ambiguity Knoble raises is narrow and does not undermine the series’ core functionality or design. However, it highlights a potential edge case in how `remote_find_tracking()` interacts with operations like `--delete-merged`. The series author has already addressed the immediate concern by ensuring the fallback is conditional, but Knoble’s hesitation suggests a deeper audit of the function’s callers might be warranted in follow-up work. For now, the patch remains uncontroversial and aligns with the series’ goal of treating `refmap` as a superset of `fetch`.
