This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git reflog` usability thread. Kristoffer Haugsbakk, an early supporter of Harald Nordgren’s `--operations` and `--historical-branch` feature, now questions whether his own long-standing pain point—losing track of reset points during rebasing—was ever real. He demonstrates that simply running `git reflog <branch>` (rather than the default `git reflog` on HEAD) already collapses a multi-commit rebase into a single “rebase (finish)” entry, making the `--operations` filter redundant for his use case. His message shifts the thread’s focus: instead of debating new flags, it now asks whether the existing per-branch reflog already solves the stated problem and whether the real issue is discoverability of that feature. The tone is self-critical and exploratory, not dismissive; he still acknowledges Patrick Steinhardt’s separate usability concerns (linked in the message) as valid, leaving room for further discussion.
