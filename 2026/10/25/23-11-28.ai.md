This is a substantive review follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread proposing `^/` syntax for root-relative submodule URLs. Jacob Keller and Stanislav Aleksandrov continue to refine the design rationale, now focusing on protocol inheritance and the trade-offs between relative traversal (`../../`) and the proposed `^/` syntax.

Jacob acknowledges the low breakage risk of repurposing `^/` as a special character, noting that `protocol.file.allow=user` (the default) already blocks most accidental uses of literal `^` directories. This removes a potential adoption blocker. The discussion then clarifies why `^/` is preferable to multiple `../` segments: while `../../org/lib.git` works for GitHub’s fixed two-level hierarchy, GitLab’s arbitrary subgroup nesting makes the required `../` count unpredictable across forks. The `^/` syntax dynamically resolves to the server root, preserving protocol (HTTPS/SSH) and credentials without manual configuration, which relative traversal cannot guarantee.

The exchange also surfaces a subtle semantic point: forking both superproject and submodule would require updating the `^/` URL to point to the new fork, whereas relative URLs (`../`) would continue pointing to the original submodule unless explicitly changed. Jacob sees this as more correct—forks should not silently inherit upstream dependencies—but it may surprise users expecting automatic fork isolation. No new technical objections are raised; the remaining open question is whether the forge-specific motivation (GitLab subgroups) justifies new syntax, given that alternatives like `url.<base>.insteadOf` and `.gitmodules` extensions exist but require per-clone setup. The thread is converging on the technical design while leaving the syntax choice unresolved.
