This is a follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread about introducing `^/` syntax for root-relative submodule URLs. The email is a back-and-forth between Jacob Keller and Stanislav Aleksandrov, clarifying the design rationale and edge cases of the proposed feature.

Jacob acknowledges the low risk of breaking existing setups, since `^` is a valid but unlikely directory name in current submodule URLs, and `protocol.file.allow` defaults to "user" (blocking unintended local paths). The discussion then circles back to the core problem: relative URLs like `../` break when forks are nested at different depths (e.g., GitLab subgroups), while absolute URLs force a protocol on all users. Stanislav’s `^/` syntax dynamically resolves to the superproject’s remote root, preserving protocol and credentials without manual configuration.

The exchange also reaffirms why alternatives like `url.<base>.insteadOf` or `.gitmodules` extensions fall short: they’re static and can’t adapt to per-clone remote URLs. Jacob concedes the logic behind `^/` but notes that forking both superproject and submodule could still require manual URL updates—though this aligns with the semantic intent of forks. The thread remains open on whether new syntax is justified, but the technical trade-offs are now clearer. The feature looks well-motivated for GitLab-style hierarchies, though broader forge adoption would strengthen its case.
