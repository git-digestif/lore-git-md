This is a substantive review of Stanislav Aleksandrov’s RFC patch introducing `^/` syntax for root-relative submodule URLs. Jacob Keller engages deeply with the design and implementation, raising practical concerns and exploring alternatives.

Jacob’s review focuses on three main areas. First, he questions whether the `^/` syntax is the right choice, noting that `^` is a shell metacharacter in both `cmd.exe` and Git Bash, which could complicate user experience. He acknowledges the precedent from `svn:externals` but remains skeptical about the ergonomics.

Second, he explores whether `.gitmodules` could be extended to carry protocol preferences (e.g., `url.<base>.insteadOf`), which would avoid introducing new syntax. Stanislav had already dismissed this in the RFC, arguing that it would require per-clone setup, but Jacob presses on whether `.gitmodules` could be made more expressive without forcing local configuration.

Third, Jacob probes edge cases: he asks what “refuses it” means in the context of `git submodule add` rejecting `^/` paths, and whether the `^` character is already invalid in directory names (it is not, which is why the patch explicitly rejects it). He also asks whether `../` paths with multiple parent traversals work today, and whether the current logic supports them.

The review is substantive: Jacob does not merely point out surface issues but engages with the design space, suggesting alternatives and asking for clarification on behavior. He does not report testing the patch himself, so the depth is “substantive” rather than “tested.” His questions are likely to shape the next iteration of the patch.
