This is a follow-up in the RFC thread proposing `^/` syntax for root-relative submodule URLs. The email is a reply from the author (Stanislav Aleksandrov) to Jacob Keller’s substantive review, clarifying design choices and edge cases.

The exchange focuses on two main points of contention. First, Jacob suggested extending `.gitmodules` to carry protocol preferences or using `url.<base>.insteadOf` as alternatives to new syntax. Stanislav explains why these don’t solve the problem: `.gitmodules` is static and can’t adapt to per-clone remote URLs, while `insteadOf` requires manual setup on every clone. The `^/` syntax, by contrast, dynamically resolves against the superproject’s remote URL, preserving protocol and credentials without configuration.

Second, Jacob probed whether `../../` could achieve the same goal by allowing deeper relative traversal. Stanislav clarifies that while multiple `..` work, they require knowing the superproject’s depth—a fragile assumption that breaks when forks are nested at different levels (e.g., GitLab subgroups). The `^/` syntax avoids this by resolving to the server root, making it portable across forks regardless of their depth.

The discussion also reaffirms edge-case handling: `git submodule add` explicitly rejects `^/` URLs to avoid ambiguity with literal `^` directories, and local paths with `^/` are blocked unless `protocol.file.allow=always` is set. Stanislav’s responses are precise and address Jacob’s concerns without conceding the core design. The thread remains open on whether new syntax is justified, but the author’s rationale for `^/`—portability across forks and dynamic resolution—is now clearer. Future iterations may need to address shell escaping (Jacob’s earlier concern) and IPv6 in SCP-style URLs, but the current exchange solidifies the technical trade-offs.
