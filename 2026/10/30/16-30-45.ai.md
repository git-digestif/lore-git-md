This is a **governance process update** in reply to Junio C Hamano’s feedback on Emily Shaffer’s proposal to formalize a voting process for the Git project. The email clarifies two critical design choices—**vote counting mechanics** and **eligibility criteria**—while leaving others unresolved.

Emily confirms that the proposal is designed for **multi-option ballots**, where voters rank or select among competing proposals (e.g., "How should we format commit messages?"). She cites tools like CIVS and devotee, which use ranked-choice voting to determine the most broadly acceptable outcome, as potential models for the unspecified `<mechanism TBD>`. This addresses Junio’s question about whether outcomes are decided by plurality (single-option) or proportional support (multi-option), but the document itself remains silent on the exact method.

On **eligibility**, Emily acknowledges Junio’s preference for a stricter standard ("two or more topics in released versions") over the current low bar (one commit in `next` or two code reviews). She notes that Jeff King’s alternative—filtering by total commit count and recency—also gained traction in the thread. Her personal stance leans toward inclusivity (citing her own reduced contributions due to role changes), but she defers to the community’s eventual consensus. The tension between inclusivity and perceived legitimacy remains unresolved, with Justin Tobler’s warning about corporate bias in stricter criteria still unaddressed.

The email does not engage with the **secret ballot** consensus (agreed by Patrick Steinhardt, brian m. carlson, and Junio) or Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s ideological critique that voting inherently favors corporate interests. These debates persist as open questions, though Emily’s focus here is purely procedural. The patch itself is likely to advance once the `<mechanism TBD>` placeholder is filled, but the broader governance implications—whether Git should adopt formal voting at all—are still contested.
