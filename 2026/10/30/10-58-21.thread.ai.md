Thread identity
Subject: [PATCH 0/2] tree-walk: fix silent truncation of >4 GiB trees
Series version: v1 (initial submission; no new version posted)
Originating author: Elia Pinto
Thread type: bugfix patch series

Core problem or goal
Git's tree-walk subsystem silently truncates trees larger than 4 GiB because `struct tree_desc` and `struct tree` store buffer sizes in 32-bit types (`unsigned int` and `long`). The series converts these fields to `size_t` (64-bit on all relevant platforms) so that every tree entry is validated by `fsck` and no silent truncation occurs.

Current status
Under review; no prior versions. The author's follow-up ping confirms the series still applies cleanly to current `master` (e1ec09b23c). Junio C Hamano has now reviewed both patches in the series and confirmed that widening the size fields to `size_t` is the obviously correct approach. For patch 1 he concluded it looks good with no objection to the core conversion; for patch 2 he approves the change to `parse_tree_buffer()` and `struct tree`, accepting it as an incremental "one thing at a time" step even though the sole caller `parse_object_buffer()` still passes an `unsigned long`.

Key technical details
- Files touched: `tree-walk.c`, `tree-walk.h`, `tree.c`, `tree.h`
- Symbols changed: `struct tree_desc` (size field), `struct tree` (size field), `init_tree_desc()`, `init_tree_desc_gently()`, `decode_tree_entry()`, `dir_state` struct (internal to `get_tree_entry_follow_symlinks()`), `parse_tree_buffer()` (patch 2 changes its size parameter from `unsigned long` to `size_t`, matching what `odb_read_object()` already returns)
- Behavior change: trees >4 GiB are no longer truncated; `git ls-tree` shows all entries and `fsck` validates every path, including security-sensitive ones like `.gitmodules`
- On-disk format: unchanged
- Test coverage: none for the 4 GiB case (impractical to construct in-memory); author verified with a simulated 16-bit environment that reproduces the same truncation behavior at 64 KiB
- Review detail: `decode_tree_entry()` contains a redundant per-call validation `if (size < hashsz + 3 || buf[size - (hashsz + 1)])` that checks the buffer ends with NUL followed by the binary OID on every invocation; Junio notes this only needs verification once in `init_tree_desc()` since callers maintain the invariant that `size` refers to the remaining unparsed portion

Open questions and loose ends
- No objections or requests for changes to the core `size_t` conversion; Junio affirmed the approach is correct for both patches
- The author's design question of whether to widen types versus rejecting oversized trees outright is answered: widening to `size_t` is the accepted approach
- New nit raised by Junio tagged `#leftoverbits`: the redundant NUL-terminator check in `decode_tree_entry()` described above is a micro-optimization opportunity in the hot parsing loop, though it does not affect correctness
- Known follow-up identified by Junio in his patch 2 review: `parse_object_buffer()` in `object.c` still passes an `unsigned long` to `parse_tree_buffer()`, so the truncation is not yet fixed end-to-end; this remains open as follow-up work to avoid scope creep
- No test for the actual 4 GiB boundary (acknowledged as impractical)

Participant positions

- Junio C Hamano (maintainer): substantive review of patch 1 confirming the `size_t` widening in `decode_tree_entry()` and related functions is the obviously correct approach; concluded the patch looks good; flagged the redundant validation check in `decode_tree_entry()` as a `#leftoverbits` optimization opportunity. Substantive review of patch 2 approving the `parse_tree_buffer()` signature change and `struct tree` widening, while noting the remaining `parse_object_buffer()` call site in `object.c` as known follow-up work
