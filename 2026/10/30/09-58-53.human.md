This is the fourth and final patch in Elia Pinto’s **v2 series fixing 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms)**. It addresses a theoretical integer-overflow risk in Git’s file-conversion logic that was flagged during review of the third patch.

The patch converts the counters in `struct text_stat` (used for CRLF and binary-detection heuristics) and the `count_ident()` function (used for `$Id$` keyword expansion) from `unsigned`/`int` to `size_t`. While the overflow risk is not Windows-specific—`unsigned` is 32-bit on all platforms Git supports—it was raised in the context of the larger Windows truncation fixes, and the change is a logical extension of the same type-safety theme. The counters only drive decisions (e.g., whether to apply CRLF conversion), so an overflow could lead to incorrect behavior, such as skipping CRLF conversion for a file with exactly 2³² lone LFs. The patch also updates `ident_to_worktree()` to use `st_add()` and `st_mult()` for buffer-size calculations, ensuring the code dies on overflow rather than wrapping.

The change is confined to `convert.c` and is purely mechanical, with no behavior change on any platform. No test is added because triggering the overflow would require more than 4 GiB of input, but the fix is a clear improvement in robustness. The patch completes the series, which now addresses all four truncation sites identified in the author’s scan and incorporates all feedback from Patrick Steinhardt’s review of v1. The series as a whole is well-motivated, narrowly scoped, and appears ready for integration.
