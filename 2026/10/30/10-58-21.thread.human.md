This thread is a two-patch bugfix series from Elia Pinto addressing a silent truncation bug in Git's tree-walk code: trees larger than 4 GiB are currently truncated because `struct tree_desc` and `struct tree` store buffer sizes in 32-bit types. The fix widens those fields to `size_t` across `tree-walk.c`, `tree-walk.h`, `tree.c`, and `tree.h`, ensuring that `git ls-tree` and `fsck` see every entry rather than silently dropping content past the 4 GiB boundary.

Junio C Hamano has now reviewed both patches and expressed approval of the approach. On the first patch he confirmed that widening the size fields in `decode_tree_entry()` and related functions is the obviously correct fix, while flagging a redundant per-call NUL-terminator check in the hot parsing loop as a leftover optimization opportunity that does not affect correctness. On the second patch he approved changing `parse_tree_buffer()` to take a `size_t` size, noting that while this matches what `odb_read_object()` already returns, the sole caller `parse_object_buffer()` in `object.c` still passes an `unsigned long`, so the truncation is not yet fixed end-to-end; he accepts this as an incremental step with the remaining call site left as follow-up work.

The series remains under review at v1 with no objections to the core conversion, and no version bump has been posted. The author verified the fix using a simulated 16-bit environment since constructing an actual 4 GiB tree in tests is impractical, so there is no direct test coverage for the boundary case. The main open item is the promised follow-up to convert `parse_object_buffer()` so the size type is consistent through the entire object-parsing path.
