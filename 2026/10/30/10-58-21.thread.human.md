Elia Pinto has sent a follow-up ping on his two-patch series fixing silent truncation of trees larger than 4 GiB in Git's tree-walk code. The series, still at v1 and applying cleanly to current master, converts the buffer size fields in `struct tree_desc` and `struct tree` from 32-bit types to `size_t` across `tree-walk.c`, `tree-walk.h`, `tree.c`, and `tree.h`, so that oversized trees are fully parsed by `git ls-tree` and validated by `fsck` instead of being silently cut off at the 4 GiB boundary. No review feedback has arrived yet.

In his ping, Pinto raises two specific questions for the list: whether this is worth fixing at all, since trees over 4 GiB can only realistically be crafted intentionally, and if it is worth fixing, whether widening the types is the right approach or whether an oversized tree should instead be rejected outright, for example in `init_tree_desc_gently()`. He notes he is willing to drop the series entirely if there is no interest. The on-disk format is unchanged, and the only acknowledged gap is the lack of a test for the actual 4 GiB case, which the author considers impractical to construct.
