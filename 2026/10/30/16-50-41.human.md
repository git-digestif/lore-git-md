This is a governance discussion about the Git project's proposed community voting process, focusing on eligibility criteria for voters. Emily Shaffer weighs in on the ongoing debate about how to balance inclusivity with ensuring voters have sufficient context and long-term investment in the project.

Emily agrees with Patrick Steinhardt that the *shape* of the voting population matters more than the raw number of voters, noting that vote-counting platforms can easily handle hundreds of participants. She pushes back gently on Junio C Hamano’s earlier suggestion that inactive contributors should be excluded, arguing that some former contributors—especially those with deep history in the project—may still have valuable perspectives on contentious issues. She cites her own situation as an example: while she hasn’t contributed code recently due to her role at Google, she still considers herself part of the community and wants a voice in decisions like the AI contribution policy.

The email doesn’t propose a specific threshold or time window but reinforces the human dimension of the debate. It highlights the tension between excluding drive-by contributors (who might skew a vote) and disenfranchising engaged but inactive community members. Emily’s perspective adds weight to the argument that strict time-based cutoffs could unintentionally silence valuable voices, though she accepts that trade-offs may be necessary. The thread remains unresolved, with no consensus on whether to prioritize recent activity, total contributions, or a hybrid approach.
