This is a refactoring patch series from Junio C Hamano that marks four functions as `static` where they are no longer needed outside their original files. The series touches `ident.c`, `add-interactive.c`, `read-cache.c`, and `unpack-trees.c`, removing unused or overly broad visibility from functions that were either introduced as global or became global through refactoring but no longer require external access.

The motivation is straightforward: reducing unnecessary global symbols improves code hygiene by enforcing tighter encapsulation, making it clearer which functions are truly part of a file's public interface. The commit messages are minimal but sufficient, and the changes themselves are mechanical—no behavior changes are introduced. The series deletes two header declarations (`author_ident_sufficiently_given()` and `init/clear_add_i_state()`) entirely, while the other two functions (`refresh_cache_entry()` and `verify_uptodate()`) remain in their original files but are now file-scoped.

Patch 3/4 (`read-cache: hide refresh_cache_entry()`) is the most invasive, moving 258 lines around to reorder `refresh_cache_entry()` so it can be declared `static` without requiring a forward declaration. Junio notes in the cover letter that while a forward declaration might have been cleaner in the short term, the reordering is future-proof if the code later moves around. This is a judgment call typical of refactoring work—prioritizing long-term clarity over short-term churn.

The series is part of the ongoing effort to keep Git's codebase clean and maintainable, though it is not tied to any of the larger architectural efforts like `the_repository` removal or ODB abstraction. It is a small, self-contained cleanup that should be uncontroversial. The only potential point of discussion is whether the reordering in patch 3/4 is worth the diff noise, but Junio's rationale (avoiding forward declarations) is reasonable.
