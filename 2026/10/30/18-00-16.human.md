This is a governance discussion about the Git project’s proposed community voting process, focusing on voter eligibility and edge cases. Jeff King (Peff) responds to Emily Shaffer’s concerns about excluding long-inactive but historically significant contributors—like Linus Torvalds or Emily herself—from voting on contentious issues such as the AI contribution policy.

Peff acknowledges the trade-offs in any algorithmic eligibility rule, noting that while a metric like "X commits in the last Y years" might include Emily, it could exclude Linus or other former maintainers. He suggests a hybrid approach: a quantitative baseline (e.g., commit count or activity window) to handle the majority of cases, supplemented by a manual override for "weird exceptions" where human judgment is needed. This would allow the Project Leadership Committee (PLC) or Junio Hamano to explicitly include contributors who don’t meet the technical criteria but remain influential in the community. The proposal is pragmatic, aiming to balance scalability with inclusivity, though it introduces new questions about how such exceptions would be managed and who decides them.

The discussion remains in the design phase, with no code or documentation changes yet. The unresolved questions—how to define the baseline metric, whether to include a manual override, and how to operationalize it—will likely shape future iterations of the voting process patch. Peff’s framing suggests a preference for keeping the system mostly automated but acknowledges that some human discretion may be unavoidable to capture the project’s qualitative nuances.
