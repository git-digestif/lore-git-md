This is the second patch in a two-part bugfix series addressing silent truncation of trees larger than 4 GiB in Git’s tree-walk subsystem. The issue stems from `struct tree` storing its buffer size in an `unsigned long`, which is 32-bit on Windows, causing truncation before the fix in patch 1/2 (which updated `struct tree_desc`) can take effect. This patch aligns `struct tree` and its parsing function with the earlier change by converting the size field to `size_t`, ensuring 64-bit capacity on all platforms.

The patch touches `tree.c` and `tree.h`, modifying `struct tree`’s `size` field and updating `parse_tree_buffer()`’s signature to match. The change is minimal and mechanical, with no behavior visible to users beyond fixing the truncation bug. The commit message notes that `parse_object_buffer()` still passes an `unsigned long` to `parse_tree_buffer()`, but this is left for a future patch—this series focuses narrowly on the tree-walk path.

This is a well-scoped, technically sound fix for a real correctness issue. The use of `size_t` is appropriate for buffer sizes, and the patch complements the earlier work in the series without introducing new risks. While the author acknowledges the impracticality of testing the 4 GiB boundary directly, the fix is verified via a 16-bit simulation that reproduces the same truncation behavior at a smaller scale. No review feedback has appeared yet, but the patch looks ready for integration.
