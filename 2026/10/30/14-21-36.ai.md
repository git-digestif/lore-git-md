This is the first patch in a two-patch series that **fixes a safety issue in the worktree subsystem**. It makes `git worktree remove` refuse to delete a worktree that is in the middle of a rebase, `git am` session, bisect, cherry-pick, or revert sequence unless `--force` is given. This aligns with Git’s general principle of protecting uncommitted or in-progress work and prevents accidental data loss when a worktree’s state (stored in its own `$GIT_DIR`) would be discarded.

The implementation is straightforward and well-tested. A new helper function `check_no_operation_in_progress()` uses existing `wt_status_*` functions to detect rebase, bisect, and sequencer state, and `is_directory()` to check for a cherry-pick or revert. The check is only performed when the worktree directory exists, preserving the existing behavior for already-deleted worktrees. The documentation is updated to clarify that such worktrees are considered unclean, and the tests cover all four in-progress operations with realistic edge cases (e.g., a rebase stopped at an `edit` line, a cherry-pick sequence after conflict resolution).

This patch directly addresses the safety concern Ben Knoble and Phillip Wood raised in the RFC discussion: that filtering worktrees by branch state (e.g., `--merged`) could surface mid-rebase worktrees, leading to data loss if used with `git worktree remove`. By making `git worktree remove` refuse deletion of in-progress worktrees unless explicitly forced, the patch mitigates the risk regardless of how the worktree list is filtered. The change is backward-incompatible for scripts that rely on being able to delete in-progress worktrees without `--force`, but the commit message does not identify any such scripts, and the safety benefit outweighs the compatibility cost.

The patch touches `builtin/worktree.c`, `Documentation/git-worktree.adoc`, and adds tests to `t/t2403-worktree-move.sh`. It is a clean, focused fix that does not attempt to solve the broader limitations of `for-each-ref` as a filtering mechanism (e.g., NUL-terminated output, main/linked worktree distinction). Those limitations remain open questions for the second patch in the series, which extends `%(worktreepath)` to include rebasing/bisecting branches. This patch, however, stands on its own as a well-motivated safety improvement.
