This is a **substantive review reply** in the ongoing discussion about the `git notes` race-condition bugfix. Kristoffer Haugsbakk responds to Junio C Hamano’s proposed retry mechanism for concurrent note additions, refining the design to avoid unintended three-way merges.

Haugsbakk agrees with Junio’s core idea—using Git’s merge/rebase machinery to allow non-conflicting updates to proceed—but raises a critical edge case: if two processes edit *different parts* of the *same note* (e.g., one modifies the start, another the end), a three-way merge might succeed, producing a combined note that neither process intended. He argues this outcome would be misleading, as the merged note doesn’t reflect either user’s explicit action. Instead, he suggests the retry mechanism should *fail* if the updates target the same commit, preserving the original patch’s strictness for conflicting cases while still allowing non-conflicting updates to proceed.

The reply advances the thread’s technical direction by narrowing the scope of the proposed retry logic. It doesn’t introduce new code or files but clarifies how the retry mechanism should handle conflicts at the *commit* level (not the *note content* level). This distinction is likely to shape any follow-up patch, as it rules out automatic merging of note content and reinforces the need for explicit conflict resolution. The tone remains collaborative, with Haugsbakk framing his concern as a refinement rather than a rejection of Junio’s proposal.
