This is a brief but clarifying reply in the ongoing governance discussion about Git’s proposed community voting process. Toon Claes, a contributor, responds directly to Weijie Yuan’s concern about feeling unqualified to vote on high-stakes decisions. Toon points out that the voting system would allow contributors to abstain if they meet the eligibility criteria but do not feel confident casting a vote. He also reassures Weijie—and the broader community—that votes would only be used for topics where opinions are genuinely divided, not for decisions where Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) already holds a strong position.

The email does not propose changes to the patch or introduce new technical details, but it adds a practical perspective to the eligibility debate. It frames the voting process as a tool for resolving contentious issues, not as a mechanism to override the maintainer’s judgment. This distinction may help ease concerns about the process’s scope and purpose, though the core question of what threshold ensures voters are sufficiently experienced remains unresolved. The tone is matter-of-fact and constructive, reinforcing the idea that the system is designed to be flexible and inclusive without forcing participation.
