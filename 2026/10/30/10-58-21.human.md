This is a bugfix patch series targeting the tree-walking code. Elia Pinto addresses a truncation issue where tree objects larger than 4 GiB are silently truncated on all platforms (due to `unsigned int` in `struct tree_desc`), and additionally on Windows (due to `long` in `struct tree`). The problem affects both user-facing commands like `git ls-tree` and internal checks like `fsck_tree()`, which may miss entries in large trees.

The series consists of two patches: the first converts `struct tree_desc` and its setup functions to use `size_t`, fixing the universal truncation; the second does the same for `struct tree` and `parse_tree_buffer()`, addressing the Windows-specific issue. The commit message explains that while trees this large are rare, they can be crafted, making this a potential correctness and security concern for `fsck`.

The motivation is clear and well-explained, and the changes are mechanical type conversions that should not alter behavior for trees under 4 GiB. No test is included, as creating a 4 GiB+ tree for testing would be impractical, but the author describes a simulation check for the effect. The series is independent of other recent size-truncation fixes from the same author and applies cleanly to `master`. This looks like a straightforward, well-justified fix that should be uncontroversial.
