This is the v5 iteration of a feature series that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes (but not their tips) have been integrated via squash merges, rebases, or patch-based workflows, even when upstream modified the same files. The series is now four patches: (1) preparatory refactoring, (2) a landing-message helper that updates the output format to show both the branch’s old tip and the landing commit, (3) the core `--delete-integrated` flag, and (4) squash-detection with upstream modifications via a path-by-path three-way merge approximation.

The CLI design is finalized: `--delete-merged <pattern>` preserves strict tip-reachability (current behavior, unchanged), while `--delete-integrated <pattern>` is an opt-in flag that detects both rebased and squash-merged branches. The two flags cannot be combined, and the error message for that case is now concise ("cannot be used together"). The help output (`git branch -h`) now lists `--delete-integrated` in the usage lines. These changes address two of Jasper Mayone’s usability nits from v4; the remaining nits (outdated cover letter flag names, silent skipping of checked-out worktrees) are still unaddressed but minor.

The first two patches (refactoring and landing-message helper) are uncontroversial and ready for integration. The third and fourth patches (core flag and squash-detection) remain under review but are technically mature: they handle upstream modifications, reverted changes, partial landings, and mixed workflows, and Jasper’s tested review confirms the output format and edge-case behaviors. The only major technical gap is the author-check mitigation (Jasper’s proposal to require that the landing commit’s author also authored at least one commit on the branch being deleted), which is still not implemented but is now the sole remaining blocker for safety in forge workflows and the "npm update" scenario.

The series touches `builtin/branch.c`, `Documentation/`, and `t/t3200-branch.sh`. The rebase-detection logic remains tree-based (no blob fetches), while squash-detection uses `ll_merge` for three-way merge approximation, which may download blobs in partial clones when upstream modifications are present. The test suite includes Junio’s false-positive test case, Knoble’s "npm update" scenario, and coverage for upstream modifications. The config variable `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` applies to both flags.
