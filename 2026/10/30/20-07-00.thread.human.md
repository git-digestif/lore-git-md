This thread began as a patch series to fix correctness bugs and optimize `git log --follow` after it became ~14× slower for merges following commit 304812ed33 (June 2026). The v5 series, posted by Justin Tobler and building on work by Miklos Vajna, addressed two key issues: `-m --follow` losing history from non-first parents and `--follow` missing edits on dropped sides of merges. It also introduced an optimization to reduce rename-detection passes, cutting the regression to ~1.6× slower than pre-304812ed33. However, this remaining gap became a deployment blocker for GitLab, prompting a project-level debate over whether to revert the original commit, accept the performance cost, or introduce a toggle.

The debate is now settled. Junio C Hamano has endorsed an opt-in toggle—disabled by default—to mitigate the performance cost while preserving the correctness fixes. In his latest email, Junio ejected the existing speedup branch and queued a single-patch replacement introducing this toggle, signaling it as the preferred path forward. The toggle will likely take the form of `--follow=merges` (per Patrick Steinhardt’s proposal) and is being fast-tracked as a standalone patch to unblock GitLab’s deployment. The v5 series, now technically complete, will proceed separately. Key participants—Junio, Patrick, and Miklos—have aligned on the toggle’s default-off design, and no further policy discussion is expected unless implementation details become contentious. The outcome balances correctness, performance, and operational needs, with the toggle serving as a pragmatic stopgap.
