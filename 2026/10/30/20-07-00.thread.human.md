This thread began as a patch series (v5) to fix correctness bugs and optimize `git log --follow` after it became ~14× slower when processing merges due to commit 304812ed33 (June 2026). The series, originally authored by Miklos Vajna and iterated by Justin Tobler, addresses two key bugs: `-m --follow` losing history from non-first parents and `--follow` missing edits on dropped sides of merges. It also introduces a targeted optimization to reduce rename-detection passes during merges, though a ~1.6× performance gap remained compared to pre-304812ed33.

The project-level resolution to this regression has now been executed. Junio C Hamano ejected the prior speedup branch and merged a standalone toggle patch, proposed by Patrick Steinhardt, which introduces a **default-disabled opt-in mechanism** (`--follow=merges` or `log.follow=merges`) to disable `--follow` in non-linear history by default. This toggle patch is now **merged to `master`**, unblocking GitLab’s deployment, and is fully decoupled from the v5 series. The toggle’s design—using both a flag and config option, with the flag as the likely primary interface—has been finalized, and its default-disabled status reflects consensus among Patrick, Miklos, and Junio.

The v5 series itself remains technically complete and under review, with no urgency for merging since the toggle patch resolves the immediate performance concern. Key changes from v4 include squashing the fix for file copies into the main optimization and expanding test coverage in `t4219` for edge cases like copy-source tracking and unrelated histories. The series touches `log-tree.c` and adds new helper functions (`follow_parent_changed_dropped_file`, `follow_has_rename_source`, and `follow_needs_renames`), but introduces no on-disk format changes. While the v5 series further mitigates the regression, the toggle patch is now the settled solution for the performance trade-off, framing the opt-in mechanism as the preferred outcome. Open questions are limited to user education (e.g., release notes) and the v5 series’ independent timeline.
