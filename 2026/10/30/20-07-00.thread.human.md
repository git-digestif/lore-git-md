This thread is about a six-patch series from Miklos Vajna that fixes correctness bugs and optimizes `git log --follow` when processing merges. The series targets a performance regression introduced in June 2026 (commit 304812ed33), where `--follow` became significantly slower for merge-heavy histories. Version 4 of the series includes fixes for two bugs—`-m --follow` losing history from non-first parents and `--follow` missing edits on dropped sides of merges—and an optimization that reduces rename-detection passes, cutting runtime for a real-world linux.git case from ~3 minutes to ~37 seconds.

The series is under active review, with Patrick Steinhardt raising two substantive correctness concerns. The first, identified in patch 5/6, questions whether the optimization incorrectly disables rename detection for commits with unrelated histories, contradicting the intent of the original regression commit. The second, raised in this latest email, reveals a regression in how the series handles file copies: edits to the *source* of a copy are no longer tracked when the merge keeps the copied file but discards the original. Patrick’s test case—a copy of file `F` to `G` on `master`, an edit to `F` on a side branch, and a merge that keeps `G` but drops `F`—shows that `git log --follow G` now misses the edit to `F` on the side branch, breaking behavior that worked before the series. This issue stems from an assumption in the optimization logic that only renames (not copies) need to be considered when the merge base lacks the path.

The series touches `log-tree.c` and the test suite (`t/t4219-log-follow-merge.sh`), introducing helper functions like `follow_parent_changed_dropped_file` and `follow_has_rename_source` to narrow rename-detection. While the optimization in patch 5/6 and the copy-handling fix in patch 6/6 are the focus of current discussion, the series also includes standalone fixes for `-m --follow` (patch 3/6) and expanded test coverage for `--diff-merges=first-parent` interactions. No maintainer decision or merge status has been reported yet, and the next step will likely be a v5 addressing both correctness gaps. For now, the series remains blocked on these unresolved issues.
