This thread concerns `git log --follow`, which became roughly 14 times slower on merge-heavy histories after commit 304812ed33 in June 2026. Justin Tobler posted a v5 five-patch series (building on earlier work by Miklos Vajna) that fixes two correctness bugs -- `-m --follow` dropping history from non-first parents and `--follow` missing edits on dropped sides of merges -- while also narrowing rename detection to speed things up, though benchmarks showed `--follow` still ran about 1.6x slower than before the regression.

The performance problem turned out to be a deployment blocker for GitLab, so Patrick Steinhardt proposed a separate, standalone patch adding an opt-in `--follow=merges` flag (and `log.follow=merges` config), disabled by default, which restores pre-regression performance for normal users while keeping the correctness fixes. Junio C Hamano settled the policy question decisively: he ejected the earlier speedup branch and merged the toggle patch to `master` as of 2026-08-12, unblocking GitLab. The v5 correctness-and-optimization series remains technically complete and under review, proceeding at its own pace now that the urgent performance issue has been addressed.

The only recent activity is procedural: Miklos Vajna acknowledged Patrick Steinhardt's request that review responses be posted as replies to specific feedback emails rather than bundled into the next patch version. No technical questions remain open beyond routine review of the v5 series itself.
