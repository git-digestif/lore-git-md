This thread is about fixing a performance regression in `git log --follow` that made it ~14× slower when processing merges after a 2026 commit. The v5 patch series from Justin Tobler (building on Miklos Vajna’s work) addresses two correctness bugs—losing history from non-first parents and missing edits on dropped sides of merges—and introduces an optimization that reduces the regression to ~1.6× slower than before. However, this remaining gap was a deployment blocker for GitLab, leading to a project-level decision to introduce a default-disabled opt-in toggle for `--follow` in non-linear history.

Junio C Hamano has now executed this resolution by ejecting the prior speedup branch and queuing a standalone patch from Patrick Steinhardt that adds the toggle (`--follow=merges` or `log.follow=merges`). This patch is nearly ready to merge, with Patrick’s review flagging only minor documentation and error-handling improvements (e.g., better config parsing and a performance warning in the docs). The toggle will restore pre-regression performance for users who don’t opt in, while preserving the correctness fixes from the v5 series. The v5 series itself remains technically complete and can proceed independently.

The policy debate is settled: the toggle is the pragmatic path forward, with consensus that it should be disabled by default. The only remaining discussion is implementation details (e.g., flag vs. config option), but Patrick’s preference for `--follow=merges` is likely to prevail. The thread now focuses on finalizing the toggle patch to unblock GitLab’s deployment.
