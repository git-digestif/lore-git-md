This thread is about fixing performance and correctness regressions in `git log --follow` introduced by a 2026 commit, now resolved through a two-part approach. The first part—a standalone toggle patch—has just received final approval and will land shortly to unblock GitLab’s deployment. The second part, a v5 patch series, remains in progress for deeper fixes.

The problem began when `git log --follow` (which tracks file renames across history) became 14× slower for merge commits after commit 304812ed33. The v5 series from Miklos Vajna and Justin Tobler fixes two correctness bugs (lost history in non-first parents and missed edits on dropped merge sides) and optimizes rename detection, reducing the regression to 1.6× slower. However, even this reduced gap was unacceptable for GitLab’s operational use, prompting a faster solution.

Junio C Hamano, Patrick Steinhardt, and Miklos agreed on a toggle mechanism—either a config option `log.follow=merges` or flag `--follow=merges`—to disable rename-following in merges by default, restoring pre-regression performance. Junio ejected the earlier speedup branch and queued the toggle patch as the settled resolution. Patrick’s review of the v2 toggle patch just resolved the last implementation details (error handling, documentation, and redundant checks), and he explicitly approved it. The toggle will land first, decoupled from the v5 series, which can proceed at its own pace.

The toggle’s design (flag vs. config) isn’t finalized, but Patrick’s preference for `--follow=merges` is likely to prevail. The default-disabled status is uncontested, and the patch now includes clear documentation about the performance trade-off. With the toggle merged, GitLab’s deployment is unblocked, and the v5 series can focus on further correctness and optimization without time pressure.
