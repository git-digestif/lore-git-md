This thread is about fixing a severe performance regression and correctness bugs in `git log --follow` when processing merges, introduced by commit 304812ed33 in June 2026. The series, now at version 5, is authored by Miklos Vajna and aims to restore performance (from ~336 seconds back to ~39 seconds for a linux.git test case) while addressing two key correctness issues: `-m --follow` losing history from non-first parents and `--follow` missing edits on dropped sides of merges.

The latest iteration (v5) simplifies the logic by merging the previous fix into the main optimization patch, ensuring correctness for file copies and unrelated histories (like subtree merges). Patrick Steinhardt’s earlier concerns—about `follow_needs_renames()` disabling rename detection for unrelated histories and failing to track edits to copy sources—have been addressed, with expanded test coverage in `t4219` validating these fixes. However, Justin Tobler’s benchmark shows the series still leaves `--follow` about 1.6x slower than the pre-regression baseline (~23 seconds), prompting his suggestion to either make the feature opt-in (via a new flag) or revert 304812ed33 entirely to restart the effort.

The thread is now at a crossroads. The series is under active review, with no maintainer decision yet, but the lingering performance gap and Justin’s proposal could spark debate about whether the trade-off is acceptable. Key participants include Patrick Steinhardt, who raised the initial correctness concerns, and Justin Tobler, who contributed performance analysis and alternative proposals. The outcome will likely hinge on whether the project accepts the current performance trade-off or pushes for further optimization.
