## 2026/11/11

- [2026/11/11/00-40-00 by Justin Tobler <jltobler@gmail.com>] Justin Tobler benchmarked `git log --stat --follow -- include/linux/rsc_table.h` on linux.git: 23.493s with 304812ed33de reverted, 335.883s at the regression, and 38.688s with v5 applied (reverted build 14.30x faster than the regression and 1.65x faster than v5). He said v5 remains a significant performance regression that he expects will cause increased server-side file-history timeouts for repositories hosted at GitLab, suggested making the rename-following behavior opt-in via a new flag instead of on by default, and proposed reverting 304812ed33de and restarting with a fresh series to make the changes easier to validate.
- [2026/11/11/10-43-50 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt agreed the performance cost is too high and that GitLab needs a way to disable the new feature altogether; he had no strong sense on reverting the whole series (finding the patches hard to review, possibly due to his own unfamiliarity with the subsystem), but said that if the series is not reverted he wants a toggle to disable the feature as a first step, ideally soon to mitigate the regression, with GitLab willing to help contribute a default-disabled toggle.
- [2026/11/11/12-41-21 by Miklos Vajna <vmiklos@collabora.com>] Miklos Vajna said he has no problem turning the behavior from default to opt-in and would prefer not to revert given the effort invested and that some see the missing history as a git-log bug; he is OK with replacing the last patch in the series with one that turns this off by default, and asked whether opting in via a new config option (similar to log.follow) would meet the need.
- [2026/11/11/13-29-22 by Patrick Steinhardt <ps@pks.im>] Patrick Steinhardt proposed first landing a single-patch series that introduces the disable knob, mergeable without the rest of the series, since GitLab deploys from Git's master and needs the regression fixed fast; for opting in he offered two options — extending `--follow`/`log.follow` with an optional parameter such as `--follow=merges`/`merges`, or adding `--follow-merges` and `log.followMerges` — and said he leans toward the first.
