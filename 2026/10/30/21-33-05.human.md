This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing `parse-options` early scanning API series. The thread is reviewing **v3 of Christian Couder’s patch series**, which introduces `early_scan_options()`—a new sub-API for safely scanning command-line arguments before full parsing. The series fixes a real bug in `git fast-import` (where `--allow-unsafe-features` was ignored when preceded by `--depth 5`) and replaces fragile hand-rolled early scans with a reusable, consistent mechanism.

Kaartic Sivaraam’s reply signals **positive progress**: he calls v3 “in a much better state” and notes that only “a few minor comments” remain. This suggests the core design—reusing `parse-options` internals (`lookup_long_opt()`) to handle negated options, unambiguous abbreviations, and aliases—has addressed his earlier concerns. The series now stops the scan at ambiguous abbreviations, short options, and unknown options to avoid misinterpreting values, a cautious approach that prioritizes correctness over completeness.

The unresolved feedback from Kaartic’s v3 review (validation checks in `early_scan_options()` and clarifying the ephemeral nature of the `option` pointer passed to callbacks) appears to be the last technical hurdles. These are **actionable and low-risk**: the validation checks can be duplicated from `parse_options_check()`, and the documentation clarification is straightforward. The series is **close to being ready for `next`**, with the `git fast-import` conversion (patch 4/6) already deemed merge-ready by Junio in earlier rounds.

For readers tracking this effort, the key takeaway is that the API’s design has stabilized, and the remaining work is polishing. The series is **worth attention** from maintainers of commands with hand-rolled early scans (e.g., `git bisect`, `git rev-parse`), as it offers a safer, more maintainable alternative. The preparatory refactorings (patches 1/6 and 2/6) are uncontroversial and could proceed independently if further iteration is needed on the core API.
