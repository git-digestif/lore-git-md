This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing RFC thread proposing the `^/` syntax for root-relative submodule URLs. Stanislav Aleksandrov clarifies a key semantic distinction between the proposed `^/` syntax and traditional relative paths (`../`).

The email confirms that `^/` is designed for submodules that remain in their original location on the remote server, even when the superproject is forked. If both the superproject and submodule are forked together, the existing `../lib.git` relative path syntax continues to work as expected, pointing to the new fork. This preserves the expected behavior for users who want their forks to track their own submodule copies, while `^/` provides a stable reference to the original submodule location. The distinction is subtle but important: `^/` is not a replacement for relative paths, but a complementary tool for scenarios where the submodule should remain anchored to its original server location regardless of superproject forking.

No new technical objections or design changes are introduced. The thread remains focused on refining the rationale and use cases for the feature, with no indication that the core design is in question.
