This is a **bugfix and ref-filter enhancement** targeting the worktree subsystem. The series addresses two related issues: (1) `git worktree remove` currently allows deletion of worktrees mid-rebase or mid-bisect, risking data loss, and (2) `git for-each-ref`'s `%(worktreepath)` atom incorrectly reports an empty value for branches being rebased or bisected, breaking composability with other commands.

The first patch makes `git worktree remove` refuse to delete a worktree that is in the middle of a rebase, bisect, cherry-pick, revert, or `am` sequence unless `--force` is given. This aligns with Git’s general principle of protecting uncommitted or in-progress work and fixes a pre-existing bug that becomes more likely to trigger once the second patch lands. The change is straightforward and well-motivated; it adds a new helper function `worktree_is_mid_sequence()` that checks for the presence of rebase, bisect, or sequencer directories, and updates the validation logic in `builtin/worktree.c`.

The second patch extends `ref-filter.c` so that `%(worktreepath)` (and consequently the `+` marker in `git branch` output) reports the worktree path for branches being rebased or bisected. This matches the behavior already implemented in `git worktree add` and `git branch -d`, and closes the gap Kristoffer Haugsbakk identified in the earlier RFC thread. The implementation reuses the existing `prepare_checked_out_branches()` logic from `branch.c` and adds a new `get_worktree_path_for_rebase_or_bisect()` helper in `ref-filter.c`. The change is backward-incompatible for scripts that rely on `%(worktreepath)` being empty for rebasing/bisecting branches, but the commit message frames this as a bug fix rather than a new feature.

The series is part of a design pivot away from the original `--branch=<branch>` proposal toward a narrower, more composable change that avoids introducing new CLI options. It directly addresses the safety and usability concerns raised by Ben Knoble and Phillip Wood in the RFC discussion: the first patch prevents data loss from accidental deletion of mid-rebase worktrees, and the second patch ensures that `for-each-ref --merged` and similar commands can reliably find all worktrees associated with a branch, regardless of their state. The patches are well-tested, with new tests covering edge cases like unborn branches, multiple worktrees on the same branch, and porcelain output.

Two minor design choices are flagged for review: whether `git branch` should suppress the `+` marker in the current worktree (currently it shows both `*` and `+`), and whether a merge stopped with `--no-commit` should be treated as a protected state (currently it is not). Neither choice affects the core functionality, but they are worth noting for consistency with Git’s user-facing output conventions. The series is under active review and looks like a solid, uncontroversial fix for a real usability gap.
