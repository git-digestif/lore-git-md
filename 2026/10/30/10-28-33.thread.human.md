Elia Pinto has posted version 2 of a five-patch series fixing loose object handling and size truncation bugs that bite hardest on LLP64 platforms like 64-bit Windows, where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit. The series addresses missing bounds checks in loose object parsing and streaming that could expose uninitialized memory or silently truncate objects, an `int` truncation in `cat-file --batch` causing buffer overreads for objects between 2 and 4 GiB, and a heap overflow in `notes.c` when concatenating notes totaling 4 GiB or more. The first three patches harden the loose object reader against header/content size mismatches in both the buffered and streaming paths, with automated tests in `t1060` and `t1006`; the remaining patches widen types to `size_t` throughout the loose object path, fix the batch writer, and fix the notes concatenation, though several of these lack automated tests because they require multi-gigabyte objects or 32-bit `long` platforms to reproduce.

The series is currently under review with no maintainer decision or objections recorded yet. It is rebased on `master` and was deemed non-security-sensitive after review on `git-security`. The patches complement two other type-widening series by the same author and drop a `diff.c` fix from v1 that is now handled separately.
