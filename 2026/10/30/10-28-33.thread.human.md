This thread is a six-patch bugfix series from Elia Pinto addressing loose object handling and size truncation issues, with particular attention to LLP64 platforms like 64-bit Windows. The series fixes two general bugs (missing size checks in loose object parsing) and four platform-specific issues, including a crash in the `diff` subsystem that occurs when processing large files on systems where `long` is 32-bit.

The latest patch, the sixth in the series, resolves a crash in `count_trailing_blank()` by converting a `long` variable to `size_t`. The bug surfaces when a buffer between 2 GiB and 4 GiB is processed, causing pointer arithmetic to wrap around and dereference invalid memory. While the fix is minimal—changing a single line in `diff.c`—it addresses a real and exploitable crash, though one that requires specific conditions (a 32-bit `long` platform and a >2 GiB blob) to reproduce. Like the other Windows-specific patches in the series, this one relies on manual verification due to the impracticality of automating such large inputs in the test suite.

The series is under review and has not yet received substantive technical feedback or objections. It is based on `master` and does not conflict with Elia’s related work on modernizing type usage in Git’s internals. While the cross-platform fixes include new tests, the platform-specific patches (including this one) are documented with manual reproduction steps in their commit messages. The series as a whole is well-motivated, with detailed explanations and a clear focus on hardening Git against truncation and overflow risks, particularly in environments where type mismatches can lead to silent failures or crashes.
