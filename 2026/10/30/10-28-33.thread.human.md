Elia Pinto has posted the third version of a five-patch series fixing loose object handling and size truncation bugs that primarily affect LLP64 platforms like 64-bit Windows, where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit. The series addresses missing bounds checks in loose object parsing and streaming, an `int` truncation bug in `cat-file --batch` for objects between 2 and 4 GiB, and a heap overflow in notes concatenation when notes exceed 4 GiB. Junio C Hamano has approved patches 1, 4, and 5 outright, and patch 3 was reworked in v3 to move error reporting to the caller as he requested; all are now in good shape. Patch 2, which widens loose-object size variables from `unsigned long` to `size_t` throughout the reading path, has also received Junio's approval of the technical direction.

The only remaining friction concerns patch 2's commit message. Patrick Steinhardt objected that the explanation for why no automated test exists reads like uninformative, AI-generated text, signaling insufficient authorial investment, and asked Elia to rewrite it in his own voice. Junio C Hamano has now echoed this concern in his latest reply, agreeing that commit messages have become the primary signal of whether an author is genuinely engaged with their work, though he offered no technical feedback on the code itself. No technical objections remain open across the series; the v3 patches are rebased on `master` and the sole outstanding item is the requested commit-message polish on patch 2.
