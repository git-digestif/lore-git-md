This is a **substantive review** from Jeff King (Peff) on Kristofer Karlsson’s quarantine performance fix. Peff engages directly with the architectural trade-offs of the proposed v2 patch, offering historical context and a measured endorsement of the parent-path deduction approach.

The review centers on the quarantine mechanism’s original design intent. Peff explains that `GIT_QUARANTINE_PATH` was always meant to be more than a boolean flag—it was designed at GitHub to serve as a rendezvous point for index-pack and hooks, allowing index-pack to write auxiliary data (e.g., a list of large blobs) that hooks could then read. This historical use case clarifies why the variable is a path rather than a simple flag, and why modifying its semantics could break existing hooks. Peff’s recollection of GitHub’s internal workflow (large-object rejection, potential secret-scanning) provides concrete motivation for preserving the variable’s current behavior.

Peff then addresses Kristofer’s v2 approach: deducing the real object directory from the quarantine directory’s parent path. He calls this "quite sensible," framing it as a natural extension of the existing rule that `--alternate-refs` should not list refs from an alternate that is actually the same repository. The quarantine setup, he notes, creates an "odd alternate situation," but the parent-path assumption aligns with the broader principle that Git should not redundantly traverse its own refs. His endorsement is matter-of-fact, with no caveats about the "icky" nature of the assumption—suggesting he views it as a pragmatic and safe solution.

No new technical objections or alternatives are raised. The review implicitly accepts the performance data and test coverage as sufficient, and the discussion remains focused on the architectural soundness of the parent-path deduction. The tone is collaborative, with Peff’s historical context serving to validate rather than challenge the direction.
