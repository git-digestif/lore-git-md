This is a governance design discussion about Git’s proposed **community voting process**, specifically a minor but important refinement to how votes will reference proposals. Junio C Hamano replies to Emily Shaffer’s status update on the patch, accepting two uncontroversial changes (subject line tweak and secret ballots) but proposing a key adjustment to the third: instead of referencing a **commit ID** in Junio’s tree, votes should use the **Message-ID** of the email containing the proposal.

The change is small but significant. Commit IDs in Junio’s integration branches (`next`, `seen`) can change if he requeues or rebases a topic, making them unreliable as stable references. Message-IDs, by contrast, are immutable and tied to the email itself, ensuring that votes always point to the exact version of the proposal being discussed. This aligns with Git’s mailing-list culture, where patches are exchanged as emails and discussions reference them by Message-ID (e.g., in `In-Reply-To` headers).

Junio’s suggestion is **technically sound** and resolves a potential source of confusion or error in the voting process. It does not alter the substance of the eligibility debate or platform choice but ensures the logistics of voting are robust. The adjustment is likely to be uncontroversial, as it addresses a practical edge case without introducing new complexity.
