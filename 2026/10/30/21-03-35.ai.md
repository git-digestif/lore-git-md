This is a governance discussion about the proposed **community voting process** for Git, specifically addressing the choice of voting platform. Jonathan Corbet, former Linux Foundation Technical Advisory Board (TAB) member, shares his experience using **CIVS** (Condorcet Internet Voting Service) for TAB elections. His email is a **substantive, tested review** of CIVS’s practicality, offering real-world context for Git’s decision.

Corbet confirms that CIVS is **straightforward for occasional polls**—a good fit for Git, which is unlikely to hold frequent votes. He acknowledges the **risk of relying on a free, third-party service** (civs2.civs.us) but notes that TAB never prioritized self-hosting despite this concern. His key operational insight: CIVS requires voters to **proactively sign up** on the platform before receiving notifications, which could create friction for Git contributors accustomed to email-based workflows. This aligns with Emily Shaffer’s earlier observation that CIVS is easier to adopt but introduces a dependency on an external service.

The email does not advocate for or against CIVS but provides **actionable context** for the trade-offs. It reinforces the thread’s focus on **platform neutrality** (avoiding GitHub) while highlighting a potential **usability hurdle** (sign-up requirement). Corbet’s offer to answer questions signals openness to helping Git navigate CIVS’s quirks, which could be valuable if the project leans toward this option.
