This is the third patch in Elia Pinto’s four-patch series (`v2 3/4`) fixing 64-to-32 truncations on Windows (LLP64 platforms). It targets the file conversion subsystem in Git, where buffer sizes are passed as `size_t` but then narrowed to `unsigned long` in helper functions like `gather_stats()` and `count_ident()`. On Windows, this truncation causes Git to misclassify files over 4 GiB as binary (or non-binary), skip CRLF conversion, or fail to expand `$Id$` keywords beyond the first 4 GiB. The patch mechanically converts all buffer-size parameters in `convert.c`, `attr.c`, and `read-cache.c` to `size_t`, eliminating the truncation path.

The change is straightforward and well-motivated: it prevents silent data corruption (incorrect CRLF handling or keyword expansion) on Windows while preserving behavior on 64-bit Linux. The patch also removes the `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` wrapper in `read_blob_data_from_index()`, which was a known truncation point when `core.autocrlf` is enabled. No new symbols are introduced, and the on-disk format remains unchanged.

This patch directly addresses feedback from Patrick Steinhardt’s review of `v1`, which flagged the truncation risk in the conversion helpers. The series as a whole is now complete, with test coverage added in `t1007-hash-object.sh` (patch 4/4) and all open questions resolved. The changes are narrowly scoped to type conversions, making them easy to review and unlikely to introduce regressions. The series appears ready for integration.
