This is a governance discussion about Git’s proposed **community voting process**, specifically the choice of voting platform. Jonathan Corbet, former Linux Foundation Technical Advisory Board (TAB) chair, shares his experience using **CIVS** (Condorcet Internet Voting Service) for TAB elections. His input is practical and focused on trade-offs: CIVS is easy to use but relies on an external, free service that could disappear or change terms. He notes that CIVS requires voters to sign up separately, which adds friction but isn’t a dealbreaker for occasional votes. His tone is neutral—he doesn’t push for CIVS over *devotee* (Debian’s self-hosted system) but confirms it’s a viable option for Git’s low-volume needs.

The email doesn’t resolve the platform debate but adds real-world context. CIVS’s hosted nature lowers the barrier to adoption, while *devotee* offers more control but requires self-hosting. Corbet’s point about voter signup is a minor operational detail, not a showstopper. For a busy reader, this is a green light for CIVS if the community prioritizes simplicity over self-sufficiency. The thread’s next step is still Emily Shaffer’s reroll of the patch, where she’ll incorporate consensus on the platform (likely CIVS) and the still-unresolved eligibility criteria.
