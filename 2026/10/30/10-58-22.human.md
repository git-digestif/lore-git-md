This is the first patch in a two-part bugfix series addressing a silent truncation issue in Git’s tree-walk subsystem. The problem occurs when processing trees larger than 4 GiB: `struct tree_desc` stores the buffer size in an `unsigned int`, which is 32-bit on all platforms, causing entries beyond the 4 GiB boundary to be ignored. This patch converts the size field in `struct tree_desc` and related functions (`init_tree_desc()`, `init_tree_desc_gently()`, `decode_tree_entry()`) from `unsigned long` to `size_t`, ensuring 64-bit capacity on all relevant platforms.

The change is straightforward and well-motivated. The commit message clearly explains the truncation bug—how it causes `git ls-tree` to silently omit entries and `fsck` to miss validation checks—and includes a verification method (simulating a 16-bit environment) to demonstrate the fix. The patch touches only the core tree-walking logic in `tree-walk.c` and `tree-walk.h`, updating type signatures and internal calculations without altering behavior for trees under 4 GiB. While no test is included (due to the impracticality of constructing a 4 GiB in-memory tree), the author’s validation approach is reasonable.

This is a targeted, low-risk fix for a real correctness issue. The use of `size_t` aligns with Git’s conventions for buffer sizes, and the patch integrates cleanly with the broader effort to eliminate size truncations in other subsystems.
