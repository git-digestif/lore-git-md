This is a **bugfix and platform-compatibility patch series** targeting Windows (LLP64 platforms) and general integer-overflow robustness. It is the second iteration (`v2`) of Elia Pinto’s effort to eliminate 64-to-32 truncations that cause silent data corruption, crashes, or incorrect behavior when Git processes files larger than 4 GiB.

The series now consists of four patches, up from three in `v1`. The first three patches remain unchanged in purpose: they widen `unsigned long` parameters to `size_t` in the object-write API, word-diff buffer allocation, and file-conversion helpers. The fourth patch is new and addresses a secondary overflow risk in `convert.c` that Patrick Steinhardt flagged during review — counters in `struct text_stat` and the `count_ident()` function, which could wrap at 2³² on *any* platform, not just Windows. While these counters are only used as size hints for `strbuf_grow()` and do not cause memory-safety issues, the patch converts them to `size_t` for consistency and to eliminate a theoretical edge case.

The most significant change since `v1` is the addition of a new test in `t/t1007-hash-object.sh`. This test verifies that writing (not just hashing) a 5 GiB file through a filter produces the correct object ID and size. Like the existing 4 GiB+ tests, it is marked `EXPENSIVE` and `SIZE_T_IS_64BIT`, and it fails on Linux if the length is artificially truncated to 32 bits. This directly addresses Patrick’s earlier concern about the lack of test coverage for the object-write truncation bug.

The series touches 11 files, including the new test. The changes are mechanical type conversions with no behavior change on 64-bit Linux, but they prevent data corruption (truncated blobs), crashes (word-diff buffer overflow), and incorrect metadata handling (binary detection, CRLF conversion, keyword expansion) on Windows. The fourth patch also improves robustness on all platforms by eliminating a 32-bit counter wrap that could lead to suboptimal buffer growth.

This iteration appears well-crafted and responsive to review. The new test is a clear improvement, and the fourth patch addresses a forward-looking concern without expanding the series’ scope. The patches are narrowly focused, well-motivated, and unlikely to be controversial.
