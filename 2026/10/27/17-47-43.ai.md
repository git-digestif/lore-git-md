This is a documentation terminology discussion in the ongoing thread about Git's glossary definitions for object identifiers. Junio C Hamano (the maintainer) replies to Ben Knoble's feedback on Julia Evans's rejected patch, which proposed making "object ID" the canonical term for the hash identifier while clarifying "object name" as context-dependent.

Junio's email is a detailed defense of the current glossary definition, where "object name" is the canonical term for the 40- or 64-character hexadecimal identifier (SHA-1 or SHA-256). He argues that the term "object name" is not ambiguous—it refers specifically to the hash, while other terms like refs, branch names, or unique prefixes are merely *shorthand ways to refer to* that name. He illustrates this with an analogy: a blob object's "name" is its hash, just as a fruit might be called "orange" in one language and "laranja" in another. The shorthand notations (e.g., `main`, `HEAD`, or a unique prefix) are interchangeable with the full hash in many contexts, but they are not themselves "object names."

Junio also addresses Ben Knoble's suggestion to use "notation" instead of "name" for these shorthands, dismissing it as unnecessary. He argues that the real issue in `gitrevisions(1)` is not the word "name" but the phrasing "Here are various ways to spell object names," which he claims should instead say "Here are various things that can be used in place of [object names]." He emphasizes that this is a wording problem, not a terminology one, and that replacing "object name" with "object identifier" would not fix it.

The email includes historical context: Junio references a 2005 commit (`f1671ecbfa`) that standardized "object name" as the canonical term after a long period of inconsistent documentation. He frames the current glossary as the result of deliberate effort and insists that any deviations (e.g., using "hash" or "identifier" in other documentation) should be corrected to align with it, rather than revising the glossary itself. He also notes that newer documentation, like the `gitdatamodel` page, has reintroduced this inconsistency and should be fixed.

This reply reinforces the maintainer's rejection of Julia Evans's patch and makes it clear that the project's official stance is to preserve "object name" as the canonical term. The discussion remains at an impasse: no alternative solution has been proposed, and Junio's position leaves little room for compromise unless someone undertakes a broader audit to align all documentation with the glossary's definition. The thread's focus has shifted from refining the glossary to defending the status quo.
