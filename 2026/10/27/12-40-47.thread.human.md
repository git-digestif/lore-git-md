This thread is about a small but impactful build-system patch by Harald Nordgren that enables ccache to cache objects built with Git’s precompiled header (introduced in 8d3a673147). Without the patch, ccache refuses to cache such objects, causing noticeable slowdowns during rebuilds and branch switches. The patch itself is minimal—just two lines in the Makefile—but its effect is significant: warm-cache rebuilds are about three times faster, and branch switching is six times faster, though the first build of each branch is about 25% slower while ccache fills its cache.

Ben Knoble recently reviewed the patch, raising two technical questions about the Makefile implementation. He wonders whether the `export CCACHE_SLOPPINESS` directive, when attached to target-specific rules, actually propagates to recipe processes as intended. He also suggests an alternative approach—setting the variable directly in the precompiled header recipes—but doesn’t push for the change. Additionally, he points out that the patch only modifies the Makefile, leaving the Meson build system untouched, which could lead to inconsistent ccache behavior depending on the build tool. His review is focused on build-system mechanics and doesn’t challenge the patch’s core premise or performance claims. The patch remains under review with no objections so far.
