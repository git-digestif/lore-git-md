This thread is about a build system tweak to restore ccache effectiveness after the recent precompiled header feature landed. Harald Nordgren identified that ccache refuses to cache objects built with Git's precompiled header (`git-compat-util.h`) unless two "sloppiness" settings are enabled: `pch_defines` and `time_macros`. The patch adds these settings to the Makefile, claiming a 3x speedup for warm-cache rebuilds and a 6x speedup when switching between branches with a warm cache, at the cost of a ~25% slowdown on the first build of each branch while ccache fills its cache.

The change is minimal—just two lines in the Makefile—and the commit message explains why the settings are safe for Git: the precompiled header is included with `-include`, so no source-level `#define` can override it, and Git's sources don't use the time-related macros that `time_macros` would otherwise make unsafe. There's one known limitation: Clang still misses the cache when switching between branches that modify headers included by the precompiled header, due to mtime recording in the PCH itself.

So far, the thread is just the patch submission; no reviews or objections have appeared yet. If this holds, it's a straightforward fix for a performance regression that should be uncontroversial.
