This thread is about a small but impactful build-system patch that enables ccache to cache objects built with Git’s precompiled header, introduced in an earlier commit. The patch itself is minimal—just two lines in the Makefile—but it delivers significant performance gains: warm-cache rebuilds are about three times faster, and branch switches are six times faster, though the first build on each branch is about 25% slower while ccache fills its cache.

The patch has been under review since its initial posting by Harald Nordgren. While the core idea is uncontested, two implementation questions remain open. First, Ben Knoble raised concerns about whether the Makefile’s target-specific export of `CCACHE_SLOPPINESS` will propagate correctly to recipe processes—a detail Harald acknowledged but hasn’t resolved. Second, there’s the broader question of whether the Meson build system should also be updated for consistency. Ben noted this would be "nice," but neither he nor Harald pushed for it, and Harald framed it as out of scope.

Patrick Steinhardt recently weighed in, suggesting that the ideal solution would be for Meson itself to handle ccache sloppiness settings upstream, pointing to an open Meson issue on the topic. He didn’t object to the current Makefile-only patch but left the door open for someone else to address the Meson side later. For now, the patch remains focused on the Makefile, and the cross-build-system question is parked as a "nice-to-have" rather than a blocker. No one has raised objections to the patch’s safety or goals, and the only unresolved technical detail is the Makefile semantics, which hasn’t been revisited since Ben’s initial review.
