This is a refactoring series targeting the `git rerere` built-in command. The goal is to modernize its subcommand parsing to match Git’s standard conventions, replacing the current custom logic with the `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism used by other multi-subcommand commands like `git notes` or `git maintenance`.

The series consists of 12 patches, all authored by Patrick Steinhardt. It builds on his earlier `ps/rerere-remove-global-state` topic (merged as b948c10c8b3), which eliminated the global rerere cache state, making this subcommand conversion possible. The patches systematically rename functions, convert flags to enums, drop `the_repository` usage, and turn each existing rerere action ("forget", "clear", "status", "remaining", "diff", "gc") into a proper subcommand with its own usage string. The final patch switches the command-line parser to `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()`, completing the modernization.

Files touched include `builtin/rerere.c` (the bulk of the changes), `rerere.h`, and several callers in `apply.c`, `builtin/am.c`, `builtin/commit.c`, `builtin/merge.c`, `builtin/stash.c`, and `sequencer.c`. The man page (`Documentation/git-rerere.adoc`) and the test suite (`t/t4200-rerere.sh`) receive minor updates to reflect the new interface. No new functionality is introduced; the series is purely internal restructuring.

The motivation is straightforward: Git’s custom subcommand parsing for `rerere` has long been an outlier, and this series brings it in line with the rest of the codebase. The commit messages are clear and the patches appear mechanical, suggesting a low-risk, high-consistency cleanup. There is no indication of controversy or unresolved design questions in the cover letter.

This is a well-scoped refactoring that should interest reviewers familiar with Git’s command-line parsing or the rerere subsystem. The series is self-contained and does not depend on any other ongoing efforts beyond the already-merged global-state removal.
