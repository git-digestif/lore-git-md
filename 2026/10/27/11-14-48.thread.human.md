This thread is about modernizing the command-line parsing in Git’s `test-chmtime` helper, a small but important part of the test infrastructure. Shashank S, an Outreachy intern, is replacing the manual `strcmp()`-based parsing with Git’s standard `parse_options()` API. The change is internal and doesn’t alter the tool’s core functionality, but it does introduce three intentional behavioral tweaks: making `--verbose` and `--get` mutually exclusive, standardizing usage-error exit codes to 129, and rejecting `--get` after a timespec rather than silently ignoring it.

The patch is now in its third iteration (v3 is forthcoming). Junio’s initial review raised concerns about logic duplication and test coverage, both of which were addressed in v2. Cynthia Uche then tested v2 under `NO_RUST=1`, confirmed it builds and passes all new tests, but pointed out two gaps: untested negative time offsets and undocumented behavioral changes. Shashank has committed to fixing both in v3—adding Cynthia’s test snippet for negative offsets and explicitly listing the three behavioral differences in the commit message. The patch touches only a few files (`t/helper/test-chmtime.c`, `t/helper/test-tool.c`, and `Makefile`) and adds no new symbols or user-facing changes. All substantive feedback has been addressed or is slated for v3, so the patch looks uncontroversial and nearly ready to merge.
