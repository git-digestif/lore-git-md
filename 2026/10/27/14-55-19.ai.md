This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Mark C. Chu-Carroll’s v2 series adding degree-of-concern reporting to `git repo structure`. The reviewer, Patrick Steinhardt, engages deeply with the implementation, raising technical and structural concerns while also acknowledging the feature’s inspiration (git-sizer) and potential value.

The review focuses on three main areas:
1. **Commit message and motivation** – Patrick suggests adding a reference to `git-sizer(1)` as the direct inspiration and documenting the empirical methodology behind the default thresholds. This would clarify the feature’s design rationale for future readers.
2. **Code structure and maintainability** – The reviewer finds the current implementation "unwieldy" due to the separation of concern thresholds into three distinct locations (an enum, a threshold array, and the data structures themselves). He proposes consolidating these into a single, central definition (e.g., extending `struct object_data`) to reduce redundancy and improve maintainability. He also flags a potential global variable issue in the config-parsing logic and suggests passing the target structure via the callback.
3. **Technical nitpicks** – Several minor issues are noted: missing `Signed-off-by`, inconsistent spacing in the threshold array, use of `strncmp` instead of `istarts_with` for case-insensitive config key matching, and a redundant loop condition (suggesting `ARRAY_SIZE` instead). He also points out a typo in `sideband.c` (a stray `#define` change unrelated to the patch) that should be dropped.

The review is **not tested** (no mention of running the code) but is **substantive** in its engagement with the design and implementation. The feedback is constructive and actionable, though the author may need to weigh the trade-offs of refactoring the concern threshold logic against the series’ scope. The open question about default thresholds (empirical methodology) remains a potential discussion point for future iterations.
