This thread is about a bugfix for the `@{push}` revision suffix, which previously behaved inconsistently with `git push` in triangular workflows when `push.default=simple`. The issue caused `git status` to omit push-branch comparisons and display misleading hints. The fix, authored by Harald Nordgren, has now been reviewed and approved by Junio C Hamano, who explicitly stated "Will queue," meaning it is ready to be merged into the integration branches (likely `next` first, then `master`).

The series consists of two patches. The first refactors `branch_get_push_1()` to improve readability by extracting the `PUSH_DEFAULT_SIMPLE` logic into a new helper function, `branch_get_push_simple()`. The second patch implements the core fix—a four-line change that aligns `@{push}` resolution with `git push` behavior when the push remote differs from the upstream remote. This ensures `@{push}` resolves to the same-named branch on the push remote, matching `git push`'s behavior. The fix is narrow, well-tested, and includes expanded test coverage in `t/for-each-ref-tests.sh` and `t/t1514-rev-parse-push.sh`.

All feedback has been addressed, including Junio’s request for refactoring, and the series is now complete. The change resolves a long-standing inconsistency in triangular workflows, a common scenario in fork-based development. No regressions or open questions remain, and the series is expected to graduate to `master` after a period of integration testing in `next`.
