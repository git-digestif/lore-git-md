The `git rerere` subcommand modernization series, originally authored by Patrick Steinhardt, has been merged to `master` as of June 25, 2025. The series replaced the command’s custom subcommand-parsing logic with Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism, aligning it with other multi-subcommand built-ins like `git notes` and `git maintenance`. The refactoring was purely internal, with no new functionality, behavior changes, or on-disk format changes, and all prior feedback from versions 1 through 3 was addressed, including the final patch adding per-subcommand usage strings.

The thread has since shifted to a latent safety issue in the `enum rerere_flags` logic, specifically the three-state system formed by `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` and `RERERE_NOAUTOUPDATE`. These flags allow callers to override the configured `rerere.autoUpdate` default (follow default, force autoupdate, or force no-autoupdate), but the consuming code in `rerere_cache_setup()` does not explicitly validate the invalid state where both bits are set. While the CLI parser (`parse_opt_tertiary()`) prevents this, Junio C Hamano noted the lack of validation as a low-priority correctness concern. Patrick’s follow-up confirms that `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` silently takes precedence in such cases, though no caller intentionally passes both flags. The discussion now centers on whether this silent precedence is robust enough or if a `BUG()` or `die()` should be added to catch misuse. No patch has been proposed yet, and the tone suggests this would be a minor follow-up rather than a blocker. The series itself remains complete and merged, with no further revisions expected.
