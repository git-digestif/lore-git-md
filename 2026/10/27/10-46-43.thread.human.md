This thread is Patrick Steinhardt's v5 series, "[PATCH 0/13] Modernize git-rerere's subcommand handling", a purely internal refactoring of `git rerere` that replaces custom subcommand parsing with the standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism. The series picks up directly from v4, which had already landed in 'master', and addresses the post-merge follow-ups Junio C Hamano had flagged: the NULL-termination memory fix in the fallback path, the restored usage synopsis for unknown subcommands, documentation of the intentional `--skip-locked` option-ordering restriction, and removal of stale `git rerere diff` documentation. Beyond those fixes, the series continues the mechanical extraction of each subcommand ("forget", "clear", "status", "remaining", "diff", and now "gc" in patch 10) into dedicated `cmd_rerere_*()` handlers, alongside preparatory cleanups such as renaming `repo_rerere()` to `rerere()`, converting flag macros to a documented `enum rerere_flags`, and removing the `the_repository` global from `builtin/rerere.c`.

The only intentional user-visible change in the whole series is in patch 10: `--skip-locked` is now accepted only after the `gc` subcommand (`git rerere gc --skip-locked`), not before it. The commit message justifies this as safe because the flag was introduced very recently and has not shipped in any release; the main `cmd_rerere()` now uses `PARSE_OPT_KEEP_UNKNOWN_OPT` so subcommand-specific options pass through, and the old manual "requires" validation and its test were removed. A point worth watching for reviewers is that the extracted handlers consistently reject extra arguments with `usage(_("too many arguments"))`, a slight tightening over the old behavior of silently ignoring them.

No objections have been raised against v5 so far; the series remains under review with no merge status claimed. Karthik Nayak had previously given a blanket approval on v4, and Junio's specific corrections are now implemented. What remains open is simply review confirmation of the fixes and the new argument-validation pattern across the subcommand handlers.
