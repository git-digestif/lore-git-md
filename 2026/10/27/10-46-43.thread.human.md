This thread began as a refactoring series to modernize `git rerere`'s subcommand handling, replacing its custom parser with Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism. The series, authored by Patrick Steinhardt, was merged to `master` on June 25, 2025, with no behavior changes or new functionality—until Junio C Hamano identified a post-merge UI regression: the new parser enforces option ordering, breaking the old syntax `git rerere --skip-locked gc` in favor of `git rerere gc --skip-locked`. This could disrupt scripts or user habits, though `--skip-locked` itself is a recent addition and likely has few users.

Patrick has now responded to the regression, framing it as a deliberate trade-off to address what he sees as a design flaw—accepting options that are ignored for most subcommands. He argues that now is the right time to enforce ordering, given `--skip-locked`'s limited adoption, but acknowledges that `--rerere-autoupdate` (a long-standing flag) might warrant grandfathering to preserve backward compatibility. The discussion is open-ended: no patch has been proposed, and Patrick expects pushback on the principle rather than the mechanics. Meanwhile, the thread has also drifted into workflow tooling, with Junio expressing interest in a hypothetical `git fixup` command to streamline his rebase workflow, though this is tangential to the `rerere` regression. The series itself is otherwise complete, with all prior feedback addressed in the final version.
