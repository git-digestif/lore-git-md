This is a policy RFC that revisits Git’s stance on AI-assisted contributions. Scott Chacon responds to Junio C Hamano’s operational concerns by clarifying the intent and practicality of the proposed `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` amendment.

The patch itself remains unchanged: it would allow AI-assisted contributions under strict conditions—human review, testing, DCO sign-off, and an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help. What’s new in this reply is Scott’s effort to address Junio’s skepticism about enforceability and reviewer bandwidth. He argues that the current policy (“do not send in AI-generated slop, period”) is already unenforceable, since unreviewed crap arrives regardless, and that opening the door to responsible AI use won’t worsen the signal-to-noise ratio. He cites his own recent `sha1dc` patch series as an example: though he didn’t hand-craft every line, he invested significant time in testing and benchmarking to ensure correctness.

On the legal front, Scott leans on Red Hat’s interpretation of the DCO—namely, that it doesn’t require every line to be the contributor’s “personal creative expression”—and on the Software Freedom Conservancy’s guidelines, which recommend disclosing the tool, version, and a brief description of its role. He’s open to aligning with the Linux kernel’s more minimalist “LLM” trailer but prefers the SFC’s more detailed approach since Git is an SFC project.

The exchange leaves two key questions unresolved: whether reviewers can realistically assess AI-assisted work, and whether the project should codify a permissive policy or leave the decision to individual contributors. Scott’s tone is pragmatic—he acknowledges Junio’s concerns but frames the change as a necessary update to match peer projects and modern legal guidance. The thread is now squarely focused on whether the proposed text strikes the right balance between openness and enforceability.
