This is a policy discussion reply in the ongoing RFC thread about whether Git should explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions. Brian m. Carlson responds to Scott Chacon’s argument that Git should follow the Software Freedom Conservancy’s (SFC) guidance on AI contributions, clarifying that while the SFC recommends not shunning contributors who submit AI-generated patches, it does not endorse accepting such contributions. Brian argues that Git’s current policy already aligns with the SFC’s recommendation to handle AI submissions graciously, and that accepting AI-generated patches would overwhelm reviewers with low-quality submissions, exacerbating existing bottlenecks in review and security triage.

Brian also highlights a practical concern: unlike projects using pull requests (e.g., Rust), Git’s mailing-list workflow means contributors who reject AI-generated code cannot avoid receiving such patches, as they are CC’d on all submissions. He notes that Git is not obligated to follow SFC guidance uncritically, pointing to QEMU (another SFC project) as precedent for maintaining a restrictive policy. While Brian acknowledges the legal uncertainty around AI-generated contributions, his primary objections are operational—reviewer bandwidth and workflow friction—rather than purely legal or ethical. The reply does not propose changes to the patch but reinforces opposition to it, framing the issue as one of project sustainability rather than principle.
