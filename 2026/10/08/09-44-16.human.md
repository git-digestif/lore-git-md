This is the first patch in a four-part bugfix series addressing a long-standing issue in Git's `reference-transaction` hook: when a branch is renamed or copied (e.g., with `git branch -m`), the hook only sees the deletion of the old ref, not the creation of the new one. The series aims to ensure hooks receive the full transactional picture, which is critical for tools relying on accurate ref updates.

This patch introduces an internal-only transaction flag, `REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL`, to distinguish backend-level operations (like packed-refs updates) from logical user-facing updates. The flag suppresses hook notifications for nested transactions, preventing duplicate or partial reports. The change is minimal—just six lines added to `refs.c` and `refs/refs-internal.h`—and lays the groundwork for the rest of the series by centralizing hook suppression logic in `run_transaction_hook()`.

The patch is well-motivated and cleanly implemented. It addresses a specific technical need (avoiding hook spam for internal operations) without altering public behavior or on-disk formats. The flag is explicitly marked as internal-only, reducing the risk of misuse. This is a refactoring patch that enables the real fix in later patches, but it stands on its own as a sensible cleanup.

No performance impact or test coverage is introduced here, which is appropriate for a preparatory change. The patch is part of a series that has already resolved blocking test failures (in `t0600.16` and `t5510.35`) and is now under active review. If the series lands, this patch will be a small but necessary piece of the puzzle. For now, it’s a low-risk, high-value infrastructure tweak.
