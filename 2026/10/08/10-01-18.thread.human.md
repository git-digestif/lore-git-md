This thread is a patch series from Patrick Steinhardt updating Git's CI configurations to modernize dependencies and simplify workflows. The v2 series, now under review, consists of eight patches addressing feedback from Junio C Hamano, Todd Zullinger, and SZEDER Gábor. Key changes include removing outdated Docker images (e.g., replacing `i386/ubuntu:20.04` with `i386/debian:latest`), dropping Python 2 support, and resolving a coverage trade-off in reftable testing.

The most significant update is to patch 8/8, where the `linux-reftable-leaks` job is being repurposed to test reftable with SHA-256 (`GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` + `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`). Junio C Hamano has now endorsed this approach, preferring SHA-256 coverage over the original leak-checking configuration. This preserves foreign-SCM interoperability (CVS, SVN, Perforce) in the existing `linux-reftable` job while adding SHA-256 testing elsewhere. The only remaining point of contention is the rename of the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-exotic` in patch 5/8, which Gábor objects to as misleading. The rest of the series—including fixes for a typo in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, dead code removal, and updates to the `linux32` and `linux-exotic` jobs—is uncontroversial and ready for integration.

The series reduces CI complexity while maintaining or improving test coverage, with no user-visible changes. A v3 reroll is expected to address the job rename and finalize the SHA-256 testing configuration.
