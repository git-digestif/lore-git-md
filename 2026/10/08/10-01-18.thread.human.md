This patch series from Patrick Steinhardt is now fully ready for integration after its fourth iteration. The goal is to modernize Git’s CI configurations by removing outdated dependencies, eliminating redundant jobs, and updating supporting scripts—all without changing Git’s behavior. The series has addressed all substantive feedback, including a key test design change in patch 1/8 that makes a corner-case archive test work in both SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories, and a naming resolution for the `linux-TEST-vars` job (now `linux-feature-testbed`).

The latest email covers patch 2/8, a simple but important fix for a typo in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` that had disabled breaking-change testing in the Fedora Meson job. The correction is uncontested and purely mechanical, but it highlights broader maintainability issues in the CI scripts, such as the fragility of job-naming conventions and the organic growth of the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag. While Jeff King (Peff) suggested future improvements like wildcard patterns to automate settings (e.g., `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH` for `*-sha256*` jobs), Patrick deferred those to a follow-up, focusing this series on immediate fixes. Junio C Hamano has already backported the third version to Git v2.56.0 for potential `maint` integration, confirming the series is in his pipeline. All patches are now uncontested and pass CI runs on both GitHub and GitLab.
