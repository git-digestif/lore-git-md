This thread is a patch series from Patrick Steinhardt aimed at modernizing Git's CI configurations by removing outdated dependencies, redundant jobs, and simplifying supporting scripts. The series, now in its initial v1 submission, consists of eight patches touching GitHub Actions, GitLab CI workflows, and related scripts.

The key changes include dropping unused jobs like "linux-clang," updating the 32-bit platform image from an unsupported Ubuntu 20.04 to Debian's latest, renaming the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-exotic` for clarity, removing Python 2 coverage, and fixing a typo in the Fedora Meson job. Most of these changes are uncontroversial and focus on reducing complexity without altering user-visible behavior.

However, Junio C Hamano raised a substantive issue with patch 8/8, which removes the `linux-reftable` job. This job was the only one exercising reftable with foreign-SCM interoperability tests (CVS, SVN, Perforce), and its removal creates a coverage gap. In response, Patrick proposed repurposing the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test reftable with SHA-256 instead, arguing this trade-off is more valuable. This shift introduces a new question: which coverage is more important—SHA-256 + reftable or foreign-SCM interoperability? The outcome will likely depend on feedback from contributors invested in reftable or SHA-256. The rest of the series is ready for integration, pending resolution of this issue.
