This patch series from Patrick Steinhardt updates Git's CI configurations to modernize dependencies and simplify workflows, now in its second iteration. The v2 series addresses feedback from Junio C Hamano and Todd Zullinger, most notably reworking patch 8/8 to preserve foreign-SCM interoperability test coverage for reftable while adding SHA-256 coverage by repurposing the `linux-reftable-leaks` job. This resolves Junio's earlier concern about losing CVS, SVN, and Perforce testing but introduces a trade-off that may still need broader discussion.

The series remains focused on housekeeping: removing dead "linux-clang" logic, switching the 32-bit job to a supported Debian image, renaming the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-exotic` for clarity, updating the exotic job to use the latest Ubuntu image, and dropping Python 2 coverage. The changes are mechanical and unlikely to be controversial, though the SHA-256 coverage decision in patch 8/8 could draw attention from contributors working on reftable or SHA-256. The net effect is a reduction in CI complexity (19 insertions, 39 deletions) while maintaining or improving test coverage.

Junio and Todd provided surface-level feedback on commit messages and factual accuracy, which Patrick has addressed. The series is now under review for the technical trade-offs in test coverage, particularly whether the project prefers SHA-256 testing over foreign-SCM interoperability for reftable. No maintainer decisions have been made yet, and the series is still cooking in the review phase.
