This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s v3 patch series updating Git’s CI configurations to modernize and simplify the build system. The series removes outdated dependencies, eliminates redundant jobs, and updates supporting scripts without changing Git’s behavior. Key participants include Junio C Hamano (the maintainer), Jeff King (Peff), and SZEDER Gábor, whose reviews have shaped the series across three versions.

The series is now at v3, with most patches uncontested and ready for integration. The main open question revolved around patch 8/8, which repurposed the `linux-reftable-leaks` job as `linux-reftable-sha256` to test reftable with SHA-256. Jeff King raised a substantive concern, questioning whether this job was redundant given that the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job already covers SHA-256 via the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag. Patrick has now clarified that the breaking-changes job does *not* test reftable, as it defaults to the traditional `files` backend even when SHA-256 is enabled. This distinction justifies the new job’s existence, as it ensures coverage for the SHA-256 + reftable combination, which is not tested elsewhere. The explanation appears to address Peff’s concern, though further feedback may determine whether the job is retained or adjusted.

Other notable changes in v3 include renaming the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-feature-testbed` (resolving a naming dispute), removing Python 2 logic (now obsolete), updating the 32-bit platform image to Debian (ensuring long-term support), and deleting dead code left behind by removed jobs. The series reduces CI complexity while maintaining or improving coverage, with all changes passing linked CI runs. The only remaining loose end is a minor test design discussion in patch 1/8, where Peff proposed making a test self-contained by creating a temporary SHA-1 repository. Patrick tentatively agreed but has not yet rerolled the patch. The series is otherwise uncontested and appears close to integration.
