This is a **feature proposal** for `git branch` that extends `--delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The email is a short reply in an ongoing discussion about whether the new behavior should be enabled by default and whether users should have a way to disable it.

Harald Nordgren pushes back gently on Phillip Wood’s suggestion that the feature should be opt-in due to performance concerns. He argues that the usability gain — automatically cleaning up branches that were squash-merged or rebased — justifies a small performance hit, since `git branch --delete-merged` is not a high-frequency command. He prefers the feature to be on by default, with a flag to turn it off if needed, rather than requiring users to opt in.

He also suggests that in partial or shallow clones, where the feature cannot search the full history, Git should warn the user and offer to unshallow the repository. This keeps the feature working as much as possible while being transparent about its limitations. The tone is pragmatic: usability first, with reasonable fallbacks for edge cases. The discussion remains unresolved, but Harald’s position is clear: he wants the feature to work out-of-the-box for most users.
