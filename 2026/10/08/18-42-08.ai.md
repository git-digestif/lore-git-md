This is a **UX and performance trade-off discussion** in the ongoing thread about extending `` `git branch --delete-merged` `` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. Harald Nordgren responds to Phillip Wood’s argument that squash detection will always be slower than the current tip-reachability check, especially in partial clones.

Harald acknowledges the performance trade-off but argues that the usability improvement justifies enabling the feature by default, with an opt-out flag rather than an opt-in. He suggests that since `` `git branch --delete-merged` `` is not a high-frequency command, a modest slowdown is acceptable for most users. For partial or shallow clones, he proposes warning users that detection may be incomplete and offering to unshallow the repository, rather than disabling the feature entirely.

The exchange sharpens the design tension: Phillip Wood advocates for a way to disable patch-id-based detection (e.g., via a CLI option or config flag) to accommodate performance-sensitive workflows, while Harald prefers a single, unified behavior that works out-of-the-box. No new technical details are introduced, but the discussion now centers on whether the feature should default to "on" with an opt-out, or require explicit user action to enable the slower detection method. The unresolved question remains how to balance simplicity with practicality for large or incomplete repositories.
