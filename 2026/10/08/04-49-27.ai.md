This is a policy discussion reply in the ongoing thread about whether Git should explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. Chacon cites Linux’s policy as precedent, noting that Linus Torvalds has accepted AI-assisted contributions under the same DCO and license, and suggests that Git’s current ambiguity is more restrictive than necessary.

The reply does not introduce new technical details or modify the proposed patch; it remains focused on policy justification. Chacon acknowledges the rarity of complex code reproduction from training sets but does not address brian’s specific concern about omitted copyright notices in AI-generated output. Instead, he frames the debate as a choice between Git’s current conservative stance and the more permissive policies adopted by larger projects with comparable legal exposure. The reference to the Contributor Summit discussion and Taylor Blau’s apparent role in synthesizing a Linux-style position suggests that this thread is part of a broader, project-sanctioned exploration of the issue. The tone is conciliatory but firm, positioning the patch as a reasoned middle ground rather than a radical departure.
