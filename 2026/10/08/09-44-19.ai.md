This is the final patch (4/4) in a well-motivated bugfix series that removes backend-specific copy and rename callbacks from Git’s ref backends. The series as a whole fixes a long-standing issue where `reference-transaction` hooks were not seeing the full picture of branch rename (`git branch -m`) and copy (`git branch -c`) operations—hooks only received the deletion of the old ref, not the creation of the new one, breaking tools that rely on transactional ref updates.

Patch 4/4 deletes 656 lines of now-unused code: the `rename_ref` and `copy_ref` callbacks from the files, reftable, and debug backends, along with their private helpers (e.g., `TMP_RENAMED_LOG`, `rename_tmp_log_callback`, `write_copy_table`). The patch is mechanical and uncontroversial; the real work was done in earlier patches (1-3), which introduced the transaction API infrastructure (`REF_TRANSACTION_FLAG_INTERNAL`, `ref_transaction_replace_reflog()`) and integrated copy/rename into ordinary transactions using per-ref-update flags (`REF_COPY_SOURCE`, `REF_NEEDS_PACK`) and a `copy_from` field in `struct ref_update`. This patch simply cleans up the backend-specific logic that is no longer needed.

The files touched are `refs/debug.c`, `refs/files-backend.c`, `refs/packed-backend.c`, `refs/refs-internal.h`, and `refs/reftable-backend.c`. The patch preserves the architectural improvements from v3 (per-ref state, centralized hook suppression) and resolves the test breakages Junio C Hamano reported in v3 (`t0600.16` and `t5510.35`). The series now passes the full test suite on macOS with both files and reftable backends, and the performance regressions (~137% for files-based rename with large reflogs, ~20-25% for reftable) are deemed acceptable for correctness.

Patch 1/4 (the infrastructure patch) is already uncontroversial and ready to proceed. This patch (4/4) is the cleanup step that follows naturally from the earlier work. The series is under active review, with no blocking objections remaining; expect it to land in `next` soon if no further regressions surface. The author (Maciej Ciemborowicz) has incorporated feedback from Karthik Nayak and addressed Junio’s test breakage concerns, and the architectural direction has Patrick Steinhardt’s implicit approval.
