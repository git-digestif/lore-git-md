This is a **licensing and policy clarification** from Sam Reis in the ongoing `sha1dc-accel` thread. Reis, the original author of the Rust `sha1dc` crate whose work Scott Chacon ported to C, responds to D. Ben Knoble’s questions about license compatibility between Git’s GPL-2 codebase and the MIT-licensed collision-detection code.

Reis confirms that the unavoidable-bit-conditions (UBC) check tables in his implementation are **fully autogenerated** by a custom generator he wrote with LLM assistance, based on the 2017 Stevens-Shumow paper. He included Stevens and Shumow in the copyright notice as a precaution, though the code itself is not a direct derivative of their work. On licensing, Reis cites GPL-MIT compatibility (MIT is GPL-compatible) and suggests that correct attribution should suffice, avoiding the need for "license-washing" the new `sha1dc-accel` code to GPL-2. He does not address Knoble’s question about whether Git’s packaging (e.g., Gentoo’s GPL-2-only claim) should reflect the mixed-license reality.

The message does not introduce new technical objections or optimizations, nor does it resolve the licensing question—it merely restates the compatibility argument. The thread’s earlier technical and provenance concerns (Schindelin’s optimizations, Junio’s AI policy queries, Reis’s endorsement) are now settled in principle, but Knoble’s licensing question remains open. Reis’s response leans toward maintaining the MIT license for the new code, aligning with the existing `sha1dc` submodule, but the project has not yet formalized a stance on mixed-license components. This exchange is unlikely to block the series on its own, but it may prompt a maintainer ruling or broader discussion before integration.
