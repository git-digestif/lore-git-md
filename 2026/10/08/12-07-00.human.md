This is the fourth patch in Mirko Faina’s RFC series introducing "precious files" to Git. It teaches `add_pattern()` to reject precious-file patterns in contexts where they don’t belong—specifically, the `$GIT_DIR/info/sparse-checkout` file.

The patch adds a new `disable_precious` flag to `add_pattern()` and its callers (`add_patterns_from_buffer()`, `add_patterns_from_file_to_list()`, etc.). When this flag is set, any pattern starting with `$` (the precious-file marker) triggers a warning and is skipped. The sparse-checkout built-in is the first consumer: it now passes `PATTERN_DISALLOW_PRECIOUS` when reading its patterns, so a `.git/info/sparse-checkout` line like `$/build` is rejected with a clear warning instead of being silently accepted.

The change is small (39 lines) and mechanical: every call site that once passed four arguments to `add_pattern()` now passes five, and the sparse-checkout code is the only place that sets the new flag. A new test in `t1091-sparse-checkout-builtin.sh` verifies the warning is emitted. The patch is clearly preparatory; without the rest of the series it does nothing visible, but it plugs a potential foot-gun once precious files are live.
