This is a policy discussion reply in the ongoing debate about whether Git should explicitly permit AI-assisted contributions. Kristoffer Haugsbakk, a frequent documentation contributor, interjects with a sharp metaphorical critique of Scott Chacon’s analogy between human and AI-generated contributions. He argues that the two are fundamentally different—comparing them is like regulating submarines and human swimmers under the same rules just because both operate in water. The reply is brief but pointed, framing the debate as a category error rather than a policy nuance.

Haugsbakk also notes that corporations already use legal tools (like non-compete clauses) to control how employees apply their knowledge, implying that Git’s policy should similarly account for the unique risks of AI-generated code. He dismisses the idea that individual contributors should bear sole responsibility for avoiding copyright risk, calling it a "prerogative" rather than a sound legal or ethical framework. The tone is skeptical, even dismissive, and the reply does not engage with the technical or procedural details of the proposed `Assisted-by:` trailer or DCO requirements. Instead, it challenges the foundational premise of Chacon’s argument: that AI assistance can be treated as just another form of human collaboration.

The reply does not advance the discussion toward a resolution, but it does crystallize a key philosophical objection that has surfaced repeatedly in the thread: AI-generated contributions are not merely a new tool for human contributors, but a qualitatively different source of risk—one that existing legal and social structures (like the DCO) may not be equipped to handle. Whether this objection will gain traction remains unclear, but Haugsbakk’s intervention signals that the debate is far from settled.
