Subject: [PATCH 0/8] ci: housekeeping updates for CI configurations
Series version: v3 (reroll addressing Junio C Hamano, SZEDER Gábor, Jeff King, and Icelandic locale resolution)
Originating author: Patrick Steinhardt
Thread type: patch series (CI/build system maintenance)

Core problem or goal
Modernize and simplify Git's CI configurations by removing outdated dependencies (EOL Docker images, Python 2 coverage), eliminating redundant or unused jobs, and updating supporting scripts. The series targets GitHub Actions and GitLab CI workflows, along with minor test adjustments, to reduce complexity and improve reliability without introducing new functionality or behavioral changes. The scope includes a resolved trade-off between reftable's foreign-SCM interoperability coverage and SHA-256 + reftable coverage, following Junio C Hamano's review of v1, and a now-resolved naming dispute over the `linux-TEST-vars` job rename to `linux-feature-testbed`.

Current status
Under review (v3 posted). The series consists of 8 patches touching `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, `ci/install-dependencies.sh`, `ci/lib.sh`, `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, and `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`. All substantive technical concerns have been resolved, and the series is now uncontested. Key changes from v2:
- **Naming resolution**: The `linux-TEST-vars` job is renamed to `linux-feature-testbed` (Junio C Hamano's preferred choice among 14 alternatives), resolving the dispute raised by SZEDER Gábor in v2. The rename is purely mechanical with no behavioral impact. Jeff King (Peff) has explicitly endorsed the new name.
- **Reftable coverage**: The `linux-reftable-leaks` job is repurposed as `linux-reftable-sha256` to test reftable with SHA-256 (`GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`), while the `linux-reftable` job preserves foreign-SCM interoperability coverage. The justification for the new job is now **fully resolved**: the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job does not change the default ref storage backend from `files` to `reftable`, meaning it tests SHA-256 with the traditional files backend, not with reftable. The inconsistency stems from the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s organic growth (it predates reftable’s inclusion as a breaking change) and the hard-coded `files` backend in `t/test-lib.sh`. While both Patrick Steinhardt and Peff acknowledge this is suboptimal, they defer a broader redesign ("punt it to another day"), prioritizing immediate test coverage over architectural purity. The discussion seeds a potential future effort to rationalize how breaking changes are tested in CI, but no objections remain to the current patch.
- **Dead code removal**: Patch 3/8 deletes unused logic in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` left behind by the removed `linux-clang` job (commit d88d727143, 2023-06-01). The deletion is purely mechanical with no behavioral impact.
- **Python 2 removal**: Patch 7/8 hard-codes `python3` in `ci/install-dependencies.sh` and `ci/lib.sh`, removing conditional Python 2 logic now that Ubuntu 20.04 jobs are gone. The change is uncontested and has no behavioral impact.
- **Test design update**: Patch 1/8, which adds the `SHA1` prerequisite to `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`, remains under discussion. Jeff King (Peff) proposed an alternative approach: making the test self-contained by creating a temporary SHA-1 repository (`git init --object-format=sha1`) for the test’s duration, allowing it to run in any environment that meets the other prerequisites (`EXPENSIVE`, `LONG_IS_64BIT`, `UNZIP`, etc.). Patrick Steinhardt tentatively agreed with Peff’s suggestion ("Hmm. Yeah, I guess that wouldn't hurt."), but no reroll has been posted yet. The current implementation (adding the `SHA1` prerequisite) remains the baseline.
- **Typo fix and future-proofing**: Patch 2/8 corrects `fedora-breaking-changes-musl` to `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, restoring the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag for the Fedora Meson job. Jeff King (Peff) suggests broadening the `case` statement pattern to `*breaking-changes*` to guard against similar typos in future job additions. Patrick Steinhardt has acknowledged the technical merit of this suggestion and explicitly deferred it to a follow-up patch, citing broader structural issues in the CI scripts (duplication, unclear separation between `ci/lib.sh` and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`). The discussion now explicitly references a prior, abandoned patch that merged the two scripts and adopted similar pattern-matching improvements, and both Peff and Patrick have grouped `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`).
- **32-bit platform update**: Patch 4/8 replaces `i386/ubuntu:20.04` with `i386/debian:latest` for the `linux32` job, ensuring support until at least 2030 and simplifying dependency logic. Jeff King’s concern about Icelandic locale (`is_IS`) support in the new image is resolved: Debian’s `locales-all` package provides the necessary locale data, making the removed Ubuntu-specific logic redundant.
- **Image updates**: Patch 6/8 updates the `linux-feature-testbed` job to use `ubuntu:latest` (dropping the explicit `gcc-8` dependency), while other jobs retain coverage for older distributions. The change is uncontested and ready for integration.
- Net effect: 20 insertions, 39 deletions, reducing CI complexity while maintaining or improving coverage. All changes are mechanical and pass linked CI runs (GitLab, GitHub).

Key technical details
Files/subsystems: CI configurations (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI), supporting scripts (`ci/`), and one test file (`t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`).
Symbols/configs: Job names (`linux-TEST-vars` -> `linux-feature-testbed`, `linux-reftable-leaks` -> `linux-reftable-sha256`), Docker images (`i386/ubuntu:20.04` -> `i386/debian:latest`, `ubuntu:20.04` -> `ubuntu:latest`), scripts (`ci/lib.sh`, `ci/install-dependencies.sh`, `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`), Python-related variables (`PYTHON_PACKAGE` removed, `PYTHON_PATH` hard-coded to `/usr/bin/python3`), test prerequisites (`SHA1` added to `t/t5004`), the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, and the `LANGUAGES` variable in `ci/install-dependencies.sh` (now confirmed to support Icelandic locale via Debian’s `locales-all` package). The `linux-feature-testbed` job now uses `ubuntu:latest` and the system-provided GCC version. Patch 3/8 removes dead code in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` that set `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha1` for the removed `linux-clang` job. Patch 7/8 hard-codes `python3` in `ci/install-dependencies.sh` and simplifies `ci/lib.sh` by removing the Ubuntu-20.04-specific branch for `MAKEFLAGS`. Patch 8/8 introduces `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable` for the `linux-reftable-sha256` job. The typo fix (s/musl/meson/) in patch 2/8 restores the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag for the Fedora Meson job. The `linux-reftable-sha256` job now provides unique coverage for the SHA-256 + reftable combination, as the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job tests SHA-256 with the traditional files backend due to `t/test-lib.sh` hard-coding `files` as the default ref storage backend unless explicitly overridden. The `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s current scope excludes reftable despite its disruptive nature, an inconsistency stemming from organic growth. The test in `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh` verifies Git’s ability to handle ZIP archives with objects larger than 4GB, requiring 64-bit `long` support, unzip with ZIP64 extensions, and the `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite; currently, only the Fedora CI job meets these requirements. Peff’s proposed alternative for patch 1/8 involves creating a temporary SHA-1 repository (`git init --object-format=sha1`) for the test’s duration, making the test self-contained and independent of the CI environment’s default hash algorithm. The discussion around patch 2/8 now identifies `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`) to reduce duplication and future-proof the scripts.

Open questions and loose ends

- **CI script structure**: The discussion around patch 2/8 now explicitly frames the `*breaking-changes*` wildcard pattern as part of a larger refactoring effort to address broader structural issues in the CI scripts, including duplication between `ci/lib.sh` and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` and the potential for consolidation. Patrick Steinhardt and Peff have grouped `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`) to automate settings (e.g., `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH`) and reduce future maintenance burden. Both agree to defer this refactoring to a follow-up patch, leaving the current patch unchanged. The discussion references a prior, abandoned patch that merged the two scripts and adopted similar pattern-matching improvements, suggesting that future work may revisit this effort.
- **Breaking changes job**: The restored `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job may now expose latent test failures or serve as a foundation for future experimental builds. The discussion about the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s behavior—specifically, whether it should enable reftable by default—remains open-ended but deferred. Patrick Steinhardt explicitly leaves the door open for revisiting the `linux-reftable-sha256` job’s necessity if the flag’s logic is ever refactored.
