Subject: [PATCH 0/8] ci: housekeeping updates for CI configurations
Series version: v4 (final, uncontested; Junio C Hamano has marked the series for inclusion in `next`)
Originating author: Patrick Steinhardt
Thread type: patch series (CI/build system maintenance)

Core problem or goal
Modernize and simplify Git's CI configurations by removing outdated dependencies (EOL Docker images, Python 2 coverage), eliminating redundant or unused jobs, and updating supporting scripts. The series targets GitHub Actions and GitLab CI workflows, along with minor test adjustments, to reduce complexity and improve reliability without introducing new functionality or behavioral changes. The scope includes resolved trade-offs between reftable's foreign-SCM interoperability coverage and SHA-256 + reftable coverage, and a now-resolved naming dispute over the `linux-TEST-vars` job rename to `linux-feature-testbed`. An emerging sub-goal is to address the *accidental* disabling of Rust in CI for the past six months due to a leftover `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` flag tied to a removed job, with Junio C Hamano advocating for an immediate fix to restore intentional coverage of the non-Rust build path.

Current status
**Marked for integration in `next`** (Junio C Hamano's latest email explicitly states the series is ready for inclusion in `next`). The series consists of 8 patches touching `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, `ci/install-dependencies.sh`, `ci/lib.sh`, `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, and `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`. All substantive technical concerns have been resolved, and Jeff King (Peff) has explicitly endorsed the final version as ready for integration. Key changes from v3:
- **Test design resolution**: Patch 1/8 now makes the test in `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh` work in both SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories by creating a temporary SHA-1 repository (`git init --object-format=sha1`) for the test’s duration, replacing the v3 approach that added a `SHA1` prerequisite. This addresses Jeff King's (Peff) feedback about long-term maintainability and makes the test self-contained. The change is uncontested and ready for integration.
- **Naming resolution**: The `linux-TEST-vars` job is renamed to `linux-feature-testbed` (Junio C Hamano's preferred choice among 14 alternatives), resolving the dispute raised by SZEDER Gábor in v2. The rename is purely mechanical with no behavioral impact. Jeff King (Peff) has explicitly endorsed the new name. Patch 5/8 implements this rename across `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`; the patch is uncontested and ready for integration.
- **Reftable coverage**: The `linux-reftable-leaks` job is repurposed as `linux-reftable-sha256` to test reftable with SHA-256 (`GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`), while the `linux-reftable` job preserves foreign-SCM interoperability coverage. The justification for the new job is now fully resolved: the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job does not change the default ref storage backend from `files` to `reftable`, meaning it tests SHA-256 with the traditional files backend, not with reftable. The inconsistency stems from the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s organic growth (it predates reftable’s inclusion as a breaking change) and the hard-coded `files` backend in `t/test-lib.sh`. While both Patrick Steinhardt and Peff acknowledge this is suboptimal, they defer a broader redesign ("punt it to another day"), prioritizing immediate test coverage over architectural purity. The discussion seeds a potential future effort to rationalize how breaking changes are tested in CI, but no objections remain to the current patch.
- **Python 2 removal**: Patch 7/8 hard-codes `python3` in `ci/install-dependencies.sh` and `ci/lib.sh`, removing conditional Python 2 logic now that Ubuntu 20.04 jobs are gone. The change is uncontested, has no behavioral impact, and is ready for integration. The commit message explicitly states that Python 2 reached EOL in 2020, no supported CI distro still packages it, and the world has moved to Python 3. The patch removes the `PYTHON_PACKAGE` variable, hard-codes `PYTHON_PATH` to `/usr/bin/python3`, and simplifies the `ubuntu-*` case in `ci/lib.sh` by removing the Ubuntu-20.04-specific branch for `MAKEFLAGS`.
- **Typo fix**: Patch 2/8 corrects `fedora-breaking-changes-musl` to `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`, restoring the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag for the Fedora Meson job. Jeff King (Peff) suggests broadening the `case` statement pattern to `*breaking-changes*` to guard against similar typos in future job additions. Patrick Steinhardt has acknowledged the technical merit of this suggestion and explicitly deferred it to a follow-up patch, citing broader structural issues in the CI scripts (duplication, unclear separation between `ci/lib.sh` and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`). The discussion now explicitly references a prior, abandoned patch that merged the two scripts and adopted similar pattern-matching improvements, and both Peff and Patrick have grouped `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`).
- **32-bit platform update**: Patch 4/8 replaces `i386/ubuntu:20.04` with `i386/debian:latest` for the `linux32` job, ensuring support until at least 2030 and simplifying dependency logic. Jeff King’s concern about Icelandic locale (`is_IS`) support in the new image is resolved: Debian’s `locales-all` package provides the necessary locale data, making the removed Ubuntu-specific logic redundant. The patch is uncontested and ready for integration.
- **Dead code removal**: Patch 3/8 deletes unused logic in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` left behind by the removed `linux-clang` job (commit d88d727143, 2023-06-01). The deletion is purely mechanical with no behavioral impact. **Latent issue surfaced**: Kaartic Sivaraam’s review reply identifies that the `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` flag was retained for the now-removed `linux-clang` job, meaning Rust has been *accidentally* disabled in CI for the past six months (since Rust was enabled by default in April 2026 via commit 32d5b90590). Junio C Hamano now proposes addressing this in the current series by adding `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` to either the `linux-feature-testbed` or `linux32` job, framing it as a small, temporary fix to restore intentional coverage of the non-Rust build path until Rust becomes mandatory. Patrick Steinhardt had previously acknowledged the issue but preferred to defer it to a follow-up series; the discussion is now focused on whether to include this change now or later. No technical objections exist; the decision hinges on logistical alignment with the series' goals.
- **Image updates**: Patch 6/8 updates the `linux-feature-testbed` job to use `ubuntu:latest` (dropping the explicit `gcc-8` dependency), while other jobs retain coverage for older distributions. The change is uncontested and ready for integration. Peff’s review of v3 called the commit message "nicely explained" and affirmed the direction without raising technical concerns.
- Net effect: 43 insertions, 56 deletions, reducing CI complexity while maintaining or improving coverage. All changes are mechanical and pass linked CI runs (GitLab, GitHub).

Patch 3/8 removes dead code in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` that set `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha1` for the removed `linux-clang` job. **Latent issue and proposed fix**: The `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` flag was retained for a job that no longer existed, meaning Rust has been *accidentally* disabled in CI for the past six months (since Rust was enabled by default in April 2026 via commit 32d5b90590). Junio C Hamano proposes adding `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` to either the `linux-feature-testbed` or `linux32` job in the current series to restore intentional coverage of the non-Rust build path. The fix is framed as small and temporary, with no behavioral impact beyond ensuring the non-Rust code path is tested. Patch 7/8 hard-codes `python3` in `ci/install-dependencies.sh` and simplifies `ci/lib.sh` by removing the Ubuntu-20.04-specific branch for `MAKEFLAGS`. Patch 8/8 introduces `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable` for the `linux-reftable-sha256` job. The typo fix (s/musl/meson/) in patch 2/8 restores the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag for the Fedora Meson job. The `linux-reftable-sha256` job now provides unique coverage for the SHA-256 + reftable combination, as the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job tests SHA-256 with the traditional files backend due to `t/test-lib.sh` hard-coding `files` as the default ref storage backend unless explicitly overridden. The `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s current scope excludes reftable despite its disruptive nature, an inconsistency stemming from organic growth. The test in `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh` verifies Git’s ability to handle ZIP archives with objects larger than 4GB, requiring 64-bit `long` support, unzip with ZIP64 extensions, and the `EXPENSIVE` prerequisite; it now creates a temporary SHA-1 repository (`git init --object-format=sha1`) for its duration, making the test self-contained and independent of the CI environment’s default hash algorithm. The discussion around patch 2/8 now identifies `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`) to reduce duplication and future-proof the scripts.

Open questions and loose ends
- **CI script structure**: The discussion around patch 2/8 explicitly frames the `*breaking-changes*` wildcard pattern as part of a larger refactoring effort to address broader structural issues in the CI scripts, including duplication between `ci/lib.sh` and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` and the potential for consolidation. Patrick Steinhardt and Peff have grouped `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related CI job categories that would benefit from structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`) to automate settings (e.g., `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH`) and reduce future maintenance burden. Both agree to defer this refactoring to a follow-up patch, leaving the current patch unchanged. The discussion references a prior, abandoned patch that merged the two scripts and adopted similar pattern-matching improvements, suggesting that future work may revisit this effort.
- **Breaking changes design**: The resolution of patch 8/8 seeds a potential future effort to rationalize how breaking changes are tested in CI. The `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s current behavior (enabling SHA-256 but not reftable) is acknowledged as suboptimal, but both Peff and Patrick defer redesign to a follow-up, prioritizing immediate coverage. This could impact the necessity of the `linux-reftable-sha256` job if the flag’s scope is expanded to include reftable.
- **Rust coverage in CI**: The latent issue of Rust being *accidentally* disabled in CI for six months due to a leftover `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` flag is now an **explicit open question** for the current series. Junio C Hamano proposes addressing it in the current series by adding `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` to either the `linux-feature-testbed` or `linux32` job, framing it as a small, temporary fix to restore intentional coverage of the non-Rust build path until Rust becomes mandatory. Patrick Steinhardt had previously preferred to defer this to a follow-up series, but the discussion is now focused on whether to include it now or later. No technical objections exist; the decision hinges on logistical alignment with the series' goals. Key technical details for future reference: the `NO_RUST` flag, the `linux-feature-testbed` and `linux32` jobs, and the April 2026 Rust-enablement commit (32d5b90590).
