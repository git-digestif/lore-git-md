This is a documentation patch series (v2) that converts Git’s `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage (`gitbreaking-changes(7)`). The goal is to make upcoming breaking changes more visible to end users, who are most affected but least likely to follow development discussions. The series is now ready for integration after resolving all review feedback.

The v2 series consists of five patches. The first two split the conversion into logical steps: the first prepares the document for manpage format, and the second renames it to `gitbreaking-changes.adoc` while preserving the original `BreakingChanges.adoc` as a pointer. The third patch replaces commit-message-style message-IDs with clickable URLs (e.g., `https://lore.kernel.org/git/xmqqa59i45wc.fsf@gitster.g/`), addressing rendering issues with `asciidoc` and `asciidoctor`. The fourth adds a note clarifying the document’s living-draft nature, and the fifth moves the "Adding new items" discussion to the end for better readability. The build system (`Makefile`, `meson.build`) and `command-list.txt` are updated to include the new manpage.

The series is technically sound and aligns with reviewer feedback. The interdiff shows minor adjustments: dropping the `git(1)` mention (due to a parallel topic removing guide references) and fixing unintended whitespace changes. The author notes two merge conflicts with topics in `master` (`ps/ref-storage-format`) and `seen` (`bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase`), which will require moving their additions from `BreakingChanges.adoc` to `gitbreaking-changes.adoc`.

Key files touched: `Documentation/gitbreaking-changes.adoc` (new), `Documentation/BreakingChanges.adoc` (retained as a pointer), `Documentation/git.txt`, `command-list.txt`, `Makefile`, and `meson.build`. No new symbols, CLI options, or on-disk format changes are introduced. The series is documentation-only and requires no test coverage.

The motivation remains clear: breaking changes are currently buried in a plain-text document, while a manpage ensures discoverability via `git help breaking-changes` or `man gitbreaking-changes`. The RFC tag has been dropped, signaling readiness for integration. The author’s attention to detail (e.g., handling URL encoding edge cases) suggests a well-crafted contribution.
