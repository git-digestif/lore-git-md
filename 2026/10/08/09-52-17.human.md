This is the first patch in a five-part bugfix series (v3) that plugs a data-loss race in `git repack -d`. The series replaces the racy `--honor-pack-keep` mechanism with a snapshot-based approach; this patch fixes a long-standing behavioral inconsistency in `git pack-objects --stdin-packs=follow` that becomes critical once the snapshot mechanism is in place.

The bug: when `--stdin-packs=follow` is used together with `--keep-pack`, the kept pack is incorrectly treated as a closed boundary (`^` pack), so the traversal stops at its objects. In reality, the caller only said “don’t repack this pack,” not “this pack is self-contained.” If the kept pack holds a commit but not its tree or blob, those objects are never rescued, and the resulting pack lacks closure, causing later bitmap writes to fail.

The fix: in follow mode, kept packs are now marked as “kept-open” (`!` packs) instead of “kept-closed” (`^` packs). The traversal skips the pack’s objects but can still walk through them to pick up any dependencies that live in other packs. The change aligns the in-core flag with the on-disk `.keep` semantics, which never were a reachability boundary.

Code impact: a single helper `add_extra_kept_packs()` gains a `stdin_packs` parameter and a conditional branch that sets `pack_keep_in_core_open` instead of `pack_keep_in_core` when in follow mode. The test `t/t5331-pack-objects-stdin.sh` adds a scenario that reproduces the bug—commit B in a kept pack, its tree and blob in an excluded pack—and confirms the fix includes the tree and blob in the output pack.

The patch is clean, well-explained, and comes with a targeted test. It is a necessary stepping stone for the snapshot-based repack fix that follows, and it does not change any existing user-visible behavior—only the internal traversal logic. No objections have been raised so far.
