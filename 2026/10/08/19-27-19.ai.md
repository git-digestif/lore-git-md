This is the fourth patch in Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s five-patch v2 series converting Git’s `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage (`gitbreaking-changes(7)`). The series is now technically complete and ready for integration after addressing all reviewer feedback.

This patch adds a short admonition (NOTE block) to the new manpage, clarifying that the document is a *living draft* and may have changed since the release the reader is using. The motivation is to preempt confusion from users who might read an outdated version and post feedback about decisions already revised in the current development cycle. The example in the commit message—someone on Debian Stable reading about Git 3.0 changes and complaining about them two years later—illustrates the real-world risk. The note is concise and avoids prescribing how to fetch the latest source, keeping the focus on awareness rather than mechanics.

The change is minimal: ten lines added to `Documentation/gitbreaking-changes.adoc`, with no build system or cross-reference updates. It directly addresses a usability concern raised during review and aligns with the series’ goal of making breaking changes more discoverable *and* actionable for end users. The patch is uncontroversial and unlikely to draw further discussion; it is a straightforward improvement to the manpage’s clarity. Merge conflicts with `ps/ref-storage-format` and `bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase` (noted in the cover letter) will require moving their additions from the old `BreakingChanges.adoc` to this file during integration.
