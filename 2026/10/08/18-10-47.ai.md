This is a philosophical commentary on the AI-assisted contributions policy RFC, not a technical review or a direct counterproposal. D. Ben Knoble steps outside the immediate policy debate to share historical and sociological perspectives on tools and their impact on human work. He links to several blog posts (his own) that explore themes from Sherry Turkle’s *Alone Together*, the Mechanical Turk, and recent research on how developers actually use LLMs in practice. The core message is that tools are never neutral—they shape both the work and the worker—and therefore the choice to adopt AI assistance should not be justified by treating it as "just another tool."

Knoble then pivots to the policy discussion, quoting a polemical essay ("AI Pascal’s Wager") that argues the safest course is to reject all AI-generated contributions now, because the potential long-term harm (project abandonment) outweighs any short-term benefit. He aligns with the essay’s conclusion: if Git must eventually allow AI assistance, it is better to regret acting too late than to regret acting too soon. The tone is cautious and values-driven rather than technical or legal; it frames the policy question as a moral and social choice about the kind of project Git wants to be. No new operational or legal concerns are raised, but the email adds a layer of philosophical weight to the existing objections from brian m. carlson and Luca Milanesio.
