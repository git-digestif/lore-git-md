This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about a `contrib/` helper for incremental rebase conflict resolution, specifically the `git-bisect-rebase` script. Alejandro Colomar responds to Phillip Wood’s proposal to extend `git rebase --update-refs` by demonstrating how his multi-shot workflow handles complex dependency graphs—something he argues cannot be automated safely or flexibly.

Colomar introduces new `--abort`, `--continue`, and `--quit` options for `git-bisect-rebase`, each with distinct behavior tailored to his workflow. These options allow him to pause, restart, or exit the rebase process at any point, enabling manual control over the order in which branches are rebased. He provides detailed shell transcripts showing how this works in practice: resolving conflicts in one branch, then moving to its descendants, and finally returning to the original branch. The key takeaway is that his workflow requires **manual intervention** to handle interdependent branches in a specific order, which he argues is both safer and more adaptable than Phillip’s proposed automation via `--update-refs`.

The email reinforces that the multi-shot approach is non-negotiable for complex cases, as it allows users to inspect changes (e.g., via `git range-diff`) and adjust their strategy mid-rebase. Colomar’s implementation is now the leading candidate for `contrib/` inclusion, though the broader question of whether Git should endorse any such helper remains unresolved. His latest refinements address usability concerns while preserving the flexibility that earlier feedback (including Phillip’s) had questioned. The discussion continues to pivot on whether this functionality belongs in `contrib/`, as a standalone tool, or integrated directly into `git rebase` itself.
