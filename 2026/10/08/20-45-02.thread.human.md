This thread is about a long-standing inconsistency in Git’s index refresh logic, where `git status` incorrectly reports files as modified when clean filters (like line-ending conversions) rewrite their contents without changing their logical meaning. Curtis Allen Smith proposed a two-patch series to fix this: the first patch combined a core fix with an optimization, while the second added a config option to opt out of the new behavior. Junio Hamano’s review questioned the complexity of the first patch, prompting the author to propose a minimal v2 with just the core fix, deferring the rest.

Torsten Bögershausen, a contributor with deep expertise in line-ending handling, provided substantive feedback on the first patch. He clarified that `git add` updates the index even when staging nothing, reinforcing the patch’s goal of consistency. He also questioned the necessity of the config option, arguing that users who don’t use clean filters are unaffected, while those who do should benefit from the fix by default. His feedback aligns with the author’s plan to simplify v2, which would drop the config option and focus on the core fix—a one-line change to disable the size-based shortcut in `ie_match_stat()` for converted files.

The thread is now focused on whether the minimal v2 will address Junio’s concerns about maintainability. The core fix is uncontroversial, but the deferred optimization (to avoid hashing files with mismatched converted lengths) and config option remain open for future discussion. No objections to the fix itself have been raised, and the author has provided performance data to justify the optimization if reviewers see value in including it later. The series is under review, with no merge status yet.
