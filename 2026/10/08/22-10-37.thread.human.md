This thread is about a bugfix patch from Karthik Nayak that addresses a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags. The regression was introduced by a recent change to use batched reference updates, which delayed committing references until after tag backfilling, causing the client to miss reporting 'have' references during negotiation and forcing the server to send all objects.

The patch splits the reference transaction into two—one for fetched references and another for backfilling tags—restoring correct negotiation behavior while preserving most of the performance gains from batched updates. It includes a new test in `t/t5510-fetch.sh` to verify the fix and has been benchmarked to run significantly faster than the pre-batched-update behavior, though slightly slower than the single-transaction approach.

Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the patch substantively, engaging with the root cause and solution. He raised a conceptual question about whether tag backfilling could skip negotiation entirely for nested annotated tags but ultimately accepted the two-transaction approach as a reasonable trade-off. He also suggested a more direct test assertion (verifying no re-fetch of objects) and noted the absence of a benchmark comparison between the current and previous versions, though neither was treated as a blocker. The patch remains under review with no major objections or unresolved questions.
