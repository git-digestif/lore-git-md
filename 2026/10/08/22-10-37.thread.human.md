This thread is about a three-patch bugfix series (v5) by Karthik Nayak that fixes a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags. The regression was introduced by a 2025 commit that batched reference updates, delaying reference commits until after tag backfilling and causing clients to report no "have" references during negotiation. The fix exposes pending transaction state via the transport's `negotiation_include_tips` field, restoring correct "have" reporting while preserving atomicity for `--atomic` fetches.

The series is now ready for maintainer review after resolving a memory management blocker in the first patch, which introduced `transport_options_release()` to fix a pre-existing leak in the remote-helper transport's cleanup path. Patrick Steinhardt, who proposed the design, has reviewed the changes and provided only surface-level feedback (commit message wording and commit ordering), which Karthik plans to address locally without re-rolling unless further feedback arrives. Justin Tobler, who previously raised substantive concerns, now endorses the design. Junio C Hamano's review is the only remaining blocker.

The core fix (patch 2/3) modifies `builtin/fetch.c` to collect fetched ref OIDs into a temporary array and append them to `negotiation_include_tips` during `backfill_tags()`. Three new tests in `t/t5510-fetch.sh` verify "have" reporting for atomic and non-atomic fetches and upstream tracking persistence after rejected updates. The series is 17× faster than pre-batched-update behavior and documents the acceptable O(n) scalability trade-off for fetched refs. No technical objections remain.
