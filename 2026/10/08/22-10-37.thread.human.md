This thread is fixing a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags, introduced by a May 2025 change that batched reference updates. The regression occurs because batched updates delay committing references until after tag backfilling, causing the client to report no "have" references during negotiation and forcing the server to send all objects. Karthik Nayak’s v3 patch proposed a two-transaction approach to restore correct "have" reporting, but after feedback from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler, the discussion has shifted toward a new design that avoids intermediate commits by exposing pending transaction state to negotiation.

The key development is that Karthik has now agreed to prototype Patrick’s proposal, which would use the `negotiation_include_tips` field to supplement the backfill fetch’s "haves" with uncommitted reference updates. This could eliminate the need for the two-transaction approach and restore atomicity for `--atomic` fetches, addressing a documented limitation in v3. Justin Tobler’s argument—that the objects are already present and protected by `.keep` locks—resolved Karthik’s earlier GC safety concerns, making the new design viable. The thread remains under active review, with readiness for Junio’s `next` branch now hinging on the prototype’s success. If the new approach works, it may replace v3 entirely; otherwise, the two-transaction design will proceed with its now-clarified error-handling logic.
