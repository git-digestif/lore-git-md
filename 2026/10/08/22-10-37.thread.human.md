This thread is about a bugfix patch series (v5) by Karthik Nayak that addresses a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags. The regression was introduced by a prior change that delayed committing references until after tag backfilling, causing clients to report no "have" references during negotiation and forcing servers to send all objects. The fix exposes pending transaction state via the transport’s `negotiation_include_tips` field, restoring correct "have" reporting while preserving atomicity for `--atomic` fetches.

The series is now in its fifth iteration, split into three commits to resolve a memory management blocker. The core fix (patch 2/3) has been implemented, and the memory leak issue raised in earlier reviews has been addressed with a precursor patch (1/3) introducing `transport_options_release()`. The series has received substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler, with all technical objections resolved. The only remaining feedback is surface-level: Patrick Steinhardt suggested reordering the test in patch 3/3 for narrative clarity, but this does not affect the series' readiness. Junio C Hamano’s review is the sole remaining blocker before the series can proceed to `next`.

The fix touches `builtin/fetch.c`, `transport-helper.c`, `transport.c`, and `transport.h`, and adds tests in `t/t5510-fetch.sh` to verify "have" reporting during tag backfilling. The performance impact is significant, with the fix being over 17 times faster than the pre-batched-update behavior. The series is now technically complete and awaiting maintainer approval.
