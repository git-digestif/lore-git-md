This thread is about a bugfix patch (v4) from Karthik Nayak that addresses a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags. The regression was introduced by a May 2025 change that batched reference updates, delaying commits until after tag backfilling and causing clients to report no "have" references during negotiation. The fix exposes pending transaction state via the transport’s `negotiation_include_tips` field, restoring correct "have" reporting while preserving atomicity for `--atomic` fetches.

The patch has evolved through four iterations, with v4 adopting Patrick Steinhardt’s proposed design to replace the earlier two-transaction approach. It’s now under active review and appears ready for Junio’s `next` branch unless new concerns arise. Key participants include Karthik (author), Patrick (who proposed the design and reviewed v4), Justin Tobler (who previously raised GC safety and error-path questions, now addressed), and Junio (maintainer, whose position on the new design is still pending).

Patrick’s latest review raises a potential scalability concern: whether the fix might cause clients to advertise *all* local references during negotiation, which could overwhelm servers in large repositories. He also suggests minor code cleanups and questions the relevance of one test case. While these points don’t block integration, they may prompt a v5 update to clarify the commit message. The patch is otherwise well-tested, thoroughly motivated, and resolves all prior feedback. If no further objections surface, it’s likely to graduate to `next` soon.
