This thread is fixing a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags, introduced by a May 2025 change to batched reference updates. The regression forces servers to send all objects during negotiation because the client reports no "have" references while backfilling tags. Karthik Nayak’s v3 patch splits the reference transaction into two phases—committing fetched references before tag backfilling—to restore correct "have" reporting, but Justin Tobler’s review raised questions about its error handling.

The discussion has now taken a significant turn. Patrick Steinhardt proposed an alternative: instead of committing references mid-transaction, the patch could expose the pending (uncommitted) reference updates to the backfill fetch’s negotiation. This might eliminate the need for an intermediate commit and could also fix the issue for `--atomic` fetches, addressing a documented limitation in the current approach. Patrick called the idea "sensible" but noted the two-transaction patch remains an acceptable fallback if the new approach proves too complex. The thread’s focus has shifted from error-path details to whether a single-transaction solution is feasible, with the patch’s readiness for `next` now depending on this higher-level design question. Karthik will likely evaluate the proposal next, and the outcome could lead to a v4 rewrite or a return to the two-transaction design.
