This thread is about a bugfix for a performance regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags, introduced by a recent change to batched reference updates. Karthik Nayak authored the fix, which splits the reference transaction into two parts—one for fetched references and another for tag backfilling—to restore correct negotiation behavior while preserving most of the performance gains of batched updates.

The patch is under review, with Patrick Steinhardt accepting the two-transaction approach as a reasonable trade-off. His main question was whether tag backfilling could skip negotiation entirely for nested annotated tags, but Justin Tobler’s reply confirms this isn’t possible: the protocol requires fetching intermediate objects to resolve chains of nested tags, making negotiation unavoidable. This clarification reinforces the correctness of Karthik’s fix.

The patch touches `builtin/fetch.c` and adds a test in `t/t5510-fetch.sh` to verify shallow fetches don’t refetch objects during tag backfilling. Performance measurements show the fix is significantly faster than the pre-batched-update behavior, though slightly slower than the single-transaction approach. No major objections remain, and the discussion has settled on the current solution.
