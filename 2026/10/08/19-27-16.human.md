This is the first patch in a five-part documentation series that converts Git’s `BreakingChanges` document into a proper manpage (`gitbreaking-changes(7)`). The goal is to make upcoming breaking changes more visible and accessible to end users, who are most affected but least likely to follow development discussions.

The patch prepares the existing `BreakingChanges.adoc` for manpage format by adding the necessary AsciiDoc boilerplate (`NAME`, `SYNOPSIS`, `DESCRIPTION` sections) and demoting the "Introduction" section to match the level of other sections like "Procedure." The changes are minimal and focused on structural adjustments, leaving content and formatting refinements for later patches. The original document remains in place for now, with a rename and redirection planned in the next patch.

The motivation is clear: the current `BreakingChanges` document is buried in the source tree and not discoverable via `git help` or `man`, despite being relevant to all Git users. This patch is a straightforward, well-explained step toward addressing that gap. The commit message is detailed and references prior discussion, making the intent and scope easy to follow.

No new symbols, CLI options, or on-disk format changes are introduced. The patch touches only `Documentation/BreakingChanges.adoc` and is part of a larger, technically sound series that has already addressed all reviewer feedback. The series is ready for integration, though it will require resolving minor merge conflicts with two other topics (`ps/ref-storage-format` and `bc/restrict-hex-to-lowercase`) during the process.
