This is a documentation patch that rewrites Git’s explanation of detached HEAD state. The patch is now in its third iteration and is ready for merging, with all prior feedback addressed. Junio C Hamano’s latest reply is a substantive review that proposes further refinements to the text’s clarity and precision, particularly around how HEAD is defined and the advantages of detached HEAD state.

Junio suggests reworking the opening definition of HEAD to better capture what is common to both branch-attached and detached states—namely, that HEAD records the starting point for your current changes. He also proposes stronger language to emphasize the practical benefits of detached HEAD, such as safely building, running, or experimenting with historical versions of a codebase (or other content like books, games, or data pipelines) without affecting branches. Additionally, he recommends moving the explicit commands for entering detached HEAD (`git checkout COMMIT_ID` and `git switch --detach COMMIT_ID`) into the list of common entry points, and using more realistic commit message examples in the warning text.

The patch itself is well-crafted and uncontroversial, touching only documentation files (`git-checkout.adoc`, a new `detached-head.adoc`, and build system updates). It introduces a new standalone guide (`gitdetached-head(7)`) and updates advice messages to reference it. The broader goal—improving Git’s documentation for a commonly confusing state—is clearly achieved, and the remaining suggestions are about polish rather than substance. This version looks like the final one before merging.
