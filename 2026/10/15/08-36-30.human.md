This is a CI/build system patch discussion, specifically a reply to part 3/8 of Patrick Steinhardt’s v4 series. The patch itself is a straightforward cleanup—removing dead code left behind when the `linux-clang` job was deleted three years ago—but Kaartic Sivaraam’s reply raises a tangential but interesting question about Rust support in CI.

The patch deletes four lines in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` that set `NO_RUST=UnfortunatelyYes` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha1` for the now-removed `linux-clang` job. Kaartic notes that when Rust was enabled by default in April 2026 (commit 32d5b90590), only this job and the `linux-musl-meson` jobs retained `NO_RUST`. Since `linux-clang` no longer exists, the flag has been effectively dead for years, and its removal here is uncontroversial. However, Kaartic wonders whether the *intent* behind disabling Rust should now be preserved by moving the `NO_RUST` flag to another Makefile-based job, such as `linux-feature-testbed`, or by creating a new job specifically for testing without Rust.

The question is well-posed but explicitly framed as outside the scope of this series. Kaartic acknowledges that the patch itself is correct and that any follow-up work can be handled separately. The discussion is more about CI hygiene than about this specific patch: it highlights a small inconsistency in how Rust support is tested across jobs, but it doesn’t challenge the patch’s validity or suggest a blocker. If anything, it seeds a potential future cleanup, but for now, the patch remains ready for integration as part of the uncontested v4 series.
