This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s **CI/build system** series (v4, 8/8). It repurposes the `linux-reftable` job as `linux-reftable-sha256` to test the combination of reftable ref storage with SHA-256, addressing a gap in test coverage. The change is uncontested and ready for integration, resolving a trade-off identified in earlier iterations: the original `linux-reftable` job (which tested reftable with SHA-1) was redundant with `linux-reftable-leaks`, but dropping it would have lost coverage for foreign-SCM tools like `git-p4` and `git-svn`. The new job preserves that coverage while adding SHA-256 testing, a combination not covered elsewhere in CI.

The patch is mechanical, touching only job names and environment variable assignments in `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`. It sets `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable` for the renamed job, ensuring the two features are tested together. The justification—laid out in the commit message and prior discussion—is that the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job tests SHA-256 with the traditional *files* backend, not reftable, due to hard-coded defaults in `t/test-lib.sh`. This patch fills that gap without disrupting existing coverage.

The series as a whole is now **ready for `next` or `maint`**, with Junio having mechanically backported v3 to Git v2.56.0. This patch is the last piece of the puzzle, and its resolution (prioritizing immediate coverage over architectural purity) was explicitly endorsed by Jeff King (Peff). Expect future discussion to revisit the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s behavior, but that’s deferred to a follow-up effort. For now, this patch delivers a clear improvement: better test coverage with no downsides.
