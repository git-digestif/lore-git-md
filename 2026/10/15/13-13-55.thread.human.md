This thread introduces a usability-focused patch series for `git reflog` and `git log -g`, adding two new options: `--operations` (collapsing multi-step operations like rebase into single entries) and `--historical-branch` (showing the branch HEAD was on when each reflog entry was recorded). The series, authored by Harald Nordgren, is now under review in its first version (v1) and touches 23 files, including core reflog-walking logic, documentation, and a new 293-line test script.

The key technical work involves refactoring reflog entry markers into constants to prevent parsing drift, adding internal flags to track user-facing operations and historical branch context, and handling edge cases like detached HEAD and branch renames. Both options require `--walk-reflogs` and are additive, preserving backward compatibility. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s review of the cover letter reinforces the real-world need for these features, describing common workarounds users currently employ to navigate reflog ambiguity—particularly during complex rebases. His endorsement of the problem statement may help the series gain visibility with maintainers.

The only open question remains the commit message for the preparatory refactoring (patch 1/3), where Haugsbakk noted the motivation should be clearer upfront. No technical objections have been raised, but the UX trade-off of replacing standard decorations with historical branch names in `--historical-branch` could spark further discussion. The series is complete and ready for broader review.
