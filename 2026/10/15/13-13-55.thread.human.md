This thread is about a patch series from Harald Nordgren that adds two new options to `git reflog` and `git log -g`: `--operations` (collapsing multi-step operations like rebase into single entries) and `--historical-branch` (showing the branch HEAD was on when each reflog entry was recorded). The series is now at v2, addressing earlier feedback on commit message clarity and output formatting.

The discussion has focused on both usability and architectural concerns. Harald argues these features address real pain points, like tracking reset points during rebases, while reviewers Kristoffer Haugsbakk and D. Ben Knoble have pointed to alternatives like `tig reflog` and `git log -g -p`, questioning whether this functionality belongs in Git’s core. Junio C Hamano’s recent review adds a new dimension: he praises the technical execution but warns that the preparatory refactoring (replacing hard-coded reflog strings with `#define` constants) might complicate a future transition to translatable, machine-readable reflog messages. This tension—whether the current approach is a step forward or a detour—remains unresolved.

The series is still under review, with no maintainer decisions or merge status reported. The latest exchange leaves open questions about the feature’s scope, its alignment with Git’s long-term design goals, and whether the usability benefits outweigh the architectural trade-offs. Harald has nudged D. Ben Knoble for further feedback on v2, but the thread is now waiting for broader input, particularly on Junio’s architectural concern.
