This is a substantive review of the fifth patch in Johannes Schindelin’s five-patch series hardening Git against integer overflows flagged by Coverity. The patch targets `approxidate_alpha()` in `date.c`, where relative-time calculations (e.g., "99999 weeks ago") could overflow an `int` multiplication, producing undefined behavior or incorrect dates.

Phillip Wood’s review engages deeply with the technical implementation, raising two main concerns. First, he points out that the new `mult_overflows()` macro introduced in the series is GCC-only, leaving non-GCC compilers (and platforms with signed `time_t`) exposed to undefined behavior. He proposes an alternative approach using `intmax_t` arithmetic to perform the multiplication on a known, wide type, then checking for lossless conversion to `time_t`—a method that would work across all compilers and `time_t` representations. Second, he questions the patch’s platform-specific handling of unsigned `time_t`, suggesting a simpler truncation check (`(timestamp_t)((int)number) != number`) that would apply uniformly to both signed and unsigned `time_t`.

The review is thorough and constructive, focusing on correctness and portability rather than style or documentation. It identifies a real limitation in the patch’s current design (GCC-only overflow checking) and proposes a more robust solution. The absence of a response from Johannes suggests this feedback may prompt a v2, particularly if other reviewers echo the portability concern. The patch remains uncontroversial in its goal—hardening user input against overflow—but the implementation details are now under scrutiny.
