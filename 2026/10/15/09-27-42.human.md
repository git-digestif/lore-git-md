This is a **substantive review** of the fourth patch in Johannes Schindelin’s five-patch series hardening Git against integer overflows. The patch adds two new macros to `git-compat-util.h`: `signed_mult_overflows` (for signed-integer multiplication) and `mult_overflows` (a type-generic wrapper that dispatches to the signed or unsigned variant). Phillip Wood’s review engages directly with the implementation’s correctness, portability, and edge-case handling.

The review raises three technical points. First, it notes that the type-generic `mult_overflows` macro relies on GNU `typeof`, making it unavailable on non-GCC compilers—an important limitation for safety-critical checks. Second, it questions whether Git still supports non-two’s-complement platforms, given that C23 mandates two’s complement; the question is left open for wider input. Third, it suggests using `BUILD_ASSERT_OR_ZERO` to enforce that both operands in `signed_mult_overflows` have the same width, preventing subtle bugs if the macro is misused. Phillip also corrects a minor documentation nit: the macro evaluates its arguments *at least* twice, not just twice.

The review is constructive and focused on robustness rather than style. It does not request a reroll but flags potential improvements that could strengthen the patch before integration. The open question about two’s-complement support may prompt broader discussion, though the practical impact is likely minimal given modern compiler standards. The series as a whole remains uncontroversial and well-motivated, targeting latent hardening rather than observed bugs.
