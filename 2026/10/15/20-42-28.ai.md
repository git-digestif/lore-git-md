This is a **substantive review** of Andy Koppe’s bugfix patch for the `%w()` pretty-printing regression. René Scharfe engages deeply with the patch’s technical rationale and historical context, raising a backward-compatibility concern while ultimately leaning toward accepting the change.

The review centers on the 18-year-old behavior introduced by commit `ae0b270230` (2007), which treats newlines differently depending on the following character: single newlines before alphanumeric characters are replaced with a space, while newlines before punctuation or whitespace are preserved—but with the first whitespace character *consumed*. René confirms Andy’s diagnosis that this consumption was likely unintentional and provides concrete examples of the current vs. proposed behavior (e.g., `%w(9)%n A` now outputs `%nA` but should output `%n A`). He also notes the behavior isn’t documented, which weakens the case for preserving it.

The backward-compatibility risk is real but narrow: René points out that the existing test in `t4205` (which uses `%+d` to emit a newline followed by decoration text) will need updating, and users who relied on the old behavior could work around it by adding an extra space. His tone suggests the fix is reasonable if the documentation gap is addressed, though he stops short of an explicit `Acked-by`. The review is thorough, citing specific commit history, edge cases, and test implications, making it a clear signal for Andy to either defend the status quo or proceed with the fix (likely with a documentation update).
