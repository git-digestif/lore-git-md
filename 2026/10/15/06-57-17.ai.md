This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s **CI/build system** series (v4, 8/8). It repurposes the `linux-reftable` job as `linux-reftable-sha256` to test the combination of **SHA-256 and the reftable ref storage backend**, addressing a gap in CI coverage. The change is uncontested and ready for integration, with all prior technical concerns resolved in earlier iterations.

The patch modifies `.github/workflows/main.yml`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, and `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` to rename the job and set two environment variables: `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`. This ensures the job exercises the SHA-256 + reftable combination, which was previously untested because the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job (which also tests SHA-256) hard-codes the traditional `files` backend in `t/test-lib.sh`. The justification for this new job was debated in v1–v3, but Jeff King (Peff) and Patrick agreed to defer broader architectural questions about the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s behavior, prioritizing immediate coverage over design purity.

Key context for follow-up discussion: the `linux-reftable-sha256` job now provides unique coverage for the SHA-256 + reftable combination, while the original `linux-reftable` job’s foreign-SCM interoperability coverage (git-p4, git-svn) is preserved by the `linux-reftable-leaks` job. The patch also ties into the thread’s recurring theme of **CI job categorization**—Peff and Patrick have grouped `sha256`, `breaking-changes`, `reftable`, and `meson` as related job types that could benefit from structural improvements (e.g., wildcard patterns like `*-sha256*` to automate settings). This patch’s resolution leaves the door open for future refactoring but does not block integration.
