This is a **substantive review** of the fourth patch in Johannes Schindelin’s five-patch series hardening Git against integer overflows. The patch introduces two new macros—`signed_mult_overflows` and the type-generic `mult_overflows`—to `git-compat-util.h`, but Phillip Wood’s feedback focuses on implementation details rather than the patch’s overall goal.

Phillip’s review is technical and constructive. He notes that the type-generic `mult_overflows` macro relies on GNU `typeof`, making it unavailable on non-GCC compilers—a limitation the commit message does not mention. He also questions whether Git still supports non-two’s-complement platforms (C23 mandates two’s complement, so this may be moot) and suggests using `BUILD_ASSERT_OR_ZERO` to enforce that the operands of `signed_mult_overflows` have the same width, preventing subtle bugs if the macro is misused. Additionally, he points out that the macro evaluates its arguments multiple times, which could have side effects if passed expressions with side effects (though this is already documented in the macro’s comment).

The review does not challenge the patch’s core purpose—hardening against overflows—but instead tightens its implementation. The discussion is narrowly scoped to the macro definitions themselves, with no broader implications for the series’ other patches or the subsystems they touch. Resolution depends on Johannes addressing the technical points, particularly the operand-width assertion and the GCC-only limitation. No platform-specific concerns (e.g., NonStop) are raised here, unlike in Phillip’s review of patch 3.
