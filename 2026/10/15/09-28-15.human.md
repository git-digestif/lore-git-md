This is a review of the fifth patch in Johannes Schindelin’s five-patch series hardening Git against integer overflows flagged by Coverity. The patch targets `approxidate_alpha()` in `date.c`, where relative-time calculations (e.g., "99999 weeks ago") could overflow an `int` multiplication, producing undefined behavior or incorrect dates.

Phillip Wood’s review is substantive, focusing on two key technical concerns. First, he notes that the new `mult_overflows()` macro (introduced earlier in the series) is GCC-only, leaving non-GCC compilers exposed to the same undefined behavior the patch aims to fix. His proposed alternative—using `intmax_t` for intermediate calculations and explicit range checks—would work across all compilers and platforms, regardless of whether `time_t` is signed or unsigned. Second, he questions the patch’s platform-specific logic, particularly the `#ifdef is_unsigned_type` guards, and suggests a simpler, more uniform approach to range-checking the parsed number before assignment.

The review does not request changes to the patch’s core goal (hardening `approxidate` against overflows) but highlights a potential footgun in the implementation: the GCC-only macro could create a false sense of security. Phillip’s feedback is precise, citing specific types (`intmax_t`, `time_t`) and edge cases (truncation, sign conversion), and includes a concrete code snippet as an alternative. The discussion is technical but not contentious, and resolution likely hinges on whether Johannes revises the macro or adopts Phillip’s cross-platform approach in a v2. No other reviewers have weighed in yet, and the patch remains under review.
