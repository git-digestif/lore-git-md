This is a CI/build system patch, part of Patrick Steinhardt’s v4 series modernizing Git’s CI configurations. This specific patch (2/8) fixes a typo in `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` that prevented the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job from enabling breaking changes. The error (`fedora-breaking-changes-musl` instead of `fedora-breaking-changes-meson`) caused the job to skip the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag and `breaking_changes=true` Meson flag, effectively running as a standard Fedora Meson build rather than testing disruptive features like SHA-256 or reftable.

The change is mechanical: a one-line correction in a `case` statement that restores the intended behavior. The typo was introduced in an earlier refactoring and went unnoticed because the job still ran (just without breaking changes enabled). Jeff King (Peff) previously suggested broadening the pattern to `*breaking-changes*` to guard against similar typos in future job additions, but Patrick deferred that improvement to a follow-up patch, citing broader structural issues in the CI scripts. The current patch is uncontested and ready for integration, with no behavioral impact beyond fixing the typo.

Key context for follow-up discussion: this patch surfaces a recurring theme in the series—the fragility of CI job naming conventions and the need for structural improvements like wildcard patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*-sha256*`) to automate settings (e.g., `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH`) and reduce duplication. The typo fix also highlights the organic growth of the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag, which currently excludes reftable despite its disruptive nature, a point Peff and Patrick grouped with `sha256`, `meson`, and other CI job categories as candidates for future rationalization. Expect references to this patch in discussions about CI script maintainability or the `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` flag’s scope.
