This is a documentation patch updating Git’s contribution guidelines to discourage an anti-pattern in patch submissions. The patch, now in its second version, explicitly advises contributors to avoid splitting a test and its corresponding code fix into separate commits. Instead, it recommends including both the test (in `test_expect_success` blocks) and the fix in a single commit, ensuring the diff context clearly shows the behavior being tested alongside the fix.

The change targets `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, a key reference for contributors. The updated text replaces the original phrasing with a more direct statement, as suggested by Junio C Hamano in the review of v1. The new wording removes any ambiguity about whether `test_expect_failure` is acceptable in submitted patches, aligning the guideline with the project’s preference for self-contained, review-friendly commits. The patch also includes links to prior mailing list feedback that motivated this clarification.

The rationale is practical: splitting tests and fixes across commits can obscure the relationship between the two, making reviews harder and increasing back-and-forth on the mailing list. The patch does not introduce new technical concepts but codifies an existing norm, reflecting the project’s emphasis on clear, reviewable contributions. It is narrowly scoped to this specific anti-pattern and does not address broader test practices like modernization or the adoption of the `clar` unit test framework.

The patch is uncontroversial and appears ready to proceed. It has already received substantive feedback from Junio, who engaged with the clarity and potential misinterpretation of the original phrasing. The author has incorporated this feedback, and the updated version is likely to be accepted once it is formally submitted. No further discussion or objections are expected.
