This is a **bugfix follow-up** targeting the ZIP archive backend (`archive-zip.c`), addressing a test infrastructure issue in René Scharfe’s patch that omits the Extended Timestamp (UT) extra field for out-of-range commit times. The patch itself is already **substantive and well-crafted**, but its tests fail on 32-bit platforms due to an incorrect prerequisite assumption.

Matthew E. Luallen identifies the root cause: the tests use `TIME_IS_64BIT` (which checks Git’s internal `timestamp_t` type) instead of `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` (which checks the system’s `time_t`). On 32-bit systems, Git’s `timestamp_t` can be 64-bit while `time_t` remains 32-bit, causing `zipinfo(1)` to lack UT field support or be missing entirely. The fix is a two-line adjustment to the test prerequisites, ensuring the tests skip only when `time_t` cannot represent the required timestamps. With this change, all 18 tests in `t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh` pass on x86-64 Linux, and the two problematic tests are correctly skipped on 32-bit systems.

The patch remains narrowly scoped to the UT extra field omission logic, which is now **technically sound and ready for integration** pending this test fix. The broader thread still has two unresolved issues: the ZIP legacy DOS date overflow (bug #1) and `fast-import` negative timestamp validation (bug #3). René’s updated position—rejecting out-of-range timestamps rather than clamping—continues to shape the discussion, but no consensus has emerged for those cases. This follow-up is a **tested, low-risk adjustment** that unblocks the UT field fix without altering its core behavior. Key symbols involved include `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` (the corrected prerequisite) and `zip_extra_mtime` (the `strbuf` introduced in the original patch).
