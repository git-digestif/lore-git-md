This is Junio C Hamano’s **substantive review** of the v2 patch series that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The review is **technical and architectural**, focusing on the patch’s organization and the underlying algorithm rather than surface-level details.

Junio questions the two-patch structure: he expected the series to split along algorithmic lines—one patch for rebase-and-merge (where commits correspond one-to-one) and another for squash merges (where the entire branch is collapsed into a single commit). Instead, the series treats both cases together in the first patch and adds a `--no-squashed` flag in the second. His comment suggests the current organization may obscure the distinct technical challenges of each workflow.

He also probes the algorithm’s design. For rebases, he suggests reusing the commit-matching logic from `git range-diff`, which aligns rewritten commits with their originals. For squash merges, he floats the idea of locally simulating a squash merge and searching upstream for a similar commit, but notes that unlike rebases, squash merges do not preserve commit messages—so message-based matching (used in patch-based workflows) may be more reliable than tree-based diffing.

The review does not reject the patch or demand a rewrite, but it **raises a fundamental design question**: whether the current unified implementation is the right long-term approach, or whether the two cases should be handled separately for clarity and maintainability. This is likely to spark further discussion about the trade-offs between code simplicity and algorithmic precision.
