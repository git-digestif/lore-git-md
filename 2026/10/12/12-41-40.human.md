This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Harald Nordgren’s patch series to extend `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The series is now at v2, with performance optimizations, correctness fixes, and a `--no-squashed` flag, but Junio C Hamano’s review has raised architectural concerns about the patch organization and algorithmic design.

Harald responds to Junio’s suggestion that the series should split rebase-and-merge and squash merge detection into separate patches. He agrees that the two cases require distinct code paths—rebase merges (where commits correspond one-to-one) can be handled more efficiently, while squash merges (where the entire branch is collapsed into a single commit) are inherently slower and need the `--no-squashed` opt-out. His proposed three-patch structure (`rebase merged`, `squash merged`, `--no-squashed`) reflects this separation, addressing Junio’s concern about clarity and maintainability.

The discussion also touches on the algorithmic approach for squash merges. Junio had suggested locally simulating a squash merge or using message-based matching, but Harald argues that comparing file contents directly is more reliable, especially since GitHub and other tools often mangle commit messages during squash merges. This aligns with the current implementation’s use of tree diffs and three-way merge approximations, though Phillip Wood’s earlier review noted that this method lacks rename detection and may be less strict than alternatives like patch-ids or test merges.

The series is still under review, and this exchange clarifies the next steps: splitting the patches along algorithmic lines and documenting the trade-offs of the chosen approach. The feature’s goal—making `git branch --delete-merged` more useful in real-world workflows—remains intact, but the implementation may evolve to better handle the distinct challenges of rebase and squash merges. For now, the `--no-squashed` flag provides a practical escape hatch for performance-sensitive users.
