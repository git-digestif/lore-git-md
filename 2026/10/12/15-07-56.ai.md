This is a **surface-level review** of Matthew E. Luallen’s test infrastructure fix for René Scharfe’s ZIP archive patch. Junio C Hamano acknowledges the correctness of the fix—replacing `TIME_IS_64BIT` with `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` in the test prerequisites—and explains why the original choice was misleading. The `TIME_IS_64BIT` prerequisite checks Git’s internal `timestamp_t` (always `uintmax_t`, effectively 64-bit everywhere), while `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` checks the system’s `time_t`, which determines whether `zipinfo(1)` can handle extended timestamp fields. On 32-bit platforms, `time_t` may still be 32-bit even when Git’s `timestamp_t` is 64-bit, causing the tests to fail when they assume `zipinfo` support. Junio’s comment confirms the fix is technically sound and unblocks integration of the UT field omission patch.

The email also hints at a broader cleanup opportunity: auditing where Git coerces `timestamp_t` values into `time_t` for external formats (like ZIP) to detect truncation risks. This is left as a `#leftoverbits` note, meaning it’s not urgent but worth revisiting later. For now, the immediate issue—CI failures on 32-bit platforms—is resolved, and the ZIP extended timestamp fix is ready to proceed. The unresolved legacy DOS date overflow and `fast-import` negative timestamp bugs remain separate from this thread. Key symbols: `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` (corrected prerequisite), `TIME_IS_64BIT` (deprecated for this use case).
