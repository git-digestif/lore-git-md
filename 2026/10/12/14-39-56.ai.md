This is a **test infrastructure and platform compatibility follow-up** to René Scharfe’s patch that omits the ZIP Extended Timestamp (UT) extra field for out-of-range commit times. The patch itself is sound, but the new tests fail on 32-bit platforms (Ubuntu 20.02 i386 and Debian i386) due to a preexisting bug in Git’s `dos_time()` function, which mishandles timestamps exceeding 32-bit range (`@2147483648 +0000`).

The failure surfaces when `git archive` is invoked with a timestamp just beyond the 32-bit limit. Instead of clamping or rejecting the value, `dos_time()` produces an invalid timestamp (`18446744071562067968`), triggering a fatal error. René identifies this as a separate bug in the legacy DOS date conversion logic, unrelated to the UT field omission patch. The issue does not affect 64-bit platforms, where the tests pass, but it blocks the patch’s integration on 32-bit systems.

This email advances the thread by pinpointing the root cause: the failure is not in the UT field logic (which works as intended) but in the older `dos_time()` function. The broader discussion about ZIP legacy DOS date overflow (bug #1 in the thread) is now directly relevant, as this bug demonstrates the risks of silent overflow in the legacy field. René’s earlier advocacy for rejection over clamping gains further support here, as the current behavior produces invalid timestamps rather than predictable clamping.

The next steps will likely involve fixing `dos_time()` to either reject out-of-range timestamps or clamp them safely, but this email does not propose a solution. The UT field omission patch remains ready for integration once the 32-bit compatibility issue is resolved, either by addressing `dos_time()` or adjusting the test prerequisites to skip the problematic cases on 32-bit platforms. The thread’s other open questions (e.g., `fast-import` negative timestamp validation) are unaffected.
