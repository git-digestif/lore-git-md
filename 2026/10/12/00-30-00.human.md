This is Junio C Hamano’s review of the v2 patch series that extends `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The series is now a two-patch set: the first implements the core functionality, and the second adds the `--no-squashed` flag to disable squash detection for performance.

Junio’s main observation is about the patch organization. He expected the rebase-and-merge case (where commits correspond one-to-one) and the squash-merge case (where they don’t) to use fundamentally different algorithms, which would naturally split into two separate patches. Instead, the series combines both cases in the first patch and adds the `--no-squashed` flag in the second. This structure is unusual but not necessarily problematic—it reflects the author’s design choice to treat both workflows uniformly under a single detection mechanism.

The review is **surface-level** in the sense that Junio does not engage with the implementation details (e.g., the `changed_paths` commit slab, blob fetching, or edge-case handling). He focuses on the high-level approach: how the algorithm might match commits after rebasing (suggesting `range-diff` as a possible model) and how it might detect squash merges (proposing a trial squash merge as a probe). His comments are more exploratory than critical, raising questions about the matching logic rather than identifying flaws.

This email does not resolve the patch’s readiness—it merely flags the organization as unexpected and hints at potential algorithmic alternatives. The series remains **well-motivated and technically sound**, with performance optimizations and usability improvements that address prior feedback. The open question is whether the unified implementation (rebase and squash in one patch) will be accepted or if reviewers will push for a split.
