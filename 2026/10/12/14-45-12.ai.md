This is a **bugfix follow-up** to René Scharfe’s patch that omits the ZIP Extended Timestamp (UT) extra field when the commit time exceeds the 32-bit signed integer range (`> 0x7fffffff`). The patch itself is **substantive and well-tested**, resolving a silent timestamp wrap issue that caused tools like UnZip to misinterpret post-2106 commit dates. However, its integration was blocked by a **test infrastructure issue** on 32-bit platforms, where the original test prerequisite (`TIME_IS_64BIT`) incorrectly assumed Git’s internal `timestamp_t` type matched the system’s `time_t`.

Matthew E. Luallen’s proposed fix—replacing `TIME_IS_64BIT` with `TIME_T_IS_64BIT`—correctly skips the problematic tests on 32-bit systems (where `zipinfo(1)` lacks UT field support or is missing) while allowing them to pass on 64-bit platforms. René acknowledges this as a pragmatic solution but hints at a deeper concern: `time_t` may not be the right abstraction for Git’s archive logic, suggesting future refactoring could eliminate its use entirely. For now, the two-line adjustment **unblocks integration** of the UT field omission patch, which remains narrowly scoped and technically sound. The broader thread’s unresolved issues (ZIP legacy DOS date overflow and `fast-import` negative timestamp validation) are unaffected by this change. Key symbols: `TIME_T_IS_64BIT` (corrected prerequisite), `zip_extra_mtime` (the `strbuf` managing the UT field).
