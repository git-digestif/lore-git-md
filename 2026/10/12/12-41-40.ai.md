This is a **feature patch series** (v2) extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect branches whose changes have been integrated via squash merges or rebases, even when the branch tip itself is not reachable. The author, Harald Nordgren, responds to Junio C Hamano’s architectural review by proposing a **three-patch reorganization** that separates rebase-and-merge detection from squash-merge detection, addressing Junio’s concern that the two workflows require distinct algorithms.

The new structure—`rebase merged` (patch 1), `squash merged` (patch 2), and `--no-squashed` (patch 3)—reflects the technical divergence Junio highlighted: rebase-and-merge preserves one-to-one commit correspondence (suitable for `git range-diff` logic), while squash merges collapse an entire branch into a single commit (requiring tree-based diffing or local simulation). Harald acknowledges that squash detection is inherently slower, justifying the `--no-squashed` flag as an opt-out for performance-sensitive workflows.

This reply **advances the thread** by accepting Junio’s architectural critique and sketching a concrete plan to split the implementation. The change does not alter the feature’s core behavior or edge-case handling (upstream modifications, blobless clones, etc.), but it may improve maintainability and clarify the algorithmic trade-offs Phillip Wood noted in his review. No response yet to Phillip’s call for greater documentation of the squash-detection algorithm’s limitations (e.g., lack of rename detection), but the reorganization could make those trade-offs easier to articulate in the commit messages.
