This is the final patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s v2 CI housekeeping series, targeting the reftable test coverage. The patch repurposes the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test reftable with SHA-256 instead of removing the `linux-reftable` job outright, addressing Junio C Hamano’s concern about losing foreign-SCM interoperability coverage (CVS, SVN, Perforce) in the original v1 proposal.

The change is minimal: it renames the `linux-reftable` job to `linux-reftable-sha256` in both GitHub Actions and GitLab CI configurations, and updates `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh` to set `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable` for the new job. The `linux-reftable-leaks` job remains unchanged, continuing to test reftable with leak sanitizer enabled. This preserves the existing coverage for reftable + leak checks while adding SHA-256 coverage, a gap the series identified.

The patch is well-motivated and clearly explains the trade-off: the project gains SHA-256 + reftable coverage without sacrificing foreign-SCM interoperability tests. The commit message is concise and avoids overstating the impact—it acknowledges that the removed job was redundant but notes the value of keeping the foreign-SCM tests. The implementation is mechanical and unlikely to introduce regressions, though contributors invested in SHA-256 (like brian m. carlson) or reftable (like Karthik Nayak) may weigh in on whether this is the right long-term balance. For now, it resolves the immediate review feedback while leaving room for future discussion.
