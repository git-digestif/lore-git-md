This is a bugfix patch for the fetch subsystem, specifically addressing a regression in shallow fetches when backfilling tags. Patrick Steinhardt's review confirms the problem and solution described in Karthik Nayak's patch, which splits the reference transaction into two to restore correct negotiation behavior while preserving most of the performance benefits of batched updates.

The review is substantive, engaging with the technical details of the fix. Patrick raises a thoughtful question about whether backfilling tags could skip negotiation entirely (answer: probably not, due to nested annotated tags), and notes that the test could more directly verify the absence of refetched objects rather than just checking for "have" lines. He also points out that the benchmark data could better support the claim that the fix is slightly slower than the original batched-update approach by including a comparison with the current `master` branch.

The patch itself looks well-crafted: it includes a clear explanation of the regression, a targeted fix in `builtin/fetch.c`, and a new test in `t/t5510-fetch.sh` that verifies the intended behavior. The performance trade-off (two transactions instead of one) is explicitly acknowledged and benchmarked, showing the fix is still significantly faster than the pre-batched-update behavior. This is a focused, high-quality contribution that addresses a real problem and is likely to proceed smoothly through review.
