This is a **CI/build system** follow-up in Patrick Steinhardt’s eight-patch series modernizing Git’s CI configurations. The email responds to Junio C Hamano’s **substantive review** of patch 8/8, which flagged a regression in reftable test coverage: removing the `linux-reftable` job eliminates the only CI job that exercised the reftable backend with foreign-SCM interoperability tests (CVS, SVN, Perforce). Patrick acknowledges the oversight and proposes a pivot: instead of restoring the original coverage, he suggests repurposing the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test **reftable with SHA-256**, a combination currently lacking in CI. This would trade foreign-SCM coverage for SHA-256 coverage, a shift he frames as more valuable given his preference for removing the foreign-SCM tools entirely.

The proposal is pragmatic but introduces a new trade-off. The thread’s focus has shifted from "should we remove this job?" to "what coverage is more important?" — a question likely to draw input from contributors invested in reftable (Patrick Steinhardt, Karthik Nayak) or SHA-256 (brian m. carlson). The outcome will hinge on whether the project considers SHA-256 + reftable coverage a higher priority than foreign-SCM interoperability. Expect a reroll of patch 8/8 with either the new SHA-256 configuration or an explicit justification for dropping foreign-SCM coverage. The rest of the series remains uncontroversial and is otherwise ready for integration.
