This is a policy discussion about whether Git should explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Scott Chacon’s RFC patch, which proposes amending `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` to permit AI-assisted patches under strict conditions (human review, testing, DCO sign-off, and an `Assisted-by:` trailer for substantial AI help).

Patrick’s core concern is operational: he argues that the current policy, while conservative, serves a useful gatekeeping function by making contributors *think twice* before submitting code they don’t fully understand. He sees the proposed change as a net negative because it could remove that friction, leading to an influx of low-quality AI-generated patches that reviewers must sift through. He reports firsthand experience: over the past few months, he’s spent more time reviewing and has increasingly caught “slop” late in the process. With reviewer bandwidth already a bottleneck, he believes loosening the AI policy without addressing the review capacity problem will make an existing issue significantly worse.

His position is pragmatic and grounded in day-to-day project realities rather than legal or philosophical objections. He doesn’t engage with the DCO or SFC guidance but focuses on the immediate cost to reviewers and the risk of degrading the signal-to-noise ratio on the mailing list. The thread remains open, with Patrick’s feedback adding a concrete operational counterweight to Scott’s legal and peer-project arguments.
