This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing discussion about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. Harald Nordgren adds a small but concrete observation: even after Junio signs off on a branch (indicating it is merged into `master`), the current `--delete-merged` logic still fails to clean it up if the merge was done via squash or rebase. This reinforces the real-world gap the patch aims to fill—signed-off branches that are semantically merged but not reachable by tip traversal remain cluttering the repository. The point is offered as anecdotal evidence rather than a technical argument, but it sharpens the motivation for the feature by showing that the problem persists even in the project’s own workflow. No new design proposals or performance considerations are introduced; the email simply underscores the practical need for the change.
