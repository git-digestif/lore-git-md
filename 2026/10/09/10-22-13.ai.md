This is a policy RFC thread reply from Patrick Steinhardt, a core Git contributor and frequent driver of large-scale efforts (ODB abstraction, reftable, repack machinery). He pushes back against Scott Chacon’s proposal to explicitly allow AI-assisted contributions in `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`, arguing that the change would worsen an already strained review bottleneck.

Patrick’s central claim is operational: Git’s real constraint is reviewer bandwidth, not code production. He reports a sharp increase in low-quality AI-generated patches over the past few months, noting that he now spends more time reviewing and that the rate of “slop” slipping through is rising. The proposed policy, he fears, would remove the current social friction that discourages contributors from submitting code they don’t fully understand, thereby flooding the list with even more unreviewable noise. His position is pragmatic rather than philosophical—he doesn’t engage with the legal or ethical objections raised by brian m. carlson or Luca Milanesio, but instead focuses on the immediate cost to reviewers and the project’s ability to maintain quality.

The reply leaves two key questions open: whether the project should prioritize openness (as Scott and the SFC advocate) or reviewer sanity (as Patrick and Junio emphasize), and whether any policy text can realistically enforce responsible AI use without becoming a dead letter. Patrick’s tone is measured but firm; he frames the change as a net-negative unless paired with concrete measures to expand review capacity or filter low-quality submissions.
