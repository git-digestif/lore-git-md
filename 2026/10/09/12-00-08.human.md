This is the first patch in a six-part documentation series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates Git’s merge-conflict resolution guidance. The series is now in its second iteration (v2) and addresses all feedback from the initial review, including mechanical fixes, foundational explanations, and terminology clarifications.

The patch adds the new `gitmergeconflicts.adoc` file (333 lines), which serves as a user-tested, example-driven guide to handling merge conflicts. The guide is structured to address common pain points identified in user feedback: inconsistent terminology ("ours" vs. "theirs"), unclear references (e.g., "2 and 3" in the original `git merge` man page), and scattered advice across multiple command pages. Key sections include a new "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" introduction (added in v2), practical resolution steps, tooling advice (e.g., `merge.conflictstyle=diff3`, `git log --merge -p`), and side-by-side examples of `diff3` vs. `zdiff3` output. The guide also clarifies the "ours/theirs" terminology by explicitly tying it to `HEAD` and the other commit, and explains the "upside down" behavior during rebases.

The patch integrates the new guide into the build system by updating `Documentation/Makefile`, `Documentation/meson.build`, and `command-list.txt`, and sets `conflict-marker-size=32` in `.gitattributes` to prevent false positives in tools like `rerere`. No runtime behavior changes are introduced; the guide is purely additive. The series retains command-specific details (e.g., `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD`) in their respective man pages while centralizing general advice, striking a balance between consolidation and nuance.

The commit message clearly articulates the motivation, and the guide’s content reflects thorough user testing and reviewer feedback. The series is likely to graduate soon, as all v1 feedback has been incorporated and the mechanical issues (e.g., `make check-docs` failure) are resolved.
