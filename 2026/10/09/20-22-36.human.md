This is a **CI/build system** follow-up in Patrick Steinhardt’s eight-patch series updating Git’s CI configurations. The email captures Junio C Hamano’s agreement with Patrick’s proposal to repurpose the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test **reftable with SHA-256** instead of its original purpose (leak detection). Junio endorses the shift, calling it "much more useful" than the current SHA-1 + leak-checking configuration.

The exchange resolves the regression Junio flagged in his earlier review—where removing the `linux-reftable` job would have eliminated foreign-SCM interoperability coverage (CVS, SVN, Perforce)—by introducing a new trade-off: prioritizing SHA-256 + reftable coverage over foreign-SCM testing. This aligns with Patrick’s preference to phase out foreign-SCM tools entirely, though the decision may still draw scrutiny from contributors invested in reftable or SHA-256 (e.g., brian m. carlson). The outcome is now baked into the v2 series as patch 8/8, which renames the job to `linux-reftable-sha256` and configures it with `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`.

No further discussion is needed on this point; the reroll reflects the consensus. The rest of the series remains uncontroversial, with only the `linux-TEST-vars` rename (patch 5/8) still under debate. For readers tracking CI improvements, this patch is a clear win: it reduces redundancy while expanding coverage for a critical combination (SHA-256 + reftable) that was previously untested in CI.
