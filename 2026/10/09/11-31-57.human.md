This is a CI/build system patch series from Patrick Steinhardt, updating the v1 submission with improvements based on initial feedback. The series modernizes Git's CI configurations by removing outdated dependencies, simplifying redundant jobs, and improving test coverage—all without changing user-visible behavior.

The key updates in v2 focus on addressing Junio C Hamano's feedback about the `linux-reftable` job. Instead of removing it entirely (as proposed in v1), patch 8/8 now repurposes the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test **reftable with SHA-256**, trading foreign-SCM interoperability coverage (CVS, SVN, Perforce) for SHA-256 coverage. This change reflects Patrick's preference for prioritizing SHA-256 but introduces a trade-off that may require broader discussion. The rest of the series remains largely unchanged: it drops unused "linux-clang" logic, switches the `linux32` job to a supported Debian image, renames the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-exotic` for clarity, removes Python 2 coverage, and fixes minor issues like a typo in the "fedora-breaking-changes-meson" job.

The series touches CI configurations (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI), supporting scripts (`ci/install-dependencies.sh`, `ci/lib.sh`, `ci/run-build-and-tests.sh`), and one test file (`t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`). The net effect is a reduction in complexity (19 fewer lines than v1) and improved reliability by removing EOL dependencies. The changes are mechanical and well-validated, with passing test runs linked for both GitLab and GitHub. The only potential point of contention is the coverage trade-off in patch 8/8, which may draw input from contributors invested in reftable or SHA-256. If this aspect is resolved, the series looks ready to merge.
