Jon Simons’ v2 patch series to speed up `git push` refspec matching for many deletions is now effectively approved by Junio C Hamano for its first patch and five others, with no substantive objections to the remaining nine. The series tackles a long-standing scalability bottleneck in `git push` when deleting many branches against a server with a large number of refs, achieving 10–100x speedups through `strmap` lookups and fixing correctness gaps exposed by a 2025 server-side change that rejects duplicate ref updates.

Junio has reviewed and explicitly endorsed patches 1, 3, 5, 8, 9, and 12, calling the optimizations "very good," "trivially correct," and "clever." Patch 1, the newest addition, adds input validation for `--force-with-lease` to reject invalid refnames early, ensuring consistent behavior before the removal of `mkpath()` in patch 3. Junio’s review of patch 1 frames the validation as a pragmatic trade-off, noting that while it might break edge cases, the likelihood of anyone relying on syntactically invalid refnames is low. The series is now complete, with all planned optimizations and fixes in place, and awaits broader review for the remaining patches. SZEDER Gábor has also weighed in, praising patch 3’s removal of `mkpath()` and its test coverage.

The series is under active review but not yet queued in any integration branch. Two minor clarification questions from Junio—about `match_parse_rule()`’s behavior with nested paths and the broader applicability of the new `strmap` helpers—remain unanswered but do not block progress. The only open technical concern is whether the removal of `mkpath()` might regress workflows that inadvertently relied on its `./` stripping behavior, though the series guards against this with new tests and early validation in patch 1. The next steps are likely to focus on gathering additional reviews for the unapproved patches and addressing Junio’s follow-up questions.
