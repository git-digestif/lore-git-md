This thread is about a 15-patch series from Jon Simons that speeds up `git push` when deleting many branches by replacing slow linear refspec matching with faster `strmap` lookups. The series, now at v2, has delivered 10–100x speedups in benchmarks—dropping a 100-refspec push from 2.5–8.9 seconds to 0.15–0.19 seconds—while also fixing long-standing correctness issues like duplicate-destination handling and refspec ordering guarantees.

The latest patch (12/12 in v2) optimizes `--force-with-lease` overhead by replacing another linear traversal with a `strmap` lookup, cutting 100-lease timings from 0.45–0.49 seconds to 0.16–0.19 seconds. This builds on earlier patches that removed `mkpath()` overhead, added client-side duplicate-destination rejection, and introduced performance baselines. The series is now complete and under review, with no substantive technical feedback yet. Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s earlier comment was purely process-oriented, and the author has addressed it by squashing failing-test commits into their fixes. The only open questions are whether the removal of `mkpath()` might break edge cases and whether the new client-side error messages could disrupt scripts expecting server-side errors.
