Jon Simons’ patch series to speed up refspec matching in `git push`—especially when pushing many branch deletions—has reached its second version with all planned optimizations and correctness fixes now complete. The series tackles a long-standing scalability bottleneck by replacing linear traversals with `strmap` lookups, achieving 10–100x speedups in benchmarked scenarios while also addressing behavioral quirks and correctness gaps exposed by a 2025 server-side change that now rejects multiple updates to the same ref.

Junio C Hamano has reviewed several patches in the series, explicitly endorsing the removal of `mkpath()` overhead in patch 3 and confirming the memory-leak fix in patch 5 as "trivially correct." His latest review of patch 8—adding client-side rejection of duplicate destinations for empty remotes—welcomes the change as a correctness improvement, noting that the test updates are merely cosmetic (adjusting error messages to reflect the new validation). This patch fixes a long-standing inconsistency where empty remotes did not enforce the same rules as populated ones. Junio’s only open question remains about `match_parse_rule()`’s behavior with nested paths in patch 3, but this has not stalled progress on the rest of the series.

The series is now fully implemented, with all patches structured to introduce behavioral changes via failing tests that are later resolved. Key optimizations include replacing `mkpath()` in `refname_match()` (patch 3), `strmap`-based lookups in `check_push_refs()` (patch 4) and `match_explicit_refs()` (patch 9), and a similar optimization for `--force-with-lease` (patch 12), which together deliver the bulk of the performance gains. The series also adds robust test coverage for edge cases like `./`-prefixed refspecs, refspec ordering guarantees, and duplicate-destination handling. While patches 3, 5, and 8 have received maintainer approval, the remaining patches (1–2, 4, and 6–7, 9–12) await broader technical review. No substantive objections have been raised, and the series is poised for integration once feedback is complete.
