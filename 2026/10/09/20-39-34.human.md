This is a brief policy discussion reply from Junio C Hamano, the Git project maintainer. Junio endorses Patrick Steinhardt’s operational objection to Scott Chacon’s RFC patch, which proposes allowing AI-assisted contributions under clear conditions. Junio’s response is minimal—just two sentences—but carries weight: he explicitly agrees that loosening the current AI policy would worsen the existing reviewer bandwidth problem unless concrete solutions are found first. The tone is matter-of-fact and aligns with his earlier concerns about enforceability and DCO compliance.

The message does not reopen the legal, ethical, or philosophical debates raised by brian m. carlson, Luca Milanesio, or D. Ben Knoble, nor does it engage with Scott Chacon’s framing of the issue as a choice between openness and conservatism. Instead, it reinforces the pragmatic consensus that reviewer capacity is the project’s binding constraint, and that any policy change must address that constraint to be viable. The thread remains open, but Junio’s endorsement shifts the burden of proof back to the RFC’s proponents to propose workable safeguards.
