This is a CI/build system patch series (v2) from Patrick Steinhardt, updating Git's CI configurations to modernize dependencies and simplify workflows. The series now consists of eight patches, with the key change being a reworked approach to reftable test coverage instead of dropping it outright.

The v2 iteration addresses feedback from Junio C Hamano and Todd Zullinger on the initial submission. The most significant update is in patch 8/8, which no longer removes the `linux-reftable` job but instead repurposes the `linux-reftable-leaks` job to test reftable with SHA-256. This resolves Junio's concern about losing foreign-SCM interoperability coverage (CVS, SVN, Perforce) by trading it for SHA-256 coverage, a decision that may still require broader discussion. The commit messages have also been improved, particularly for patch 4/8, which now clarifies Ubuntu's 32-bit support timeline.

The series continues to focus on housekeeping: dropping unused "linux-clang" logic, switching the `linux32` job from the unsupported `i386/ubuntu:20.04` image to `i386/debian:latest`, renaming the `linux-TEST-vars` job to `linux-exotic` for clarity, updating the `linux-exotic` job to use `ubuntu:latest`, and removing Python 2 coverage. The net effect is a reduction in complexity (19 insertions, 39 deletions) while maintaining or improving test coverage. The changes are mechanical and unlikely to be controversial, though the SHA-256 vs. foreign-SCM coverage trade-off in patch 8/8 could draw attention from contributors invested in either area.

Key technical details for follow-up discussion include the job names (`linux-reftable-leaks`, `linux-exotic`), Docker images (`i386/debian:latest`, `ubuntu:latest`), and the supporting scripts (`ci/lib.sh`, `ci/install-dependencies.sh`). The `linux-reftable-leaks` job's new SHA-256 configuration and the removal of Python 2 logic are likely points of reference. The series also includes a minor test adjustment in `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh` to skip a SHA-1-specific test in SHA-256 repositories.
