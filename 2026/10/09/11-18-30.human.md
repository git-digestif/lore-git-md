This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing discussion about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. The author, Harald Nordgren, adds a small but concrete observation: even branches that have been *properly* merged (not squashed or rebased) can become "orphaned" from the perspective of the current `--delete-merged` logic if Junio later amends or signs off on the merge commit. This means the branch tip is no longer reachable from the upstream, so the branch isn’t cleaned up automatically. The point reinforces the argument that the current behavior is too strict and misses real-world cases where branches should be considered "merged" even if their exact tip isn’t in the history. It’s a minor detail, but it strengthens the case for the patch’s broader goal of making branch cleanup more flexible and user-friendly. The discussion remains focused on performance trade-offs and default behavior, with no new technical proposals here.
