This is a **CI/build system** follow-up in Patrick Steinhardt’s series to modernize Git’s CI configurations. The email responds to Junio C Hamano’s observation that removing the `linux-reftable` job eliminates the only CI coverage for reftable’s foreign-SCM interoperability tests (CVS, SVN, Perforce). Patrick acknowledges the gap but expresses skepticism about the value of those tests, suggesting instead that the remaining `linux-reftable-leaks` job could be repurposed to test reftable with SHA-256, an area with currently poor coverage.

The tone is pragmatic and collaborative. Patrick does not defend the original patch’s omission but proposes a constructive alternative that would address the coverage gap while also improving test diversity. This shifts the discussion from "should we drop this coverage?" to "how can we make the remaining job more valuable?" — a move likely to appeal to reviewers who prioritize test robustness. The suggestion to add SHA-256 testing is particularly timely given the ongoing (if slow) adoption of SHA-256 in the project.

For a busy reader, this email signals that the series is not yet final: expect a reroll that either restores foreign-SCM testing, adds SHA-256 coverage, or explicitly documents the trade-off. The patch itself is otherwise uncontroversial, and the conversation remains focused on test coverage rather than implementation details. If you care about reftable or SHA-256, this is a thread worth watching.
