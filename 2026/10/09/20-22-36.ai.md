This is a **CI/build system** follow-up in the eight-patch series modernizing Git's CI configurations. The endorsement is brief but unambiguous: Junio explicitly prefers SHA-256 coverage over the existing leak-checking setup, aligning with Patrick's suggestion to prioritize a currently untested combination (`GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH=sha256` + `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT=reftable`).

The exchange resolves the immediate technical question raised in Junio's earlier review (whether to preserve foreign-SCM interoperability coverage or introduce SHA-256 coverage). With Junio's agreement, the path is now clear for Patrick to reroll patch 8/8 to rename `linux-reftable-leaks` to `linux-reftable-sha256` and configure it for SHA-256 testing. The foreign-SCM interoperability tests (CVS, SVN, Perforce) will remain in the `linux-reftable` job, preserving that coverage while adding SHA-256 testing elsewhere.

No new technical concerns or trade-offs are introduced here; the focus is purely on implementation details for the reroll. The broader question of whether SHA-256 coverage is more valuable than foreign-SCM coverage is implicitly settled in favor of SHA-256, at least for this series. Contributors invested in reftable or SHA-256 (e.g., brian m. carlson, Karthik Nayak) may still weigh in on the long-term prioritization, but this reply removes the blocker for the v3 reroll. The rest of the series remains uncontroversial and ready for integration.
