This is a performance-focused patch series targeting client-side refspec matching in `git push`. The author, Jon Simons, addresses a long-standing bottleneck where pushing many branch deletes (e.g., 100 refspecs) to a server with a large number of refs (e.g., 100k) was painfully slow—taking up to 6.6 seconds for a mirror push. The series achieves dramatic speedups (up to 97% reduction in runtime) by replacing linear ref traversals with `strmap` lookups and fixing inefficiencies in `refname_match()`.

The series is well-structured, with early commits introducing failing tests to document behavioral changes and edge cases before the optimizations land. Key changes include:
- Removing `mkpath()` from `refname_match()` (commit 5), which strips leading `./` from refnames, altering matching behavior.
- Converting three linear traversals in `remote.c` to `strmap` lookups: `check_push_refs()` (commit 6), `match_explicit_refs()` (commit 12), and `apply_push_cas()` (commit 15).
- Fixing a subtle bug where duplicate refspec destinations were incorrectly allowed when pushing to an empty remote (commits 7–11).

The performance gains are substantial and well-documented in the included benchmark (`t/perf/p5516-push-delete-refspec.sh`). For example, pushing 100 deletes to a mirror with 100k refs drops from 6.6s to 0.18s. The series also adds tests for edge cases like `./`-prefixed refspecs and `--force-with-lease` validation, improving robustness.

This looks like a serious, well-tested contribution. The commit messages are detailed, the test coverage is thorough, and the performance improvements are compelling. The behavioral change in `refname_match()` (no longer matching `./refs/heads/foo` against `refs/heads/foo`) is clearly motivated and documented. If the mailing list discussion confirms the trade-offs are acceptable, this series is likely to land.
