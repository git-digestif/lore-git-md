This thread is a small but important part of the ongoing SHA-256 transition effort in Git. Patrick Steinhardt sent a one-line test fix as part of a larger CI housekeeping series (1/8) to address a failing test in the Fedora CI job. The test in question, which extracts SHA-1 objects from a ZIP archive, was silently failing because of a typo in the job configuration that suppressed breaking changes—once fixed, the job would default to SHA-256 and break. The patch adds a `SHA1` prerequisite to the test, ensuring it only runs in SHA-1 repositories, where it belongs.

The fix is minimal and well-justified, with no behavioral impact beyond skipping the test where it would fail. It’s a practical step toward improving CI reliability, particularly for SHA-256 testing, and reflects the broader effort to ensure Git’s test suite behaves correctly across different hash algorithms. There’s no controversy here—just a straightforward adjustment to keep the test suite aligned with the project’s transition goals. The series as a whole appears to be focused on cleaning up CI configurations, so expect more small, targeted fixes like this one.
