This thread is about improving Git’s handling of index lock contention, a common pain point for users working with IDEs that run frequent background `git status` calls. Jun Wu’s four-patch series (v2) introduces a configurable timeout (`core.indexLockTimeout`, default 1 second) for the index lock, allowing commands like `git add` or `git commit` to wait briefly instead of failing immediately. The series also fixes long-standing write-race conditions in `git commit` and `git reset` where concurrent index updates (e.g., from `git add`) could be silently discarded.

Mihai Cosma has now proposed an alternative approach: modifying `git status` to defer acquiring the index lock until it actually needs to write, rather than holding it for the entire command. His two-patch series demonstrates a 6× speedup in `git add` latency in a gcc repository with background `status` loops, showing that lock contention can be significantly reduced without adding new configuration. Mihai’s change is a root-cause fix that aligns with earlier feedback from D. Ben Knoble, who preferred eliminating lock contention at the source over adding a timeout knob. The two approaches are largely complementary—Mihai’s reduces the frequency of conflicts, while Jun Wu’s provides a fallback for remaining cases—and could be merged in either order with only trivial conflicts.

Key participants include Jun Wu (author of the timeout series), Junio C Hamano (maintainer, focused on edge cases and clarity), Jeff King (Peff, who identified the pre-existing race conditions and pushed for framing the lock-order changes as correctness fixes), and D. Ben Knoble (who initially opposed the configurability but deferred to maintainer judgment after seeing the data). The default timeout value (1 second vs. Mercurial’s 10 minutes) and whether to prioritize configurability or simplicity remain open questions, but Mihai’s empirical results give reviewers a concrete benchmark for evaluating the trade-offs. The series is still under review, with no merge status reported.
