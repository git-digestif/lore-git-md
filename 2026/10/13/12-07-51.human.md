This is the v8 iteration of a two-patch bugfix series targeting the `rerere` subsystem. The patches address a long-standing race condition where concurrent `rerere` operations contend for the shared `MERGE_RR.lock`, a problem exacerbated by the geometric maintenance strategy introduced in Git 2.54. The series is now technically complete and ready for integration, with all prior feedback incorporated.

The first patch makes all `rerere` writers wait for the lock with a configurable timeout (`rerere.lockTimeout`, defaulting to 1000 ms). This replaces the previous behavior of immediate termination when the lock is held, ensuring that processes like `git rebase` can proceed once the lock is released. The timeout is sufficient to cover typical `git rerere gc` operations, and the patch preserves backward compatibility: a timeout of 0 restores the old behavior. Test coverage verifies the behavior under various scenarios, including waiting for the lock, failing after timeout, and handling explicit `rerere` commands like `forget`, `clear`, and `gc`. Junio C Hamano’s feedback on v7 led to minor test hygiene improvements in this version, such as tightening error-message matching and moving cleanup steps into `test_when_finished` blocks.

The second patch introduces a `--skip-locked` mode for `git rerere gc` (hidden and undocumented), which allows background maintenance tasks (`git maintenance run --auto` or `git gc --auto`) to skip silently when the lock is held. This aligns with the behavior of other auto-maintenance tasks and avoids disrupting concurrent operations. Manual invocations of `git rerere gc` continue to wait and fail if the lock remains held after the timeout. Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback on v7 led to reverting to the original approach of using a single `RERERE_NOWAIT` flag in the existing bitmask instead of introducing a dedicated `enum rerere_gc_flags`, as the change is purely internal and does not affect user-visible behavior. The patch also updates `builtin/gc.c` to pass `--skip-locked` when running in auto mode, following established patterns.

The series touches `rerere.c`, `rerere.h`, `builtin/gc.c`, `builtin/rerere.c`, `Documentation/config/rerere.adoc`, and the test suite (`t/t4200-rerere.sh` and `t/t7900-maintenance.sh`). The changes are well-motivated, with clear test coverage for both positive and negative cases, and the commit messages explain the rationale and trade-offs. The conservative approach (failing outright if the lock remains held after the timeout) prioritizes correctness over resilience, which aligns with the project’s general stance on locking issues.

No substantive objections remain, and the series has received positive feedback from Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, and Phillip Wood. The patches are a targeted fix for a real-world problem that became more pronounced with the geometric maintenance strategy, and they follow established Git patterns for lock-timeout mechanisms. The v8 iteration is a final polish, addressing minor test and documentation nits without altering the core design.
