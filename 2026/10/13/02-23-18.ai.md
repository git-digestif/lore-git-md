This is Junio C Hamano’s latest architectural reflection on the `--delete-merged` series, posted as a reply to Harald Nordgren’s agreement to explore `git range-diff`’s commit-matching logic. The email steps back from the immediate technical discussion to question the **fundamental goal** of the feature and its **CLI design**, proposing a sharp conceptual split between two distinct user needs: (1) deleting branches whose *exact* commits have been merged (the current `--delete-merged` behavior), and (2) deleting branches whose *changes* have been integrated via rebase, squash, or cherry-pick (a new, looser concept he tentatively calls `--delete-irrelevant`).

Junio argues that these two workflows are not merely implementation variants but **different user intents** with different safety guarantees. The current `--delete-merged` is "stable and strict" — it only deletes branches whose commits are reachable in the upstream history, providing a clear, conservative signal that the work is safely preserved. In contrast, detecting rebased or squash-merged branches requires a looser definition of "the same patch" (e.g., text similarity, authorship, patch IDs) and introduces ambiguity: a branch might be irrelevant because its changes were independently solved by someone else, or because they were partially superseded. This ambiguity, Junio suggests, makes the feature inherently riskier and less predictable, especially for users who rely on `--delete-merged` as a safe housekeeping tool.

The email proposes **decoupling the two concepts entirely**: leave `--delete-merged` unchanged (strict, tip-reachability only) and introduce a new `--delete-irrelevant` option for the looser, change-based detection. This would address his earlier concern about CLI scalability — the current `--delete-merged` + `--no-squashed` approach doesn’t scale to future workflows (e.g., selective cherry-picks) — while preserving the safety of the existing command. It also implies that the squash-merge and rebase-merge detection logic, which Harald has been implementing as a unified feature, may need to be **revisited or deferred** until the project agrees on whether `--delete-irrelevant` is a worthwhile addition.

Junio’s example — an AI-generated patch series that becomes irrelevant because someone else independently solved the same problem — illustrates the real-world ambiguity the feature would need to handle. The email does not prescribe a specific algorithm for `--delete-irrelevant` (e.g., whether to use `git range-diff`, patch IDs, or test merges) but frames the problem as one of **user intent and safety**, not just technical implementation. This shifts the discussion from "how to detect squash merges" to "should we detect them at all, and if so, under what CLI contract?"

The email does not engage with Harald’s latest agreement to explore `git range-diff` or D. Ben Knoble’s patch-ID proposal, suggesting that the architectural question takes precedence over algorithmic details. It also leaves open whether the series should proceed as a two-phase implementation (rebase detection first, squash detection later) or be rethought entirely in light of this new framing. The key technical detail for future discussion is the **CLI design**: if `--delete-irrelevant` is adopted, the series would need to split into two distinct commands, each with its own detection logic, performance trade-offs, and documentation.
