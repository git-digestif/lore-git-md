This is a substantive review of Curtis Allen Smith’s v5 patch addressing the CRLF conversion size-trusting bug in `read-cache.c`. Junio C Hamano raises two main concerns: the patch’s interaction with Git’s performance trade-offs (particularly the racy Git logic) and the risk of unintentionally breaking other carefully balanced optimizations.

The core issue is that the patch unconditionally modifies `ie_modified()` to fall back to content verification when a size change occurs and conversion is active, but this could interfere with code paths that deliberately tolerate false positives for performance reasons. Junio suggests the fix should avoid affecting cases where `CE_MATCH_RACY_IS_DIRTY` is set, as these are already handled by higher-level logic that filters out false positives. He also cautions that the patch might disrupt other performance trade-offs elsewhere in the codebase, though he doesn’t identify specific examples.

On the test front, Junio notes that the new tests in `t/t0020-crlf.sh` could inadvertently trigger racy Git conditions due to their speed, potentially masking the very bug they’re meant to catch. He implies the tests should be designed to avoid this pitfall, though he doesn’t propose a specific fix.

The review stops short of outright rejection but signals that the patch needs deeper scrutiny of its performance implications. The unresolved architectural debate (whether the fix belongs in `git add` or index refresh operations) looms in the background, but Junio’s feedback here focuses on narrower technical risks rather than reopening that discussion. Curtis will likely need to address the racy Git interaction and provide further justification for the unconditional nature of the change.
