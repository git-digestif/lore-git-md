This is a **substantive review** of the `git log --follow` performance regression, providing detailed diagnostic findings.

SZEDER Gábor confirms the regression is real and measurable, though less severe in the Git repository itself (2.7× slowdown) than in the Linux kernel (14×). His benchmarks isolate the bottleneck: the new `propagate_follow_pathspec_to_parent()` logic in commit 304812ed33 spends 4.4 seconds comparing a merge commit with its *non-first* parents, versus only 1.4 seconds for the first parent. This suggests the algorithm’s quadratic or worse behavior when handling merge commits with many parents.

He also notes that `git log -L` (line-level history) remains significantly faster than `--follow` even before the regression, implying the two features use fundamentally different rename-detection strategies. The key difference appears to be that `-L` skips diffing against non-first parents if the file is unchanged in the first-parent history, while `--follow` always diffs against every parent. This observation is likely to shape any eventual fix.
