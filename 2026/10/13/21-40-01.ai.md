This is a **substantive review** of Jun Wu’s `[PATCH 1/2]` introducing `core.indexLockTimeout`. D. Ben Knoble engages with the patch’s design choices rather than just its implementation, raising two key concerns.

First, he questions the performance rationale behind `--no-optional-locks`: the patch claims that skipping the index lock forces `git status` to re-hash files, but Knoble points out that only the *newly refreshed* stat info is lost, not the entire cached index. He asks for clarification on how this leads to continual slowdowns, suggesting the underlying problem might be misunderstood or overstated.

Second, he challenges the use of a config knob (`core.indexLockTimeout`) as the primary solution. Knoble argues that config options are overwhelming for users and that this one is particularly opaque—users are unlikely to know when or how to adjust it. He prefers addressing the root cause (e.g., improving `git status`’s ability to retain work or better communication with IDEs) rather than adding another setting. While he acknowledges the patch’s sensible defaults, his critique centers on whether the approach is the right long-term fix.

The review is thoughtful but stops short of suggesting concrete alternatives, leaving the discussion open for others to weigh in on the trade-offs between configurability and simplicity. No code-level feedback is provided.
