This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Jun Wu’s two-patch series introducing `core.indexLockTimeout`. D. Ben Knoble engages with the design choices rather than just the code: he questions whether a new config option is the right way to solve the IDE contention problem and suggests addressing the root cause (performance of `--no-optional-locks status`) instead.

The review starts by probing the performance trade-off of `--no-optional-locks`: Knoble is trying to understand why skipping the index lock forces `git status` to re-hash files on the next run, and whether that penalty is continuous or one-time. He then expresses unease about adding yet another config knob (`core.indexLockTimeout`) that users are unlikely to discover or tune effectively. While he acknowledges the sensible default (1 second), he clearly prefers a solution that doesn’t require user-facing configuration—ideally one that lets `git status` retain its refreshed stat info even when run without the lock.

The tone is constructive and curious rather than dismissive; Knoble is raising architectural concerns, not nit-picking implementation. His alternative suggestion—improving the performance of `--no-optional-locks` or finding a way for IDEs to poll without discarding work—is a direct challenge to the series’ fundamental approach. This is the kind of feedback that could shift the discussion toward a different design if others agree.
