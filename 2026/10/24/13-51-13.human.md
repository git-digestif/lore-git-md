This is a **feature patch series** for `git reflog` and `git log -g`, specifically a **substantive review** of v2 by Phillip Wood that raises significant concerns about the `--operations` option and suggests a different approach for `--historical-branch`.

Phillip’s review is skeptical of the `--operations` option in its current form. He argues that not all commands update the reflog, and some (like `git cherry-pick A B C`) create multiple indistinguishable entries, making it impossible to reliably show "one entry per command." He cites an earlier mailing-list discussion that proposed a separate operations log as the proper solution to these limitations. Phillip suggests that most of the intended functionality could be achieved by defaulting `git reflog` to the current branch (rather than HEAD) and supports *some* filtering or simplification, but warns against pretending the feature can accurately represent one entry per command. His critique is technical and grounded in edge cases, not just stylistic preference.

For `--historical-branch`, Phillip likes the idea but proposes a different implementation: a **format specifier** (e.g., `%gb`) rather than a dedicated option. This would let users include the branch name in custom formats, making the feature more flexible and composable. He also suggests that the default format for HEAD could include the branch name (possibly guarded by `isatty()` to avoid breaking scripts), and notes that accurate branch tracking during rebases (especially `git rebase <upstream> <branch>`) is complex—requiring comparison of the rebased branch name (from the "finish" message) with the branch checked out before the rebase (which isn’t recorded in the same message). He references an existing patch of his own that fixes similar issues for `@{-N}` and offers to adapt it for this use case.

The review is **substantive and tested**: Phillip has clearly engaged with the code, identified real edge cases, and proposed concrete alternatives. His feedback shifts the discussion from "how to implement these options" to "whether these options are the right design at all," particularly for `--operations`. The architectural concerns he raises—about reflog reliability and the trade-offs of dedicated options versus format specifiers—are likely to shape the next iteration of the series. For `--historical-branch`, his format-specifier proposal could resolve the UX trade-off (replacing commit decorations with branch names) while keeping the feature’s core value. The series is not yet ready for merging, but Phillip’s review provides a clear path forward for v3.
