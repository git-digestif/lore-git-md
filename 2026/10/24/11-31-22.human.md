This is a follow-up in the ongoing SHA-256 interoperability discussion, focusing on how to handle unknown commit headers like `mergetag` and `predecessor` during repository conversion. Adam Majer responds to brian m. carlson’s concerns with a pragmatic argument for treating these headers as opaque text rather than dropping them entirely.

Adam clarifies that his proposal isn’t about ignoring hash validation—Git could still attempt to resolve the referenced objects using all supported hash functions—but about preserving the *text* of headers even when their objects are missing. He emphasizes consistency: if Git sometimes converts these headers and sometimes skips them, the resulting history becomes nondeterministic, depending on whether the converter could locate the missing objects. His examples from the Linux kernel (where some `mergetag` objects are unreachable even via their original URLs) and the Node.js repository (where decade-old submodules are gone) underscore the real-world need for flexibility.

The email sharpens the debate: Adam frames the issue as a trade-off between *preserving history* (even if imperfect) and *enforcing correctness* (by dropping invalid data). He suggests that treating headers as text is the lesser evil, as it avoids breaking conversions for large repositories while still allowing future tools to reinterpret the data. Brian’s earlier counterproposal—dropping such headers entirely—isn’t directly addressed here, but Adam’s pushback suggests the discussion is far from settled. The thread remains an RFC, with no patches yet, but the tension between these two approaches is now the central question.
