This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing `git reflog` feature series that proposes `--operations` and `--historical-branch` options. The author, Harald Nordgren, responds to Phillip Wood’s substantive review of v2, which raised two major technical concerns: the viability of `--operations` and the correctness of `--historical-branch`.

Harald signals openness to Phillip’s alternative proposal—implementing `--historical-branch` as a format specifier (`%gb`) instead of a dedicated flag—but notes this would require a ground-up redesign of the series, effectively dropping `--operations` altogether. He does not engage with Phillip’s critique of `--operations` (that it oversells its capabilities and would require a separate subsystem to be truly complete) or the correctness gap in `--historical-branch`’s handling of `git rebase <upstream> <branch>`. Instead, he defers to Phillip’s linked patch, which demonstrates the complexity of the rebase edge case, and expresses interest in seeing it developed further.

The tone is collaborative but non-committal. Harald does not push back on Phillip’s technical concerns, nor does he outline a revised plan for the series. The thread remains in an exploratory phase, with the architectural direction of the features still unresolved. Readers following this effort should watch for Phillip’s forthcoming patch, which may redefine how branch context is exposed in reflog output. For now, the series is unlikely to advance without addressing these fundamental design questions.
