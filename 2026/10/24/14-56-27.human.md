This is a governance discussion about Git’s proposed community voting process, specifically the eligibility threshold for voters. Junio C Hamano responds to Tuomas Ahola’s observation that the current proposals (e.g., five commits) are far lower than Peff’s original suggestion at the Contributor Summit (50 merged patches).

Junio acknowledges the discrepancy but frames it as a practical trade-off: while 50 (or even 200) patches might be a more realistic threshold to ensure voters have a meaningful track record, the current discussion is focused on the *mechanism* of counting contributions (commits vs. topics) rather than the exact number. He reiterates his earlier point that the bar in Emily’s draft was too low and clarifies that his "2 vs. 5" comparison was meant to illustrate the difference between counting topics and commits, not to reopen the debate about whether the threshold itself is sufficient. The email does not propose a new threshold or resolve the tension between inclusivity and ensuring voters have sufficient context, but it reinforces that the current proposals are still under refinement. No new technical details or files are introduced.
