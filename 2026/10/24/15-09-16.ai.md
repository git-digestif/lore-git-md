This is the third patch in Harald Nordgren’s v10 series implementing a "matching-inspired" fetch mode for shallow repositories. The patch introduces the core logic: when a remote has `remote.<name>.refmap` configured but no `remote.<name>.fetch`, a refspec-less `git fetch` now infers which branches to fetch by enumerating the local branches whose `@{upstream}` points to that remote. D. Ben Knoble’s review focuses on documentation clarity and implementation details, raising several non-blocking but actionable suggestions.

Knoble’s most substantive feedback is about the commit message and documentation. He suggests rephrasing the justification to better reflect the series’ goal—allowing refmaps to dynamically infer branches to fetch—rather than framing it as a fix for an error case. The current phrasing ("Configuring remote.<name>.refmap without remote.<name>.fetch used to make a refspec-less 'git fetch' fail...") makes the change sound like a bugfix for Patch 1/5, when it is actually the main feature of the series. Knoble also flags confusing wording in the updated `fetch-options.adoc`, where the phrase "branches from the remote that are used as the `@{upstream}` of our local branches" could be clearer. He questions whether the documentation should explain how the refmap interacts with the inferred branches (e.g., whether it narrows the scope or just maps the fetched refs), noting that the current text does not connect these dots for users.

On the implementation side, Knoble points out a potential inefficiency in the use of `string_list` in `get_ref_map()`. The code uses `STRING_LIST_INIT_DUP` and iterates over the list with `for_each_string_list_item`, but since the list is populated from configuration (which is inherently ordered), a `strvec` might be more appropriate to avoid unnecessary sorting and string duplication. This is a minor optimization, but it aligns with Git’s preference for `strvec` in ordered contexts. He also notes a small duplication in `do_fetch()`: the `follow_remote_head` logic and the call to `collect_upstream_from_remote()` appear in two nearly identical blocks, suggesting a refactoring opportunity to reduce redundancy.

The review is substantive but non-blocking. Knoble does not identify any functional issues or regressions, and his suggestions are about clarity and efficiency rather than correctness. The patch remains ready for merging to `next` pending an additional Ack from another reviewer, as requested by Junio. The only unresolved question from prior discussion—the fallback logic in `remote_find_tracking()`—is not addressed here, but it is orthogonal to this patch and does not affect its functionality.
