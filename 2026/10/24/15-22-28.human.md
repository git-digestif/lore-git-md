This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about Harald Nordgren’s reflog usability series (`--operations` and `--historical-branch` options). D. Ben Knoble largely endorses Phillip Wood’s substantive critique of the series, agreeing with both major technical concerns.

Ben Knoble calls out that `--operations` is inherently heuristic and will never be fully reliable, since not all commands update the reflog and some (like `git cherry-pick A B C`) create indistinguishable entries. He echoes Phillip’s skepticism about presenting this as a true "operations log," noting that a separate subsystem would be needed to overcome these limitations. Like Phillip, he suggests that most of the feature’s goals could be achieved by filtering the branch reflog instead, though he stops short of outright opposition.

On `--historical-branch`, Ben Knoble is more positive but fully supports Phillip’s proposal to implement it as a format specifier (e.g., `%gb`) rather than a dedicated flag. He praises the composability this would offer and acknowledges the correctness challenges Phillip identified, particularly for `git rebase <upstream> <branch>`. Ben Knoble’s comment that "it seems somehow simpler to have an event or ops log with more structure" reinforces the architectural tension: the current approach relies on parsing existing reflog strings, which may not be sustainable for accurate branch tracking.

The reply adds no new technical details but strengthens the case against the series’ current design. It aligns with Phillip’s tested review, which identified specific edge cases and proposed a more flexible alternative for `--historical-branch`. The thread remains unresolved, with both reviewers now questioning whether the reflog subsystem is the right place for these features.
