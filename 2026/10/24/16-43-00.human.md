This is a **substantive review** in the ongoing debate over Harald Nordgren’s patch to make `git status` default to comparing against both `@{upstream}` and `@{push}`. Junio responds directly to D. Ben Knoble’s user-centric argument, sharpening his philosophical objection to the patch.

Junio’s core point is that `@{push}` is fundamentally different from `@{upstream}` because it is under the user’s sole control. Unlike `@{upstream}`, where unexpected changes can appear (e.g., from other contributors), `@{push}` only reflects the user’s own actions—commits, reverts, or force-pushes. He argues that while users *might* forget the state of `@{push}`, they would immediately recognize their own work when they see it, making the need for default monitoring less compelling. This undermines the patch’s justification that users benefit from being reminded of divergence between local and `@{push}` branches.

The exchange reveals a deeper cultural divide: Junio sees Git’s defaults as tools for *discovering surprises* (e.g., unexpected upstream changes), while Knoble and Harald frame them as *workflow aids* (e.g., reminding users of their own actions). Junio’s position remains firm—defaults should prioritize surfacing what users *don’t* control, not what they already know. The thread is still **open**, with no clear path to resolving this philosophical stalemate. The next move likely rests with Harald, who may need to either refine the patch’s justification or accept that the change may not align with Junio’s vision for Git’s defaults.
