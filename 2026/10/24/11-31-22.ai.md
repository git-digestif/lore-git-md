This is a follow-up in the ongoing SHA-256 interoperability discussion, focusing on how `convert_commit_object` should handle unknown commit headers like `mergetag` and `predecessor`. Adam Majer responds to brian m. carlson’s concerns with additional real-world context and a defense of his pragmatic approach.

Adam clarifies that the missing `mergetag` objects in the Linux kernel repository are not just transient but often point to external repositories (some now defunct or unreachable). He argues that treating these headers as opaque text during conversion is the only way to preserve history deterministically, since Git cannot validate or rewrite their OIDs without the original objects. His key point is consistency: if Git sometimes skips these headers and sometimes converts them, the resulting SHA-256 repository becomes nondeterministic, depending on the converter’s ability to track down missing objects. He suggests that as long as the original repository’s hash function remains unchanged, the headers can later be retrieved and interpreted correctly, even if their contents are not validated during conversion.

Adam also raises the broader issue of submodules with unavailable upstream repositories (e.g., the decade-old Node.js repo), questioning whether Git should attempt to skip them or accept that some histories may be "unconvertible" without manual intervention. His tone suggests frustration with the strictness of brian’s position, framing the problem as a practical limitation rather than a design flaw in the headers themselves. The email does not propose a specific implementation but reinforces the tension between compatibility (preserving headers as text) and correctness (dropping them to avoid invalid data). The discussion now hinges on whether Git should prioritize real-world usability for large repositories or enforce stricter handling to avoid propagating misleading or broken data. Key technical details to watch include the handling of external references, the potential for loose-object-table lookups, and whether the solution will involve opt-in flags or unconditional behavior changes.
