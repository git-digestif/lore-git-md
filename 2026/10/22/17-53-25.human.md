This is a short design-discussion reply in the ongoing RFC thread about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to handle branches whose upstream has been deleted. Harald Nordgren questions the necessity of the `--into` flag itself, suggesting the feature could be even simpler: if a branch’s upstream is gone and its tip is reachable from a user-specified ref (like `origin/master`), it should be treated as merged and deleted without requiring an explicit opt-in.

The reply nudges the discussion toward **option (D)** from the original RFC—relaxing the existing `--delete-merged` behavior by default for gone-upstream branches—rather than adding new flags like `--into`. This aligns with Nordgren’s earlier preference for simplicity but goes further by eliminating the need for any modifier. It does not address the composability debate with D. Ben Knoble or the broader workflow concerns raised earlier, leaving the thread’s core tension (explicit flags vs. default behavior) unresolved. The observation is brief but sharp: if the goal is to reduce manual cleanup, why not make the feature automatic?
