This is a governance-focused reply in the ongoing discussion about Git’s proposed community voting process. Brian m. Carlson weighs in on two key aspects: the voting mechanism and eligibility criteria.

He expresses a preference for Debian’s *devotee* system over GitHub polls, citing Git’s historical neutrality on forges and tooling. This aligns with Emily Shaffer’s earlier suggestion but adds weight to the idea of avoiding GitHub-dependent solutions. His stance is pragmatic—he’s not volunteering to maintain the system but endorses *devotee* based on his familiarity with Debian’s processes.

On eligibility, he argues Emily’s proposed threshold (one merged commit or two code reviews) is too low, risking "drive-by" contributors who participate solely to influence a vote. While he doesn’t propose a specific number, his concern is about preserving the integrity of the process: voting should be a *consequence* of sustained contribution, not an incentive. This echoes Phillip Wood’s earlier review but frames the issue as a matter of project culture rather than technical implementation.

The email is substantive but narrowly scoped—it doesn’t address other open questions (e.g., voting mechanism details, non-code contributions) or propose alternatives. It’s a measured critique that could shape the final criteria, though it leaves the heavy lifting (defining a higher threshold) to others. For readers tracking the thread, this signals that eligibility will likely be tightened before the process is finalized.
