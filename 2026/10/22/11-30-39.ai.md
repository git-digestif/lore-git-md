This is a follow-up from Patrick Steinhardt on the v2 series to move `pack.packsizelimit` into the repository-scoped `repo_config_values` struct. The series consists of two patches: a preparatory `memset` cleanup and the core refactoring. Patrick’s email confirms that the series meets his expectations and is technically sound, with only minor cosmetic nits remaining on the first patch.

The preparatory patch (1/2) modifies `repo_config_values_init()` to use `memset()` for zero-initialization, reducing boilerplate and standardizing the initialization pattern. Patrick’s feedback here is surface-level, requesting small adjustments to the commit message or comments. The core patch (2/2) moves the `pack.packsizelimit` value from a global variable into `repo_config_values`, completing the refactoring. Patrick’s earlier review of this patch explicitly stated it "reads well" with no further feedback, indicating technical approval.

The series is part of the ongoing `the_repository` removal effort, aimed at reducing global state and improving Git’s modularity. Joy Gundu, an Outreachy contributor, has incorporated all feedback from the v1 review, including Patrick’s suggestion to add the `memset` cleanup. The changes are mechanical, well-tested (Joy reports successful runs of `t1300-config.sh` and `t5300-pack-object.sh`), and uncontroversial. No objections or edge cases have been raised, and the series aligns with Patrick’s own work in this area.

Files touched include `builtin/pack-objects.c`, `environment.c`, `environment.h`, and `object-file.c`. The symbols involved are `pack_size_limit_cfg` (global, removed) and `repo_config_values.pack_size_limit` (new field). The on-disk format remains unchanged, and the refactoring introduces no behavioral differences. The only remaining work is addressing Patrick’s minor nits on the first patch, which are cosmetic and unlikely to affect functionality.

This series is ready for integration, pending the small adjustments to the preparatory patch. It represents a clean, incremental step in the global-state-removal effort, with strong support from Patrick Steinhardt and no outstanding technical concerns.
