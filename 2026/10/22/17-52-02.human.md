This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing RFC thread about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to handle branches whose upstreams have been deleted. Harald Nordgren responds to D. Ben Knoble’s suggestion of a composable `--option=value,value,value` syntax (e.g., `--delete-merged='origin/*,into:origin/master'`) by rejecting it as overly complex and inconsistent with Git’s CLI conventions.

Nordgren’s objection centers on the **specific syntax** `--delete-merged=into:origin/master`, which he compares to `find -exec`—a pattern he views as hacky and unintuitive. He does not engage with Knoble’s alternative of "stacking" (repeatable options like `--into=ref1 --into=ref2`), nor does he address the broader trade-offs between composability and simplicity. Instead, he reinforces his preference for a **standalone `--into` flag** (the RFC’s original proposal), which he sees as more explicit and aligned with Git’s existing design.

The thread remains stuck on **interface design**, with no movement toward consensus or implementation. Nordgren’s reply does not advance the technical discussion (e.g., reachability checks or edge cases) but clarifies that his opposition is stylistic rather than functional. For readers tracking this RFC, the key takeaway is that the debate is now narrowly focused on **CLI aesthetics**—whether Git should prioritize composability (Knoble’s preference) or simplicity (Nordgren’s preference)—rather than the feature’s underlying mechanics.
