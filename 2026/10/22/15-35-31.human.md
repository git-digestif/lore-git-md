This is a **governance process discussion** in the ongoing thread about formalizing a voting process for the Git project. Justin Tobler weighs in on the eligibility criteria proposed in Emily Shaffer’s patch, arguing that the current bar (one commit in `next` or two code reviews) is too low and risks including contributors with only historical involvement.

Justin suggests two refinements: first, that contributions should be recent (e.g., from one of the last *X* releases) to ensure voters have a current stake in the project; second, that the criteria should avoid over-representing corporate-affiliated contributors, who dominate the most active roles. He acknowledges the tension between inclusivity and bias, noting that overly restrictive rules could skew the electorate toward those employed to work on Git. His feedback aligns with Junio’s earlier proposal to count *topics* (not individual commits) in released versions, which Justin endorses as a more robust measure of sustained contribution.

The email advances the thread by surfacing a new trade-off: recency vs. inclusivity. While Justin doesn’t propose a specific time window (e.g., "last 5 releases"), his framing suggests the community may need to debate how to balance historical contributions against current engagement. This adds a layer to the eligibility discussion, which now spans three dimensions: *quantity* (how many contributions), *quality* (topics vs. commits), and *recency* (how recent those contributions must be). The broader question of whether voting should favor long-term contributors or cast a wider net remains unresolved.
