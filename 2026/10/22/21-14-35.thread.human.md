This "What's cooking" report from Junio Hamano in October 2026 paints a picture of a project at a crossroads. The Git mailing list is seeing a surge in contributions, with around 100 topics spread across the integration branches, but only about 30 have progressed to or near the `next` branch where they're being tested for graduation. This backlog has raised concerns about maintainer capacity, with Junio explicitly noting the risk of dropping stalled topics due to unaddressed reviewer feedback - a rare admission that signals potential burnout or bandwidth constraints.

Several notable changes have recently graduated to the `master` branch. These include updates to Unicode width tables (now at Unicode 18), optimizations for lazy fetching with `uploadpack.lazyFetchTrusted`, and improvements to `git status` that now suggests `merge --continue` instead of `commit` during conflicts. The ODB (object database) subsystem continues to see significant refactoring work, and new features like commit signing support for `git history` and `git replay` have landed. Some user-facing changes might raise eyebrows, particularly the new default behavior for `git status` that compares against both upstream and push branches, which could spark discussion about workflow impacts.

The report highlights several ongoing architectural efforts that represent the future direction of Git. The SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability work continues with pack index v3, while the long-running effort to remove the `the_repository` global variable shows progress through multiple patches. Performance optimizations are also in focus, with improvements to connectivity checks, fetch operations in shallow repositories, and `git stash` performance. Some controversial changes are cooking, including a Rust crate reorganization and a proposal to make `git blame` ignore `.git-blame-ignore-revs` files by default.

The most concerning aspect of this report is the maintainer's explicit warning about the backlog. With such a volume of contributions and limited maintainer bandwidth, the project may need to consider structural changes to how patches are reviewed and merged. The coming months will be critical in determining whether Git can maintain its development velocity while preserving its careful review culture.
