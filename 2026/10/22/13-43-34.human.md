This is a **workflow discussion** in the ongoing RFC thread about extending `` `git branch --delete-merged` `` to handle branches whose upstream has been deleted by hosting services. Harald Nordgren, a contributor with related in-flight patches, chimes in to endorse D. Ben Knoble’s critique of GitHub’s triangular workflow and to advocate for better defaults that make triangular workflows more intuitive.

Harald fully agrees with Knoble that most GitHub/GitLab workflows are inherently triangular, even if users don’t realize it. He echoes the call for `` `push.default=current` `` to become the default setting, and for `` `branch.autosetupmerge=inherit` `` to be more widely adopted. These changes, he argues, would make `` `@{upstream}` `` (where you merge) and `` `@{push}` `` (where you push) behave more predictably, improving the usability of commands like `` `git pull` `` and `` `git status` ``.

He also reiterates his support for a default `` `status.comparebranches=@{upstream} @{push}` `` configuration, which he previously proposed in a separate patch series. This setting would help users keep track of both their merge target and their push target, reducing confusion when switching tracking branches.

While Harald doesn’t directly address the RFC’s interface proposals (A, B, or C), his comments reinforce the broader context: the proposed `` `--delete-merged` `` enhancement is part of a larger effort to make Git’s branch management more ergonomic in triangular workflows. His feedback suggests that improving defaults and visibility of triangular relationships could reduce the need for manual workarounds like the one the RFC aims to solve. The discussion remains focused on user experience rather than implementation details.
