This is the first patch in a documentation and terminology cleanup series (now numbered 1/5 instead of 1/6 as in the cover letter). It updates Git’s glossary to formally define "root ref" and clarify the definition of "pseudoref" following the 2024 terminology change (commit 993d57eded).

The patch adds a new "root ref" entry to `Documentation/glossary-content.adoc`, moving the naming rules and list of irregular root refs (e.g., `AUTO_MERGE`, `BISECT_EXPECTED_REV`) from the existing "ref" entry. It also rewrites the "pseudoref" entry to explicitly state that pseudorefs (like `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD`) are *not* refs, cannot be written by `git-update-ref` or listed by `git-for-each-ref`, and yield the first object ID when they contain multiple entries. The glossary now cross-references `gitrevisions(7)` for descriptions of commonly used root refs and pseudorefs.

The changes are mechanical and align the glossary with the 2024 redefinition, but they are not purely cosmetic: the updated definitions resolve ambiguities in the old wording (e.g., whether pseudorefs are refs) and provide a clearer foundation for the rest of the series. No behavioral changes or new functionality are introduced. The patch is well-scoped and uncontroversial, though it may prompt discussion about whether the glossary’s new structure is sufficiently clear for users unfamiliar with the 2024 change. Key symbols introduced: `root ref` (glossary term), updated `pseudoref` definition. Files touched: `Documentation/glossary-content.adoc`.
