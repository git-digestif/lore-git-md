This is a documentation and terminology cleanup series that aligns Git’s glossary, man pages, code comments, and tests with the 2024 redefinition of "pseudoref" (now limited to FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD) and the introduction of "root ref" for refs outside the "refs/" hierarchy (like HEAD and ORIG_HEAD). The series is now under active review, with Junio C Hamano’s latest reply signaling cautious approval.

Junio’s response is brief but positive: he calls the series "a pleasant read," which suggests the patches are well-structured and the rationale is clear. Earlier in the thread, he had raised a substantive concern about the glossary’s definition of "pseudoref," arguing that omitting the phrase "different semantics" could obscure why pseudorefs like FETCH_HEAD cannot be written with `git update-ref`—specifically, because they can record multiple object names, not just one. That objection remains unresolved, but Junio has not repeated it here, so the door is still open for the author to adjust the wording in a follow-up iteration.

The series touches 36 files, including the glossary, six man pages, built-in commands, refs internals, and test scripts. The changes are mechanical—replacing outdated uses of "pseudoref" with "root ref"—but thorough, and they correct minor inaccuracies in related documentation (e.g., which refs get reflogs, the behavior of `core.logAllRefUpdates`). One deliberate omission is the completion helper `__git_pseudoref_exists()`, whose name is left unchanged because there is no clear term that covers both pseudorefs and root refs.

For a busy reader, this looks like a low-risk, high-value cleanup. The patches are narrowly scoped to terminology alignment, with no behavioral changes or new features. The unresolved glossary wording is the only potential sticking point, and even that is a matter of phrasing rather than substance. If the author addresses Junio’s concern in a v2, this series is likely to be merged.
