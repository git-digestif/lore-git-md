This is a **governance process discussion** in reply to Junio C Hamano’s feedback on Emily Shaffer’s voting process proposal. Justin Tobler weighs in on the eligibility criteria debate, aligning with Junio’s concerns about the current bar being too low but also cautioning against over-correction that could skew representation toward corporate-affiliated contributors.

Justin’s core argument is that eligibility should prioritize *current* and *vested* contributors, not just historical participation. He questions whether contributors inactive for 15 years should retain voting rights, suggesting a time-bound filter (e.g., contributions within the last *X* releases) to ensure relevance. This echoes Junio’s stricter proposal (two or more *topics* in released versions) but adds a temporal dimension to avoid dormant contributors influencing decisions.

He also raises a countervailing concern: overly restrictive criteria could disproportionately favor the most active contributors—many of whom work for tech companies—potentially biasing outcomes toward corporate interests. This introduces a tension between inclusivity and representativeness, framing eligibility as a balancing act rather than a technical detail. His phrasing ("I worry it may not be entirely reflective of wider community sentiment") suggests skepticism about whether any formal criteria can fully capture the project’s diversity.

Justin explicitly endorses Junio’s "topics in released versions" metric over tracking commits in `next`, reinforcing the shift away from transient integration branches. However, he does not propose specific thresholds (e.g., *X* releases) or address Junio’s vote-counting/versioning questions, leaving those open. The email advances the thread by surfacing the trade-offs in eligibility design but does not resolve them. The discussion now hinges on whether the community can agree on a standard that balances recency, impact, and inclusivity—key to the proposal’s perceived legitimacy.
