This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about extending `git branch --delete-merged` to detect squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. Junio C Hamano weighs in on the naming and structure of the proposed new flag, which is now the main point of contention after the safety concerns raised by earlier reviews.

Junio rejects the negative connotation of Harald Nordgren’s proposed `--delete-irrelevant` flag and suggests several alternatives: `--delete-integrated`, `--delete-subsumed`, `--delete-absorbed`, `--delete-included`, or `--delete-done`. He leans toward "integrated" as the most semantically accurate, though he acknowledges it is a bit long. His preference is to avoid reusing `--delete-merged` with an optional value (e.g., `--delete-merged=squash`), as Git’s option parser treats optional arguments inconsistently.

More importantly, Junio proposes a hierarchical structure for the new flag: `--delete-foo=merged` (strict tip-reachability, equivalent to the current `--delete-merged`), `--delete-foo=rebased` (includes rebased branches), and `--delete-foo=squashed` (includes squash-merged branches *and* all previous modes). This design ensures users don’t accidentally exclude normally merged branches when opting into looser detection. For example, `--delete-foo=squashed` would delete branches merged via squash *or* rebase *or* normal merge, avoiding the confusion of a granular but non-hierarchical approach.

The email does not address the algorithmic or performance trade-offs (e.g., patch IDs vs. tree-based diffing) but refines the CLI design debate. The naming and structure are now open for further input, and the next steps will likely involve revisiting the patch series to implement a consensus design. The discussion remains focused on usability and safety, with the hierarchical approach offering a clear path forward.
