This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing discussion about Harald Nordgren’s patch series to extend `git branch --delete-merged` with squash-merge and rebase-merge detection. Harald acknowledges D. Ben Knoble’s real-world example of identical "npm update" commits in separate PRs rebased onto `main`—a scenario where the current rebase-detection logic could incorrectly delete a branch whose changes were already integrated by another author. While he still considers this edge case rare, he concedes it is plausible in automated workflows (e.g., dependency updates) and suggests that an **author check** (comparing commit authors) could mitigate the risk.

The exchange reinforces the earlier consensus that the current algorithm’s leniency (checking for *containment* of changes rather than *exact* integration) introduces safety concerns, even for rebase detection. This aligns with Junio C Hamano’s earlier test case and the broader push for a CLI redesign (e.g., `--delete-irrelevant` or `--delete-merged=<kind>`) to decouple strict tip-reachability from looser change-based detection. Harald’s suggestion of an author check is a lightweight way to address the "npm update" scenario without overhauling the algorithm, but it remains unclear whether this will be incorporated into the next revision of the series. The discussion continues to highlight the tension between usability (one-step maintenance) and safety (avoiding false positives).
