This thread covers a critical bugfix for Git’s ODB transaction layer, where temporary object directory migration could fail when using compatibility hashes (e.g., SHA-1/SHA-256 interop) and batch fsync of loose objects. The issue stemmed from the "loose-object-idx" file—a mapping between objects and their compatibility hashes—not being correctly merged during migration, risking ODB transaction failures and potential data loss. Justin Tobler’s four-patch series (v3) resolves this by introducing atomic writes (via temp-file-then-rename) and a finite lock timeout, addressing earlier concerns from Patrick Steinhardt about atomicity and locking.

The series is now functionally complete and merged to Junio C Hamano’s `seen` branch, with no outstanding technical objections. Key improvements in v3 include reading the source loose-object-idx file outside the lock to minimize contention and adding a clarifying comment to the migration function. While systemic design flaws in the loose-object-idx mechanism (unsorted files, lack of deduplication, and stale entry pruning) remain unaddressed, they are acknowledged as long-term liabilities deferred to future work. Patrick Steinhardt’s follow-up confirms satisfaction with v3, and Junio’s merge to `seen` signals readiness for integration into `next` and eventually `master`. Justin has a minor post-merge cleanup queued to remove a leftover function declaration in `loose.h`, but this is purely a code hygiene matter. The series is uncontroversial and resolves the immediate bug with robust, well-reviewed changes.
