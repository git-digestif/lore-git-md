This thread fixes a critical bug in Git’s object database (ODB) transaction layer that could cause data loss when migrating temporary object directories under compatibility hash configurations (e.g., SHA-1/SHA-256 interop) with batch fsync enabled. The issue—where the "loose-object-idx" file (mapping objects to compatibility hashes) failed to merge correctly—was resolved in a four-patch series by Justin Tobler, now at version 3 and merged to Junio’s `seen` branch.

The series addresses all blocking concerns raised during review, including atomicity (via temp-file-then-rename) and locking (a 1000ms timeout to avoid indefinite blocking). While systemic flaws in the loose-object-idx mechanism—like unsorted files and lack of deduplication—were acknowledged, they were deferred to avoid scope creep. Key changes include new helper functions (`loose_object_map_read()`, `loose_object_map_write()`) and a regression test (`t/t1016-compatObjectFormat.sh`). Patrick Steinhardt, the primary reviewer, approved the design after v2, and Junio merged the series without functional changes, though a minor cleanup (a leftover declaration in `loose.h`) remains for post-merge hygiene.

The latest email from Karthik Nayak adds two trivial nits to the final patch: renaming `loose_object_idx_merge()` for consistency and clarifying an error message. Neither affects functionality, and the series is technically complete. With all substantive feedback addressed and no open technical questions, the patches are ready for graduation to `next`. The thread reflects a well-scoped, collaborative effort to fix a subtle but serious bug in the ODB layer.
