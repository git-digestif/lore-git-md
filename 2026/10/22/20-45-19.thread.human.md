This thread is about a four-patch series from Miklos Vajna that aims to speed up `git log --follow` when tracking file history through complex merge topologies, such as diamond merges. The current implementation is slow because it performs redundant diff calculations when following renames across non-linear histories. The series is now at version 3, with changes since v2 including a new test case for rename-following through forks with `-m` and a more granular split of the refactoring steps.

The key technical work is in `log-tree.c`, where the rename-following logic is being refactored to avoid redundant diffs. The series also adds a new test file, `t/t4291-log-follow-merge.sh`, to cover merge/rename edge cases. So far, the series has received one substantive review from Patrick Steinhardt, who identified three distinct issues addressed by the third patch: two bugs (one where `--follow` lost history on the second parent of a merge, and another where `git log -m --follow` behaved incorrectly) and a performance regression. Patrick suggested splitting the patch into smaller, issue-specific changes for clarity but did not raise any major objections. His review did not include benchmark results, so the claimed performance improvement remains unvalidated. The series appears to be advancing without major controversy, but no other reviewers have weighed in yet.
