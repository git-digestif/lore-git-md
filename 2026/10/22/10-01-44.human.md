This is a substantive review of Emily Shaffer’s community voting process patch. Phillip Wood engages thoughtfully with the eligibility criteria, raising practical concerns about how to define and measure contributions fairly while preventing manipulation. He suggests several refinements: counting patch *series* rather than individual patches, measuring contributions by when they reached `master` (not `next`), requiring contributions to be at least a month old before a proposal is posted, and limiting eligibility to contributors active in the past two years. He also questions how to quantify "substantial" code reviews (does "LGTM" count?) and whether bug reports should qualify, noting that even mailing-list-based eligibility is harder to script than it first appears. His tone is constructive, focusing on trade-offs between inclusivity, practicality, and resistance to dogpiling. The review advances the thread by surfacing concrete implementation challenges that will need resolution before the process can be finalized.
