This is a user-focused review in the ongoing discussion about how to name and structure the new `--delete-integrated` flag for `git branch --delete-merged`. Jasper Mayone, who previously reported usability concerns with the series, now weighs in on the naming debate between Harald Nordgren and Junio C Hamano.

Jasper strongly endorses `--delete-integrated` as the flag name, aligning with Harald’s preference. He argues that "integrated" is already used in Git’s documentation, avoids implying a specific workflow (like "merged" or "squashed"), and clearly conveys the goal of branch cleanup. His proposal keeps the existing `--delete-merged <pattern>` for strict tip-reachability and introduces `--delete-integrated <pattern>` as a separate flag that includes both rebased and squash-merged branches by default. For users who want finer control (e.g., only rebased branches), he suggests a config variable to adjust the behavior, though he doesn’t specify the exact syntax.

On the false-positive issue (e.g., Ben Knoble’s "npm update" scenario), Jasper suggests a simple mitigation: require that the landing commit’s author also authored a commit on the branch. This would exclude cases where another author independently implemented the same change, but it would still allow "git am" workflows (which preserve authorship) and forge-style squash merges (which typically keep the PR author as the committer). He acknowledges this is untested but offers it as a practical idea to explore.

The email doesn’t address the unresolved questions about whether the new flag should enable both squash and rebase detection by default or require explicit opt-in, nor does it engage with the algorithmic trade-offs (e.g., patch IDs, author checks, or `git range-diff`). Instead, it focuses narrowly on making the CLI intuitive for users who primarily work with squash merges. The next steps will likely involve testing Jasper’s author-check proposal and gathering broader feedback on the flag’s structure.
