This is the third patch in Andy Koppe’s five-part documentation series aligning Git’s terminology around "pseudorefs" and "root refs" with the 2024 redefinition. The patch updates six manual pages to replace outdated uses of "pseudoref" (now reserved for FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD) with the correct term "root ref" for refs outside the "refs/" hierarchy, such as HEAD and ORIG_HEAD.

The changes are mechanical but precise: `git-ls-remote(1)` now describes `--refs` as omitting refs outside "refs/" rather than pseudorefs, and its example clarifies that HEAD is the only root ref advertised by `git-upload-pack`. `git-update-ref(1)` drops the incorrect mention of ORIG_HEAD in its reflog section, noting that only HEAD gets a reflog by default. `git-worktree(1)` updates its sharing rules to state that both root refs and pseudorefs are per-worktree, removing the outdated reference to their location under `$GIT_DIR` (which doesn’t apply to the reftable backend). Minor corrections—like fixing "symbolics" to "symbolic refs" in `git-ls-remote(1)` and clarifying `core.logAllRefUpdates`’s scope—round out the patch.

The work is uncontroversial and well-scoped, focusing on consistency rather than behavior changes. The commit message clearly explains the rationale and the specific adjustments, making it easy to follow. This patch, like the rest of the series, looks like a straightforward cleanup that should help users and contributors avoid confusion from outdated terminology. No objections or open questions have surfaced yet, and the changes align neatly with the 2024 terminology shift.
