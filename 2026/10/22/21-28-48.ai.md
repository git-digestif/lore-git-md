This is a documentation and terminology cleanup series targeting the glossary and scattered references to "pseudorefs" and "root refs" in Git's codebase, documentation, and tests.

The series stems from a 2024 change (993d57eded) that redefined "pseudoref" to mean only FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD—special files not stored in ref backends—while other refs outside the "refs/" hierarchy (like HEAD and ORIG_HEAD) are now called "root refs." Despite this, many comments, docs, and tests still use the old, broader meaning of "pseudoref." The series addresses this by:

1. Adding a formal "root ref" entry to the glossary and clarifying the "pseudoref" entry to reflect the current definition.
2. Updating the glossary entry for per-worktree refs, which was outdated.
3. Systematically replacing outdated uses of "pseudoref" with "root ref" across documentation, code comments, and tests.
4. Fixing minor inaccuracies in related documentation, such as which refs get reflogs in `git-update-ref(1)` and the behavior of `core.logAllRefUpdates`.

The patch touches 21 files, including glossary updates, man pages (e.g., `git-update-ref`, `git-worktree`), built-in commands (`for-each-ref`), refs internals (`refs.h`, `revision.c`), and test scripts. The changes are mechanical but thorough, ensuring consistency with the 2024 terminology shift. The author notes one deliberate omission: `__git_pseudoref_exists()` in the completion script is left unchanged (except for its comment) due to ambiguity about what term would cover both pseudorefs and root refs.

The series appears well-scoped and uncontroversial, focusing on terminology alignment rather than behavioral changes. It’s a follow-up to an earlier refactoring and should help avoid confusion for users and contributors encountering inconsistent documentation. No functional regressions or new features are introduced.
