This thread is a small but useful update to Git’s contribution guidelines. Jon Simons proposed a patch to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` that explicitly discourages splitting a test and its corresponding fix into separate commits—an anti-pattern that forces reviewers to hunt through multiple commits to see the behavior being tested. The patch aims to formalize what has long been an informal reviewer preference: that bugfix patches should include their tests in `test_expect_success` blocks, ensuring the diff context shows both the breakage and the fix in one place.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, replied with a suggested revision to Jon’s text. While he agrees with the goal, he found the original phrasing too permissive, particularly the mention of `test_expect_failure` as a local development tool, which he worries could be misread as an endorsement of practices the project doesn’t actually recommend. Instead, he proposed a more direct alternative: a single sentence stating that bugfix patches should include tests that demonstrate the breakage when the fix is omitted. The discussion remains narrowly focused on wording, not the underlying principle, and no technical objections have been raised. Once the text is adjusted, the patch is likely to be accepted, as it codifies existing norms rather than introducing new ones. No other participants have weighed in yet.
