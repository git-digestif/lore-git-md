This thread is about a small but practical update to Git’s contribution guidelines. Jon Simons has proposed a patch to `Documentation/SubmittingPatches` that discourages the common but inefficient practice of splitting a test and its corresponding fix into two separate commits—one introducing a failing test (`test_expect_failure`) and another converting it to a passing one (`test_expect_success`). The patch argues that this split makes reviews harder because the diff no longer shows the behavior being tested alongside the fix. Instead, it recommends combining the test and fix into a single commit, while acknowledging that `test_expect_failure` can still be useful during local development.

The change is narrowly scoped, touching only the documentation file, and doesn’t introduce new technical concepts. It formalizes an existing reviewer preference, likely based on observed inefficiencies in the review process, and frames the update as a way to reduce mailing list back-and-forth. The patch has been sent to key figures like Junio Hamano (the maintainer) and Patrick Steinhardt (a prolific contributor), suggesting it’s intended for broad visibility. So far, there’s no indication of controversy—it codifies a norm rather than proposing a new one—and the tone is collaborative, focusing on streamlining reviews. If accepted, this will subtly shift how contributors structure their test-related patches, making them more review-friendly by default.
