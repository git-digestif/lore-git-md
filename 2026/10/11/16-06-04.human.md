This is a bugfix patch targeting the ZIP archive backend, addressing one of the three date-handling inconsistencies reported by Matthew E. Luallen. Specifically, it fixes the **ZIP extended timestamp wrap** issue (issue #2 in the thread), where commits dated after 2106-02-07 (epoch 2³²) produce invalid extended timestamps that wrap to 1970, causing silent data inconsistencies in tools like UnZip.

The patch modifies `archive-zip.c` to **omit the Extended Timestamp Extra Field (ID 0x5455)** entirely when the commit time exceeds the 32-bit signed integer range (i.e., when `args->time > 0x7fffffff`). This avoids emitting a malformed timestamp that would be misinterpreted by tools. The change is minimal and surgical: it uses a `strbuf` to conditionally prepare the extra field, simplifying the output logic and avoiding redundant checks. The central directory and local header now both respect the same condition, ensuring consistency.

Test coverage is added via `t/t5004-archive-corner-cases.sh`, verifying that:
1. A ZIP archive with a 32-bit-safe timestamp (2147483647) includes the UT extra field.
2. A ZIP archive with a timestamp exceeding 32-bit (2147483648) omits the UT extra field.
3. The presence/absence of the field is detectable via `zipinfo -v`.

The patch aligns with René Scharfe’s updated position in the thread: **rejection (omission) is preferable to clamping or wrapping** for out-of-range timestamps, as it avoids silent misinterpretation by tools. This is a narrower fix than Matthew’s original proposal (which leaned toward rejection for both legacy DOS and extended timestamps), but it directly addresses the most acute issue—the extended timestamp wrap—without waiting for broader policy consensus. The commit message is clear and cites the original report, making the context easy to trace.

The patch looks well-crafted: it’s focused, avoids behavior changes for in-range timestamps, and includes tests. It’s a pragmatic step toward resolving the ZIP export inconsistency while the broader policy debate (e.g., whether to reject all out-of-range timestamps) continues.
