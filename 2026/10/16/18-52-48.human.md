This is a reply in the ongoing discussion about Git’s inconsistent branch name validation, specifically the usability trap around the `@` branch name. Junio C Hamano responds to Kristoffer Haugsbakk’s workaround (`git symbolic-ref refs/H HEAD`) with a blunt dismissal: while the workaround is clever, it does nothing to address the core issue—`@` remains a problematic shorthand that conflicts with Git’s ref name rules and creates a usability trap when used as a branch name.

The email reinforces Junio’s unambiguous opposition to allowing `@` as a branch name, framing it as a "bad habit" that violates Git’s design principles. His stance aligns with the stricter validation argument (reject bare `@` in porcelain commands) and explicitly rejects the convenience trade-off defended by Bence Csókás and D. Ben Knoble. The reply does not propose a technical solution or engage with the implementation details of tightening validation in `check-ref-format.c`, but it adds maintainer weight to the position that the inconsistency should be resolved by rejecting `@` outright, not by preserving it as a shorthand.

For readers following the thread, this email signals that the design debate is effectively settled in favor of stricter validation, despite lingering convenience arguments. The next step will likely be a patch to enforce this in `--branch` mode, though no such patch has been proposed yet. The workaround discussion is now moot—Junio’s reply makes it clear that the project’s priority is consistency with Git’s ref name rules, not user convenience for a problematic shorthand.
