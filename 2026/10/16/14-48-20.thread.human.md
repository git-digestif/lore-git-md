This thread began with Patrick Steinhardt’s patch to fix GitLab CI job failures in merge requests with over 50 commits by forcing full clones. The patch itself—a three-line addition to `.gitlab-ci.yml`—is uncontested and will likely be queued by Junio C Hamano. However, the discussion has expanded into whether the "check-whitespace" and "check-style" jobs are worth keeping at all.

Junio initially questioned their value, suggesting they might encourage poorly formatted patches without providing real enforcement. Patrick conceded the jobs are unreliable and not authoritative, particularly "check-style," which suffers from inconsistent clang-format adoption. Karthik Nayak has now weighed in, offering a middle ground: while the jobs have false positives and aren’t enforced, they still catch occasional issues and serve as "guidance." He outlined deeper challenges—version fragmentation, stylistic leeway, and missing rules—that make full clang-format adoption unlikely, leaving the jobs in their current, imperfect state.

The patch will likely proceed, but the broader question remains unresolved. No one is defending the jobs as strict gatekeepers, but Karthik’s perspective suggests they may linger as low-overhead aids unless someone proposes a more robust alternative. For now, the thread reflects a pragmatic acceptance of their limitations.
