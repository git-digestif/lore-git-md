This thread began with Patrick Steinhardt’s patch to fix GitLab CI job failures in large merge requests by forcing full clones for the "check-whitespace" and "check-style" jobs. The patch itself is uncontested and technically settled—it’s a three-line change to `.gitlab-ci.yml` that mirrors an existing GitHub CI fix. Junio C Hamano has indicated he will queue it, likely to `next` or `seen`.

The broader discussion, however, centers on whether these CI jobs are worth maintaining at all. The "check-style" job is of limited value due to Git’s inconsistent adherence to clang-format (version fragmentation, missing rules, and stylistic leeway like line wrapping), while the "check-whitespace" job, though less noisy, is still seen as unreliable. Patrick has acknowledged these flaws and doesn’t strongly defend the jobs’ current utility, while Junio has softened his initial skepticism but remains unconvinced of their value. Karthik Nayak argues they provide marginal guidance, catching issues "here and there," but even he concedes their imperfections. The deeper challenges around fully adopting clang-format—such as inconsistent enforcement and version mismatches—remain unresolved, making it unlikely the jobs will improve soon.

The patch will proceed, but the thread leaves open the question of whether the jobs’ marginal benefits justify their overhead. No other contributors have weighed in, and the discussion has stalled without a clear consensus. For now, the jobs stay, but their long-term future is uncertain.
