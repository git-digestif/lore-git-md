This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about Git’s inconsistent branch name validation. Junio C Hamano pushes back firmly on D. Ben Knoble’s defense of `@` as a convenient shorthand, framing it as a poorly designed feature that creates more problems than it solves. His position is clear: the convenience of `@` does not justify the usability trap it introduces, and he considers its use a "bad habit" that should be discouraged rather than preserved.

The thread remains split on whether to tighten validation to reject `@` as a branch name. Junio’s stance carries maintainer weight and aligns with the stricter validation argument, but the convenience argument—backed by real-world usage data—has not been fully addressed. No patch has been proposed yet, and the discussion still hinges on whether the project will prioritize porcelain safety over interactive ergonomics. If a patch emerges, it will likely target `check-ref-format --branch` to reject bare `@` while preserving its use in compound names like `foo@bar`.
