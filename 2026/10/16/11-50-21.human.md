This is the **implementation patch** (4/4) of the incremental connectivity check series, now in its fourth iteration. The email is a follow-up to Patrick Steinhardt’s substantive review, focusing on **performance trade-offs, error handling, and code hygiene**.

Kristofer Karlsson presents **new benchmark data** across repositories of varying sizes (small: `git.git`, medium: `linux.git`, large: proprietary repos), confirming that a **1,000-commit threshold** is a conservative but effective cutoff for switching between incremental and full connectivity checks. The data shows incremental mode is **2.6–2.8x faster** for large/medium repos at 1,000 commits, while small repos (like `git.git`) see a **~0.7x slowdown**—but both modes remain fast enough to be negligible. Based on this, he proposes **three config modes**: `full` (default), `incremental` (always use the new mode), and `auto` (dynamically switch based on commit count). This makes the feature safer to roll out and allows gradual confidence-building before potentially changing the default.

On error handling, Kristofer concedes to Patrick’s point about avoiding `die()` in new library code, agreeing to **centralize `die()` calls at the subprocess boundary** while keeping existing ones. He also switches counters (`trees_loaded`, `blobs_checked`) to `size_t` for consistency. The patch remains **technically sound**, with Junio C Hamano’s earlier endorsement still standing. The next reroll (v4) will include these changes, along with the **dynamic fallback mechanism** and mechanical fixes from prior reviews. The series is now **effectively cleared for integration**, pending final feedback.
