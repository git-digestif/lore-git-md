This thread is Junio C Hamano’s October 2026 "What’s cooking in git.git" report, tracking the status of all in-flight topics in Git’s integration branches. The main focus is on recently graduated topics, new patches under review, and a handful of open questions. One graduated topic—`ps/pack-check-bigfile-threshold`, which prevents false corruption reports for large files—had a macOS-specific test portability issue caused by BSD `mv` prompting when overwriting read-only files. The fix, replacing `mv` with `mv -f`, is now finalized after a minor typo (`mv -v` instead of `mv -f`) was caught and corrected by SZEDER Gábor. The root cause (BSD `mv`’s POSIX-compliant behavior) and solution (`mv -f`) are uncontroversial, and no further changes are expected.

Sixteen new topics are now under review in Junio’s `seen` branch, including fixes for `git log --follow` performance, refs transaction handling, and deepen-fetch optimizations. Three topics have open questions: `mc/refs-rename-transaction` breaks integration and needs rework, `iw/connected-deepen-fetch-no-alternate-refs` requires clarification on its scope, and Junio has asked whether `mv/log-follow-speedup` might drop history on renamed branches. Five topics are awaiting rerolls, and four need review or testing. The rest of the graduated topics—covering reftable timezone storage, ODB alternates ordering, delta base cache corruption, and in-core stash apply—are now in `master` without further action.
