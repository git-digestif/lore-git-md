This is a brief, surface-level review in the ongoing discussion about how to fix a loose ref cache inconsistency in the files backend. The thread is focused on a bugfix for root refs (like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`), where the current behavior is unpredictable depending on when the cache is first created.

Patrick Steinhardt acknowledges Andy Koppe’s explanation for the original design—creating a temporary "cache" for root refs on demand rather than maintaining a persistent one. He concedes that the overhead is negligible since the `REFS_FOR_EACH_INCLUDE_ROOT_REFS` flag is only used in two places, both invoking it just once. While he initially questioned whether the solution could be more efficient or conceptually cleaner, he now accepts the approach as "good enough," noting that the term "cache" is somewhat misleading here but a historical quirk.

The discussion remains open on whether to adopt Andy’s minimalist fix, Junio C Hamano’s proposed dual-cache alternative, or Andy’s more ambitious suggestion to drop the flag entirely. This reply doesn’t advance the technical debate but signals Patrick’s willingness to move forward with the current patch if no better solution emerges. The patch itself is small, well-scoped, and unlikely to be controversial, but the architectural direction is still under consideration.
