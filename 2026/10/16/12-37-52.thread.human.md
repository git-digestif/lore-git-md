This thread is about a critical bugfix in Git’s signal-handling subsystem (`sigchain`). Ian Hu has identified and fixed a race condition in `sigchain_push()`/`sigchain_pop()` where concurrent signal deliveries across threads can corrupt the signal handler chain, causing the process to hang indefinitely at 100% CPU. The bug is triggered by operations like `git clone --depth 1` over SSH, making it user-visible and disruptive (requiring a `SIGKILL` to terminate).

The fix is minimal and targeted: it adds a repair mechanism to `sigchain_pop()` that reinstalls the pre-chain signal disposition when the chain is found empty but was previously pushed. This avoids the need for locking (which would add overhead) while ensuring the process can terminate cleanly. The patch is small (16 insertions, 1 deletion), touches only `sigchain.c`, and introduces a `pushed` counter to track chain state. The commit message is exceptionally detailed, including a reproduction scenario, the exact interleaving that triggers the bug, and validation via fault-injected A/B testing (300 runs).
