This thread is about a bugfix for a race condition in Git’s signal-handling subsystem (`sigchain`), where concurrent signal deliveries to different threads can corrupt the internal handler chain, causing an infinite CPU loop. The issue is triggered by common operations like `git clone --depth 1` over SSH and has no workaround. Ian Hu proposed a minimal patch that repairs the chain after corruption by restoring the original signal disposition, which Junio has queued in his `seen` branch for review.

Phillip Wood’s recent review shifts the discussion toward a deeper architectural question: whether the patch’s "repair after corruption" approach is sufficient or if the subsystem needs a broader overhaul to prevent the race entirely. He argues that concurrent unwinding of the same signal-handler chain risks not just corruption but also undefined behavior (e.g., double-free or use-after-free) due to handlers being invoked simultaneously in multiple threads. His proposed solutions—blocking signals during thread creation and adopting `sigaction()` to prevent reentrancy—aim to address the root cause rather than the symptom. This feedback could lead to a v2 series that either incorporates these changes or justifies why the repair approach is preferable.

The thread is now at a crossroads: the project must decide whether to prioritize the immediate correctness of the repair approach or invest in a more robust solution that serializes signal handling. Jeff King (Peff), the original author of `sigchain`, has been CC’d for input on this trade-off, but his position is still pending. No regression test has been submitted yet, though the commit message includes a reproducer. The patch remains under review in Junio’s `seen` branch.
