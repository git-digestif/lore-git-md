This is a **bugfix** targeting the low-level signal-handling machinery in Git. The patch addresses a race condition in `sigchain.c` where concurrent deliveries of the same signal to different threads can corrupt the signal handler chain, leaving the process stuck in an infinite loop rather than terminating as expected.

The issue manifests during operations like `git clone --depth 1` over SSH, where two SIGTERM handlers are pushed onto the chain (one for cleanup, one for tempfile removal). If two SIGTERMs arrive nearly simultaneously on separate threads, both handlers may read the same chain length before decrementing it, causing the chain to unwind incorrectly. The result is a process that survives the signals but spins at 100% CPU, unresponsive to further signals until killed with SIGKILL.

The fix takes a "repair after the fact" approach rather than adding synchronization. It tracks the total number of pushes for each signal and, when a pop operation finds an empty chain but knows a handler was previously installed, reinstalls the original signal disposition (typically `SIG_DFL`). This ensures the stuck handler's subsequent `raise()` will land on a valid disposition, allowing the process to terminate normally. The change is minimal—just 16 lines added—and leaves the fast path untouched.

The commit message is exceptionally thorough, detailing the reproduction scenario, the corruption mechanism, the repair strategy, and validation results from a fault-injected test harness. The author also notes an alternative locking-based fix but chose this approach for its simplicity and lack of platform-specific code. The patch looks well-crafted and addresses a real, demonstrable problem with clear evidence of correctness.
