This is a reply in the ongoing design discussion about Git’s inconsistent branch name validation, specifically whether the `@` shorthand should be allowed as a branch name. Junio C Hamano responds to D. Ben Knoble’s defense of shorthands by reinforcing his opposition to allowing `@` as a branch name, framing it as a poorly designed convenience that conflicts with Git’s ref name rules.

Junio’s argument is technical and principled: while shorthands can be convenient, `@` as a standalone branch name violates Git’s clearly defined ref name rules and creates a usability trap (the branch can be created but not checked out via porcelain commands). He dismisses the convenience argument outright, emphasizing that not all conveniences are worth preserving if they introduce inconsistency or footguns. This reply solidifies his stance against the change, aligning with his earlier position that `@` usage is a "bad habit" that should be unlearned.

The thread remains at an impasse, with Junio’s maintainer authority now explicitly rejecting the convenience trade-off. No patch has been proposed, and the discussion is unlikely to advance without a compromise (e.g., preserving `@` in compound names like `foo@bar` while rejecting it as a standalone branch name) or a shift in Junio’s position. The next step may depend on whether another contributor proposes a targeted fix that addresses the usability trap without preserving the problematic shorthand.
