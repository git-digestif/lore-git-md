This is a **substantive review follow-up** in the `sigchain` concurrency bugfix thread. Phillip Wood responds to Ian Hu’s proposal for a thread-level fix, endorsing it as the safest architectural solution. He suggests waiting a few days for broader input before Ian invests significant effort, but his preference is clear: block signals during thread creation and use `sigaction()` to mask signals while handlers run, then drop the current "repair-after-corruption" patch entirely.

The exchange clarifies two technical points: (1) blocking signals (unlike ignoring them) delays delivery rather than suppressing it, so SIGPIPE deaths remain visible as EPIPE; (2) because `sigchain_push_common()` installs the same handler for multiple signals, masking should cover at least the common set (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT) to prevent reentrancy issues in non-reentrant handlers. Phillip’s tone is pragmatic and collaborative, focusing on correctness over minimalism. The next step is likely a v2 series implementing the thread-level fix, unless another reviewer (notably Jeff King, the original `sigchain` author) raises objections.
