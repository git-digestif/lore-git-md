This is a **substantive review** of the `sigchain` bugfix patch, focusing on the race condition's root cause and potential architectural improvements.

Phillip Wood engages deeply with the underlying issue: concurrent unwinding of the same signal-handler chain in multiple threads doesn't just risk chain corruption (the focus of Ian Hu's patch) but also creates a more fundamental problem—handlers being invoked twice simultaneously, which could lead to double-free, use-after-free, or other undefined behavior. His proposed solution shifts the framing from "repair after corruption" to "prevent corruption entirely" by ensuring signals are blocked during thread creation, so they're handled in only one thread. He also suggests adopting `sigaction()` to prevent signal handler reentrancy, a more robust alternative to the current `signal()`-based approach.

The review advances the thread by surfacing a critical design question: whether the patch's minimalist repair approach is sufficient or if the subsystem needs a broader overhaul to address the concurrency model. Phillip's feedback implies the current fix might be treating a symptom rather than the cause, and his suggestions (blocking signals in new threads, `sigaction()`) target the root issue. This could lead to a v2 series that either adopts these changes or justifies why the repair approach is preferable. The discussion now hinges on whether the project prioritizes immediate correctness (repair) or long-term robustness (serialization/blocking).
