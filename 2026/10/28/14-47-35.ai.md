This is a **bugfix follow-up** in the `sigchain` signal-handler concurrency thread. The author (Ian Hu) replies to Phillip Wood’s substantive review, clarifying the trade-offs between the current "repair-after-corruption" patch and two architectural alternatives: a per-signal spinlock (serialized chain updates) and a thread-level fix (blocking signals during thread creation plus `sigaction()`).

Ian acknowledges that the current patch is a minimal band-aid: it recovers from the corrupted chain state but does nothing to prevent concurrent execution of handler bodies (e.g., `close()`/`unlink()`), which Phillip identified as the deeper risk. He describes an earlier, unreleased attempt that used a spinlock to serialize chain updates, but notes it still allowed concurrent handler execution and introduced POSIX async-signal-safety issues, deadlock risks, and Windows portability problems. The thread-level fix—blocking signals in new threads and using `sigaction()` to mask signals during handler execution—would address both the chain corruption and the handler concurrency, but at the cost of a more invasive change.

The email advances the discussion by asking Phillip to weigh in on whether the spinlock approach is sufficient or if the thread-level fix is required. Ian signals willingness to implement the latter if that’s the consensus, and suggests the current patch could either be dropped or kept as a fallback. The tone is collaborative, and the technical framing is precise, making it clear this is a design decision point rather than a simple review nit. No code changes are proposed yet, but the next step is likely a v2 series depending on Phillip’s response.
