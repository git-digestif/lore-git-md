This is a **design clarification** in the `fetch: introduce +: refspec for matching-inspired fetch mode` series, now in its twelfth iteration and under active review for merging to `master`. The series aims to solve a long-standing performance problem in shallow repositories: a refspec-less `git fetch` negotiates history for all remote branches, which can be slow or hang when the remote has many branches. The v12 design introduces `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` to decouple the "what" (which refs to fetch) from the "where" (where to store them), resolving earlier objections to overloading `remote.<name>.refmap`.

The email addresses a **newly identified edge case**: how to handle `remote.<name>.fetch` entries that contain colons (e.g., `+refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/cabal/master`) when `remote.<name>.refmap` is also configured. Phillip Wood suggests either silently ignoring such entries or erroring out to avoid ambiguity. Junio C Hamano notes that the command-line equivalent (`git fetch --refmap=... refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/origin/master next`) stores the ref in *both* locations (the refmap destination and the explicit destination), implying that the same behavior could apply to config entries. This would mean `remote.<name>.fetch` entries with colons take precedence over `remote.<name>.refmap` for those specific refs, while the refmap still applies to others.

The discussion is **collaborative and low-stakes**, with no disagreement on the broader design. The outcome will affect users migrating from older configurations but is not a blocker for the series, which is otherwise ready for graduation. The resolution will likely involve updating `remote.txt` to clarify the precedence rules and adding test cases to `t/t5586-fetch-refmap.sh` to verify the behavior. This is a minor but user-visible decision that underscores the care being taken to avoid surprising behavior in mixed legacy/new configurations.
