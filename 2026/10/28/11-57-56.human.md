This is the fourth patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch series refactoring `builtin/commit-graph.c` to prepare for pluggable commit-graph backends. The patch removes the global `--object-dir=` switch, which previously allowed users to specify the object directory either before or after the subcommand (e.g., `git commit-graph --object-dir= write` or `git commit-graph write --object-dir=`). This behavior was atypical for Git commands, undocumented, and required global state to track the option. The change restricts `--object-dir=` to subcommand-specific use, aligning the command with Git’s standard option-handling conventions.

The patch itself is small—just three lines removed from `builtin/commit-graph.c`—and mechanically straightforward. It eliminates the concatenation of global and subcommand-specific options, ensuring `--object-dir=` is only parsed in the context of a subcommand. This simplification paves the way for later patches in the series to remove global state entirely. The commit message explicitly flags this as a user-visible change and invites discussion on whether it’s justified, making it the most likely point of contention in the series.

For users, this means the global syntax (`git commit-graph --object-dir= write`) will no longer work, though the subcommand-specific form (`git commit-graph write --object-dir=`) remains available. The change is motivated by the broader goal of eliminating globals in preparation for pluggable backends, but it’s worth noting that no tests actually relied on the global syntax, suggesting its removal may not break real-world workflows. The patch is well-explained and directly supports the series’ refactoring goals, though reviewers may push back on the user-facing impact.
