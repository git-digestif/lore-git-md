This is a **bugfix review** for the `sigchain` signal-handler race condition patch. Phillip Wood, a seasoned reviewer, raises a fundamental concern about the patch's approach.

Phillip argues that the root issue isn't just the double-unwind of the handler chain, but that the same signal can be delivered to multiple threads simultaneously, leading to concurrent execution of the same handler. This could cause undefined behavior (e.g., double-free, race conditions in cleanup routines) beyond the infinite loop the patch addresses. He suggests a more proactive fix: blocking signals when spawning new threads to ensure signals are handled in only one thread, and switching from `signal()` to `sigaction()` to prevent signal handler reentrancy.

The review is **substantive**—it questions the patch's architectural assumptions rather than just its implementation. Phillip doesn't dismiss the current fix outright but implies it might be a band-aid over a deeper design flaw. His proposal would require broader changes (thread creation, signal masking) but could eliminate the race entirely. The author, Ian Hu, has already acknowledged the trade-off in the thread and has a spinlock-based alternative ready, but Phillip's feedback suggests neither may be sufficient if the real problem is signal delivery itself.

This exchange is likely to shape the patch's future: either the current fix will be deemed acceptable as a minimal solution, or the discussion will pivot to a more invasive redesign. For now, the patch remains in `seen` awaiting further review.
