This thread is about a performance fix for Git’s object quarantine mechanism, which was unnecessarily loading all refs—including hidden ones—from the repository’s own object directory during connectivity checks. The issue caused a measurable slowdown, particularly in repositories with many hidden refs. After three iterations, the discussion settled on Jeff King’s minimal 10-line fix in `odb.c`, which compares the alternate’s path directly against the repository’s commondir to skip self-referential alternates during quarantine. This approach delivered a **65.5% speedup** in repositories with 200,000 hidden refs (0.29s → 0.10s) and a **16.7% improvement** even without hidden refs, as confirmed by the new perf test (`p5552-push-hidden-refs.sh`).

The patch, authored by Kristofer Karlsson, was **merged to `master` on June 12, 2025**, following explicit endorsements from Jeff King ("what's in your v3 looks good to me") and Junio C Hamano. The fix touches `odb.c` (core logic), updates documentation, and adds a perf test, while avoiding quarantine-specific logic entirely. A minor merge conflict with Patrick Steinhardt’s concurrent ODB series was resolved in v3, and the commit message was adjusted to Junio’s satisfaction. The only theoretical caveat—arcane working tree setups where `GIT_OBJECT_DIRECTORY` lies outside the commondir—was acknowledged but deemed too rare to block the fix.

Key participants included Kristofer Karlsson (author), Jeff King (who proposed and endorsed the commondir comparison), Justin Tobler (who shifted from a long-term ODB transaction proposal to supporting the short-term fix), and Junio C Hamano (who merged the patch). The thread is now closed, with no remaining technical objections or expected changes. The broader question of how `GIT_QUARANTINE_PATH` might interact with future hook use cases remains open but is not actionable for now.
