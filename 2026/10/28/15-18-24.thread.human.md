This thread is about a performance regression in Git’s object quarantine mechanism, where `rev-list --alternate-refs` unnecessarily loads all refs—including hidden ones—from the repository’s own object directory during connectivity checks, causing measurable slowdowns. Kristofer Karlsson identified the issue and proposed a fix in v2 of his patch, which repurposes `GIT_QUARANTINE_ENVIRONMENT` to mark alternates as "borrowed" (from another repository) or not, avoiding redundant ref listings. The patch shows a 67.7% speedup in repositories with 200,000 hidden refs and a 17.6% improvement even without hidden refs, as measured by the perf test `p5552-push-hidden-refs.sh`.

The patch has gained broad support from key reviewers. Jeff King (Peff) called the parent-path deduction approach "quite sensible," and Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, endorsed it as a pragmatic short-term fix while acknowledging the fragility of the alternate object store interface. Justin Tobler, an ODB maintainer, engaged with the root cause—lack of an explicit ODB transaction concept—but did not object to the v2 approach, framing it as a stopgap pending a longer-term callback-based solution. The patch is now ready for integration into Junio’s `next` branch, though a mechanical merge conflict with Patrick Steinhardt’s concurrent `odb-reload-alternates` series must be resolved first. Junio is evaluating whether to handle the conflict himself or ask the author to rebase.

No new technical concerns have emerged, and the patch remains focused on its well-motivated goal: eliminating the performance regression without introducing new APIs or fragile path comparisons. The conflict is logistical rather than conceptual, and the patch is expected to land in `next` soon. The broader alternate interface fragility noted by Junio and Peff may prompt follow-up work, but for now, the fix is seen as the most immediate solution to the regression. ODB maintainers Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler have not yet commented on the conflict, but their earlier feedback was non-objectional.
