This thread is about a performance regression in Git’s object quarantine mechanism during `receive-pack` connectivity checks. When a quarantine environment includes the repository’s own object directory as an alternate, `rev-list --alternate-refs` redundantly lists every ref twice, causing measurable slowdowns—especially in repositories with many hidden refs. Kristofer Karlsson’s patch (v2) fixes this by marking alternates as "borrowed" or not, using heuristics to exclude the repository’s own object directory from the ref listing.

The patch was merged to Junio’s `seen` branch on June 12 after he resolved a mechanical conflict with Patrick Steinhardt’s concurrent `odb-reload-alternates` series. The resolution preserved the patch’s core logic, and Kristofer has since confirmed that the two efforts are compatible, removing any lingering technical concerns. Junio and Jeff King endorsed the parent-path deduction approach as a pragmatic short-term fix, though both noted the alternate interface’s broader fragility. The patch is now on track for `next` and eventual graduation to `master`, with no open technical objections or requested changes.

Key details include a 67.7% speedup in repositories with 200,000 hidden refs and a 17.6% improvement even without hidden refs, as measured by the perf test `p5552-push-hidden-refs.sh`. The fix targets the ODB layer (`odb/source-files.c`) and introduces a `borrowed` flag to exclude self-referential alternates. While the broader alternate interface remains a known pain point, this patch addresses the immediate regression without disrupting ongoing ODB abstraction work.
