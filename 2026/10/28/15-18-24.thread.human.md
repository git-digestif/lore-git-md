This thread tackles a performance regression in Git’s object quarantine mechanism, where `rev-list --alternate-refs` unnecessarily loads hidden refs twice during connectivity checks, slowing down pushes. The issue was first addressed in **v2 of Kristofer Karlsson’s patch**, which was merged to `next` on June 12, 2025, after resolving conflicts with Patrick Steinhardt’s concurrent ODB abstraction work. However, the discussion has since expanded to explore more robust solutions, with **Jeff King’s minimal "commondir comparison" approach** emerging as the leading contender for short-term adoption.

Kristofer, the original author, initially proposed a pragmatic fix (v2/v3) that avoided self-referential alternates by deducing the parent object directory from the quarantine path. While effective—delivering a 67.7% speedup in repositories with 200,000 hidden refs—this approach relied on heuristics and was criticized for being too narrowly scoped. Jeff King then suggested a simpler, more localized fix: comparing the alternate’s path directly against the repository’s commondir in `refs_from_alternate_cb()`. This method is minimal (10 lines), future-proof, and avoids quarantine-specific logic entirely. Kristofer has since endorsed Jeff’s approach as cleaner and more maintainable than his own.

The thread now centers on whether to adopt Jeff’s commondir comparison for the short term or pursue a more principled solution tied to the ongoing ODB abstraction effort. Justin Tobler proposed propagating ODB transaction context via environment variables, which would integrate cleanly with the ODB work but requires adapting `odb_transaction_env()` to support a "read-only" mode. Kristofer has deferred to Justin’s judgment on the path forward, presenting four options: (1) adopt Jeff’s approach, (2) implement Justin’s read-only transaction mode, (3) add a regression test and defer the fix, or (4) wait for the long-term solution. No technical objections exist to any of these, and the v2 patch remains in `next` unless Justin or Patrick Steinhardt (who proposed an "append-to-alternates" alternative) suggest a redesign. The choice hinges on balancing short-term pragmatism with long-term robustness.
