This is the latest exchange in the ongoing effort to introduce a matching-inspired fetch mode for shallow repositories. The author, Harald Nordgren, responds to Junio C Hamano’s review of the v6 series, which identified two blocking issues: a glitch in handling simultaneous `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.refmap` configuration, and an unresolved design question about what the behavior should be in that case.

Harald proposes a resolution: when both configurations are present, `remote.<name>.fetch` should take precedence over `remote.<name>.refmap`. This is a straightforward tie-breaker that avoids the current fatal error, but it is not yet clear whether Junio or other reviewers will accept it. The proposal is minimal and does not address the broader design ambiguity—it simply picks one setting to win, without explaining why or documenting the choice for users.

The rest of the email is procedural: Harald acknowledges Junio’s test script (which demonstrates the glitch and the design question) and thanks him for providing it. There is no new code, no revised patch, and no discussion of the other open questions from earlier in the thread (e.g., whether the new mode should be available outside shallow repositories, how to handle remotes with no default branch, or migration of existing shallow repositories).

The series remains technically complete but blocked on this one design decision. Until the tie-breaker is agreed, it will not advance to Junio’s integration branches. The next step is for Harald to send a v7 that implements the proposed resolution and updates the documentation and tests accordingly. If the tie-breaker is accepted, the series could be queued quickly; if not, further discussion will be needed.
