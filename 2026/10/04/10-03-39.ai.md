This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about integrating incremental rebase conflict resolution into Git, either as a `contrib/` script or as a built-in `git rebase` option. Phillip Wood weighs in with a substantive review, endorsing Patrick Steinhardt’s preference for integrating the functionality directly into `git rebase` rather than as a standalone script. He proposes `--incremental` or `--progressive` as potential option names and strongly advocates for a single-shot workflow where `git rebase --continue` automatically advances to the next conflicting commit, rather than requiring the user to repeatedly invoke a separate command. This preference addresses a usability concern with the current multi-shot approach (e.g., `git-rebase-walk` or `git-bisect-rebase`), which he argues makes it unclear when the rebase is fully complete.

Phillip’s feedback is technical and focused on interface design and workflow clarity. He does not engage with the bisection-based performance trade-offs or the naming debates (`--first-conflict` vs. `--onto-first-conflict`) but instead introduces a new dimension: the importance of a seamless, single-command experience. His suggestion aligns with the broader consensus that the functionality belongs in `git rebase` itself, though no implementation details or code are provided. The review is substantive, as it identifies a concrete usability issue and proposes a specific direction for the interface.

The thread remains open on whether the project will accept any helper script in `contrib/` (with `git-bisect-rebase` still the leading candidate), but Phillip’s input reinforces the momentum toward direct integration into `git rebase`. His preference for a single-shot workflow may influence future design decisions, particularly if the functionality is eventually implemented as a built-in option. No objections to the core idea are raised, and the discussion continues to focus on *how* to implement the feature rather than *whether* to do so.
