This is a design-discussion reply in the ongoing `git brebase` RFC thread. Nico Williams responds to Alejandro Colomar’s implementation questions with concrete suggestions and shares his personal workflow to explain why detached-HEAD support matters.

Nico first links to a gist containing a `getopts_long`-like function for Bash, acknowledging that Git’s `--` separator convention makes standard `getopts` tricky but not impossible. He then sketches how the callback script could be eliminated: instead of writing a temporary file, the main script would expose a sub-command or flag that `git bisect run` invokes directly, passing any needed state via environment variables. This sidesteps the detached-HEAD limitation (which Alejandro had just acknowledged as a bug) and removes the need for temporary-file cleanup.

The second half of the email is a short autobiography of Nico’s detached-HEAD workflow. He routinely checks out `origin/master`, commits directly, and pushes to a remote branch without ever creating a local branch name. He relies on `git log --oneline` and `git reflog` to navigate, treating remote branches as the canonical symbolic names. This habit, he explains, is why detached-HEAD support is non-negotiable for him—any tool that assumes a local branch will break his flow. The tone is matter-of-fact, not confrontational, but it underscores a real-world constraint that any eventual implementation must satisfy.
