This is a **design discussion** about the proposed "limbo" object-format state for empty Git repositories, continuing the RFC thread started by Jeff King.

Brian m. Carlson responds to Peff’s proposal with a mix of cautious support and technical concerns. He agrees the usability goal is worthwhile but points out a major compatibility problem: advertising `object-format=limbo` would break every existing Git client from 2.29 onward, since `limbo` is not a recognized hash algorithm. He suggests that advertising *both* SHA-1 and SHA-256 capabilities might be a more compatible approach, though he notes uncertainty about whether older clients handle multiple `object-format` directives correctly and recalls past crasher bugs in this area.

Beyond compatibility, Brian raises policy questions: forges and organizations may want to enforce a specific hash algorithm (e.g., disallowing SHA-1), so the design would need a way to express such constraints. He also notes that the "first write" triggering the format adoption need not be a push—it could be a local commit or `git add`—implying the logic must be generalized beyond the wire protocol. His tone is pragmatic: the idea is feasible but not simple, and the devil is in the details of client compatibility and policy control.

The thread remains in the conceptual phase, with no implementation yet. If this moves forward, expect discussion to focus on backward compatibility, atomic state transitions, and how to expose policy knobs for hosting providers. For now, this is a heads-up for anyone running a Git forge: the 3.0 transition may require more than just flipping a switch.
