This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing documentation effort to introduce `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, the new man page consolidating merge conflict resolution guidance. D. Ben Knoble chimes in to reinforce a pragmatic stance on the `git commit` versus `git merge --continue` ambiguity Julia Evans raised. He advises that for most users, `git <thing> --continue` (where `<thing>` is `merge`, `rebase`, etc.) is the safer, more predictable choice—it’s what `git status` recommends and is unlikely to surprise. However, he acknowledges that there may be edge cases where a user might deliberately choose `git commit` instead, typically when they understand the nuanced differences in behavior. His framing is user-centric: simplify the decision for the majority while leaving room for experts to deviate when needed. This aligns with the series’ broader goal of making conflict resolution more accessible without oversimplifying to the point of inaccuracy. The exchange doesn’t introduce new technical content but clarifies the intended messaging for the guide, which will likely recommend `--continue` as the default path while avoiding a definitive claim of equivalence.
