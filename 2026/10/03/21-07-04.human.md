This is a design discussion about whether the proposed `git brebase` functionality should be integrated into `git rebase` itself or remain a separate command. Alejandro Colomar responds to Nico Williams' counterproposal with a defense of keeping `git brebase` as a standalone tool, arguing that `git rebase` should remain a simple, fundamental plumbing command while `git brebase` serves as a higher-level porcelain tool for complex rebases.

The core of Alejandro's argument is about separation of concerns. He suggests that `--abort` and `--continue` in `git brebase` should operate at the level of a single "iteration" (a segment of the rebase up to the first conflict), not the entire rebase operation. This behavior is essential for his use case of rebasing entire trees of branches, where users need fine-grained control over each segment. He believes integrating this logic into `git rebase` would muddy its purpose and confuse users about its role. Instead, he proposes that `git rebase` remain a low-level tool, while `git brebase` builds on it to provide a more sophisticated workflow.

Alejandro also questions Nico's suggestion of a `--direct` flag to disable the new behavior, seeing it as evidence that the functionality belongs in a separate command. His tone is pragmatic, emphasizing the practical benefits of keeping the tools distinct rather than debating the theoretical merits of integration. The discussion remains unresolved, with no clear consensus on whether the incremental rebase workflow should be part of `git rebase` or live alongside it as a new command. The thread is still in the design phase, with no implementation changes proposed for either approach.
