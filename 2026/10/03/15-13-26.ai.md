This is a user support thread about Git's bisect plumbing, continuing the discussion of how to robustly check whether `git bisect` has isolated a single commit.

Alejandro Colomar acknowledges D. Ben Knoble's earlier feedback, first noting a shell-scripting nit about redundant `test $? -eq 0` usage (now removed) and then confirming that Knoble's proposed plumbing pipeline—using `git rev-list` to count commits between `bisect/bad` and `bisect/good-*` refs—works for his current script. He shares a working one-liner: `git refs list | grep refs/bisect/ | sed '/good/s/^/^/' | cut -f1 -d' ' | xargs git rev-list`. However, he now raises a new concern: this approach does not account for skipped commits, which he is adding to his script. The thread remains open, with the question of how to handle skipped commits unresolved. No objections or alternative proposals have been offered yet. The key technical details introduced here are the need to track `bisect/skip` refs alongside `bisect/good` and `bisect/bad`, and the working plumbing pipeline itself.
