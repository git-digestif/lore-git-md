This is a brief but illuminating exchange in the ongoing documentation thread about the new `gitmergeconflicts(7)` man page. Junio C Hamano and D. Ben Knoble discuss the term "unstaged" in the context of merge conflicts, specifically Julia Evans' proposed phrasing: *"Git will not try to guess how to combine the changes, because it can’t tell which one is correct. Instead, it marks the file as conflicted and leaves it up to you to decide. The successfully merged files are staged, and the conflicted files are unstaged."*

Knoble, writing from the perspective of a user who adopted Git in 2016, finds "unstaged" intuitive and unambiguous in this context, interpreting it as "changed but not updated in the index" (i.e., not yet `git add`-ed). Junio acknowledges this interpretation is plausible but points out the term's inherent ambiguity: "unstage" could imply either reverting to the `HEAD` version (`git reset HEAD -- path`) or removing the file from the index entirely (`git rm --cached -- path`). Neither meaning aligns with what happens during a conflict, where Git preserves the three stages (base, ours, theirs) in the index for later resolution. His concern is that users might misinterpret "unstaged" as meaning the file is *gone* from the index, rather than preserved in a special conflicted state.

The discussion is a minor stylistic footnote to the larger series, but it underscores the care being taken to refine the guide's wording. Julia Evans has already incorporated most of Junio’s earlier feedback on this section, and this exchange suggests she may tweak the phrasing further to avoid the "unstage" verb—perhaps by saying the conflicted files "remain in the index with conflict markers" or similar. The disagreement is playful and low-stakes; Junio even notes that "we can call it being more honest" about Git’s limitations, and Knoble’s endorsement of the section’s clarity remains intact. No technical changes are required, and the series is otherwise ready for its v2 revision.
