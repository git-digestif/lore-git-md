This is a follow-up in the `git brebase` RFC thread, where Nico Williams responds to Alejandro Colomar’s proposal for a new tool to simplify complex rebases. The discussion remains focused on design and integration rather than implementation, with Nico pushing back on some of Alejandro’s assumptions while offering technical suggestions.

Nico argues that the proposed functionality—bisect-driven conflict detection and incremental rebasing—should be integrated into `git rebase` itself, either as a new flag (like `--first-conflict` or `--onto-first-conflict`) or even as the default behavior. He dismisses Alejandro’s concern about flag dependencies as a non-issue, pointing to existing Unix/Linux commands where flags only make sense in combination. While he likes the `--pre-exec` and `--post-exec` features, he suggests the tool’s broader utility might justify a standalone command, though he still leans toward making it part of `git rebase` because "it should have been this useful" from the start.

On implementation details, Nico offers several concrete suggestions: using `getopts` (or a `getopts_long`-like function) for option parsing, setting a `trap` to clean up temporary files, and avoiding writing the callback script to disk by passing it via command-line arguments or environment variables. He also flags a usability issue: the script forces the user to be on a branch, breaking detached HEAD mode, which he relies on. This is a clear pain point for his workflow, and he asks Alejandro to address it.

The email is a mix of high-level design debate and low-level feedback. Nico’s tone is collaborative but opinionated, framing the incremental rebase workflow as an obvious improvement rather than a niche feature. His questions about `jj` (an alternative VCS) and detached HEAD support suggest he’s thinking about broader adoption and edge cases, though he doesn’t engage with the more complex aspects of Alejandro’s proposal, like recursive resolution of "trees of branches" or merge commit handling. The discussion remains open-ended, with no clear consensus on whether the tool should be a new command, a `git rebase` flag, or a default behavior.
