This is a design discussion about whether the proposed `git brebase` functionality should be integrated into `git rebase` itself or remain a separate command. Alejandro Colomar responds to Nico Williams' counterproposal with technical and philosophical objections, sharpening the debate over the right integration path.

Alejandro pushes back against Nico’s idea of making bisect-driven conflict resolution the default behavior of `git rebase`. His core argument is architectural: `git rebase` is a low-level plumbing command, while `git brebase` is a higher-level porcelain tool built *on top* of it. He worries that merging the two would blur responsibilities and confuse users about what `git rebase` is supposed to do. To support this, he points to a concrete design question: if `--abort` in Nico’s proposal would only undo the current bisection segment (not the entire rebase), then `--continue` should similarly only advance to the end of that segment, not all the way to the final target. This symmetry, he argues, belongs in a separate tool, not in `git rebase`.

The email also reveals a subtle but important implementation detail: `git brebase` is designed to handle complex branch topologies (e.g., "trees of branches") by allowing recursive resolution, where each segment of the rebase can itself be rebased incrementally. This capability is central to its value proposition but is not addressed in Nico’s counterproposal, which focuses on linear rebases. Alejandro’s insistence that `--abort` and `--continue` operate at the segment level underscores this recursive design, suggesting that Nico’s proposal would either need to be significantly expanded or risk losing functionality.

Alejandro’s tone is polite but firm. He does not dismiss Nico’s idea outright but frames it as a category error: `git rebase` should remain simple and predictable, while `git brebase` can afford to be more complex and opinionated. His closing remark about `--direct` ("That hints it might just be a different command") is a gentle but clear signal that he sees the two approaches as fundamentally incompatible. The thread remains unresolved, but Alejandro’s response narrows the discussion to a key question: is incremental rebase a *mode* of `git rebase`, or a *separate workflow* built on top of it?
