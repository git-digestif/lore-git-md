This is a user report from Maciej Ciemborowicz that provides real-world motivation for the `post-worktree-*` hook series. Maciej describes a concrete scenario where his tool (which creates isolated container environments for each worktree) cannot rely on command wrappers because worktrees are often created by IDEs (e.g., VS Code's Codex) or other agents outside his control. This bypasses any `git-wt` wrapper, leaving no reliable way to provision environments or clean them up. Polling or documentation-based workarounds introduce race conditions and unreliability.

The email directly addresses Junio C Hamano's earlier objection that wrappers could achieve the same functionality. Maciej argues that wrappers are insufficient when the actor performing the operation (e.g., an IDE or AI agent) is not the one invoking the wrapper. This reinforces Domen Kožar's framing of the hooks as necessary for observability in uncontrolled scenarios, rather than as a convenience for users who already control the invocation. The report is brief but substantive, grounding the discussion in a specific use case that aligns with the series' goals.
