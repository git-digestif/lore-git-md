This is a documentation patch thread, part of Julia Evans’ seven-patch series introducing `gitmergeconflicts(7)`, a new man page that consolidates merge conflict resolution guidance. The series aims to replace scattered advice across multiple command man pages with a single, user-friendly guide.

Julia responds here to D. Ben Knoble’s feedback on the second patch, which updates `git-merge.adoc` to replace its redundant conflict-resolution content with a cross-reference to the new guide. Knoble had noted that the original text included a useful explanation of *what* a merge conflict is and *why* Git cannot automatically resolve overlapping changes. Julia now proposes a new "WHAT IS A MERGE CONFLICT?" section for the guide, adopting a deliberately lightweight, example-driven approach that avoids technical jargon like "3-way merge" or "common ancestor." The section explains that conflicts occur when overlapping changes cannot be automatically combined, describes Git’s behavior during conflicts (staging successfully merged files, leaving conflicted files unstaged with markers), and uses a concrete example (`hello.py`) to illustrate the concept. Knoble endorses the addition, calling it clear and harmlessly simplified.

The new section directly addresses the abruptness of the updated `git-merge` man page for users unfamiliar with merge conflicts, while maintaining the series’ focus on practical, user-tested clarity. It is likely to be included in the next revision (v2), alongside previously agreed improvements like the `git log --merge -p` guidance. No new technical blockers or controversies are introduced; the series remains on track pending mechanical fixes (e.g., `command-list.txt` registration, `.gitattributes` integration) and the resolution of cherry-pick-specific content retention in the cherry-pick man page. Julia’s responsiveness to feedback continues to refine the guide’s completeness and accessibility.
