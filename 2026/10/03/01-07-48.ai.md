This is a **design discussion** about the proposed "limbo" object-format state for empty Git repositories, specifically a **substantive review** of the feasibility and compatibility implications.

Brian M. Carlson responds to Jeff King’s RFC with a mix of cautious support and technical concerns. He agrees the usability goal is worthwhile but flags a critical compatibility issue: advertising `object-format=limbo` would break every existing Git client from 2.29 onward, since `limbo` is not a recognized hash algorithm. He suggests exploring whether multiple `object-format` directives could work instead, though he notes uncertainty about older clients’ support and recalls past crasher bugs in this area.

Beyond compatibility, Brian raises policy considerations: forges and organizations may want to enforce specific hash algorithms (e.g., disallow SHA-1), so the design must accommodate configurable restrictions. He also hints at broader implications—this mechanism might need to apply to operations beyond pushes (e.g., local commits), requiring careful handling of the repository’s config during the initial state transition.

The tone is constructive but pragmatic: while the idea is feasible, it’s not simple. The discussion now turns on whether the complexity is justified and how to address the compatibility and policy challenges. Key technical details for follow-up include the wire protocol’s handling of multiple `object-format` capabilities, atomicity of the state transition, and server-side configuration hooks for enforcing hash-algorithm policies.
