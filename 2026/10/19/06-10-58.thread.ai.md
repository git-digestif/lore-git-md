Core problem or goal
Restore the performance of `git log --follow` after a severe regression introduced by commit 304812ed33 ("log: improve --follow following renames for non-linear history"). The regression caused a test case on linux.git to balloon from ~28s to ~6m43s; the v3 series reduces this to ~37s while also fixing edge cases, improving test coverage, and splitting the fix into reviewable increments.

Current status
Under review. The v3 series is the first multi-patch iteration, replacing the original standalone patch. The series is a direct response to a known regression with a clear performance impact and operational urgency (GitLab.com incident).

Key technical details
- **Files touched**: `log-tree.c` (core logic), `t/t4219-log-follow-merge.sh` (new test cases).
- **Subsystems**: revision walking, diff machinery, merge commit handling.
- **New functions**: `commit_has_path()`, `follow_needs_renames()`, `log_tree_merge_diff()` (refactored from existing code).
- **Behavior changes**: Preserves `--follow` functionality for merge commits with `-c`, `--cc`, or `--remerge-diff` (previously untested); correctly follows renames on the side of a merge that wasn’t taken.
- **Performance**: Test case improves from 6m43s to 37s.
- **Edge cases**: Handles merges where one parent kept a file while the other renamed and modified it; avoids expensive rename detection for back-merges where the path didn’t exist on one side.
- **Series structure**: v3 splits the original patch into four parts: (1) new tests, (2) refactor, (3) rethink of the approach, (4) final performance optimization.
