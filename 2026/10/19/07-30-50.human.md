This is the **review feedback** on the implementation patch (4/4) of the incremental connectivity check series. The exchange between Kristofer Karlsson and Patrick Steinhardt focuses on **configuration design, performance trade-offs, and error handling** for the new opt-in feature.

Kristofer presents **new benchmark data** across repositories of varying sizes (small: `git.git`, medium: `linux.git`, large: proprietary repos), confirming that a **1,000-commit cutoff** is a conservative but effective threshold for switching between incremental and full connectivity checks. The benchmarks show incremental mode is **2.6–2.8x faster** for large/medium repos at 1,000 commits, while small repos (like `git.git`) see a **~30% slowdown**—but absolute times remain negligible (0.1–0.3s). Based on this, he proposes **three config modes**: `full` (default), `incremental` (force incremental), and `auto` (dynamic fallback at 1,000 commits). Patrick agrees the cutoff is reasonable but suggests making it configurable via `auto[=<cutoff>]`, defaulting to 1,000.

On **error handling**, Kristofer reverses his earlier stance and agrees to **avoid introducing new `die()` calls** in library code, aligning with Git’s libification goals. He’ll refactor the patch to centralize `die()` calls at the subprocess boundary, preserving the existing connectivity check’s error-reporting model while avoiding new anti-patterns. The discussion also touches on **counter types** (`size_t` vs. unsigned), with Kristofer opting for `size_t` to match Git’s conventions.

The patch remains **technically sound** and nearly ready for integration. Junio C Hamano’s prior endorsement ("This is exciting") still stands, and Patrick’s feedback—while thorough—has been addressed without introducing blockers. The next reroll (v4) will include the **three-mode config**, **no new `die()` calls in library code**, and likely **additional explanatory comments** around merge-walk logic. The **stack overflow risk** in recursive tree verification (raised in prior reviews) remains unaddressed but is acknowledged as a potential follow-up. The series continues to target **large-repository performance**, with benchmarks showing **up to 190x speedup** for small pushes.
