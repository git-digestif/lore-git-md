This is a **design discussion reply** in the ongoing debate about `branch.autoSetupMerge=inherit` behavior. Ben Knoble shares his personal workflow to illustrate how users currently work around Git’s tracking limitations. He typically creates branches with `` `git switch -c topic origin` ``, which automatically sets up tracking for the new branch. For branches that don’t start at `origin` but should still track it, he manually runs `` `git branch --set-upstream-to=origin` `` afterward. His comment underscores that users already rely on post-creation adjustments to achieve their desired tracking setup, reinforcing the idea that `inherit`’s current behavior (and even Harald’s proposed fixes) may not fully align with real-world workflows. The reply doesn’t take a stance on the three competing approaches (minimal fix, Junio’s modes, or Harald’s current-branch inheritance) but implicitly supports the broader goal of making tracking setup more intuitive. It’s a lightweight contribution—more observational than technical—but it adds weight to the argument that the status quo is suboptimal for users who frequently adjust tracking after branch creation.
