This thread concerns a small but user-visible bugfix from Elia Pinto that rewords the misleading "the upstream is gone" message printed by `git status` and `git checkout` when a branch's tracked upstream is absent -- for example after cloning an empty repository. The message now reads neutrally as "does not exist", and the advice is conditional: it suggests `git push` only when a plain push would actually recreate the missing upstream, otherwise it suggests unsetting the upstream. The short-format `[gone]` indicator is deliberately left unchanged to avoid breaking scripts.

The patch is at v3, which incorporates Junio C Hamano's requested structural change -- moving the `push_creates()` computation inside `format_missing_upstream()` so it is skipped when `advice.statusHints` is disabled -- plus roughly 150 new lines of tests in `t/t6040-tracking-info.sh` covering mirror remotes, narrow push refspecs, `push.default` variants, and `pushRemote` configurations. Junio has now acknowledged the approach in his latest reply, confirming he understands the edge-case logic behind the `@{push}`-based check and signaling that the direction is acceptable; the series remains queued in `seen` as `ep/status-missing-upstream` with no graduation to `next` or `master` announced. The only other reviewer, Harald Nordgren, offered a bare "looks good to me" with no substantive engagement.
