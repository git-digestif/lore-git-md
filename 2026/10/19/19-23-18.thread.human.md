This thread is about a proposed change to `git status` that would make it compare the current branch against both `@{upstream}` and `@{push}` by default, rather than just `@{upstream}`. The patch, now in its second version, is authored by Harald Nordgren and aims to improve usability for users working in triangular workflows—common in GitHub-style forking models—who may not realize they’re missing upstream changes when their push branch diverges from upstream.

The discussion has taken a philosophical turn. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has challenged the patch’s justification, arguing that the need for this change reflects a "sad world" where users lack foundational understanding of Git’s branching concepts, likely due to GitHub’s influence. He contends that `@{push}` is typically under the user’s control and thus less likely to introduce surprises, making the default comparison unnecessary. Harald counters that the project should adapt to reality rather than clinging to idealism, noting that users often set their push branch to match upstream initially and may be caught off guard when upstream changes later.

The technical implementation is uncontested: the patch modifies `remote.c`, updates documentation, and adds tests for triangular workflows. Users can restore the old behavior via a config setting, but Junio dismisses this as insufficient justification for changing the default. The debate now hinges on whether the project should prioritize pragmatism (Harald’s stance) or maintain its current defaults while expecting users to develop a deeper understanding of Git (Junio’s stance). No other contributors have weighed in to break the stalemate, leaving the patch’s fate uncertain.
