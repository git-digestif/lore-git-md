This thread is about a patch by Harald Nordgren to change the default behavior of `git status` in triangular workflows—where users push to a different branch than they pull from—so that it compares the current branch against *both* `@{upstream}` and `@{push}` by default, rather than just `@{upstream}`. The patch aims to make Git more useful for less experienced users who may not realize they’re missing upstream information when working in such setups.

The discussion has evolved into a philosophical and cultural debate about whether Git should adapt its defaults to accommodate users shaped by GitHub’s fork-and-pull model (Harald’s position) or resist such changes to avoid reinforcing what Junio Hamano calls a "sad world" where users lack a proper understanding of Git’s branching concepts. Junio has critiqued the patch’s justification, arguing that `@{push}` is typically under the user’s control and thus less likely to introduce "surprises" that warrant default monitoring. He now proposes an alternative: external tools (like GitHub’s UI or documentation) should promote the necessary configuration (`status.compareBranches = @{upstream},@{push}`) alongside the `-u` flag, shifting the burden of adaptation away from Git itself. D. Ben Knoble has countered with a pragmatic, user-centric argument, describing his own workflow where he frequently checks divergence between local and `@{push}` branches to answer questions like *"Have I rebased lately?"*—suggesting the patch would reduce friction for users who already rely on similar manual checks.

The patch itself is technically sound—v2 addresses all feedback from v1, including expanded test coverage and historical context—but the core justification debate remains unresolved. Junio’s latest reply refines his critique by distinguishing between the *nature* of changes in `@{upstream}` (which may introduce surprises from others) and `@{push}` (which reflects the user’s own work). This narrows the discussion but doesn’t resolve the broader philosophical divide. The patch is under review, with no clear path to merging until the debate over its underlying assumptions is settled. Key participants include Harald (the author), Junio (the maintainer), and Knoble (a supporter), with Phillip Wood’s earlier feedback now addressed.
