This is a **substantive review reply** from D. Ben Knoble in the ongoing thread about Jun Wu’s configurable index lock timeout series (`core.indexLockTimeout`). Knoble, who previously raised design-level concerns about the series, now steps back from opposing it after further reflection.

He acknowledges that while he personally is unaffected by the underlying issue (IDE lock contention), the trade-off between command success rate and responsiveness leaves him uneasy. His concern is that introducing a timeout—even a short one—adds complexity to what should be simple workflows, and users *will* notice hangs in commands that normally execute instantly. However, he defers to the judgment of more experienced contributors (notably Jeff King and Junio C Hamano) who have expressed support for the direction, and chooses not to push back further.

The reply does not introduce new technical objections or suggest alternatives; it is a concession rather than a critique. For readers following the thread, this signals that one of the earlier skeptics is no longer actively opposing the series, though the broader debate about the default timeout value (1 second vs. longer) and whether a configurable knob is the right long-term solution remains unresolved.
