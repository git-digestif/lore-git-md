This is a documentation discussion about how best to explain Git’s `HEAD` concept in the new `gitdetached-head(7)` man page. Julia Evans responds to Junio C Hamano’s review feedback with user-research context, defending her phrasing choice (“`HEAD` is where Git stores your current branch”) against Junio’s alternative (“HEAD records the state from which your current changes started”).

Julia argues that the wording should align with how users *already* think about `HEAD`, citing quotes from a March 2024 survey of experienced Git users. The responses she shares reveal a wide range of mental models—some technically precise, others vague or even contradictory—but all using everyday language like “points at,” “is,” or “means.” She contends that Junio’s phrasing (“records the state”) diverges from this natural vocabulary and risks confusing readers who lack a formal Git internals background. The email is a plea for user-centered documentation: rather than imposing a technically correct but unfamiliar definition, she advocates building on the imperfect but widely held intuitions users bring to the concept.

The exchange is part of the broader detached-HEAD rewrite, which has already been merged to `master`. This specific subthread is unlikely to change the merged text, but it surfaces an important tension in Git’s documentation: balancing technical accuracy with accessibility. Julia’s research-driven approach—treating user misconceptions as a starting point rather than a problem to correct—may influence future documentation efforts, even if it doesn’t alter this patch. For readers following the thread, this email clarifies why the final wording was chosen and offers a rare glimpse into the project’s documentation philosophy.
