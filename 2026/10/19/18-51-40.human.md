This is a CI/build system discussion continuing the thread about Patrick Steinhardt’s patch to fix GitLab CI job failures in large merge requests. The patch itself remains uncontested and is likely to proceed, but Karthik Nayak’s reply adds nuance to the broader debate about whether the "check-whitespace" and "check-style" jobs are worth keeping.

Karthik, who works on Patrick’s team at GitLab, treats both jobs as helpful guidance rather than strict enforcement. He acknowledges their limitations — occasional false positives and incomplete coverage — but argues they still catch real issues and reduce reviewer burden. His tone is pragmatic, not dogmatic: the jobs are useful enough to justify their maintenance overhead, even if they are not perfect.

The email also surfaces the deeper challenges around adopting clang-format more fully. Karthik outlines four obstacles: the need for project-wide commitment, version compatibility across CI environments, inconsistent enforcement of existing style rules (like line wrapping), and gaps in clang-format’s coverage of Git’s coding conventions. These issues explain why the jobs are currently allowed to fail and why a full switch to automated style enforcement remains elusive. The discussion about clang-format’s future is deferred, but Karthik’s points suggest it would require significant effort to resolve.
