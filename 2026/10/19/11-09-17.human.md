This is the first patch in Igor Wiedler’s two-patch bugfix series (v3) targeting a performance regression in Git’s connectivity check for deepening fetches. The patch addresses the immediate issue by ensuring `--alternate-refs` are negated in *all* connectivity checks, not just non-deepening fetches, preventing multi-minute delays when shallow-fetching from large alternates (e.g., 7M objects).

The change is surgical: in `connected.c`, the `--not` argument is moved before the branch that adds `--all` for non-deepening fetches, ensuring `--alternate-refs` is always negated. The commit message explains the regression’s origin (commits 39b44ba771 and cf1e7c0770) and the real-world impact—reducing a 227-second connectivity check to near-instant for CI workflows using shared clones. Tests in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh` verify the `rev-list` arguments for both regular and deepening fetches via `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT`.

This patch carries forward approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler (from v1) and directly implements Junio Hamano’s design feedback. The fix is minimal, well-motivated, and backward-compatible, with no on-disk format changes. The only loose end is the deferred handling of partial clones (promisor remotes), which the series explicitly punts to future work. The patch looks ready for integration, with no outstanding technical concerns.
