This is the **substantive review** of the incremental connectivity check implementation patch (4/4), now targeting the v4 reroll. Patrick Steinhardt engages with the **performance trade-offs, configuration design, and error handling** introduced in Kristofer Karlsson’s latest response.

The review centers on three key refinements for v4. First, Patrick accepts the **1,000-commit cutoff** as a reasonable heuristic after Kristofer provides new benchmark data across small (`git.git`), medium (`linux.git`), and large (proprietary) repositories. The data shows incremental mode is **2.6–2.8x faster** at 1,000 commits for medium/large repos, while small repos see a **~30% slowdown** (but absolute times remain negligible). Patrick suggests making the cutoff configurable via `auto[=<cutoff>]`, defaulting to 1,000, to allow future tuning without breaking changes.

Second, Patrick endorses the **three-mode config design** (`full`/`incremental`/`auto`) as a safe rollout mechanism, though he prefers `auto` to support an explicit cutoff value. This aligns with Kristofer’s goal of enabling gradual adoption and potential future default changes.

Third, Patrick reiterates his concern about **new `die()` calls in library code**, a blocker for Git’s libification efforts. Kristofer reverses his earlier stance and agrees to refactor the patch to **centralize `die()` calls at the subprocess boundary**, avoiding new anti-patterns while preserving the existing error-reporting model. This addresses Patrick’s "code smell" objection without requiring a broader refactor.
