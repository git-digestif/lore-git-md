This is the second patch in Igor Wiedler’s two-patch bugfix series (v3) targeting the connectivity-check performance regression in deepening fetches. It addresses the root cause identified in the thread: alternate repositories that are shallow or grafted cannot be trusted as unconditional boundaries because their refs point to commits with missing history. The patch teaches `odb_for_each_alternate_ref()` to skip refs from any alternate that has a `shallow` or `info/grafts` file, ensuring fetch-pack and receive-pack no longer treat those refs as valid "have"s.

The change is minimal and surgical: three new checks in `odb.c` (`access(path.buf, F_OK)` for `shallow` and `info/grafts`), plus a documentation update to `rev-list-options.adoc` clarifying that shallow and grafted alternates are now ignored. The test in `t/t5618-alternate-refs.sh` verifies the new behavior by creating a shallow clone, adding it as an alternate, and confirming its refs are excluded from `--alternate-refs`. The patch is backward-compatible—existing alternates without those files continue to work as before—and leaves the on-disk format unchanged.

This patch directly implements Junio’s proposed root-cause fix from the v1 discussion, completing the two-step solution the series set out to deliver. Peff’s review of the series praised the low-level fix in `odb.c` and explicitly endorsed the design direction, while noting that partial clones (promisor remotes) remain undetected; the commit message defers that edge case to future work. The only procedural blocker—a Windows CI failure in the test script—was resolved in v3 by replacing `$TRASH_DIRECTORY` with `$(pwd)`.

The patch is code-complete, test-stable, and unblocked, with no outstanding objections. Approvals from Patrick Steinhardt and Justin Tobler carry forward to the core regression fix in patch 1, and Junio’s design feedback is fully incorporated here. This looks like a clean, well-motivated fix for a real-world performance pain point, now addressing both the immediate regression and its underlying cause.
