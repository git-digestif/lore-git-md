This thread is about fixing a severe performance regression in `git log --follow` that was introduced by an earlier commit. The regression made a test case on linux.git take nearly seven minutes instead of 28 seconds. Miklos Vajna originally sent a standalone patch to address this, but has since split the fix into a four-patch series (v3) to make the changes easier to review.

The series is now under active review. Patrick Steinhardt, a prolific contributor, has chimed in to acknowledge the urgency—this regression has already caused an incident on GitLab.com—and expressed a preference for landing the fix upstream rather than backporting it later. However, he also noted that he isn’t deeply familiar with the revision-walking or diff machinery involved, so his review will be more about process than technical correctness. The patch touches core logic in `log-tree.c` and adds new test coverage, and while the initial version was self-contained, the current series breaks the fix into smaller, more digestible steps. No substantive objections or open questions have been raised yet, but the thread is still early in the review process.
