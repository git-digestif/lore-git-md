This is a **substantive follow-up review** in the ongoing `sigchain` concurrency bugfix thread. Phillip Wood refines his earlier endorsement of a thread-level fix, offering two practical optimizations and clarifying signal-masking behavior.

The key new points are:
- **Optimization for thread-creation loops**: Instead of blocking and unblocking signals around every `pthread_create()` call, block signals once before the loop and unblock them after. This applies to functions like `preload_index()` that spin up multiple worker threads in a tight loop.
- **Signal-masking clarification**: Blocking a signal (unlike ignoring it) delays delivery until the signal is unblocked in at least one thread, so SIGPIPE deaths remain visible as EPIPE. This confirms the thread-level fix will not suppress synchronous signals.
- **Handler masking scope**: Because `sigchain_push_common()` installs the same handler for multiple signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT), masking should cover at least that set to prevent reentrancy issues in non-reentrant handlers.

Phillip remains firm that the current "repair-after-corruption" patch should be dropped in favor of the thread-level fix, which he now describes as handling signals in a single thread to avoid the issue entirely. The tone is collaborative, focusing on implementation details rather than revisiting architectural trade-offs. The next step is likely Ian Hu preparing a v2 series incorporating these suggestions, unless Jeff King (the original `sigchain` author) raises objections.
