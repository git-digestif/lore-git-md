This is a follow-up in the ongoing `sigchain` concurrency bugfix thread. Phillip Wood refines his earlier endorsement of a thread-level fix, offering two practical optimizations and clarifying signal-masking behavior.

He suggests that when Git creates multiple worker threads in a loop (e.g., in `preload_index()`), it is more efficient to block signals once before the loop and unblock them afterward, rather than blocking and unblocking around each `pthread_create()` call. This reduces the number of system calls without changing the safety properties.

On signal masking, Phillip confirms that blocking signals (unlike ignoring them) merely delays delivery until the signal is unblocked in at least one thread, so SIGPIPE deaths remain visible as EPIPE. He also notes that because `sigchain_push_common()` installs the same handler for multiple signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT), the handler should mask all of them during execution to prevent reentrancy issues in non-reentrant code.

The tone remains collaborative; Phillip still prefers dropping the current "repair-after-corruption" patch entirely in favor of the thread-level fix. No new objections have surfaced, and the next step is likely a v2 series implementing these refinements.
