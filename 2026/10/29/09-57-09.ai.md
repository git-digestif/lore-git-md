This is the v2 iteration of Patrick Steinhardt’s 12-patch refactoring series that converts `git rerere` to use Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism. The series is now fully aligned with Junio C Hamano’s feedback: the fallback subcommand has been renamed from `rerere_record()` to the simpler `rerere()`, the enum flags in patch 2 are now fully documented, and the cosmetic follow-up for patch 2 that Junio requested has been incorporated. All other patches remain unchanged from v1 except for the mechanical rename of the fallback function and its callers.

The series touches `builtin/rerere.c` (primary), `rerere.h`, `Documentation/git-rerere.adoc`, and callers in `apply.c`, `builtin/am.c`, `builtin/commit.c`, `builtin/merge.c`, `builtin/stash.c`, `rerere.c`, and `sequencer.c`. The test suite (`t/t4200-rerere.sh`) receives a minor update in patch 10 to drop a negative test for the old global-flag behavior. No new CLI options, config keys, or test files are introduced; the series is purely internal restructuring.

Key symbols renamed or introduced: the fallback function is now `rerere()` (replacing `repo_rerere()`), and the six subcommands are implemented as `cmd_rerere_forget()`, `cmd_rerere_clear()`, `cmd_rerere_status()`, `cmd_rerere_remaining()`, `cmd_rerere_diff()`, and `cmd_rerere_gc()`. The flags are now passed as an `enum rerere_flags` with all four values (`RERERE_AUTOUPDATE`, `RERERE_NOAUTOUPDATE`, `RERERE_READONLY`, `RERERE_NOWAIT`) documented in the header. Patch 4 hard-codes `RERERE_READONLY` for the "status" and "diff" subcommands, removing the unused `--[no-]rerere-autoupdate` flag from their parsers. Patch 11 replaces the hand-rolled `if (!strcmp(...))` ladder with an `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` table, and patch 12 adds per-subcommand usage strings to the man page and to each subcommand function.

The series is now uncontested and ready for integration once Junio picks it up. The only remaining action is Junio’s final sign-off, which is expected to be routine given the alignment with his feedback. The patches are mechanical, well-scoped, and bring `git rerere` in line with the rest of Git’s multi-subcommand built-ins.
