However, the review then pivots to a broader design question: should `git notes add` fail only when two processes try to update the *same* commit’s notes, or should it fail on *any* concurrent update to the notes ref, even if the updates target different commits? Haugsbakk argues that the latter is overly strict, since two processes adding notes to unrelated commits should not interfere with each other. He sketches an alternative approach where a losing process would retry after checking whether the winning process’s update affected the commit it cares about, and only fail if there is a true conflict.

Key technical details referenced: the `base_oid` field, the `git update-ref` command’s expected-old-value semantics, and the notes ref’s commit structure. The review does not challenge the patch’s correctness or implementation, but it does open a discussion about whether the current fix is the best long-term solution for the notes subsystem’s concurrency model.
