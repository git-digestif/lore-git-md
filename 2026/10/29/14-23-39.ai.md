This is a **substantive review** in the `git worktree list` filtering thread, responding to Phillip Wood’s endorsement of Jasper Mayone’s pivot to extending `git for-each-ref`’s `%(worktreepath)` atom. Ben Knoble raises a critical edge case: whether filtering worktrees by branch state (e.g., `--merged=origin`) should include worktrees that are mid-rebase, and whether `git worktree remove` should allow—or even attempt—deletion of such worktrees.

The review highlights a potential workflow hazard: if `for-each-ref --merged=origin --format='%(worktreepath)'` includes worktrees in a rebase state, scripts or users might inadvertently delete them, risking data loss. Ben suggests that `git worktree remove` should fail by default for mid-rebase worktrees unless explicitly forced, aligning with Git’s general principle of protecting uncommitted or in-progress work. This introduces a new dimension to the discussion: the interaction between filtering (via `for-each-ref`) and safety guarantees in porcelain commands like `remove`.

The question of whether a mid-rebase worktree would appear in `--merged=origin` output is left open, but it underscores a broader concern about the composability of the proposed mechanism. The thread has already identified limitations of `for-each-ref` as a filtering tool (e.g., lack of NUL-terminated output, no main/linked worktree distinction), and Ben’s comment adds another: the risk of surfacing worktrees in transient, high-risk states. This reinforces Phillip Wood’s earlier observation that `for-each-ref` may not be the right tool for the job, even if the `%(worktreepath)` behavior change itself is a bug fix.

No specific solution is proposed, but the review adds weight to the argument that the project may need to revisit the design—either by tightening the filtering logic (e.g., excluding rebasing worktrees from `--merged` output) or by exploring alternatives like a `git worktree foreach` mechanism, as Eric Sunshine previously suggested. The comment is brief but substantive, focusing on a concrete risk rather than implementation details.
