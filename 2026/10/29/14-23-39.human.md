This is a **substantive review** in the ongoing `git worktree list` filtering discussion. Ben Knoble responds to Phillip Wood’s endorsement of Jasper Mayone’s proposal to extend `git for-each-ref`’s `%(worktreepath)` atom to report paths for rebasing or bisecting branches. Ben raises a practical concern: if the goal is to use this output to feed `git worktree remove`, removing a worktree mid-rebase could discard uncommitted work, which users may not intend. He suggests that `git worktree remove` should fail by default for worktrees in a rebase or bisect state unless a `--force` flag is provided, aligning with Git’s general principle of protecting work in progress.

The comment also touches on a broader question about the composability of the proposed change: whether a rebasing worktree would appear in `--merged=origin` output, which could affect scripts that rely on this filtering. Ben does not propose a specific implementation but highlights a potential usability pitfall that the project may need to address if the `%(worktreepath)` change is adopted. The review is concise but substantive, as it identifies a real-world scenario where the proposed behavior could lead to data loss.
