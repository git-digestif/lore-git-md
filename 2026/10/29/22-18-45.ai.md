This is a **substantive design discussion** in the `fetch: introduce +: refspec for matching-inspired fetch mode` series, specifically addressing the unresolved edge case about how command-line refspecs (or their config-based equivalents) should interact with `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`. Junio C Hamano responds to Phillip Wood’s concern that the current behavior—where a command-line refspec with a colon (e.g., `refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/origin/master`) *coexists* with the refmap (storing the ref in both `alt/master` and `origin/master`)—is surprising and potentially problematic.

Junio acknowledges the ambiguity in the current design but defends the existing behavior as intentional: the refmap was originally introduced to simplify fetching multiple branches *without* specifying destinations (e.g., `git fetch --refmap='refs/heads/*:refs/remotes/alt/*' next`), so a colon-separated refspec on the command line was treated as an *additive* request rather than an override. He frames the question as a choice between two interpretations:
1. **"Do more than the refmap"** (current behavior): The refspec with a colon adds a new destination alongside the refmap’s mapping.
2. **"Do something else instead of the refmap"** (Phillip’s preference): The refspec with a colon *replaces* the refmap’s mapping for that ref.

Junio does not explicitly endorse either option but notes that the second interpretation ("overriding") is plausible. This leaves the door open for Phillip’s proposed **override semantics**, where command-line refspecs (or colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries) would take precedence over `fetchRefmap`, aligning with user expectations and preserving backwards compatibility. The resolution will directly impact the implementation in `remote.c` (precedence logic in `get_ref_map()`) and documentation, which must clarify the chosen behavior.

The discussion remains **high-stakes for user experience**: the outcome will determine whether users face silent behavior changes (dual storage) or explicit overrides (Phillip’s model). Junio’s response suggests the project may lean toward **override semantics**, but no final decision has been made.
