This is a **tested review** of Harald Nordgren’s v4 series that adds `--delete-integrated` to `git branch` for detecting squash-merged and rebase-merged branches. Jasper Mayone confirms the series behaves as documented: `--delete-merged` remains unchanged (strict tip-reachability only), while `--delete-integrated` correctly deletes branches whose changes were squashed, rebased, or modified upstream, including cases where the same file was edited on both sides. The review is substantive and tested—Jasper ran the series against master on generated repositories, verified all test cases in `t3200-branch.sh`, and reported precise outcomes for edge cases like partial landings, reverts, and upstream modifications.

The feedback is **surface-level** but actionable: the help text (`git branch -h`) omits `--delete-integrated`, the error message for conflicting flags is overly verbose, and the cover letter retains outdated flag names (`--squashed`, `--delete-irrelevant`). Jasper also notes that branches checked out in worktrees are silently skipped, which could confuse users. His **Tested-by** trailer is a strong signal that the core functionality works as intended, but the usability nits and lingering author-check mitigation (not yet implemented) mean the series isn’t ready for `next`. The CLI design is finalized, so these are polish issues, not design flaws.
