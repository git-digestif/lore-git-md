This is a follow-up discussion in a thread about a bugfix targeting signal handling in Git's threading code. The original patch proposed a fix for concurrent double-pops of the same signal in the `sigchain` subsystem, but the author (ian4hu) is now revising the approach based on feedback from Phillip Wood.

The key technical points in this email are:
- The revised plan blocks `SIGINT`, `SIGHUP`, `SIGTERM`, and `SIGQUIT` (but not `SIGPIPE`) during thread creation to prevent signal delivery to worker threads. This preserves the expected behavior where upstream pipe failures trigger `SIGPIPE` in the main thread.
- Signal blocking will be implemented via a guard pattern (block once around thread spawn loops, restore afterward) rather than per-thread blocking. A `git_pthread_create()` wrapper will handle single-thread call sites.
- The affected subsystems include `preload_index()`, `index-pack`, `pack-objects`, `grep`, `name-hash`, and the read-cache entry loader. Long-running threads like `fsmonitor--daemon` and `simple-ipc` are excluded for now.
- The `sigaction` handling will be made more robust by saving/restoring the entire `struct sigaction` (including `sa_mask` and `sa_flags`) to avoid clobbering caller settings.
- A known limitation is that threads created by external libraries (e.g., libcurl's threaded resolver) cannot be protected by this mechanism, and this will be documented in the cover letter.

The email reflects a substantive review discussion, with Phillip Wood’s feedback leading to a more comprehensive design. The author plans to send a revised patch series (v2) after allowing time for additional comments from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio Hamano. The focus is on correctness and thread safety in signal handling, particularly for multi-threaded Git commands.
