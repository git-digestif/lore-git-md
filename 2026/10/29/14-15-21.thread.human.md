This thread began as a bugfix for a race condition in Git's `sigchain` subsystem, where concurrent calls to `sigchain_pop()` for the same signal could corrupt the signal handler stack. The original patch has been abandoned in favor of a redesigned approach.

Ian4hu is now proposing a new design that blocks common signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT) during thread creation to prevent worker threads from inheriting the main thread's signal handlers. The signals will be blocked once per spawn loop (rather than per-thread) using a guard pattern, then restored before joining threads. The design also plans to use `sigaction` with `sa_mask = sigfillset()` to prevent handlers from being interrupted by other signals sharing the same handler. The fix targets thread pools in several Git commands (`preload_index()`, `index-pack`, `pack-objects`, `grep`, `name-hash`, and the read-cache entry loader) but excludes `fsmonitor--daemon` and `simple-ipc` threads due to their distinct lifecycles. A known limitation—threads created by external libraries (e.g., libcurl's threaded resolver) cannot be protected—will be documented in the cover letter.

The author is awaiting further feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio before proceeding with a v2 series. Phillip Wood has already reviewed the proposal, but no decisions or approvals have been made yet. The thread is still in the design phase, with no code submitted.
