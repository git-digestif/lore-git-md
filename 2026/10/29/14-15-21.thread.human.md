This thread is about fixing a race condition in Git’s `sigchain` subsystem, where concurrent calls to `sigchain_pop()` for the same signal can corrupt the signal handler stack. Ian4hu has proposed a redesign that blocks common signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT, and SIGPIPE) during thread creation to prevent worker threads from inheriting the main thread’s signal handlers, using a guard pattern to block signals once per spawn loop rather than per-thread. Phillip Wood has endorsed the approach, agreeing that all signals—including SIGPIPE—should be blocked during thread creation.

Oswald Buddenhagen recently joined the discussion to clarify a technical nuance: while the practical outcome of blocking SIGPIPE aligns with the proposal, SIGPIPE is inherently thread-directed, so its handling requires separate reasoning. He does not object to the design but insists the distinction be documented in the rationale. The thread remains in the proposal stage, with no objections or alternative approaches raised so far. Feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio is still pending before a v2 patch series is posted. Key targets for the fix include thread pools in `preload_index()`, `index-pack`, `pack-objects`, `grep`, and `name-hash`, while threads like `fsmonitor--daemon` and `simple-ipc` are excluded due to their distinct lifecycles.
