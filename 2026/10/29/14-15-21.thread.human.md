This thread is about fixing a race condition in Git’s `sigchain` subsystem, where concurrent calls to `sigchain_pop()` for the same signal can corrupt the signal handler stack. The proposed redesign blocks common signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT, SIGPIPE) during thread creation to prevent worker threads from inheriting the main thread’s signal handlers, using a guard pattern to block signals once per spawn loop rather than per-thread. The author, ian4hu, initially framed the solution, and Phillip Wood endorsed the approach, agreeing that all signals—including SIGPIPE—should be blocked during thread creation.

However, Oswald Buddenhagen later clarified that SIGPIPE (along with SIGBUS, SIGFPE, SIGILL, and SIGSEGV) is thread-directed, meaning it targets specific threads rather than the entire process. This revelation prompted Phillip Wood to reconsider the design: blocking thread-directed signals could prevent the process from being killed if one occurs in a worker thread. He now suggests that concurrent unwinding of the `sigchain` stack for SIGPIPE might still be a practical concern and proposes using an atomic flag in the signal handler to serialize unwinding. The thread remains in the design-proposal stage, with no updated patch series posted yet. Feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio is still pending, and the discussion is now focused on whether the atomic flag approach is necessary or if the original design can be adjusted to account for thread-directed signals.
