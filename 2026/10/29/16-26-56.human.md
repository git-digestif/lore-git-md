This is a **design discussion** in the ongoing `fetch: introduce +: refspec for matching-inspired fetch mode` series (v12), now focused on a **user-experience edge case**: how command-line refspecs should interact with the new `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` configuration. Phillip Wood’s reply to Junio C Hamano surfaces a **potential inconsistency** in the current design: the command-line precedent (using `--refmap` with explicit refspecs) already implements **dual storage** (storing the ref in both the refmap’s destination and the explicit destination), but this behavior may not align with user expectations when applied to configuration. Phillip argues that users might reasonably expect a command-line refspec to **override** the `fetchRefmap` config, rather than supplement it, and proposes two alternative mental models for how the two config keys should interact.

The discussion is **collaborative and low-stakes**, with no disagreement on the broader v12 design (which is finalized and endorsed by Junio and Phillip). The outcome will affect **user experience for mixed configurations** (e.g., `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` + colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries) but is not expected to delay the series’ graduation. The resolution will likely involve **clarifying the precedence rules in documentation** and possibly **adjusting the implementation** to match user expectations, but the series remains on track for merging in the current cycle.
