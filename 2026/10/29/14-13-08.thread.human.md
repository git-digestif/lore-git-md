This thread is about a bugfix for signal handling in Git’s threading code, specifically addressing a race condition where the same signal could be processed twice due to concurrent pops in the `sigchain` subsystem. The original patch proposed a narrower fix, but after feedback from Phillip Wood, the author (ian4hu) has revised the approach to block `SIGINT`, `SIGHUP`, `SIGTERM`, and `SIGQUIT` (but not `SIGPIPE`) during thread creation to prevent signals from being delivered to worker threads. The fix uses a guard pattern to block signals around thread spawn loops, with a `git_pthread_create()` wrapper for single-thread call sites, and aims to preserve the expected behavior where `SIGPIPE` is still delivered to the main thread for upstream pipe failures.

The revised design touches several multi-threaded Git commands, including `preload_index()`, `index-pack`, `pack-objects`, `grep`, `name-hash`, and the read-cache entry loader, while excluding long-running threads like `fsmonitor--daemon` and `simple-ipc` for now. The `sigaction` handling will also be made more robust by saving and restoring the entire `struct sigaction` to avoid clobbering caller settings. A known limitation is that threads created by external libraries (e.g., libcurl’s threaded resolver) cannot be protected by this mechanism, which will be documented in the cover letter.

The discussion has been substantive, with Phillip Wood’s feedback shaping the new design. The author plans to send a revised patch series (v2) after allowing time for additional input from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio Hamano. The focus remains on correctness and thread safety, particularly for multi-threaded Git commands. No decisions have been finalized yet, and the thread is still open for further review.
