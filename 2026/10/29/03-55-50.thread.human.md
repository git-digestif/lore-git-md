This thread began with Farid Zakaria’s patch to fix a race condition in Git’s notes subsystem, where concurrent `git notes add` operations could silently overwrite each other. The original patch, which prevents data loss by failing on any concurrent update, is widely seen as a correct interim fix, but the discussion has expanded into two intertwined tracks: the technical design of a long-term concurrency model for notes, and a procedural debate about disclosing AI assistance in contributions.

On the technical side, the core question is whether Git should implement a retry mechanism that allows non-conflicting note updates to proceed, rather than failing fast. Junio Hamano proposed using Git’s existing merge/rebase machinery to handle retries, but Kristoffer Haugsbakk raised a critical constraint—merging notes targeting the same commit is unsafe because note content is opaque and could produce invalid results. This suggests a hybrid approach: retry only for non-conflicting updates (notes on different commits) while failing fast for conflicting ones. Farid has deferred implementing this, but the door remains open for another contributor to take the lead. Meanwhile, Farid has offered to post two additional race-condition fixes for `credential-cache` and `fsmonitor`, which may reignite the same debate about strict failure versus retry mechanisms in other subsystems.

The procedural side centers on Farid’s failure to disclose AI assistance in his review, as flagged by Kristoffer Haugsbakk. Farid’s response treats the entire thread as his disclosure but questions the lack of explicit guidance in `SubmittingPatches`, suggesting the policy may not be widely known. This could prompt the project to clarify the rule, though no resolution is yet in sight. Patrick Steinhardt’s sarcastic remark about AI’s role underscores the sensitivity of the issue but doesn’t address the procedural question.

The thread is now at a crossroads: technically, the next step is either a v2 patch implementing the retry mechanism (likely from someone other than Farid) or the posting of the additional race-condition fixes. Procedurally, the project may need to clarify its AI disclosure policy to resolve the ambiguity Farid highlighted. The outcome of this thread could set precedents for both Git’s concurrency model and how AI-assisted contributions are handled.
