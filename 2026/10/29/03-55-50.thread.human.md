This thread tackles a race condition in `git notes add` where concurrent updates can silently overwrite each other, leading to data loss. Farid Zakaria proposed a fix that makes such operations fail outright, preventing silent corruption but potentially being overly strict for non-conflicting updates. The discussion has since evolved into a broader debate about the notes subsystem’s concurrency model, with Junio C Hamano suggesting a retry mechanism using Git’s merge/rebase machinery to allow non-conflicting updates to proceed.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk has now raised a critical concern: while Junio’s approach is elegant, it risks producing semantically incorrect results when two processes edit different parts of the *same* note. Unlike source code, notes are opaque blobs, so a three-way merge could technically succeed but logically corrupt the note’s content. Haugsbakk argues that failing fast (as in Farid’s original patch) is preferable to silent corruption, though he leaves room for a hybrid approach—retrying only when updates are provably non-conflicting. This narrows the scope of Junio’s proposal but adds complexity to any follow-up implementation.

The thread remains under review, with no follow-up patch yet. Farid has deferred implementing the retry mechanism, leaving the door open for others to take the lead. Meanwhile, a procedural issue lingers: Haugsbakk flagged Farid’s failure to disclose AI assistance in reviewing the patch, a point that hasn’t been addressed despite Patrick Steinhardt’s sarcastic remark about AI’s role in contributions. The technical direction is now clearer—any v2 must either justify merge/rebase safety for notes or implement granular conflict detection—but the procedural question could still impact the patch’s acceptance.
