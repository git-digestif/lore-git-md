This thread addresses a race condition in Git’s notes subsystem, where concurrent `git notes add` operations can silently overwrite each other, leading to data loss. Farid Zakaria proposed a fix that prevents silent corruption by failing on any concurrent update, which has been endorsed as a correct interim solution. However, the discussion has evolved into a broader debate about Git’s concurrency model for notes, with Kristoffer Haugsbakk and Junio C Hamano advocating for a retry mechanism that would allow non-conflicting updates to proceed by leveraging Git’s existing merge/rebase machinery. Farid has deferred implementing this more complex approach, leaving the door open for others to take the lead.

The thread took a procedural turn when Kristoffer Haugsbakk noted that Farid failed to disclose AI assistance in his review, citing Git’s `SubmittingPatches` guidelines. While this doesn’t affect the technical merits of the patch or the proposed retry mechanism, it introduces a layer of scrutiny about transparency in AI-assisted contributions. Farid’s response to this request will be worth watching, as it may set a precedent for how such work is handled in the project. Meanwhile, the technical discussion remains focused on whether the retry mechanism is justified, how it should interact with Git’s porcelain/plumbing divide, and whether Farid’s other discovered race conditions (in `credential-cache` and `fsmonitor`) will follow a similar review trajectory.
