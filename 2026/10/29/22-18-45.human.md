This is a **design discussion** in the ongoing `fetch: introduce +: refspec for matching-inspired fetch mode` series (v12), now focused on a critical edge case: how command-line refspecs should interact with the new `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` configuration. Junio C Hamano responds to Phillip Wood’s concern that the current behavior—where a command-line refspec with a destination (e.g., `refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/origin/master`) *coexists* with the refmap (storing the ref in both `alt/master` and `origin/master`)—is surprising and potentially problematic.

Junio acknowledges the ambiguity but defends the existing behavior as intentional: the refmap was designed to simplify fetching multiple branches *without* repeating destinations, so a colon-separated refspec on the command line is treated as an "additive" request rather than an override. He suggests two interpretations of "something different":
1. **Additive**: The refmap and command-line refspec coexist (current behavior).
2. **Override**: The command-line refspec replaces the refmap’s mapping for that ref.

This exchange is **high-stakes for the series’ graduation**. The resolution will determine whether users must use `--refmap` to override `fetchRefmap` (Phillip’s preference) or accept that command-line refspecs with destinations are additive (Junio’s current stance). The outcome will require updates to `remote.c` (precedence logic in `get_ref_map()`) and documentation to clarify the behavior. Given that the series is otherwise ready for `master`, this is the last substantive hurdle.
