This is a follow-up discussion in a thread about a bugfix targeting signal handling in Git's threading code. The patch itself has been dropped in favor of a redesigned approach, so this email is not a patch submission but a design proposal for a future series.

The author (ian4hu) is responding to feedback from Phillip Wood on a previous attempt to fix a race condition in Git's `sigchain` subsystem, where concurrent calls to `sigchain_pop()` for the same signal could corrupt the signal handler stack. The new design will block common signals (SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGTERM, SIGQUIT) during thread creation to prevent worker threads from inheriting the main thread's signal handlers, then restore them before joining threads. The blocking will be done once per spawn loop rather than per-thread, using a guard pattern to simplify the code. The author also plans to use `sigaction` with `sa_mask = sigfillset()` to prevent handlers from being interrupted by other signals sharing the same handler.

Key technical details for future reference: the fix targets thread pools in `preload_index()`, `index-pack`, `pack-objects`, `grep`, `name-hash`, and the read-cache entry loader, while leaving `fsmonitor--daemon` and `simple-ipc` threads unchanged due to their distinct lifecycle. The author acknowledges a known limitation: threads created by external libraries (e.g., libcurl's threaded resolver) cannot be protected by this mechanism, and this will be documented in the cover letter. The author plans to wait for additional feedback from Jeff King (Peff) and Junio before proceeding with a v2 series.
