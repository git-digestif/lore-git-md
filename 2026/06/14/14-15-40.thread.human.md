This thread began with a memory leak fix in `git history reword`, part of Patrick Steinhardt’s `git history` command work. The leak occurred when commit buffers were dynamically allocated—such as when loading commits from the commit-graph—rather than cached in Git’s commit slab. Kaartic Sivaraam’s fix, a one-line addition in `builtin/history.c` to call `repo_unuse_commit_buffer`, was merged to `master` after minor editorial feedback from Junio. The patch also resolved related test failures (`t345x`), confirming the fix’s correctness.

The discussion expanded to reveal broader slab management gaps, including a separate leak in the topological walk slab (`init_topo_walk`), which Peff identified and fixed in an earlier patch. While the immediate leaks are resolved, the thread left open whether to address slab cleanup systemically (e.g., defensive freeing at program boundaries) or continue handling leaks case-by-case. Peff also suggested documenting reproduction recipes for future contributors, though this remains unaddressed.

This latest email is a v3 resend of the already-merged `git history reword` fix, with an improved commit message but no functional changes. The resend clarifies the leak’s root cause—commit-graph parsing bypassing raw object reads—and includes a minimal reproduction recipe. No further review or action is needed for this patch, though the broader slab management questions may resurface in future work.
