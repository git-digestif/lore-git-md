This thread is about a proposed improvement to `git worktree` that introduces two features: copy-on-write (CoW) worktree creation via `--reflink` and automatic reaping of ephemeral branches. The series, now at version 3, is authored by Jason Newton and has sparked significant debate, particularly around the ephemeral branch reaping feature.

The reflink implementation, which avoids full checkout overhead on supported filesystems (Linux btrfs/XFS/bcachefs, macOS APFS), is contested over whether it should be opt-in or default for tracked files. Windows ReFS reflink support has been permanently disabled due to a silent corruption risk, and macOS clonefile() support has been validated in CI. The ephemeral branch reaping feature, designed to automatically delete branches created by `git worktree add` when the worktree is removed, is actively disputed as either a usability improvement or an unnecessary redundancy given the existing `--detach` flag. Jason argues it provides a more natural branch-like experience, while Eric Sunshine and Phillip Wood contend it is disruptive and lacks a clear justification.

The thread has become polarized due to a heated exchange between Jason and Eric Sunshine, with Jason accusing Eric of nitpicking and working in a silo, and Eric criticizing Jason’s use of AI-generated responses and the patch’s design. Phillip Wood’s recent email further criticizes Jason’s confrontational tone and lack of constructive engagement, arguing that the patch fails to clearly articulate the problem it solves or justify its design choices. This procedural and interpersonal friction may need to be addressed before substantive progress can resume on the technical merits of the proposal. The series remains under active review, with key open questions about the default behavior of reflink and the necessity of ephemeral branch reaping.
