This thread is about a proposed set of improvements to `git worktree`, specifically copy-on-write (reflink) support for faster worktree creation and automatic reaping of ephemeral branches when worktrees are removed. The series, now at version 3, is authored by Jason Newton and has become contentious both technically and interpersonally.

The reflink feature, which avoids full checkout overhead on supported filesystems (Linux btrfs/XFS/bcachefs, macOS APFS), is now permanently disabled on Windows ReFS due to a known silent corruption bug. While the macOS implementation has been validated in CI, the feature remains contested: Eric Sunshine argues it should default to automatic for tracked files rather than requiring opt-in, as the untracked-file copying concern doesn’t apply there. The ephemeral branch reaping feature, which automatically deletes branches created by `git worktree add` when the worktree is removed, is even more disputed. Eric Sunshine and Phillip Wood argue it’s unnecessary, disruptive, and poorly justified, particularly objecting to its unconditional deletion of branches and redundancy with the existing `--detach` flag. Jason maintains that ephemeral branches provide a more intuitive, branch-like experience during the worktree’s lifetime, avoiding the "degraded halfway state" of detached HEADs.

The thread has taken a sharp turn toward interpersonal conflict. Jason’s latest reply escalates tensions by "matching energy levels" rather than addressing substantive objections. He dismisses `--detach` as an inadequate alternative and reiterates his technical justification for ephemeral branches, but fails to engage with Phillip Wood’s critique that the patch lacks a clear problem statement or design justification. Both Eric Sunshine and Phillip Wood have condemned Jason’s confrontational tone and lack of constructive engagement, with Phillip explicitly stating the discussion falls short of the mailing list’s standards. The series remains under active review, but consensus seems distant unless the discussion can be reframed around problem definition and opt-in alternatives.
