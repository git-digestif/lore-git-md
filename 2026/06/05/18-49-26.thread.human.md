The reflink patch (1/2) adds cross-platform support for CoW worktrees on btrfs/XFS/bcachefs (Linux), APFS (macOS), and ReFS (Windows), while the ephemeral branch patch (2/2) automatically deletes branches created by `git worktree add` when the worktree is removed.

The series is under active review, with the reflink implementation drawing particular scrutiny. In the latest feedback, brian m. carlson raised critical platform-specific concerns: Windows’ `FSCTL_DUPLICATE_EXTENTS_TO_FILE` (used for ReFS) is known to cause silent corruption due to a long-standing Git LFS bug, and macOS CI may not reliably test the `clonefile()` path. This suggests the reflink feature may need to be disabled on Windows or guarded by stricter platform checks before merging. The ephemeral branch reaping, meanwhile, remains uncontroversial, with its narrow scope (only auto-created branches) and unconditional deletion seen as a safe tradeoff.

Key participants include Jason Newton (author), who addressed v1 feedback by focusing the series and documenting edge cases, and Junio, who previously questioned the opt-in nature of reflink. The thread’s open questions now center on platform safety and whether the reflink implementation should be more conservative. A v3 revision is likely to resolve these concerns.
