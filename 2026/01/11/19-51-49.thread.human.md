This thread tackles two macOS-specific compatibility issues, both now technically resolved but with one still mired in architectural debate. The first, a two-patch series fixing an ISO-2022-JP iconv bug in utf8.c, is complete and ready for merging—it preserves conversion state during buffer growth and automatically detects Homebrew/MacPorts/Fink libiconv at build time.

The second, Curtis Allen Smith’s five-iteration patch series addressing a CRLF conversion size-trusting bug in read-cache.c, has become a flashpoint over Git’s design philosophy. The bug causes false positives in commands like `git status` when external tools rewrite files with CRLF line endings, and Curtis’s v5 patch fixes it by adding a conversion check in `ie_modified()`. While reviewers agree the patch works, Johannes Sixt argues the fix belongs in `git add` (to record the correct post-conversion size), and Junio C Hamano warns that unconditional content comparisons could regress performance by disabling Git’s lstat()-based optimization. Torsten Bögershausen’s recent proposal to move the check to `ie_match_stat()` avoids racy Git interference but doesn’t resolve the core tension between correctness and efficiency. The series is well-tested and technically sound, but the debate over where to place the conversion check—and whether the performance trade-off is justified—remains unresolved. Curtis has ruled out a proposed optimization due to edge cases, leaving the door open for further redesign.
