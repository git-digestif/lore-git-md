This thread tackles two related macOS compatibility issues, both now technically resolved but awaiting final architectural decisions. The first, a two-patch fix for an ISO-2022-JP iconv bug in utf8.c, is complete and ready for merging—it preserves internal state during buffer growth with automatic libiconv detection for Homebrew, MacPorts, and Fink.

The second, a long-running series on CRLF conversion size-trusting in read-cache.c, has reached its fifth iteration from Curtis Allen Smith, incorporating feedback from Phillip Wood, Junio C Hamano, Torsten Bögershausen, and Johannes Sixt. The core issue—Git incorrectly reporting modifications when clean filters or CRLF conversion alter file size without changing content—is now fixed, but the architectural debate remains unresolved. Five competing approaches are on the table: Curtis’s v5 placement in `ie_modified()`, Torsten’s new RFC optimization moving the check to `ie_match_stat()` (which eliminates redundant attribute lookups and reduces code size), Johannes Sixt’s preference for fixing size recording during `git add`, Torsten’s earlier `ie_match_stat()` proposal, and Junio’s insistence on preserving the lstat()-based optimization that avoids unnecessary content comparisons.

Torsten’s RFC patch refines the v5 implementation by consolidating the conversion check in `ie_match_stat()`, addressing inefficiency concerns but leaving Sixt’s fundamental objection and Junio’s performance trade-off unanswered. The patch is narrowly scoped, affects `git diff-files` and `git diff-index` in addition to index-refresh commands, and preserves all existing behavior for non-converted files. Curtis’s benchmark data shows negligible overhead in realistic scenarios, but Junio’s latest review frames the lstat() optimization as a baseline requirement, suggesting none of the current proposals may yet meet the project’s standards. The series is technically sound but may need a hybrid approach to reconcile these priorities before merging.
