This is a review of the final patch in a five-part series of Coverity-inspired integer-overflow fixes, specifically an acknowledgment from Phillip Wood that patch 5/5 -- which hardens the relative-date parser in `date.c` -- is ready. The patch itself modifies `approxidate_alpha()` to perform unit-length multiplication in `uintmax_t` using `unsigned_mult_overflows()` and `date_overflows()`, rejects parsed quantities exceeding `INT_MAX` before narrowing to `int`, and propagates parse failures so callers like `git reflog expire` report "invalid timestamp" instead of computing wrapped offsets. Tests in `t/t0006-date.sh` cover both a large valid input ("4000 weeks ago") and an absurdly large rejected one.

Phillip Wood's reply is brief but substantive: he confirms the v2 approach "looks good and is much simpler than the previous version," signaling that the earlier portability concerns he had raised about generic overflow macros have been resolved by switching to direct `uintmax_t` checks. His endorsement carries weight given his prior technical engagement with the series, and it leaves no outstanding objections to this specific `date.c` change. The series as a whole remains in Junio C Hamano's integration queue marked "Waiting for response," with patches 1 and 2 unchanged from v1 and patches 3 through 5 reworked to address review feedback.
