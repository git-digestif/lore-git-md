This is a documentation patch review reply in the ongoing thread for Julia Evans's `gittutorial-branches` beginner tutorial, the second part of a two-part series where the first (`gittutorial`) is already merged. Evans responds to D. Ben Knoble's substantive pedagogical review, agreeing to revise the patch for a v2 based on his two structural concerns.

On the first concern -- Step 8's use of hypothetical `one` and `two` branches to explain why new branches should start from `main`, which confusingly references merges before that concept is introduced -- Evans accepts the feedback. She plans to replace the hypothetical explanation with a simpler statement that you usually want to start new branches from `main` to keep them independent, and agrees that discussing stacked branches is too advanced for a beginner tutorial.

On the second concern -- the unclear transition between the hands-on practicum ending at Step 13 and the subsequent conceptual sections in the flat-text format -- Evans outlines a reorganization for v2. She will move the merge-conflict material into a note, relocate the "3 ways to think about branches" discussion inline after Step 6 where it directly relates to the example, rename the workflows section to "Examples of using branches to collaborate" to clarify that `git pull` is a subsection, and add an introductory overview listing the document's four main parts (introduction, step-by-step tutorial, collaboration workflows, and tips/resources) so readers can see the structure upfront.

Evans also briefly engages with Knoble's praise for the imaginary characters (like Emilia the PhD student), suggesting that future guides would likely re-introduce characters or choose different ones appropriate to more advanced workflows rather than carrying the same ones forward. The patch remains under review with no merge status change; Evans commits to seeing the review through despite her funding ending November 30.
