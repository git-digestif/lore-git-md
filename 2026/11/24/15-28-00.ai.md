This is a substantive review comment on the v2 notes `the_repository` removal series, specifically responding to Okhuomon Ajayi's explanation of why the `USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE` guard remains in `notes.c`. Christian Couder points out an apparent inconsistency in the series' scope: while the changes to `notes.c` itself are deliberately limited to functions that already receive a `struct notes_tree *`, patch 6/6 removes the guard entirely from `notes-utils.[c,h]`, yet functions in `notes-utils.c` such as `create_notes_commit()` call `write_notes_tree()` which depends on the hash-size helpers that still rely on the global `the_hash_algo`. This creates a mixed state where the utility layer appears fully converted while the core layer retains its guard, potentially making the API boundaries harder to reason about.

Christian suggests restructuring the series to improve coherence: limit it to the first five patches and explicitly frame it in the cover letter as a preparatory series that makes significant progress in `notes.[c,h]` and its callers without claiming to complete the subsystem conversion. He recommends the cover letter enumerate what remains undone across `notes.c`, `notes-utils.[c,h]`, and `notes-merge.[c,h]` -- including the `default_notes_tree` global, the display notes code's reliance on globals, and the command entry points (`log`, `format-patch`, `diff-tree`, `format-rev`) that receive a repository but do not propagate it to `format_note()`. This would position the series as a clean incremental step while setting clear expectations for follow-up work.

The recommendation explicitly encourages Okhuomon to send a rebased v2 incorporating reviewer feedback but dropping patch 6/6 (which removes the guard from `notes-utils.[c,h]`), thereby keeping the guard consistently present in both `notes.c` and `notes-utils.[c,h]` until a future series can address the remaining global dependencies comprehensively. This contrasts with Patrick Steinhardt's earlier question about eliminating the guard entirely, shifting the discussion toward whether partial removal creates more confusion than it resolves. The email does not dispute the technical correctness of the refactoring itself, focusing instead on series organization and the logical grouping of changes.
