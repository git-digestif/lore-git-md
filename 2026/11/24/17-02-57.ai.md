This is a substantive review of patch 2/4 in Elia Pinto's v3 series fixing 64-to-32 truncations on LLP64 platforms.

Phillip Wood engages with the type change in `diff_words_fill()` in `diff.c`, questioning whether converting only `alloc` to `size_t` is sufficient given that the loop indices `i` and `j` -- also used as offsets into `buffer->text` -- remain `int`. He notes that `i` and `j` are ultimately converted to `regoff_t`, which on 32-bit platforms is typically bounded to `INT_MAX`, implying that word-diff buffers larger than roughly 2 GiB may remain problematic even after the patch on those platforms. He also suggests that `buffer->orig_nr` and `buffer->orig_alloc` might need the same treatment for consistency. The review identifies a real edge-case limitation: while the patch fixes the specific overflow in the allocation counter, the underlying `regoff_t` constraint may still cap practical buffer sizes on 32-bit systems.
