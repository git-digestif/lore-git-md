This is a substantive review of Phillip Wood's feature patch adding `GIT_COMMENT_STRING` to `git var`. Kristoffer Haugsbakk engages with the patch's motivation, documentation, and test coverage, and surfaces one edge-case behavior worth noting.

Haugsbakk agrees that the previous workaround -- piping `git config --get-regex` output through `sed` to determine the effective comment character -- was inconvenient, which validates the patch's core premise. He suggests a wording tweak in the commit message, changing "comment character" to "comment string" to match the new variable name, and proposes that the `GIT_COMMENT_STRING` documentation in `Documentation/git-var.adoc` should also mention the `core.commentChar` and `core.commentString` config keys and cross-reference `git-config(1)`.

On testing, he confirms that the additions to `t/t0007-git-var.sh` cover the relevant cases: the default `#` value and the situation where `core.commentString` overrides `core.commentChar`, which is the intended precedence.

He also points out an edge case: setting `core.commentString` to an empty string causes `git var GIT_COMMENT_STRING` to fail with a fatal error ("core.commentstring must have at least one character") and a non-zero exit status. This is consistent with Git's handling of invalid configuration elsewhere, but script authors should be aware of it.

Overall, the review is constructive and technically engaged without being obstructive. The underlying patch appears well-crafted -- small, self-contained, touching only three files, and addressing a real scripting pain point with appropriate tests.
