This is a review comment on Jelle van der Waa’s RFC patch series adding `--verbose` support to `git history reword`. The reviewer, D. Ben Knoble, raises a thoughtful concern about the naming and scope of the `--verbose` flag in the context of `git history`.

Knoble’s feedback is substantive but narrowly focused. He questions whether `--verbose` is the right name for this feature, given that "verbose" could imply other behaviors in `git history`—such as showing a summary of rewritten commits after execution (e.g., `commit abc => def`). He suggests alternative names like `--commit-verbose` or `--diff-in-editor` to make the intent clearer and avoid overloading the term "verbose" in a way that might conflict with future use cases.

The comment is constructive and forward-looking, not a rejection of the feature itself. Knoble acknowledges the value of the idea and frames the discussion as one of interface design rather than implementation correctness. His concern is about long-term clarity and consistency, particularly as `git history` is still evolving and may grow additional subcommands.

This is not a surface-level nit; it engages with the conceptual design of the command and could influence how the feature is received. The author’s response (if any) will likely address whether the existing precedent from `git commit --verbose` is sufficient justification for reusing the same flag, or whether a more specific name is warranted. For now, this remains an open design question in the thread.
