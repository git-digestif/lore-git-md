This thread is about a critical bug in Git’s index subsystem that causes silent corruption and crashes when both `core.splitIndex` and `index.skipHash` are enabled. The issue was reported by Andy Koppe, who provided reproduction steps and confirmed the symptoms—null OIDs, missing index entries, and a crash in `wt-status.c`. Tanga Joris identified the root cause: `write_shared_index()` generates a shared index filename from a trailing checksum, which is written as zeros when `index.skipHash` is enabled, leading `read_index_from()` to discard the shared index entries entirely.

Tanga Joris is now working on a fix after Andy confirmed they would not be addressing the issue. The bug remains under review with no maintainer response or indication of integration status (e.g., `pu`, `next`). The severity is high, as it blocks users of split-index or skipHash optimizations, particularly in large repositories with `feature.manyFiles`. No test coverage or regression tests have been proposed yet, and the thread has not attracted additional reviewers beyond Tanga’s root-cause analysis. The next step is Tanga’s patch, which is expected to address the filename generation logic to ensure the shared index is correctly identified regardless of checksum state.
