This thread is about a small but impactful test-infrastructure improvement: replacing slow `tr(1)` invocations in the Git test suite with a new `test-tool genbytes` command, which speeds up macOS CI jobs by 70–79%. The series is now at v2, with v3 likely to refine the interface further.

Kristofer Karlsson introduced the series to address a performance bottleneck in `EXPENSIVE` tests, where `tr(1)` runs several times slower on macOS than on Linux when generating large amounts of data. The solution—a new `test-tool genbytes` command—accepts a two-digit lowercase hexadecimal byte value (e.g., `00` for NUL, `20` for space) and an optional count, avoiding platform-specific quirks of `tr` and `printf`. The v2 series consists of two patches: one implementing `genbytes` and converting 14 test scripts, and another removing the now-obsolete `test-tool genzeros` and updating its remaining callers.

Review feedback has been largely positive, with Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt engaging on implementation details. Junio’s latest concern—a 256 KB stack-allocated buffer in `test-genbytes.c`—was addressed by Kristofer in the new email, where he proposed three options (making the buffer `static`, keeping it on the stack, or shrinking it) without favoring any. Patrick’s earlier proposal to extend the byte-specification interface to support escape sequences (e.g., `\xNN`) for better readability remains open, as does his mechanical suggestion to use `git_parse_signed()` instead of a custom parser. The `printf` vs. `genbytes` debate is now closed, with Johannes Sixt conceding that `printf` is unreliable on macOS for large padding values and NUL-byte generation. No major objections remain, and the series is on track for v3 to finalize the interface.
