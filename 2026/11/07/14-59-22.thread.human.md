This thread is about a small but impactful test-infrastructure improvement: replacing slow `tr(1)` invocations in the Git test suite with a new `test-tool genbytes` command. The goal is to eliminate a performance bottleneck that makes `EXPENSIVE` tests run several times slower on macOS than on Linux when generating large amounts of data (up to 2 GB).

The series is now on its third iteration, with the design settled on a two-command interface: `test-tool genzeros` for NUL bytes and `test-tool genbytes <char> [<count>]` for arbitrary bytes. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, has addressed all prior feedback, including adopting a cleaner interface and squashing related changes into a single commit. The latest version consists of two patches: one implementing `genbytes` and converting 14 test scripts, and another removing the now-obsolete `genzeros` and updating its remaining callers.

The only remaining loose end—a minor implementation detail about whether to declare a 256 KB buffer as `static` or keep it on the stack—has just been resolved. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, dismissed the need for thread-safety concerns, leaving the choice to the author. With this settled, the series is ready for submission as v3. Performance measurements show macOS CI jobs improving by 70–79% (from ~800–900 seconds to ~200 seconds), while Linux jobs see modest gains of 2–7%. The change is narrowly scoped, well-motivated, and unlikely to be controversial.
