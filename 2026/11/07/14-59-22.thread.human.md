This thread is about a small but impactful test-infrastructure improvement: replacing slow `tr(1)` invocations in Git’s test suite with a new `test-tool genbytes` command. The series, now at its fourth iteration, is ready for integration and has been thoroughly reviewed by Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt.

The bottleneck was most acute on macOS, where generating 2 GB of data in `t4205-log-pretty-formats.sh` could take two minutes of CPU time due to macOS’s slow `tr` implementation. The new `genbytes` command writes arbitrary bytes directly, cutting macOS CI job times by 70–79% while also improving Linux performance by 2–7%. The interface is simple: `test-tool genzeros [<count>]` for NUL bytes and `test-tool genbytes <char> [<count>]` for any other byte. Sixteen test scripts have been updated to use the new command, including a recently added callsite in `t1600-index.sh` that the author will convert in the final version.

All design questions are settled, and the implementation is uncontroversial. The series touches only test infrastructure, with no changes to Git’s on-disk formats or user-facing behavior. Junio and Patrick have both signaled readiness for the topic to graduate to `next` once the trivial rebase is complete.
