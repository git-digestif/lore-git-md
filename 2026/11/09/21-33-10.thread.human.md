This thread introduces a beginner-friendly tutorial on Git branches, `gittutorial-branches`, authored by Julia Evans and Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan. The tutorial is designed for users without a CS background, focusing on practical workflows like open-source contributions and team projects through conversational explanations and step-by-step commands. The patch is a standalone addition to Git’s documentation, integrating seamlessly into the build system and accessible via `git help gittutorial-branches`. The first part of the series (`gittutorial`) is already merged, and this new tutorial has undergone extensive user testing with over 70 feedback comments from 16 test readers.

The patch itself is uncontested and technically sound, with no open questions about its content. However, Junio C Hamano raised a broader procedural concern about review bandwidth on the mailing list, noting that too many in-flight topics can slow progress. Julia acknowledged this concern and committed to prioritizing the review of existing topics over submitting new ones. In a follow-up, Julia detailed the steps she took to mitigate bandwidth issues while working under funding from the Sovereign Tech Agency, including securing review commitments from past reviewers, limiting the scope of her work, and conducting extensive user testing before submission. These strategies helped manage the load for documentation but may not apply as easily to code changes.
