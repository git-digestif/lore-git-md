This thread concerns a new beginner-friendly tutorial, `gittutorial-branches`, authored by Julia Evans and Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan, which aims to teach Git branches to users without a computer science background through conversational examples and `git log --graph` visualizations. The patch adds `Documentation/gittutorial-branches.txt` and wires it into the build system so it appears as `git help gittutorial-branches`; the first part of the series, `gittutorial`, is already merged, and this second part remains under review with no indication yet of integration into any of Junio's branches.

The most substantial feedback so far comes from D. Ben Knoble, who reviewed the tutorial with an eye toward pedagogy rather than line-level details. He praised the use of recurring characters like Emilia the PhD student as anchors for future guides on collaboration and remotes, and shared a practical heuristic for unstuck users -- find where you are, decide where you want to go, discover how to get there -- emphasizing `git log` as essential to the first step. His main concern targets Step 8, where hypothetical branches `one` and `two` illustrate why starting from `main` keeps work independent; since merges have not yet been introduced, he finds this confusing and suggests instead establishing "one branch per independent idea" as the norm upfront, then revisiting stacked branches only after merge concepts are covered. He also noted a structural ambiguity: readers may not realize that Step 13 and its merge-conflict discussion conclude the hands-on practicum before the text returns to conceptual material, proposing transition paragraphs or reordering examples to clarify the flow. He closed enthusiastically, expressing excitement for the guide's eventual availability.

Julia Evans had previously posted reflective observations about potential future enhancements -- such as a table of contents, a companion `git help remotes` guide, and mentioning legacy `git checkout` alongside `git switch` -- and committed to seeing the review through despite her funding ending November 30. Junio C Hamano used the patch's arrival to reflect on the project's limited review bandwidth, emphasizing prioritization of nearly-complete topics, but offered no content feedback. With D. Ben Knoble's substantive review now on the table, the series has engaged technical discussion, though his suggestions about restructuring Step 8 and the practicum flow remain open questions rather than stated blockers.
