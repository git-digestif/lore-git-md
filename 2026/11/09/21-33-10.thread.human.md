Julia Evans has posted version 2 of her new beginner tutorial on Git branches, `gittutorial-branches`, which will be accessible via `git help gittutorial-branches` once merged. This is the second part of a two-part series, with the first part `gittutorial` already in master. The tutorial targets users without a computer science background, using imaginary characters and `git log --graph` output to explain branching concepts without jargon.

The v2 incorporates all feedback from the initial review. Evans alphabetized the example characters' names (Anita, Bruno, Carmen, Daniela, and Emilia), added `$` prefixes to shell commands to distinguish input from output, and restructured the document with an introductory table of contents listing its four main parts. She moved the "3 ways to think about a branch" section to immediately follow the first branch-creation example, relocated the merge-conflict explanation into a note at the end of Step 13, and renamed the workflows section to "Examples of using branches to collaborate." Both D. Ben Knoble and Junio C Hamano's specific concerns have been addressed: Knoble confirmed his structural objections about hypothetical branches and practicum flow are resolved, and Hamano's correctness notes (using `git commit -a -m`, switching back to `main` before `git branch -D`, clarifying that Emilia commits before `git pull --no-rebase`) are all incorporated.

The patch remains under review with no indication yet of movement into Junio's integration branches. Evans has committed to seeing the review through despite her current funding ending November 30, and the community response has been enthusiastic, with Knoble expressing excitement about the guide becoming available to beginners.
