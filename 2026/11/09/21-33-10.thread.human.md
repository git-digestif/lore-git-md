This thread introduces a new beginner-focused tutorial, `gittutorial-branches`, authored by Julia Evans and Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan, which fills a gap in Git's documentation by teaching branch workflows through practical, jargon-free examples aimed at users without a CS background. The first part of the series, `gittutorial`, is already merged, and this standalone patch adds the branches guide along with the necessary build-system integration so it is accessible via `git help gittutorial-branches`. The patch remains under review with no indication of a merge status change.

Junio C Hamano used the submission to raise a broader concern about review bandwidth on the mailing list, noting that an excess of in-flight topics can slow progress by forcing reviewers to context-switch. Julia Evans acknowledged this and detailed steps taken to reduce reviewer burden, including securing review commitments from Patrick Steinhardt and D. Ben Knoble and conducting extensive user testing with feedback from 16 readers. In a recent follow-up after a week away, Julia shared four reflective observations to help reviewers understand the design tradeoffs: recruiting test readers for branches proved harder than expected, suggesting a table of contents might help; the tutorial introduces collaborative workflows without explaining remotes or upstreams to keep length manageable, possibly pointing to a future `git help remotes` guide; some long-time users were confused by `git switch` and she suggests also mentioning the older `git checkout`; and she defends the exclusive use of `git log --graph` for branch visualizations despite the ASCII art being difficult, as there is no better alternative or precedent for SVG in HTML docs. She also noted she will see the review through even though her current funding ends November 30. No technical objections or requests for changes have been raised against the patch itself.
