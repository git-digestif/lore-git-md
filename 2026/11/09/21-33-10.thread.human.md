This thread introduces `gittutorial-branches`, a new beginner-focused documentation page authored by Julia Evans and Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan to complement the already-merged `gittutorial`. The patch adds a single new help topic accessible via `git help gittutorial-branches` and integrated into the build systems, but it remains under review with no evidence of movement into Junio's integration branches.

D. Ben Knoble has now engaged twice with the proposal, most recently replying to Evans's reflective post about design tradeoffs. He endorsed her suggestion of a future `git help remotes` guide to explain upstreams and remotes, volunteering to draft it himself during his upcoming paternity leave while asking Evans for early feedback. He also weighed in on whether to mention legacy `git checkout` alongside `git switch` (deeming a brief parenthetical acceptable but not essential) and defended the use of ASCII `git log --graph` output as a core skill for reading history, while noting `git show-branch` as a possible supplementary visualization. His earlier substantive concerns remain unresolved: the tutorial introduces hypothetical branches before merges are explained in Step 8, and the transition from hands-on exercises back to conceptual material at Step 13 lacks clear signaling for readers.

Junio C Hamano used the thread to reflect on the project's review-bandwidth constraints but offered no content feedback on the patch itself. Julia Evans has committed to seeing the review through despite her current funding ending November 30. The series awaits resolution of Knoble's pedagogical restructuring suggestions before it can progress further.
