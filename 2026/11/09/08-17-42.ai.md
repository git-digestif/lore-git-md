This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing `the_repository` removal effort, specifically about how to measure progress in that project. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano’s critique of the `USE_THE_REPOSITORY` macro and the incremental refactoring approach.

Patrick acknowledges Junio’s point that the current macro gives a false sense of progress—files can compile without `USE_THE_REPOSITORY` defined even if they still call functions that internally depend on `the_repository`. However, he argues that the macro *is* still useful: it ensures that a file cannot *accidentally* reintroduce explicit references to `the_repository`, which is a meaningful step toward the ultimate goal. He concedes that the macro does not guarantee full "libification" (the ability to work with arbitrary repositories), but maintains that it is a pragmatic way to make incremental progress without introducing excessive churn.

The exchange also revisits an earlier disagreement: Junio had previously proposed hiding symbols that implicitly depend on `the_repository` from header files as a way to enforce progress, but Patrick rejected that approach as too disruptive. Patrick reiterates his preference for moving "forward" by continuing to refactor functions one by one, trusting that the problem will resolve itself once the entire codebase no longer relies on `the_repository`. No new patches or technical direction emerge from this email—it is purely a strategic discussion about how to track and incentivize the refactoring effort. The tone is collaborative but reflects a fundamental difference in how the two view the trade-off between incremental progress and strict correctness.
