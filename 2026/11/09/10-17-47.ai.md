This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Jelle van der Waa’s RFC series adding `--verbose` support to `git history reword`. Phillip Wood engages deeply with the implementation, raising several technical and design questions while also praising the feature’s usefulness.

The review focuses on four main areas: **config support**, **diff generation logic**, **code organization**, and **test coverage**. On config, Phillip argues that `commit.verbose` should be supported now rather than deferred, as the plumbing is straightforward and omitting it creates inconsistency with `git commit --verbose`. For diff generation, he suggests reusing more of `wt-status.c`’s existing logic (which already handles `status.relativePaths`, `status.renames`, and `status.renameLimit`) instead of rolling a custom helper, and questions whether the current implementation respects those settings. He also proposes minor cleanups: using a `bool` for the `verbose` flag to avoid double-negation, and consolidating the config callback across all `git history` subcommands.

On tests, Phillip critiques redundancy: the "no diff without `--verbose`" and "bare repository" tests could be folded into existing coverage, and the merge-commit test could extend an existing merge test rather than creating a new repository. He also suggests tightening assertions by comparing the full editor output (including the original commit message) against expectations, rather than just the diff portion. Throughout, he balances constructive criticism with appreciation, calling the feature "a really useful improvement" and thanking the author for working on it. The tone is collaborative, not prescriptive, and the feedback is actionable.
