Thread identity: New tutorial documentation (`gittutorial-branches`) for Git beginners, authored by Julia Evans and Marie Claire LeBlanc Flanagan, introduced via GitGitGadget. Thread type: documentation patch series (single patch).

Core problem or goal: Address the lack of beginner-friendly, example-driven documentation for Git branches. The tutorial targets users without a CS background, avoiding jargon and focusing on practical workflows (e.g., open-source contributions, solo/team projects) through conversational explanations, step-by-step commands, and `git log --graph` visualizations.

Open questions and loose ends: D. Ben Knoble raised two substantive concerns: (1) Step 8 ("Create another branch") uses hypothetical `one` and `two` branches to explain independence, but merges have not yet been introduced at that point, leaving readers in suspense; he suggests leading instead with the "branch per independent idea" principle and revisiting stacked branches after merge concepts are covered. (2) The flat-text format does not clearly signal that Step 13 and its merge-conflict note conclude the hands-on practicum before returning to conceptual material; he proposes transition paragraphs or reordering examples (e.g., moving the solo project earlier and returning to it later for push/backup details). In his latest reply, Knoble responded to Evans's four observations: he agrees a table of contents might help and wonders whether test readers were genuinely new to the concepts; he concurs that a future `git help remotes` guide makes sense and volunteers to write it during upcoming paternity leave, requesting early feedback from Evans when he begins; on `git switch` versus `git checkout`, he suggests a brief parenthetical ("switch is the modern form, but you may see older guides teaching checkout") is acceptable but not essential; on ASCII art in `git log --graph`, he acknowledges the difficulty but defends teaching log-reading as a core Git skill, noting `git show-branch` as a possible supplementary visualization tool while emphasizing that future guides should build reader confidence with textual log output. The author's prior observations about potential enhancements remain noted as future considerations rather than required changes.
