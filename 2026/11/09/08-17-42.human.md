This is a follow-up discussion in the ongoing `the_repository` removal effort, specifically about how to measure progress in eliminating Git’s global repository variable. The exchange between Patrick Steinhardt and Junio C Hamano centers on the limitations of the current `USE_THE_REPOSITORY` macro as a progress indicator.

Patrick acknowledges Junio’s point that the macro can create a misleading sense of achievement—files may compile without it even if they still depend on `the_repository` indirectly, as seen in `merge.c`’s call to `unpack_trees()`. However, he argues that the macro *does* serve a purpose: it prevents new explicit references to `the_repository` from being added, which is a meaningful step toward the long-term goal. He also notes that the problem will resolve itself once the entire codebase no longer relies on the global variable, making incremental tracking mechanisms unnecessary.

Junio had previously proposed hiding symbols that depend on `the_repository` from header files as a way to enforce progress, but Patrick rejected this approach in an earlier thread, calling it overly disruptive. Here, he reiterates that stance, emphasizing that the effort should focus on forward momentum rather than perfect measurement. The discussion is philosophical rather than technical—no new patches are proposed, and the thread doesn’t resolve the tension between incremental tracking and practical progress. For now, the project continues to rely on the macro as a coarse but useful guardrail.
