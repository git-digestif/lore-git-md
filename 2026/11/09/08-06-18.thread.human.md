This thread is about a performance regression in `git log --follow` that was introduced in June 2026 (commit 304812ed33). The issue made rename-following in merge-heavy repositories like linux.git painfully slow—one test case jumped from 28 seconds to nearly 7 minutes. Miklos Vajna’s patch, the fifth and final in a performance-focused series, fixes the problem by optimizing how `log_tree_diff()` handles merges.

The fix introduces a two-phase diff strategy: first, it checks merges against parents *without* rename detection to find a "same" parent (where the path is unchanged). If such a parent exists, further diffs against other parents are skipped unless renames are still needed. The patch also refines `remember_follow_pathspec()` to prioritize paths that exist in the commit. The changes are confined to `log-tree.c` and add about 140 lines of new logic, including helper functions like `follow_same_parent` and `follow_needs_renames`. Performance is restored to near pre-regression levels—the linux.git test case now takes 42 seconds instead of 6 minutes and 43 seconds.

The patch has received input from SZEDER Gábor and Patrick Steinhardt (both credited with Helped-by trailers), indicating prior review engagement. However, the commit message doesn’t clarify whether the change is strictly behavior-preserving or if it introduces subtle differences in rename detection. There’s no mention of test failures or edge-case regressions, but the series is now complete and appears ready for further review or integration.
