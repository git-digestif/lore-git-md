This is a follow-up in the ongoing test infrastructure discussion about replacing slow `tr(1)` invocations on macOS. Kristofer Karlsson acknowledges that Johannes Sixt’s revised `printf`-based workaround—splitting the 2 GB padding into three smaller chunks—does resolve the macOS performance bottleneck in `t4205-log-pretty-formats.sh`, cutting test time from 800–900 seconds to 170–280 seconds. However, he points out two practical drawbacks: the bash 3.2 builtin on macOS silently fails for field widths exceeding `INT_MAX - 15`, and the approach consumes about 2.1 GB of RSS because it builds each field in memory. As a mitigation, he proposes a local shell helper (`gen_ascii_zeros`) that writes the data in 1 MiB chunks, avoiding both issues while maintaining the same speed.

Kristofer has this alternative implemented locally as a single-patch drop-in replacement for the entire `test-tool genbytes` series. He is not pushing it as the preferred solution yet, but frames it as a viable fallback if the community prefers to avoid adding another test-tool command. The trade-off is clear: the shell-based approach keeps the codebase simpler at the cost of introducing a third distinct way to generate test data (alongside `genzeros` and `genzeros | tr`). No decision has been made; he is waiting for broader input before proceeding.
