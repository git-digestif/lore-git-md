This is a test infrastructure discussion about the ongoing effort to eliminate a macOS-specific performance bottleneck in Git's test suite. Johannes Sixt (Hannes) responds to Kristofer Karlsson's proposal for a chunked-`printf` helper function, which successfully speeds up the `EXPENSIVE` tests on macOS but risks spawning thousands of processes on shells without a built-in `printf`.

Hannes dismisses the chunked-`printf` approach as over-engineering ("painting bikesheds") and reaffirms his preference for the original plan: two separate test-tool commands, `genzeros` for NUL bytes and `genbytes` for arbitrary bytes. This design avoids both the memory overhead of naive `printf` and the process-spawning risk of the chunked version, while keeping the test scripts readable. The thread remains focused on choosing between these two approaches; no new technical objections or design alternatives have been raised in this email. The ball is back in Kristofer's court to either send a v3 series with the dual-command design or defend the chunked-`printf` helper.
