This thread is about an Outreachy proposal from Tanga Joris for the December 2026 round, focusing on improving Git’s command argument and option parsing. The goal is to address inconsistencies in how early requests like `-h`, `--help-all`, and `--git-completion-helper` are handled across built-in commands, which currently leads to uneven behavior (e.g., some commands fail when `--git-completion-helper` is used outside a repository).

Tanga’s proposal is well-researched and demonstrates strong preparation. They’ve already contributed several patches to Git, including a microproject that was merged to `master`, a four-iteration series removing `NO_PARSEOPT` from built-ins, and a fix for `git annotate` to prevent segfaults with `--git-completion-helper` outside a repository. Their plan is incremental: first standardizing entry points (e.g., ensuring `--git-completion-helper` works consistently), then tackling commands like `merge-index` where option parsing order matters, and finally exploring a "dry run" mode for `parse_options()` to unify early scanning. The proposal aligns with Junio’s long-term direction for `parse-options` and references prior work by Christian Couder.

The timeline is realistic, with fallback options if progress stalls, and the focus on testing—ensuring every change is guarded by a failing test on `master`—is a standout feature. Tanga’s motivation and technical grounding are strong, and their active participation in bug triage and patch reviews further bolsters confidence in their ability to execute the project. While mailing list feedback may refine sections 4 and 5, the core direction is sound and addresses a real usability and maintainability gap in Git.
