This is Junio’s reply to Farid Zakaria’s v2 cover letter for the credential-cache daemon race-condition series. Junio clarifies that he never had a discussion about the architectural concern he raised—he merely pointed out a potential problem in his review of v1, and Farid’s v2 cover letter mischaracterizes the state of the conversation. Junio did not endorse or dismiss the concern; he simply noted that Farid’s v2 does not address it, leaving the question open.

The unresolved issue is whether mixing `unix_ss_*` and `tempfile` APIs in patch 3 is safe. Junio’s original review warned that `tempfile` registration bypasses `unix_ss_was_stolen()`, risking socket removal by a dying daemon that no longer owns it. Farid’s cover letter treats this as a settled matter ("I can do that if it's a requirement in a V3"), but Junio pushes back: he does not know whether Farid has a technical justification for the current approach or simply overlooked the concern. Until Farid responds with a clear rationale or a revised implementation, the series remains blocked on this architectural question.

The rest of the series—patches 1 and 2, the test coverage, and the documentation improvements—are uncontroversial and well-reviewed. This reply does not revisit those; it focuses solely on the open design question in patch 3. Readers following the thread should watch for Farid’s next reply to see whether he provides a technical defense of the current approach or proposes an alternative.
