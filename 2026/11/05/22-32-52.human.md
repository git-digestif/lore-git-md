This is a **design discussion** in the HTTP retry series, focusing on the default backoff delay for `http.retryAfter`. Brian M. Carlson responds to Ravi Mistry’s compromise proposal (defaulting to 1 second) with a firm objection, arguing that 1 second is too short for Git’s typically expensive HTTP requests. Carlson reiterates their preference for a 30-second initial delay, citing server-operator experience: aggressive retries exacerbate load issues, and Git’s requests (e.g., fetch/clone) are far more resource-intensive than the static-file requests curl handles. They suggest a minimum of 10 seconds as a lower bound, emphasizing that shorter delays risk worsening rate-limiting rather than alleviating it.

The email does not engage with the technical implementation or test coverage but centers on the policy trade-off between fast recovery and server protection. Carlson’s position remains unchanged from their earlier review, and their argument is grounded in real-world operational concerns rather than theoretical design. This leaves the series at an impasse: while Junio C Hamano has endorsed the 1-second compromise, Carlson’s objection suggests broader input may be needed before the default is settled. The outcome will likely hinge on whether other contributors or Junio prioritize server stability over responsiveness.
