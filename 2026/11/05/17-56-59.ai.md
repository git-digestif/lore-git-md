This is a continuation of the architectural discussion about how to handle `the_repository` dependencies in the notes subsystem. Junio C Hamano responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s review of Okhuomon Ajayi’s withdrawn protective measure patch, clarifying why the original approach guarded the entire `notes-utils.h` header rather than a subset of functions.

Junio explains that the protective measure was intended to prevent contributors from mistakenly believing they had removed all `the_repository` dependencies in `notes-utils.c` while still calling unsafe helpers from `notes.c`. He acknowledges that the ideal approach would be to mark only a small subset of functions as unsafe, but notes that in this case, *almost every function* in `notes-utils.h` ultimately depends on `notes.c` functions that use `the_repository`. This widespread dependency made a targeted guard impractical, leading to the decision to guard the entire header.

The email does not propose a new solution or withdraw the protective measure (that was already done in a prior message). Instead, it provides context for why the original patch took its specific form. The thread now awaits Okhuomon Ajayi’s forthcoming series to refactor `notes.c` itself, which will address the root cause by making `notes.c` functions use `struct notes_tree->repo` instead of `the_repository`. This refactoring will eliminate the need for guards entirely, aligning with Patrick’s preference for fixing unsafe functions at the source. No merge status; the discussion remains focused on architectural strategy.
