This is a review of Okhuomon Ajayi’s protective measure patch for `notes-utils.h`, part of the `the_repository` removal effort. Patrick Steinhardt questions the approach, arguing that the patch makes the entire header effectively unusable by guarding all its declarations with `#ifdef USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE`. He suggests that the better solution is to fix the underlying `notes.c` functions (like `write_notes_tree()`) to stop hardcoding `the_repository`, rather than hiding the entire API behind a guard.

Patrick’s concern is architectural: he sees the protective measure as an overreach, noting that many other subsystems still depend on `the_repository` without being similarly guarded. He points out that the project’s usual practice is to guard only a small subset of functions when the refactoring effort is too large for a single series, not entire headers. His review implies that the patch may be premature or overly cautious, and that the real work should focus on `notes.c` instead of `notes-utils.h`.

The patch itself is minimal and mechanical, but Patrick’s feedback introduces a potential shift in direction. If his view gains traction, the follow-up work could pivot back to refactoring `notes.c` directly, rather than deferring it behind a guard. For now, the patch remains under review, and the architectural debate is still open. No other reviewers have weighed in yet.
