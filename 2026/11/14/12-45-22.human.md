This is a post-merge follow-up about a subtle user-interface regression in the recently merged `git rerere` subcommand modernization. Junio C Hamano reports that the new `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` parser now requires options to come *after* the subcommand name, breaking the old syntax `git rerere --skip-locked gc` in favor of `git rerere gc --skip-locked`. The change is not documented in the man page or commit messages, and Junio expresses concern that it may affect users who have scripts or muscle memory relying on the old ordering.

The regression is confirmed by Junio’s transcript: the old syntax works on `master` (pre-merge) but fails on `next` and `seen` (post-merge). The error message is unhelpful—it suggests `--skip-locked` is unknown rather than misplaced—so users are unlikely to realize the issue is ordering rather than a missing option.

This is a low-severity but real behavioral change that slipped through the refactoring. The series was framed as purely internal, but the parser switch has visible consequences. The thread is now open for discussion on whether to accept the new ordering as a breaking cleanup or to restore the old flexibility. Junio’s tone suggests he leans toward the latter, but he is also mindful of the project’s scale and the risk of silent breakage.
