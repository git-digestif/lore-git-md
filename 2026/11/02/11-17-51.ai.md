This is the third patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch CI/build system cleanup series. It mechanically deduplicates 14 lines of redundant test environment variable assignments in `ci/lib.sh` by replacing explicit job-name lists (`fedora-breaking-changes-meson`, `linux-breaking-changes`, `linux-sha256`, `linux-reftable-sha256`, `linux-reftable-leaks`, `osx-reftable`) with three pattern-matching `case` statements that match job names containing `*breaking-changes*`, `*reftable*`, or `*sha256*`. The change is purely refactoring: no behavior is altered, and the same variables (`WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES`, `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT`, `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH`) are exported in the same contexts. The patch is uncontroversial and has already been endorsed by Jeff King ("obviously good") and Phillip Wood ("nice") in earlier review rounds.

The patch touches only `ci/lib.sh` and is part of a broader effort to centralize CI environment variable overrides, making the configuration more maintainable and resilient to future job additions. By using glob patterns instead of hardcoded job names, the patch future-proofs the configuration against job renames or new job additions that should inherit the same settings. This aligns with the series’ goal of reducing duplication and improving consistency across GitHub Actions and GitLab CI workflows.

Key symbols introduced or modified are the three pattern-matching `case` statements in `ci/lib.sh` that replace the explicit job-name lists. The patch does not introduce new variables or change on-disk formats. Reviewers have not raised any technical concerns about this patch, and it is likely to be merged as-is alongside the rest of the series. The only open question is whether the temporary duplication introduced in patch 1/7 (a deliberate stepping stone) is necessary, but that does not affect this patch’s correctness or design.
