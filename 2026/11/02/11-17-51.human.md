This is the third patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch CI/build system cleanup series. It mechanically deduplicates test environment variables in `ci/lib.sh` by replacing explicit job-name lists with pattern-matching case statements. The change removes 14 lines of redundant configuration while adding 19 lines of consolidated logic, making the CI scripts more maintainable and resilient to future job additions or renames.

The patch targets three recurring test variables: `WITH_BREAKING_CHANGES` (for jobs testing SHA-256 or other breaking changes), `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_REF_STORAGE_FORMAT` (for reftable backend jobs), and `GIT_TEST_DEFAULT_HASH` (for SHA-256 jobs). Instead of listing every job that needs these variables (e.g., `linux-breaking-changes`, `linux-reftable-sha256`), the patch uses glob patterns (`*breaking-changes*`, `*reftable*`, `*sha256*`) to match job names dynamically. This approach eliminates redundancy and ensures new jobs with similar names automatically inherit the correct settings.

The change is uncontroversial and aligns with the series’ broader goal of centralizing CI configuration. Earlier patches in the series (1/7 and 2/7) laid the groundwork by moving hardcoded variable assignments into `ci/lib.sh` and replacing per-distribution rules with platform-wide defaults. This patch continues that refactoring by reducing duplication in the job-specific logic. Reviewers like Jeff King and Phillip Wood have already endorsed the series as "obviously good" and "nice," respectively, and this patch is likely to be merged without further debate.

No behavior changes are introduced—only the internal structure of the CI scripts is improved. The patch touches only `ci/lib.sh` and does not affect on-disk formats, test coverage, or user-facing functionality. It is a pure refactoring, consistent with the project’s preference for small, self-contained patches.
