This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread. Derrick Stolee endorses Junio C Hamano’s proposal to **amortize the performance cost of `ensure_generations_valid()` by writing out a fresh commit-graph** after the expensive walk. This would ensure that future invocations of `git describe` (or other commands) reuse the updated graph, avoiding repeated full-graph traversals.

Stolee’s response is brief but substantive: he agrees the idea is sound and suggests that the time may be right to **shift the commit-graph from an opt-in to an opt-out feature**, given its maturity and widespread adoption. He acknowledges a potential concern—read-only commands becoming mutators of on-disk state—but implies this could be managed with appropriate guardrails, without elaborating further. The tone is constructive and aligns with the thread’s earlier consensus on prioritizing correctness over performance. This moves the discussion closer to a concrete implementation plan, though details about those guardrails and how the write-back would be triggered remain open.
