This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Jasper Mayone’s two-part series extending `%(worktreepath)` in `ref-filter.c` to report worktree paths for branches being rebased or bisected. Phillip Wood’s feedback focuses on code duplication, documentation clarity, edge-case behavior, and usability concerns, while reinforcing earlier concerns about the limitations of `for-each-ref` as a filtering mechanism.

Phillip’s review identifies three key issues. First, he objects to the **code duplication** in `ref-filter.c`, where the logic for mapping branches to worktrees is copied from `branch.c`’s `prepare_checked_out_branches()`. He argues this violates the DRY principle and suggests exposing a shared helper in `branch.c` instead. Second, he flags a **behavior change** in `populate_worktree_map()`: the patch silently drops support for printing the `git_dir` of bare worktrees, which he considers a breaking change that should be documented in the commit message. Third, he critiques the **documentation** for being unclear, particularly the phrase “also in the worktree that is rebasing or bisecting it,” which he finds confusing and unnecessary for human-readable output.

On usability, Phillip reiterates his earlier concerns about `for-each-ref`’s limitations as a filtering mechanism. He proposes adding a **warning in the documentation** about parsing `%(worktreepath)` when paths contain newlines, recommending the use of quoting options or NUL-terminated output (`%00`). He also notes that `%(worktreepath)` cannot distinguish between main and linked worktrees or handle branches checked out in multiple worktrees, reinforcing the argument that `for-each-ref` may not be the right tool for this job.

The review is **substantive but not blocking**: Phillip endorses the core behavior change (extending `%(worktreepath)` to rebasing/bisecting branches) as a bug fix, but his feedback highlights technical debt and usability risks that could affect scripts relying on the current behavior. The author will need to address the code duplication, document the bare-worktree behavior change, and clarify the documentation before the patch can be considered ready for `next`. The broader design questions—whether `for-each-ref` is the right mechanism for filtering worktrees—remain unresolved but are now compounded by this review.
