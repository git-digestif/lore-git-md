This is a maintainer follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread. Junio C Hamano responds to Derrick Stolee’s endorsement of Patrick Steinhardt’s concern about the fix’s dependency on commit graph availability. The patch itself—a one-line change to prioritize commit generation numbers over commit dates—has already been queued for integration, but the thread remains active due to unresolved questions about correctness guarantees.

Junio proposes a pragmatic middle ground: when the expensive `ensure_generations_valid()` function is triggered (because the commit graph is missing or stale), its cost could be amortized by writing a fresh commit graph afterward. This would prevent repeated full-graph walks for future invocations of `git describe` or other commands that rely on generation numbers. The suggestion is brief and speculative—Junio does not provide implementation details or weigh in on whether the performance penalty is acceptable—but it directly addresses the core tension between correctness and performance raised by Steinhardt and Stolee.

The email is a light but substantive intervention. It does not resolve the trade-off but reframes it: instead of treating the cost of `ensure_generations_valid()` as a one-time penalty, Junio suggests making it a long-term investment in commit graph freshness. This could make the fix more palatable for users who frequently run `git describe` in repositories without up-to-date graphs. No new objections or alternatives are introduced; the focus remains on mitigating the acknowledged downside of the proposed solution. The thread’s next steps likely hinge on whether Junio or another contributor volunteers to implement this optimization or whether the project accepts the performance hit as the price of correctness.
