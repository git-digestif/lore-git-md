This is a governance discussion about the Git project’s proposed community voting process, specifically the hybrid eligibility model introduced by Jeff King (Peff). Patrick Steinhardt’s reply engages directly with Peff’s suggestion of supplementing an algorithmic threshold with a human-curated list for edge cases, raising two key points.

First, Patrick questions whether it is truly unexpected or problematic to exclude long-inactive contributors from voting. He argues that informed voting requires up-to-date context on how a given topic affects the community, and that even well-intentioned former contributors may lack the nuance needed to cast meaningful votes. This aligns with earlier concerns from Kristoffer Haugsbakk and Phillip Wood about reducing noise and administrative overhead in contentious votes, but it pushes back against the idea that former maintainers or deeply involved but inactive contributors should automatically retain voting rights.

Second, Patrick insists that if the project adopts a human-curated list, the process must be fully transparent: who is added, by whom, and with what reasoning. He proposes that additions be published well in advance of any vote to allow community discussion and potential challenges. This addresses the governance risk of opaque decision-making but adds a procedural burden that the project will need to manage.

The email advances the thread by sharpening the trade-off between inclusivity and practicality. It does not resolve the open questions—such as the specifics of the algorithmic threshold, the governance of the human-curated list, or the operational details of transparency—but it frames them in terms of real-world consequences for vote quality and community trust. Future discussion will likely focus on whether the hybrid model can balance these concerns or if the project should adopt a simpler, purely algorithmic approach.
