This is a **maintainer policy update** from Junio C Hamano, formalizing a new rule for the `seen` integration branch: topics that remain unreviewed for more than four weeks will be automatically ejected. The email reflects on a months-long experiment with a permissive intake policy and concludes that while `seen` has helped reviewers by providing ready-to-test patches, it has also accumulated many stagnant topics that no one is actively reviewing.

Junio’s proposal is a compromise. He acknowledges Phillip Wood’s earlier feedback—that reviewers find it easier to engage with patches already in `seen`—but notes that the branch has become cluttered with topics that linger indefinitely without attention. The new rule aims to balance reviewer convenience with quality control: patches will still enter `seen` initially, but if they fail to attract substantive review within a month, they will be dropped. This prevents `seen` from becoming a graveyard for stalled contributions while preserving its role as a low-friction staging area for active topics.

The policy is now active. Junio has already sent notices to authors of topics that have been in "Needs review" for over a month, with the oldest dating back to mid-July. The move is framed as a mechanical, scalable way to manage the volume of in-flight patches, aligning with existing rules for `next` and "Waiting for response" topics. No technical changes or patch specifics are involved, but the update has clear implications for contributors: to keep a patch in `seen`, they will need to proactively solicit reviews or risk automatic removal. The thread remains open-ended, but the policy is now in effect.
