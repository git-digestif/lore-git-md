This is a **workflow policy discussion** about Git’s integration branches, specifically the `seen` branch. Junio responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback on his proposal to eject unreviewed topics from `seen` after a set period, weighing two approaches: a strict, review-gated intake versus a more permissive but time-limited one.

Junio clarifies that the "Will merge to 'next'?" state (with a question mark) is his way of signaling openness to feedback—essentially an invitation to object if the plan seems misguided. This aligns with his broader practice of using tentative language to encourage discussion, though Patrick notes it often confuses newcomers.

On the ejection policy, Junio presents a stark choice. The first option is to continue queuing all submitted topics in `seen` but eject them after N weeks if they receive no review, as previously proposed. The second is to adopt a stricter gate: only queue topics in `seen` if they’ve already garnered enough positive review to justify a "Will merge to 'next'?" label. The latter would shrink `seen` and reduce the friction of topics being ejected later, but it shifts the burden to contributors to attract review before inclusion. Junio leans toward the stricter approach, citing lower emotional stress for himself, but acknowledges uncertainty about which path contributors would prefer—being ignored entirely or seeing their work briefly queued and then discarded.

The discussion remains unresolved, with no clear consensus on which policy better serves the project. The trade-off is between reviewer convenience (easier access to all patches in `seen`) and contributor experience (avoiding the frustration of ejection). Junio’s framing suggests he may default to the stricter gate unless the community voices a strong preference for the time-limited alternative.
