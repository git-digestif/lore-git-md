This thread is about a small but important consistency fix in `git rev-list`'s connectivity check. The patch, from Kristofer Karlsson, makes `rev-list` ignore replacement objects during connectivity checks, aligning it with other Git commands like `fsck` and `prune` that already do so. This prevents a scenario where a pack might pass the connectivity check (because replacements are visible) but later fail `fsck` (because replacements are ignored and the original objects are missing).

The patch itself is straightforward: it adds a `--connectivity-check` flag to `rev-list`, modifies `tree-verify.c` to drop the `OBJECT_INFO_LOOKUP_REPLACE` flag, and updates the test suite (`t5413`) to verify the new behavior. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has endorsed the core idea but raised a conceptual question: whether the connectivity check should instead verify *both* original *and* replacement objects, treating replacements as additional tips in the connectivity graph. Kristofer responded pragmatically, noting that replacement objects are stored as regular refs, so their reachable objects are already covered by ordinary connectivity checks during fetch or push. This suggests the current patch’s approach is sufficient for now, though the door remains open for a stricter check in the future.

The discussion is purely about design intent—there are no objections to the implementation, and the patch is small and well-motivated. The thread is still under review, with the key question being whether to proceed with the current approach (ignoring replacements) or explore Junio’s alternative (verifying both originals and replacements). No other participants have weighed in yet, and the patch remains unchanged since its initial submission.
