This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Jasper Mayone’s two-patch series, which adds a safety check to `git worktree remove`. The patch itself is already merged, but Phillip Wood’s review offers detailed feedback on the implementation, documentation, and tests.

Phillip’s comments are technical and constructive, focusing on clarity, edge cases, and maintainability. He identifies a minor but important edge case: the patch does not detect a cherry-pick or revert that has completed all its commits (and thus appears clean to `git status`) but still has `CHERRY_PICK_HEAD` or `REVERT_HEAD` present. This is a real safety gap—deleting such a worktree would still discard in-progress state, even though the files are clean. He suggests a simple fix: check for the existence of those files in the worktree’s `$GIT_DIR`.

On documentation, Phillip critiques the phrasing in `git-worktree.adoc`, pointing out grammatical issues and suggesting a clearer list of conditions that require `--force`. He also recommends using subshells in the test scripts to improve readability and proposes factoring out the conflict-setup logic to avoid duplication across test cases.

The review is thorough but not nitpicky—Phillip explicitly notes that the core logic of the patch is correct and that his comments are about refinement, not correctness. His feedback is actionable and would strengthen the patch if addressed in a follow-up, though the patch as merged already delivers its primary safety benefit.
