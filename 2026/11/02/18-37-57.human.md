This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread, specifically Linus Torvalds responding to Patrick Steinhardt’s concern about the fix’s dependency on commit graph freshness. The exchange clarifies the real-world context behind the bug (Linux kernel version reporting) and explores trade-offs for ensuring consistent behavior.

Linus explains that the Linux kernel’s versioning script (`setlocalversion`) predates `git describe` and was never tightly coupled to it, though it reimplements similar logic. This historical detail underscores why the bug was noticed in practice: the kernel’s versioning relies on accurate commit distance counts, and the regression caused visible discrepancies during the v7.4 merge window.

On the technical side, Linus acknowledges Patrick’s point that the fix’s effectiveness depends on having a recent commit graph. Without one, `git describe` falls back to the old (buggy) behavior, potentially producing inconsistent results for the same repository state. While he agrees this is suboptimal, he argues it’s no worse than the current "consistently wrong" behavior. His proposed mitigation—a `--exact` flag to force generation number validation via `ensure_generations_valid()`—balances correctness and performance, though he notes the latter could be prohibitively expensive in large repos. The assumption is that most users already have generation numbers cached, making the fallback rare.

The tone is pragmatic, prioritizing incremental improvement over perfection. Linus’s suggestion of a flag (rather than automatic validation) reflects a preference for user control, though it leaves the door open for future systemic changes like Junio’s and Derrick Stolee’s proposal to write out a fresh commit-graph after validation. This email doesn’t resolve the broader design question but adds nuance to the trade-off discussion, framing the fix as a step forward even if imperfect. The thread remains focused on a regression fix, but the conversation is now explicitly about how to handle edge cases in a way that scales.
