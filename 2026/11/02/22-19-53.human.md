This is a documentation-focused follow-up in the ongoing effort to clean up Git’s terminology around "root refs" and "pseudorefs." The email is part of a thread refining how these concepts are explained in the glossary and pedagogical documentation, specifically addressing questions raised by Julia Evans about pseudoref behavior.

Andy Koppe responds to Julia’s suggestions by pointing to the updated glossary entry in the v2 patch series, which now explicitly documents that pseudorefs (like `FETCH_HEAD`) *may contain multiple hashes* and that commands like `git log` use the *first hash* when multiple entries are present. The glossary also lists `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD` as the only pseudorefs, clarifying their purpose and limitations (e.g., they cannot be written by `git update-ref` or listed by `git for-each-ref`). This addresses Julia’s questions directly and improves the clarity of the documentation for users who encounter pseudorefs in practice.

The exchange is collaborative and uncontroversial, focusing on making the documentation more precise and user-friendly. The updates align with the series’ goal of resolving inconsistencies introduced by the 2024 terminology change (commit 993d57eded), which narrowed "pseudoref" to `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD` while introducing "root ref" for refs like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`. No code changes are proposed here—this is purely about improving the explanatory text.

The series as a whole remains ready for merging, with all substantive review feedback addressed in v2. The only remaining loose ends are two minor documentation nits in Patch 3/5, which Andy has deferred to a future effort.
