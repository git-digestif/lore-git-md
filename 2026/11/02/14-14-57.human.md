This is a review reply to Jasmeet Bhatia’s performance patch for `git stash`. The patch itself is a well-motivated optimization that eliminates duplicate index preparation and untracked-file detection when `do_push_stash()` calls `do_create_stash()`, yielding measurable speedups (6–18 %) on real-world repositories.

Phillip Wood’s response is substantive. He acknowledges the performance win but points out that his own, already-queued series (`pw/stash-change-check-only-once` in `seen`) takes a different approach: it keeps the `check_changes()` call inside `do_create_stash()` and removes the redundant calls from `create_stash()` and `do_push_stash()` instead. The two approaches are technically compatible—both reduce duplicate work—but they represent competing visions of how the stash code should be structured. Phillip invites Jasmeet to review his series, which suggests the discussion is now about which direction the project should take rather than whether the optimization itself is worthwhile.
