This is a governance discussion refining the Git project’s proposed community voting process, specifically the hybrid eligibility model introduced by Jeff King (Peff). Patrick Steinhardt responds to Peff’s suggestion of a human-curated list for edge cases (e.g., former maintainers or inactive but deeply involved contributors) by raising two key points: the legitimacy of excluding long-inactive contributors and the need for transparency in the human-curated process.

Patrick’s first argument questions whether it’s surprising or unfair to exclude contributors who haven’t been active for years. He frames informed voting as requiring up-to-date context about how a topic affects the community, suggesting that long-inactive contributors—even if historically significant—may lack the necessary perspective to vote meaningfully. This aligns with earlier concerns from Kristoffer Haugsbakk and Phillip Wood about "drive-by" participation in contentious votes (e.g., the AI contribution policy) but contrasts with Junio C Hamano’s stance that engaged but inactive contributors should retain a voice. The tension here is between inclusivity (ensuring all stakeholders are heard) and practicality (avoiding noise or uninformed votes).

His second point addresses the mechanics of the human-curated list. While he accepts the need for exceptions, he insists on full transparency: the list must publicly document who added each contributor, the reasoning behind their inclusion, and be published well in advance of any vote to allow for discussion or challenges. This safeguard responds to potential concerns about bias or arbitrary decisions in the curation process, particularly if Junio or the PLC are the arbiters. The requirement for advance notice also mitigates last-minute "dogpiling" risks, a concern Phillip Wood raised earlier.

The email advances the thread by narrowing the debate to two operational questions:
1. **Should the human-curated list be opt-in (contributors request inclusion) or opt-out (maintainers proactively add contributors)?** Patrick’s transparency requirements suggest an opt-in or at least a documented opt-out process to avoid perceptions of favoritism.
2. **How will the list be governed?** His call for public reasoning and advance notice implies a need for a lightweight but formal process (e.g., a mailing-list announcement or a documented PLC decision) rather than ad-hoc additions.

Patrick does not propose specific thresholds or time windows for the algorithmic part of the hybrid model (e.g., Peff’s "X commits and active in the last Y years"), nor does he engage with the earlier debate about counting patch series vs. individual commits. His focus is solely on the human-curated list’s governance, leaving the algorithmic criteria unresolved. The discussion remains in the design phase, with no code or documentation changes yet. Future replies will likely address how to balance transparency with administrative overhead (e.g., whether to automate list updates or rely on manual reviews) and whether the list should be maintained by Junio, the PLC, or another body.
