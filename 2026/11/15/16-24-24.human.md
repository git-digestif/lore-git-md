This is a security-focused reply in the ongoing `change-id` preservation thread. Brian m. carlson pushes back against Ewout ter Hoeven’s workflow examples by highlighting a fundamental trust issue: `change-id` headers are user-modifiable, so they cannot serve as a reliable identity anchor for automated review systems. The core concern is that a malicious actor could exploit preserved `change-id`s to bypass review by submitting a completely different patch series under the same identifiers, tricking servers into accepting previously approved-but-unrelated code. This objection directly challenges the thread’s premise that Git should preserve these headers by default, framing it as a potential security vulnerability rather than just a privacy risk.

The response is narrowly targeted at the stacked PR use case, but the implications are broader. Brian’s argument suggests that any solution relying on `change-id` preservation would need server-side enforcement (e.g., rebasing on the hosting platform) to mitigate tampering risks. This introduces a new constraint for Nico Williams’ policy/hook proposal, which currently assumes client-side flexibility. The email does not engage with Ewout’s specific examples (e.g., `gh-stack` failures) or Phillip Wood’s operational scenarios, instead focusing on the theoretical attack vector. Whether this objection will derail the thread’s momentum depends on whether other participants view it as a solvable technical problem (e.g., cryptographic signing) or an inherent flaw in the approach.
