This is a surface-level review reply from Christian Couder on Elia Pinto's `[PATCH v3 2/4]` (diff: use `size_t` for the allocation size of word-diff buffers), part of the four-patch series fixing 64-to-32 truncations on LLP64/Windows platforms.

The patch itself converts the `alloc` field in `struct diff_words_buffer` and the corresponding local in `diff_words_fill()` from `unsigned long`/`long` to `size_t` in `diff.c`, preventing a signed-overflow crash when the word-diff buffer grows past ~700 MiB on 32-bit-`long` systems. Couder's comment addresses only the commit-message justification: Pinto had noted that while `diff_words_append()` is currently bounded by `MAX_XDIFF_SIZE`, removing that limit would make `ALLOC_GROW()` truncate and the subsequent `memcpy()` overflow. Couder suggests this framing overstates the change's effect, since other subsystems would break first if `MAX_XDIFF_SIZE` were lifted, and proposes instead justifying the type change by consistency with the `size` member of `mmfile_t` and the `alloc` variable used in `diff_words_fill()`. He concludes "Otherwise the patch looks good to me," signaling technical approval with a minor wording nit.
