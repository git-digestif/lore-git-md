This is maintainer commentary on review practices, posted as a reply within the `git credential` `OPT_SUBCOMMAND` series thread.

Junio C Hamano thanks Laveesh Bansal for testing the three-patch series and attaching a `Reviewed-by` trailer, acknowledging that encouraging authors through positive feedback is valuable even though writing such reviews is harder than pointing out bugs. He then uses the moment to offer broader guidance on what makes a positive review credible: outside of trivial fixes or nods from known area experts, a one-line "Looks good to me" leaves readers unable to gauge how deeply the reviewer engaged with the changes.

Junio suggests a practical technique for giving substantive positive reviews -- walking through the patch aloud and explaining in one's own words what the old and new code do and why the tricky parts are handled correctly. This exposes the reviewer's reasoning to the list and gives the author a chance to correct any misreading. The email closes with a simple "Thanks," and introduces no new technical feedback on the credential refactoring itself, leaving the series in the same state as before: approved by multiple reviewers and awaiting only the commit-message clarification about the `--` end-of-options marker that Tanga Joris previously noted.
