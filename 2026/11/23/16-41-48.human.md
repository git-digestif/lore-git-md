This is a follow-up reply in Adrian Ratiu's three-patch bugfix series for the `compatObjectFormat` interoperability layer, specifically addressing maintainer feedback on patch 3/3. The email clarifies a subtle point about the `object-file-convert.c` changes: Adrian confirms that switching the length checks from `<` to `<=` for the `"tree "`, `"parent "`, and `"mergetag "` header comparisons is what prevents a malformed line like `"parent \n"` (with nothing after the space) from falling through to the unknown-header path and being copied verbatim. Instead, the `<=` keeps it in the recognized-header branch where the subsequent OID parse fails and the line is rejected, matching the behavior of the existing converter.

Adrian acknowledges that this logic is hard to follow in the current diff and commits to splitting it into a separate preparatory patch in v3. That would leave patch 3/3 focused solely on its core purpose: making unknown commit headers copy unchanged in both existing and newly created commits, removing the 2023 safety check that rejected them. This separation should make the series easier to review and land.

The exchange shows the patch is under active review with maintainer approval of the overall direction, though the specific edge-case handling of empty header values still needs cleaner presentation. Adrian's willingness to restructure the patch in response to Junio's question suggests the series is converging toward a mergeable state.
