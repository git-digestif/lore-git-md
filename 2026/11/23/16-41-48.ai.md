This is a review reply in the v2 `compatObjectFormat` bugfix series, specifically addressing Junio C Hamano's question about patch 3/3 (`object-file-convert: copy unknown commit headers unchanged`). Adrian Ratiu clarifies that the change from `<` to `<=` in the length checks for `"tree "`, `"parent "`, and `"mergetag "` in `convert_commit_object()` (`object-file-convert.c`) is intentional and serves to reject malformed headers rather than copy them. With `<=`, a line consisting solely of `"parent \n"` (or `"tree \n"`, `"mergetag \n"`) enters the `else if` branch and is passed to `parse_oid_hex_algop()`, which fails and causes the commit to be rejected; under the old `<` comparison, such a line would fall through to the unknown-header path and be copied verbatim, matching the behavior of `git commit --amend` but producing a commit that cannot be read back correctly.

Ratiu acknowledges that the commit message does not adequately explain this distinction and commits to splitting the `<=` change into a separate preparatory patch in v3. The current patch will then focus exclusively on the unknown-header copying behavior, removing the 2023 safety check that rejected unknown headers and unifying `convert_commit_object()` with `convert_commit_extra_headers()` to copy all non-object-ID headers verbatim, including continuation lines. This separation addresses the maintainer's concern that the two changes -- header classification by length and unknown-header policy -- are distinct and should be reviewed independently.

The email confirms that the v3 iteration will restructure the series to make the logic easier to follow: a preparatory patch establishing the `<=` boundary semantics (so empty-value known headers are rejected via `parse_oid_hex_algop()` failure), followed by the patch that removes the special cases for `author`, `committer`, `encoding`, and `gpgsig` and copies unknown headers unchanged. This maintains the series' core goal of making `git hash-object -w` and `git commit --amend` consistent in their handling of unknown commit headers, while ensuring malformed known headers continue to be rejected rather than silently copied.
