This is a discussion reply in an ongoing feature-request thread about safeguarding `git reset --hard`, not a patch. Steven Rostedt responds to Mirko Faina's suggestion that he could simply run `git reset` without `--hard` and restore unwanted changes afterward, or use `--keep` to abort on dirty trees. Rostedt explains that he tried the non-hard reset and was confronted with a massive diffstat showing over 72,000 files and millions of changed lines, which reminded him why he always reaches for `--hard` in the first place: when fast-forwarding his topic branches to devel branches with divergent histories, the intermediate diff is enormous and impractical to inspect. He concludes by confirming that he will write a personal wrapper script to achieve his goal, effectively accepting the maintainer's earlier position that no upstream change to `git reset --hard` semantics is forthcoming. The email closes Rostedt's participation in the thread with a practical workaround rather than a request for new Git functionality.
