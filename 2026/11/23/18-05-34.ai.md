This is a substantive review of Julia Evans's documentation patch adding the `gittutorial-branches` beginner tutorial. D. Ben Knoble reads through the thread with foreknowledge of the design discussions and offers detailed pedagogical feedback rather than line-level nits.

He praises the use of imaginary characters (such as Emilia the PhD student) as extremely helpful, noting that these well-known scenarios can anchor future guides on collaboration and remotes. He also shares a practical heuristic he uses at work for unstuck users -- find where you are, decide where you want to go, discover how to get there -- positioning `git log` as crucial for the first step.

His main substantive concern targets Step 8 ("Create another branch"), where the text uses hypothetical `one` and `two` branches to explain why starting from `main` keeps branches independent. D. Ben Knoble finds this confusing because merges have not yet been introduced, leaving the reader in suspense. He suggests instead leading with the principle of "branch per independent idea" as the norm, then revisiting stacked branches after merge concepts are covered, since stacking unrelated work is generally inadvisable anyway.

He also flags a structural issue in the flat-text format: it is not evident that Step 13 and its merge-conflict note conclude the hands-on practicum, while the following sections return to conceptual material. He proposes transition paragraphs or reordering the examples (perhaps moving the solo project earlier and returning to it later for push/backup details) to clarify the flow.

Overall he is enthusiastic, closing with "quite excited to see this be available to folks." The review touches `Documentation/gittutorial-branches.txt` only; no code or build-system changes are at stake here.
