This is a closing reply in a design discussion thread about adding safety mechanisms to `git reset --hard`.

Steven Rostedt responds to Mirko Faina's suggestion of using `git reset` without `--hard` or relying on `--keep` by reporting that he tested the no-`--hard` approach and found it impractical for his workflow. When he ran `git reset` without `--hard` to update a branch, the resulting `git diff` showed changes across 72,642 files with millions of line insertions and deletions, demonstrating why he relies on `--hard` to fast-forward branches cleanly. He explains that his workflow involves creating temporary topic branches, recording their SHA-1 hashes, and then running `git reset --hard <sha>` on his main development branches to move them forward, which makes the destructive command a frequent part of his normal operation rather than an occasional recovery tool.

Rather than continuing to advocate for upstream changes, Rostedt accepts the maintainer's position and announces he will implement a personal client-side wrapper script that checks for pending changes with `git diff` and prompts before invoking `git reset --hard`. This effectively concludes the thread from the original poster's side, confirming that no built-in pre-command hook or config-based safety mechanism will be pursued for `builtin/reset.c`. The email contains no patch or new technical proposal; it serves as a resolution marker indicating the feature request has been withdrawn in favor of a local workaround.
