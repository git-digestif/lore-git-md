This is a cover letter for the second iteration of a five-patch series that hardens several Git subsystems against integer overflows flagged by Coverity. Johannes Schindelin sends it as part of his ongoing effort to drive Git for Windows' Coverity reports to zero, though the fixes apply to upstream Git and target latent undefined behavior rather than observed crashes.

The series touches `archive-tar.c`, `http.c`, `wrapper.c`, and `date.c`. Patch 1 guards `get_path_prefix` against zero-length input, patch 2 changes a byte-position variable in `fwrite_sha1_file()` from `int` to `size_t` to prevent heap under-reads on responses over 2 GB, and patches 3–4 rework `write_in_full()` and add `read_in_full_or_error()` to reject implausibly large byte counts before they overflow `ssize_t`. Patch 5 caps user-supplied numbers at `INT_MAX` in `approxidate_alpha()` before multiplying them by time-unit lengths, preventing signed overflow in relative-date parsing like "99999 weeks ago".

Version 2 responds to review feedback by dropping the `signed_mult_overflows` and `mult_overflows` macros introduced in v1 in favor of simpler, portable checks, and by changing `write_in_full()`'s return type from `size_t` to `int` to eliminate the overflow risk by design. The series now includes test coverage in `t/t0006-date.sh` and a new unit test file `t/unit-tests/u-wrapper.c`, addressing a gap in the original submission.

Patches 1 and 2 remain uncontroversial and ready for integration, while the wrapper and date changes resolve Phillip Wood's earlier portability concerns. The series currently sits in Junio's integration queue marked "Waiting for response," with a warning that it will expire without further activity. Overall this looks like a solid, well-motivated defensive cleanup that has already benefited from substantive review.
