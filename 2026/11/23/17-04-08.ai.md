This is a substantive review by Junio C Hamano of patch 4/4 in Dirk Gouders's v5 series fixing UTF-8 bounds handling. The patch modifies `utf8_strnwidth()` in `utf8.c` so that the inner call to `utf8_width()` receives a boundary argument instead of NULL: the code now declares a `size_t remainder`, computes it as `orig + len - string`, and passes `&remainder` to `utf8_width(&string, &remainder)`. This prevents `utf8_width()` from reading past the `len`-bounded region when a multibyte character is chopped by the boundary. A new test `test_utf8_width__strnwidth_cjk_chopped()` in `t/unit-tests/u-utf8-width.c` verifies that chopped CJK sequences (e.g., `"三bytes..."` with len 1 and len 3) return the expected widths of 1 and 2 respectively.

Junio approves the technical change as a "very good change" for ensuring the called function does not read beyond the known end-of-region, but offers two refinements: first, he suggests tightening the commit title to distinguish this patch from 3/4 (which also touches `utf8_strnwidth()` boundaries), proposing something like "utf8: bound utf8_width() calls in utf8_strnwidth()"; second, he notes that if the convention is to mark functions with a `()` suffix in commit messages, `utf8_width` should consistently become `utf8_width()`. No merge status is indicated; the series remains under review.
