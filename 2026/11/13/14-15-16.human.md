This is a surface-level review of the second patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s two-part series improving `git repo structure`. The patch avoids fully parsing trees when only the object type is needed, delivering an 80% reduction in memory usage (from 3.4 GiB to 0.7 GiB) and a 20% speedup in wall-clock time. Kristofer Karlsson, who recently made a similar optimization for the incremental connectivity check, confirms the approach is sound and notes the memory savings align with his own work. No substantive concerns are raised; the feedback is purely positive and observational. The patch looks well-motivated and uncontroversial, targeting a real-world performance bottleneck in large repositories.
