This thread is about a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that improves the performance and memory usage of `git repo structure` by optimizing how it counts objects. The series is now at version 2, which addresses the only substantive feedback from the first round—a minor rewording of a comment to clarify the role of `repo_parse_commit()` as a failsafe rather than a dependency.

The key change is that objects are now parsed only when necessary, rather than unconditionally. This yields an 80% reduction in memory usage and a 20% speedup, as confirmed by both the author and Kristofer Karlsson, who reviewed the series and benchmarked it. The only remaining open question is a low-priority design choice: whether the fallback `repo_parse_commit()` call should trigger a `BUG()` assertion for fail-fast behavior or remain silent. This doesn’t affect functionality or performance, and the series is otherwise ready for Junio to pick up.

Kristofer’s latest reply simply acknowledges that his feedback was addressed and restates his endorsement of the series. No new concerns or objections have been raised, and the technical discussion is complete. The patches touch only `builtin/repo.c` and introduce no on-disk format changes or new CLI options.
