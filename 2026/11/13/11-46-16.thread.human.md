This thread is about a two-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that improves the performance and memory usage of `git repo structure` by optimizing its object-counting logic. The series, now at v2, delays object parsing until necessary and skips full tree parsing when only the object type is needed. These changes yield an 80% reduction in memory usage and a 20% improvement in wall-time, benchmarks confirmed by Kristofer Karlsson.

The v2 update addresses the only substantive feedback from v1—a reworded comment in patch 1 to clarify the role of `repo_parse_commit()` as a failsafe. Kristofer Karlsson, who provided the initial review, has since restated his endorsement of the series, confirming the comment change satisfies his concerns. Derrick Stolee has also acknowledged the improvements, noting the 80% memory reduction, though his replies have been surface-level and do not engage with the code or design trade-offs.

The series is technically complete and ready for maintainer pickup. The only remaining open item is a low-priority design choice about whether the fallback `repo_parse_commit()` call should trigger a `BUG()` assertion or remain silent, but this has no impact on functionality or performance. Both reviewers have signaled their approval, and no further changes are expected.
