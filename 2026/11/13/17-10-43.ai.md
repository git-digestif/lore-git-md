This is a **subsystem maintainer response** to Junio’s design query about the `promisor.fetchOrder` feature. Christian Couder, the promisor-remote maintainer, acknowledges the complexity of reconciling client-side configuration (`promisor.fetchOrder`) with server-side promisor remote advertisement. His reply suggests the current patch’s approach—treating the two mechanisms as independent—may be insufficient.

Christian highlights that the problem is inherently multi-party: different remotes (A, B) may advertise different sets of promisor remotes (A1/A2 vs. B1/B2/B3), and the optimal order for lazy fetches could depend on which remote provided related objects. A single static order, as implemented in the patch, may not handle these cases well. He implies that a more dynamic or context-aware mechanism might be needed, though he does not propose a specific solution in this email.

The response also notes a procedural concern: the patch addresses the main issue of an Outreachy microproject, despite the project’s guidelines advising against immediate implementation. This suggests the feature may have been prematurely developed without prior design discussion, which could complicate its integration.

No technical feedback on the patch itself is provided here; the focus remains on the unresolved design question. The thread’s next steps likely hinge on whether Christian or others propose a revised approach to handle the multi-remote scenario he describes. Until then, the patch’s RFC status remains appropriate.
