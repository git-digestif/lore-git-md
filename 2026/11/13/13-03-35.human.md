This is a **follow-up review comment** in the ongoing RFC thread adding merge commit support to `git history`. Philippe Blain clarifies and refines his earlier feedback on the pre-seeding logic introduced in patch 1/5.

Blain now acknowledges that the pre-seeding behavior—mapping negative revisions to the `--onto` target—is intentional and aligns with Elijah Newren’s design. However, he identifies a **usability gap**: the current implementation forces users to avoid the convenient `M^!` syntax (which expands to `M^1..M`) and instead use the more verbose `--ancestry-path ^M^1 M` when replaying a merge whose second parent is outside the rewrite range. This is not obvious and could frustrate users expecting `M^!` to work as it does in other Git commands.

He suggests two documentation improvements: (1) explicitly mention in the `git replay` man page that negative revisions are mapped to `onto`, and (2) update the commit message to explain the pre-seeding logic. He also floats the idea of splitting the pre-seeding logic into its own commit for clarity, following Elijah’s example.

This is a **surface-level but high-impact review**: the feedback doesn’t challenge the technical correctness of the patch but highlights a **user experience pitfall** that could block adoption. The author (Dscho) will likely need to address this before the series can progress beyond RFC. The thread remains focused on integrating Phillip Wood’s single-parent-rebase optimization and fixing the boundary commit regression Blain previously identified.
