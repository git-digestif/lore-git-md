This is a performance patch that reduces memory usage in the commit subsystem by reordering fields in `struct commit`. The change is now in its second iteration, addressing feedback from Junio C Hamano.

The patch eliminates 8 bytes of padding in `struct commit` on 64-bit platforms by moving the `index` field into the gap after `struct object`. This shrinks the struct from 80 to 72 bytes (a 10% reduction) with no behavioral change. On platforms with 4-byte alignment (like i386), the size remains unchanged. The author measured a ~32 MiB reduction in peak RSS for `git rev-list --all` on a monorepo with 4.5 million commits, closely matching the expected savings.

Version 2 adds explanatory comments for the `index` and `date` fields and clarifies that only `struct object` must come first, while the order of other fields is chosen for compactness. The patch remains minimal—just a one-line field reordering in `commit.h` plus added documentation. Derrick Stolee previously called the change "obviously correct," and Junio’s feedback has been incorporated. No technical objections remain, and the patch appears ready for merging unless discussion emerges about enforcing struct bloat awareness.

The author has prototyped a compile-time assertion (`BUILD_ASSERT_OR_ZERO`) to enforce the `struct object`-first invariant and is open to submitting it as a follow-up. They also considered a regression test but omitted it, citing maintainability concerns. The broader question of how to prevent future struct bloat remains open, but it doesn’t block this patch. For now, this is a clean, well-motivated optimization with measurable benefits.
