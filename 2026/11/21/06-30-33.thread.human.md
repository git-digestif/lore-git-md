Wonseok Oh has posted v2 of a two-patch series fixing how `git cherry-pick` handles multi-commit sequences on unborn (orphan) branches. When cherry-picking onto a branch where HEAD does not yet exist, the sequencer was writing an uninitialized value to `.git/sequencer/head` and an empty file to `.git/sequencer/abort-safety`, causing `git cherry-pick --abort` to fail with "Could not parse object" and `--skip` to die in `rollback_is_safe()` instead of producing clean error messages. Patch 1 makes `sequencer_pick_revisions()` write the null OID to the head file, and patch 2 makes `update_abort_safety_file()` do the same for the abort-safety file.

Junio C Hamano has reviewed both v2 patches and the cover letter, endorsing the series as "Looking good" with no objections. He notes that splitting the two state-file fixes into separate patches makes each change easy to verify, and approves the reworked regression test in `t/t3510-cherry-pick-sequence.sh` that checks `.git/sequencer/head` contains the null OID and that `--abort` fails cleanly with "cannot abort from a branch yet to be born." Phillip Wood had earlier traced the root cause to commit `0f974e2124` and confirmed the null-OID representation for backwards compatibility. The series remains under review with no integration status reported, and Wonseok Oh has deferred broader behavioral questions -- such as whether abort should be allowed at all on unborn branches -- to separate follow-up work.
