This is a substantive review reply in Harald Nordgren's six-patch series on rebased push branches, specifically addressing the performance objection to patch 2/6. Aniket Gupta responds to Nordgren's user-experience concern about inconsistently skipping the clean-rebase check, agreeing that unpredictable output would be confusing, but arguing that the computational cost is narrowly bounded. He notes that the expensive `--cherry-mark` comparison in `stat_outside_upstream()` only processes commits outside the upstream branch, which for a typical rebased branch is a small number, and that the 5-8x slowdown he measured earlier only manifests when thousands of commits have diverged. He concludes that the current implementation is "probably acceptable as is," effectively withdrawing his earlier performance objection without requiring the two-phase optimization. The exchange settles the immediate performance question in favor of keeping the always-on detection, leaving the series free to advance on that front while other edge cases and review points remain open.
