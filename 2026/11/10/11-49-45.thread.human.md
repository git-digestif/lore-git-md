Karthik Nayak has posted v2 of a twenty-patch series that breaks apart the monolithic `t9300-fast-import.sh` test file into smaller, self-contained scripts grouped by functionality. The goal is to eliminate the side effects and shared state that make the nearly 4000-line file hard to maintain, shrinking `t9300` to roughly 1200 lines while distributing the remaining tests across new files like `t9307-fast-import-validation.sh` and `t9311-fast-import-refs.sh`. Junio C Hamano has already endorsed the direction with strong praise, calling the cleanup "long overdue," though the series remains under review with no integration status reported.

Review activity so far has come primarily from Kaartic Sivaraam, who has now commented on patches 1 through 4. His reviews have been substantive on the early patches -- questioning whether later work will adopt `test_config` for better self-containment and asking for timing measurements on the overhead of spinning up additional repositories -- and approving but surface-level on patches 3 and 4. On the fourth patch, which converts the shared "S: initialize for S tests" setup into a reusable `setup_marks_repo()` helper so each series S test manages its own repository, Sivaraam agreed that encapsulating the more involved setup in a function is the right approach, raised no objections, and offered no formal `Reviewed-by` trailer.

Several loose ends remain open: Sivaraam's questions about `test_config` usage and the performance cost of creating and tearing down extra repositories are unanswered, and the remaining test groups (`A`, `C`, `D`, `H`, `L`, `M`, `N`, `U`) still share state in `t9300` and will need further refactoring in future work. No other reviewers have weighed in yet, and the series has not been picked up into any of Junio's integration branches.
