This thread is about a long-overdue cleanup of Git's `t9300-fast-import.sh` test file, which had grown to nearly 4,000 lines of tightly coupled tests. Karthik Nayak has split it into smaller, self-contained test files (like `t9301-fast-import-notes.sh` and `t9307-fast-import-validation.sh`), reducing `t9300` to about 1,200 lines. The series, now at version 2 with 20 patches, has strong support from maintainer Junio Hamano, who called the effort "long overdue."

The latest patch is the final cosmetic step: removing legacy "series letter" prefixes (like `Q:` or `R:`) from test descriptions in the new files. These prefixes made sense in the original monolithic file but are meaningless in the isolated tests. The change is purely mechanical—138 lines of prefixes removed across 7 files—and doesn’t affect functionality. Earlier patches did the heavy lifting of isolating tests, ensuring each runs in a fresh repository with consistent configuration (like `fastimport.unpackLimit=0` for pack edge verification).

The series is now effectively complete, with no open technical questions. The remaining work is just review to confirm the prefix removal is consistent and that test descriptions still read clearly. The only loose end is that some test groups (`A`, `C`, `D`, etc.) still live in `t9300` due to unresolved dependencies, but those are minor compared to the bulk of the cleanup. No merge status has been reported yet, but the maintainer’s praise suggests this is on track to land soon.
