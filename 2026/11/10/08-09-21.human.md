This is the final wrap-up in the `test-tool genbytes` series, a **test infrastructure** improvement targeting a long-standing macOS performance bottleneck in Git's test suite. The author, Kristofer Karlsson, replies to Collin Funk (a coreutils contributor) to clarify why shell-based workarounds (`tr`, `printf`, `yes`) are unreliable and why the new `test-tool genbytes` command is the right solution.

Kristofer confirms that macOS's `tr` lacks a single-byte fast path, even in the C locale, which explains why generating 2 GB of data takes minutes on macOS versus seconds with GNU `tr`. He also notes that `yes` is well-optimized but cannot avoid the slow filtering step needed to remove newlines. The core issue is portability: `tr` and `printf` behave inconsistently across platforms (e.g., macOS truncates `printf` output for large fields, and neither tool reliably generates NUL bytes). A dedicated test helper is simpler and more predictable than shell tool combinations.

The series is now **ready for submission as v3**. The design is settled: `test-tool genzeros [<count>]` for NUL bytes and `test-tool genbytes <char> [<count>]` for arbitrary bytes. All review feedback has been addressed, including the buffer allocation detail (Junio dismissed thread-safety concerns for this "throw-away" code). Performance gains are substantial: macOS CI jobs improve by 70–79%, while Linux jobs see modest 2–7% speedups. The patches touch 16 test scripts and introduce no on-disk format changes. No further design or implementation blockers remain.
