This thread is about a patch series from Wyatt Holden (Microsoft) that adds a configuration option, `checkout.orphanedCommitWarning`, to disable warnings about orphaned commits during `git checkout` or `git switch`. The goal is to address a performance bottleneck in large mono-repos, where the reachability check for orphaned commits can consume over 90% of checkout time. The series is currently at version 1 and under review.

Derrick Stolee (Microsoft) has endorsed the design, noting its alignment with prior patterns like `status.aheadBehind` and Scalar integration. However, Junio C Hamano has raised substantive concerns about whether a configuration knob is the right approach, suggesting instead that the reachability check itself could be optimized—such as by adding an early cutoff in the commit-traversal loop—to preserve partial safety while reducing cost. Junio has also questioned the omission of a command-line option, arguing that config knobs are less discoverable and harder to use in scripts. While Junio recently described the advice-triggering mechanism (which warns users when the check exceeds 2 seconds) as "sensible," this does not resolve his earlier objections about the design.

The series introduces a new boolean config key (`checkout.orphanedCommitWarning`, defaulting to `true`), a Trace2 region to expose the check’s duration, and an advice message that guides users to disable the warning if needed. It also integrates with Scalar to disable the warning by default in certain workflows. The patches touch `builtin/checkout.c`, `advice.c`, `scalar.c`, and related documentation and tests. No merge status has been reported, and the author has not yet responded to Junio’s design questions. The thread remains open, with the core debate centering on whether the performance issue should be addressed through optimization or configurability.
