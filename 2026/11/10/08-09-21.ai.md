This is a **test infrastructure** follow-up in the `test-tool genbytes` thread. Kristofer Karlsson replies to Collin Funk’s technical context about `tr` and `printf` behavior, confirming that macOS’s `tr` lacks a single-byte fast path and explaining why shell-based workarounds remain impractical.

Kristofer clarifies that macOS’s `tr` (from `text_cmds`) processes every byte through `getwchar()`/`putwchar()`, even in `LC_ALL=C`, which accounts for the 2-minute runtime when generating 2 GB of NUL bytes. He notes that while GNU `yes` is highly optimized, it lacks an option to omit newlines, forcing a slow filter step on macOS. His ideal primitive—`repeat <string> [<count>]`—isn’t available in standard tools, reinforcing the need for `test-tool genbytes` as a portable, predictable alternative. The email underscores the series’ core motivation: shell tools’ inconsistent behavior across platforms makes a dedicated helper more maintainable than fragile workarounds. No new design decisions or implementation changes are proposed; this is purely a clarification of the status quo.
