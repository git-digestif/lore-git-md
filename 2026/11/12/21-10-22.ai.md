This is a follow-up to Okhuomon Ajayi’s Outreachy patch that renames `credentialStore.lockTimeoutMS` to `credentialStore.lockTimeout` while keeping the old name as a deprecated alias. Junio C Hamano’s reply questions the fundamental rationale behind the change, not just its implementation.

The core issue Junio raises is whether Git’s timeout variables are consistently measured in milliseconds. If they are, dropping the unit suffix (`MS`) makes sense for simplicity. If they are not—if some timeouts are in seconds, others in milliseconds—then the suffix is necessary for clarity, and removing it would be misleading. The patch’s commit message does not address this ambiguity, leaving future readers to wonder why the change was made.

Junio’s feedback is substantive: he is not objecting to the patch’s mechanics but to its conceptual foundation. The patch currently cites consistency with `rerere.lockTimeout` as its sole justification, but that precedent is only valid if the unit (milliseconds) is uniform across all timeout variables. Without that context, the change appears arbitrary. The thread now hinges on whether the project has a consistent unit policy for timeouts, and if so, what it is. Until that is clarified, the patch cannot proceed.
