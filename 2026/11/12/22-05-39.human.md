This is the third patch in Andy Koppe’s v4 series (5 patches total) that aligns Git’s documentation with the 2024 terminology change distinguishing "pseudorefs" (now only `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD`) from "root refs" (refs outside `refs/`, like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`). This patch focuses on updating seven manual pages to replace outdated uses of "pseudoref" with "root ref" and correct related inaccuracies.

The changes are precise and well-motivated. In `git-worktree(1)`, the patch clarifies that both root refs and pseudorefs are per-worktree, drops the misleading description of them as being directly under `$GIT_DIR` (which doesn’t apply to the reftable backend), and explicitly states that the list of per-worktree prefixes will not be extended. In `git-ls-remote(1)`, it updates the description to state that `HEAD` and refs under `refs/` are shown by default, and rewords `--refs` and examples to avoid the term "pseudoref". The patch also corrects inaccuracies in `git-update-ref(1)` (removing the incorrect mention of `ORIG_HEAD` from reflog documentation and clarifying that `core.logAllRefUpdates` applies to root refs) and in `gitdatamodel.adoc` (distinguishing root refs and pseudorefs and adding glossary cross-references).

The updates are uncontroversial and address all prior review feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt. The series as a whole is ready for merging, and this patch is a key part of ensuring consistency across Git’s documentation. No behavioral changes are introduced—this is purely a terminology cleanup. The patch touches seven files and is a clear improvement over the current state.
