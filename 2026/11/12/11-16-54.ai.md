This is the **latest substantive review** in the `fetchRefmap` series, now in its 13th iteration and under final review for merging to `master`. Phillip Wood **revisits a fundamental design objection** to Patch 3/6 (dynamic branch enumeration) and **proposes an alternative architecture** that could simplify the series and eliminate the need for special-casing refspec-less fetches. His critique **challenges the core design direction** of the series and may require **significant rework** if adopted, potentially unblocking the dependent topic sooner by splitting the series.

Phillip’s key objection is that Patch 3/6 **overloads the meaning of `remote.<name>.fetch`** by using it for both "what to fetch" and "where to store it" when no refspec is given. He argues that this violates the separation of concerns introduced in Patch 1/6 (`remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` for "where to store it") and creates unnecessary complexity. Instead, he proposes **eliminating the special case for refspec-less fetches** entirely: the default values of `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` should determine what to fetch and where to store it, with users configuring these keys to achieve their desired behavior. This would **remove the need for dynamic branch enumeration** (Patch 3/6) and simplify the precedence logic in `get_ref_map()`.

The proposal has **major implications for the series**:
- **Patch 3/6 (dynamic branch enumeration)** would become **unnecessary**, as the default values of `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` would handle the refspec-less case.
- The **precedence rules** in `remote.c` would simplify, as there would be no special case for refspec-less fetches.
- The **override semantics discussion** (whether command-line refspecs or colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries override `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`) would become **less critical**, as the behavior would be determined by configuration rather than runtime logic.
- The **dependent topic** (`ch/fetch-followremotehead-lazy-validation`) might be unblocked sooner, as the foundational plumbing (Patches 1–2) could graduate independently.

Phillip’s critique **directly contradicts the thread’s earlier consensus**, which endorsed the current design (including dynamic branch enumeration) as a way to avoid overloading `remote.<name>.refmap`. Junio’s summary of Patches 1–3 (provided in the parent email) **implicitly endorsed the current approach**, framing Patch 3/6 as the logical culmination of the refmap decoupling. However, Phillip’s alternative **offers a cleaner separation of concerns** and aligns with the series’ original goal of making refspec behavior more predictable.

The **trade-offs** of Phillip’s proposal are:
- **Pros**: Simpler code, no special cases, clearer separation of "what" and "where," and potential to unblock the dependent topic sooner.
- **Cons**: Requires users to explicitly configure `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` to achieve the current default behavior (fetching only branches tracked by local branches), which may be less ergonomic. The current design’s dynamic branch enumeration is **backwards-compatible** with existing shallow repositories, while Phillip’s proposal would require users to update their configurations.

Phillip also **raises concerns about the "doubling up"** that occurs when both `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` are set. This is the **override semantics edge case** that has been under discussion: should colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entries override `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` (Phillip’s preference) or coexist with it (the current behavior)? His critique **reinforces the need to resolve this edge case** before graduation, as the current dual-storage behavior is **confirmed as incorrect** and contradicts the series’ design goals.
