This is the v4 iteration of a documentation patch series that aligns Git’s glossary, man pages, code comments, and tests with the 2024 terminology change distinguishing "pseudorefs" (only FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD) from "root refs" (refs outside the `refs/` hierarchy, such as HEAD and ORIG_HEAD). The series is now in its final form, incorporating all review feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt.

This specific email is Andy Koppe’s reply to Junio’s review of Patch 1/5 (v3), confirming the two editorial tweaks Junio requested: removing redundant contrasts between pseudorefs and root refs in the glossary’s "pseudoref" entry. Andy also notes that v4 expands the glossary’s "ref" entry to explicitly mention remote-tracking branches alongside local branches and tags, while omitting notes as a less essential example. The email briefly acknowledges Junio’s tangential observation about Git’s current leniency in treating arbitrary files outside `refs/` as refs, deferring that issue to a future effort.

The series touches 36 files, including the glossary, seven man pages, four code files, ten test scripts, and the bash completion script. Key updates include: a new "root ref" glossary entry with an exhaustive list of irregular root refs and naming rules; a rewritten "pseudoref" entry that emphasizes their unique semantics (multi-object storage, metadata) and incompatibility with the ref backend; and consistent terminology updates across documentation, error messages, and test scripts. The `per-worktree ref` entry is now an itemized list for clarity, and the `worktree` entry cross-references it. No behavioral changes are introduced—this is purely a terminology and documentation cleanup.

The series is uncontroversial, addresses all prior feedback, and is ready for merging. The only unresolved technical point (Git’s leniency with arbitrary files as refs) is explicitly deferred.
