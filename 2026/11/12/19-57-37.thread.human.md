Adrian Ratiu's three-patch v2 series fixes bugs in Git's SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability layer (`compatObjectFormat`). Patch 1 prevents a crash that left repositories unusable when `compatObjectFormat` and `objectFormat` were set to the same value; patch 2 makes `git rev-parse --output-object-format` report an error instead of silently returning a wrong OID when a mapping fails; and patch 3 makes conversion of unknown commit headers consistent between existing and new commits, copying them verbatim rather than rejecting them. Junio C Hamano has approved patches 1 and 2 outright, and in his latest substantive review endorsed the direction of patch 3 -- agreeing that only known object-ID headers (`tree`, `parent`, `mergetag`) need conversion and that the special cases for `author`, `committer`, `encoding`, and `gpgsig` can be removed -- while questioning the change from `<` to `<=` in the header-length checks and asking why a bare `"parent "` line should be fed to the OID parser rather than treated as an unknown header. He has since said the v2 changes overall "looked reasonable," though he noted he is neither Eric Sunshine nor brian m. carlson, the two reviewers Adrian specifically asked to weigh in on patch 3.

Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the series and his feedback -- cosmetic fixes to patch 1, commit-message rewording and a reworked test approach for patch 2, and the `<=` classification fix with a new empty-mergetag test in patch 3 -- has all been incorporated in v2. Adrian has pushed back on adding generic header validation inside the converter, arguing it would diverge from what `git commit --amend` accepts and suggesting `fsck` as the proper place for stricter checks; he also acknowledged Patrick's forward-looking idea of an `x-obj-` prefix for custom headers carrying object IDs as future work. The series remains under review, with the main open items being Junio's questions about the `<=` edge cases in patch 3 and the still-unanswered request for review from Eric Sunshine or brian m. carlson on that patch's removal of the 2023 unknown-header rejection.
