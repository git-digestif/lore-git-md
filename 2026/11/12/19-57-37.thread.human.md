This thread is about a three-patch series from Adrian Ratiu that fixes bugs in Git’s `compatObjectFormat` interoperability layer, which handles conversions between SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories. The first two patches—fixing a crash when both formats are set identically and correcting silent incorrect OID returns from `git rev-parse`—have received minor feedback (cosmetic adjustments to error messages and test syntax) but are uncontroversial. The third patch, which removes a 2023 safety check to reject unknown commit headers during conversion, has sparked deeper discussion.

Patrick Steinhardt’s latest review engages substantively with the third patch’s trade-offs. He raises two points: first, whether malformed commit headers (like `"tree"` without a trailing space) should be rejected rather than copied unchanged, though he acknowledges this might not be worth the added complexity. Second, he suggests a future-proofing idea—using a standardized prefix (e.g., `x-obj-`) for custom headers containing object IDs—to allow Git to convert such IDs automatically. However, he clarifies this is beyond the current series’ scope. His feedback is exploratory rather than blocking, and he endorses the patch’s direction while refining its implementation.

The series remains under review, with the third patch’s fate hinging on whether other reviewers weigh in on the malformed-header edge case or the `x-obj-` proposal. The fixes are well-justified and likely to land, but the discussion may shift toward tighter validation or clearer documentation of the trade-offs. No merge decisions have been made yet.
