Adrian Ratiu has posted the second iteration of his three-patch series fixing bugs in Git's SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability layer (`compatObjectFormat`). The series addresses a crash when `compatObjectFormat` matches `objectFormat`, a silent incorrect OID return from `git rev-parse --output-object-format`, and inconsistent handling of unknown commit headers during object conversion. Junio C Hamano has approved patches 1 and 2, and while still reviewing the third patch's details, has endorsed the overall direction of the series. In his latest reply, Ratiu clarified that the rev-parse fix covers invocations with multiple explicit single revisions like `git rev-parse --output-object-format=sha1 main next` but not range arguments such as `A..B` or internal shorthands like `rev^!`, committing to spell out this distinction in the v3 commit message; he also credited Patrick Steinhardt for the test design in `t1500-rev-parse.sh` that Junio had praised as "clever and clean."

The first patch prevents repositories from becoming unusable when `extensions.compatObjectFormat` is set to the same value as `objectFormat`, replacing a `BUG()` abort with a clear error message. The second makes `git rev-parse --output-object-format` die with "cannot map object" when an object lacks a compat mapping rather than silently printing the storage OID. The third unifies the handling of unknown commit headers so they are copied verbatim in both existing and new commits, removing a 2023 safety check; Junio questioned the specific `<=` length-check change but approved the approach of converting only known headers like `tree`, `parent`, and `mergetag`.

The series remains under review. Ratiu has explicitly requested additional review from Eric Sunshine or brian m. carlson on patch 3's trade-offs -- Junio noted he is neither of those reviewers when approving -- and Junio's specific questions about why malformed headers like `"parent \n"` should enter the object-parsing branch rather than being copied as unknown headers remain to be addressed in a future version.
