This thread is a three-part bugfix series from Adrian Ratiu aimed at fixing issues in Git’s `compatObjectFormat` interoperability layer, which handles conversions between SHA-1 and SHA-256 repositories. The first two patches address a crash when both formats are set identically and a silent incorrect OID return from `git rev-parse`, respectively—both uncontroversial and well-justified. The third patch, however, is the focal point of discussion: it removes a 2023 safety check that rejected unknown commit headers during conversions, arguing that consistency with external tools (like Jujutsu, which relies on custom headers) outweighs the risk of copying potentially problematic headers.

Adrian has provided thorough test coverage for all three fixes, and the first two patches are likely to be merged without issue. The third patch, though, touches on a deliberate design decision from 2023, and its removal may face pushback. Reviewers have not yet weighed in, but the trade-off—consistency for tool compatibility versus safety—will likely dominate the discussion. If accepted, the change would unify how unknown headers are handled in both existing and new commits, simplifying the code but potentially introducing edge cases. The series is currently under review, with no prior versions, and its fate hinges on whether the project prioritizes interoperability over the existing guardrail.
