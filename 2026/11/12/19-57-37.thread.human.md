Adrian Ratiu has sent a three-patch series fixing bugs in Git's SHA-1/SHA-256 interoperability layer (`compatObjectFormat`), and the review thread is converging toward a v2. The series addresses a crash when `compatObjectFormat` equals `objectFormat`, incorrect output from `git rev-parse --output-object-format` when OID mapping fails, and inconsistent handling of unknown commit headers during object conversion. Patrick Steinhardt has reviewed all three patches: his feedback on the first two is limited to cosmetic issues—error-message capitalization, test-script syntax, commit-message wording, and a more robust test approach for `t1500-rev-parse.sh` that avoids manually copying loose objects—which Adrian has accepted and promised to fold into v2. The third patch, which removes a 2023 safety check that rejected unknown commit headers, has drawn more substantive discussion; Patrick explored the trade-offs between consistency and safety, raised edge cases around malformed headers, and proposed a future `x-obj-` prefix convention for extensibility, while endorsing the patch's overall direction. The series remains under review with no integration status yet, and Adrian notes he will update Jujutsu to reject repositories using this extension, keeping the fix scoped to Git itself.
