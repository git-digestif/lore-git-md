This thread is about a performance optimization to reduce memory usage in Git's commit subsystem. Kristofer Karlsson proposed reordering fields in `struct commit` to eliminate padding on 64-bit platforms, shrinking the struct from 80 to 72 bytes (a 10% reduction) with no behavioral change. The patch has been through two iterations, with feedback from Derrick Stolee (who called it "obviously correct") and Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, who suggested minor comment clarifications that have now been addressed.

The change is ready for maintainer pickup, with Junio indicating he will queue it. It touches only `commit.h`, moving the `index` field after `struct object` and adding explanatory comments for `index`, `date`, and the direction of safe casting between `struct commit *` and `struct object *`. Measurements on a large repository show a 32 MiB peak-RSS reduction for `git rev-list --all`, matching expectations. While the author prototyped a regression test for struct size, it was omitted for maintainability and offered as a follow-up if requested.

A broader discussion about how to enforce awareness of struct bloat remains open but doesn't block this patch. Proposed approaches include compile-time assertions, compiler-specific `-Wpadded` warnings, or architecture-specific dummy members to satisfy the compiler without affecting behavior. Oswald Buddenhagen suggested the latter as a pragmatic alternative to the author's proposed `BUILD_ASSERT_OR_ZERO` approach. No technical objections remain, and the patch is now one step away from being picked up for the `next` branch.
