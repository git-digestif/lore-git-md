This is the latest exchange in the long-running `fetchRefmap` series, now in its thirteenth iteration. The series aims to solve a performance problem in shallow repositories: a refspec-less `git fetch` currently negotiates history for *all* remote branches, which can be slow or hang when the remote has many branches. The solution introduces `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` to decouple "which refs to fetch" from "where to store them," defaulting to fetching only branches tracked by local branches when no explicit refspec is given.

Phillip Wood’s reply here is substantive and advances the thread in two key ways. First, he **reiterates his fundamental objection to Patch 3/6**, which implements dynamic branch enumeration (the "special case" for refspec-less fetches). He argues that this logic should instead be controlled by the *default values* of `remote.<name>.fetch` and `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`, making the behavior configurable rather than hard-coded. This is not a new critique—it was raised earlier in the thread—but Phillip’s framing here is sharper: he sees the current implementation as an unnecessary special case that complicates the design. The objection is technical and principled, not a nit, and it directly challenges the series’ core behavior.

Second, Phillip **asks a critical process question**: which other topic is blocked by this series, and would merging just the foundational patches (1–3) unblock it? Junio had mentioned that this series is keeping another topic waiting, but Phillip notes that nothing in the latest "What’s cooking" report marks any topic as dependent on this one. The exchange also reveals a subtle but important disagreement about the role of `remote.<name>.fetch`. Phillip is concerned about "doubling up"—the current behavior where a colon-separated `remote.<name>.fetch` entry (e.g., `refs/heads/master:refs/remotes/origin/master`) coexists with `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`, storing the ref in both destinations. This was previously discussed as a potential ambiguity, but Phillip’s phrasing here ("I am concerned about the doubling up") suggests he sees it as a concrete problem, not just a theoretical one. This aligns with the thread’s converging consensus toward *override semantics* (where `remote.<name>.fetch` entries override `fetchRefmap` entirely), but Phillip does not explicitly endorse that resolution here—he’s focused on the inconsistency itself.
