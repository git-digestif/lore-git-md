This is a substantive review of patch 1/4 in Patrick Steinhardt's four-patch performance series on reflog expiration during auto-maintenance. Karthik Nayak replies to the patch that makes the reftable backend skip writing a new table to disk when `git reflog expire` runs with `--dry-run`, confirming that the previous behavior was indeed wrong and that the fix looks sensible. His review goes beyond a simple approval, however: he notes that a code comment in `refs/reftable-backend.c` -- the "Future improvement" remark about skipping unchanged records -- does not appear to be removed by the subsequent patch in the series (patch 2/4), which implements that very improvement. This shows he is reading the series as a whole rather than reviewing each patch in isolation. The patch under review addresses a real inefficiency where dry-run invocations were committing identical reflog tables to disk, yielding roughly an 11 percent wall-time reduction in benchmarks, and it serves as a prerequisite for the series' larger goal of preventing `git maintenance run --auto` from spawning unnecessary expiration processes. No merge status is indicated; the series remains under review.
