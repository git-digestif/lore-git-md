This is a substantive review exchange within the `[PATCH v2]` thread for the bounds-safety refactoring of ANSI escape sequence handling in `utf8.c`. Andy Koppe replies to Dirk Gouders to settle a technical question about the ECMA-48 grammar for SGR sequences, confirming that the regular expression `"\033\[[0-9:;]*m"` covers the complete syntactic range of valid SGR sequences. He answers Dirk's specific probe by confirming that `"\033[:;m"` is indeed a valid SGR sequence -- parsed as two parameters, where the first consists of two empty subparameters and the second is an empty parameter -- and offers a decoding hint for the ECMA-48 specification itself (that 3/0 denotes 0x30, i.e. `'0'`, and 3/11 denotes 0x3B, i.e. `';'`).

The exchange advances an unresolved debate over whether the new `display_mode_esc_sequence_len_counted()` parser should accept colon-separated subparameters alongside digits and semicolons. Dirk had refined his earlier objection -- conceding that the existing parser already acts as a finite state machine for `"\033\[[0-9;]+m"` -- to question whether simply adding `:` to the accepted alphabet without new states would still restrict acceptance to valid SGR sequences. Andy counters with the grammar-based argument that no additional states are needed because the parameter field legitimately permits any mix of digits, colons, and semicolons, with empty subparameters equivalent to zero.

This email does not change the status of the patch itself: v2 remains under review with the authorship transfer from Junio C Hamano to Dirk Gouders confirmed but the repost as `[PATCH v2 1/2]` (with clar unit tests as `2/2`) still pending. No decision has been reached on extending the parser to handle colon separators, and the discussion illustrates the difficulty of defining the exact language the parser intends to accept without consulting the full ECMA-48 specification.
