This is a review reply to the third patch in Patrick Steinhardt's four-part performance series on reflog expiration. Toon Claes responds to patch 3/4, which reworks the `reflog-expire` auto-condition in `builtin/gc.c` to skip the expensive reachability walk and compare entries only against `gc.reflogExpire`.

Toon engages substantively with the core tradeoff: he asks whether Patrick meant "not calling" `reflog_expiry_prepare()` (a wording question in the commit message) and where that function was previously invoked, showing he has read the surrounding code. He then explicitly endorses the approach, noting that pulling out the first condition from `should_expire_reflog_ent()` while skipping the reachability check makes sense. After Patrick explains that the whole point of auto-maintenance heuristics is to avoid blocking on expensive foreground walks, Toon agrees that undercounting prunable unreachable entries is a worthwhile tradeoff given the roughly 21x wall-time improvement in `git maintenance run --auto`.

The review is supportive with no objections or requests for changes. It confirms that the performance motivation -- eliminating a near-second hang after commands that trigger auto-maintenance -- justifies the heuristic simplification, and that users who need unreachable-entry pruning can run maintenance without `--auto` or invoke `git reflog expire` directly.
