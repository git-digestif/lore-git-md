This is a follow-up commentary from the patch author on the `gittutorial-branches` documentation patch, not a new revision. Julia Evans replies to her own submission after a week away to share four observations about the tutorial's content and reception, and to note that she will see the review through even though her current funding ends November 30.

The first note addresses test-reader recruitment: despite interest from people wanting to learn about branches, it was harder to get test readers for this topic than expected, though those who completed it finished in under an hour; she suggests a table of contents might help readers gauge the document's structure upfront. Second, she acknowledges the tutorial attempts to introduce collaborative workflows without explaining remotes or upstreams (to keep length manageable), which may leave readers with questions and suggests a future `git help remotes` guide. Third, she observes that some long-time Git users (10+ years) still wanted a branches tutorial and were confused by `git switch`, suggesting the document should also mention the older `git checkout` command for familiarity. Fourth, she defends the exclusive use of `git log --graph` for branch visualizations despite readers finding the ASCII art difficult, noting there is no better alternative and no precedent for SVG in HTML docs.

The email contains no technical objections or requests for changes to the patch itself; it is purely reflective author commentary intended to help reviewers understand the design tradeoffs. The patch remains under review with no merge status change indicated.
