This is a thread-management reply in an ongoing bounds-safety refactoring of ANSI escape sequence handling in `utf8.c`. Junio C Hamano suggests to Dirk Gouders that he take over authorship of the patch, proposing it become a `[PATCH v2 1/2]` series under Dirk's name. This follows Dirk's concession that correctness outweighs the micro-optimization of an early-return check for short strings, and his explicit permission for Junio to use the clar unit test code he provided.

The underlying patch adds `display_mode_esc_sequence_len_counted(const char *s, size_t len)` in `utf8.c` (declared in `utf8.h`) to scan exactly `len` bytes for ANSI SGR sequences without reading past `s[len]`, returning the sequence length or 0. The original `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` remains as a wrapper passing `strlen(s)`. Three callers inside `utf8.c` -- `utf8_strnwidth()`, `strbuf_add_wrapped_text()`, and `strbuf_utf8_replace()` -- were switched to the counted variant in v2. Only `utf8.c` and `utf8.h` are touched (27 insertions, 9 deletions).

Dirk had prepared clar unit tests slated for `t/unit-tests/u-utf8-ansi.c` (registered via `CLAR_TEST_SUITES += u-utf8-ansi` in the `Makefile` and corresponding `t/meson.build` entry) that assert the counted function returns 0 for a truncated `"\033[m"` at `len=2`, 3 at `len=3`, 5 for `"\033[31m"`, and 7 for `"\033[0;31m"`. Andy Koppe separately raised whether the parser should accept colons as parameter separators per ECMA-48 and xterm behavior (e.g. `ESC[38:5:123m`); Junio responded by questioning whether Git itself generates such sequences, leaving that question unresolved. No integration branch status is mentioned; the topic remains under review.
