This is the fourth and final patch of a v2 series (cover letter not provided) that fixes a boundary-handling bug in `utf8_strnwidth()`. The commit message explains that when `utf8_strnwidth()` is called with a `len` that cuts through the middle of a multibyte UTF-8 sequence, the inner call to `utf8_width()` was being made without its optional boundary argument, causing it to read past the requested length and potentially mis-count the display width. The fix computes the remaining bytes (`remainder = orig + len - string`) at each iteration and passes `&remainder` to `utf8_width()`, so incomplete sequences at the boundary are handled correctly and the function returns `len` as the commit message states it should in such cases.

The patch touches two files: `utf8.c`, where the one-line logic change plus a new `remainder` variable are introduced, and `t/unit-tests/u-utf8-width.c`, which gains a new clar unit test `test_utf8_width__strnwidth_cjk_chopped`. That test exercises a CJK string (`"三bytes long, 二columns wide"`) with boundary values of 1 and 3 bytes -- both of which split multibyte characters -- and asserts the resulting display widths are 1 and 2 respectively, verifying that the boundary is now respected.

From a downstream perspective, the key names to watch are `utf8_strnwidth()` and `utf8_width()` in `utf8.c`, and the new test function in the unit-test suite. The behavioral change is subtle but real: callers that previously relied on `utf8_strnwidth()` to stop at exactly `len` bytes now get correct width counts instead of potentially reading past the buffer or miscalculating width when the cutoff lands mid-character. This looks like a well-crafted, narrowly scoped bugfix with appropriate test coverage.
