This is a substantive review by Patrick Steinhardt of the first patch in Elia Pinto's v2 series fixing large-object truncation on 32-bit platforms. Steinhardt raises two distinct concerns: first, that the commit message is excessively verbose and documents obvious mechanical details rather than focusing on the non-obvious design decisions, suggesting it could be condensed significantly; second, and more substantively, he questions the introduction of `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` guards inside `write_object()` where the size is passed to `odb_write_object()` and `parse_object_buffer()`. Because a separate patch series already in flight is converting those downstream functions to `size_t`, these casts would become redundant and require yet another cleanup series once both land, prompting Steinhardt to ask whether it would make more sense to defer this entire series until the downstream conversions have landed. The review engages directly with the patch's sequencing and temporary-solution strategy rather than surface-level details, identifying a real maintainability cost in the current approach. No resolution is recorded yet; the exchange leaves an open question for the author about whether to reorder the series dependencies.
