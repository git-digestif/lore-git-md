This is a substantive maintainer review of the first patch in Elia Pinto's two-part bugfix series fixing silent truncation of trees larger than 4 GiB. Junio C Hamano endorses the core change -- swapping `unsigned long` for `size_t` in `decode_tree_entry()` and related tree-walk functions -- as "obviously the right thing," noting that the type widening cannot introduce new wraparound bugs if the original code was correct.

The bulk of the review, however, digs into the logic of `decode_tree_entry()` itself rather than the type change. Junio walks through the tree entry format (octal mode, space, name, NUL, binary OID) and examines the function's sanity checks. He observes that the minimum-length check (`size < hashsz + 3`) is pessimistically short but valid, and flags the repeated check that the buffer's final byte is NUL as redundant across iterative calls -- since the caller advances the buffer and shrinks the size in lockstep, that check could be done once in `init_tree_desc()` instead, an optimization he marks as `#leftoverbits`. He also explains why the subsequent `strlen()` on the path is safe given that trailing NUL guarantee.

The review closes by confirming the patch looks good. It is a thorough, code-level engagement that validates the fix while surfacing a minor performance consideration in the existing parsing logic. No objections are raised to the series; the tone is approving, with the maintainer treating the size_t conversion as settled and the deeper function analysis as the more interesting part of the discussion.
