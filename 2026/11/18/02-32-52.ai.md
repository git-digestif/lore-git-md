This is a substantive maintainer review of the first patch in Elia Pinto's two-part series fixing silent truncation of trees larger than 4 GiB. Junio C Hamano confirms that widening the size fields from `unsigned long` to `size_t` in `decode_tree_entry()` and related functions is the obviously correct approach, but directs attention to the entry-parsing logic inside `decode_tree_entry()` itself. He explains that each tree entry is a variable-length record formatted as `<octal mode> SP <name> NUL <binary oid>`, and that `update_tree_entry()` repeatedly calls `decode_tree_entry()` by advancing `buf` and decrementing `size` for each entry parsed. He identifies the sanity check `if (size < hashsz + 3 || buf[size - (hashsz + 1)])` as containing a redundant validation: the second clause checks that the buffer ends with NUL followed by the binary OID on every single call, when it only needs to be verified once in `init_tree_desc()` since the caller maintains the invariant that `size` always refers to the remaining unparsed portion. He notes that this redundancy currently makes the `strlen(path)` call safe (guaranteeing a NUL terminator exists within bounds), but flags the repetition as an optimization opportunity tagged `#leftoverbits`. Despite this nit, he concludes that the patch itself looks good. No objections are raised to the core `size_t` conversion; the review focuses on a potential micro-optimization in the hot parsing loop that does not affect correctness.
