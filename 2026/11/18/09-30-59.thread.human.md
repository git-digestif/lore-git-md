This thread covers a four-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that fixes a performance regression where `git maintenance run --auto` would spawn `git reflog expire` unnecessarily, causing roughly a one-second hang. The root cause was in `builtin/gc.c`: the auto-condition logic attempted expensive reachability checks on reflog entries, treating them as unreachable and triggering expiration against the wrong threshold. The series addresses this across the reftable backend and the maintenance machinery, with benchmarks on git.git showing the full series drops auto-maintenance wall time from about 853 ms to 18.6 ms -- roughly a 45x speedup -- while cutting memory usage by about 31 percent. Patch 1/4 and 2/4 optimize `reftable_be_reflog_expire` in `refs/reftable-backend.c` to skip disk writes during dry-runs and no-op expirations, patch 3/4 reworks `reflog_expire_condition()` in `builtin/gc.c` to compare timestamps only against `gc.reflogExpire` without reachability walks, and patch 4/4 fixes a documentation typo in `Documentation/config/maintenance.adoc`.

Karthik Nayak has provided substantive review on the first two patches, confirming the technical approach while raising a concern about a now-stale "future improvement" comment in the reftable backend, which Patrick clarified refers to a finer-grained optimization than the all-or-nothing skip implemented in patch 2/4. In his latest reply, Patrick also addressed two smaller points: he declined a suggestion to add a comment about skipped writes at the early-return site since the behavior is self-evident, and he noted that the test added in patch 2/4 already exercises patch 1/4's dry-run path because it includes a `git reflog expire --all --dry-run` invocation verifying that `tables.list` remains unchanged.

The series remains at version 1 and under review, with no integration into `next`, `seen`, or `master` reported. Patches 3/4 and 4/4 have not yet received review feedback, leaving the reachability-logic fix and the doc correction open for comment.
