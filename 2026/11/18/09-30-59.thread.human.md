Patrick Steinhardt has sent a four-patch series fixing a performance problem where `git maintenance run --auto` spawned `git reflog expire` on every run, causing nearly a second of hang time even when nothing needed expiring. The root cause was in `builtin/gc.c`: the auto-condition tried to determine reachability of reflog entries, which treated everything as unreachable and forced an expensive expiration pass. Patch 3/4 fixes this by comparing timestamps only against `gc.reflogExpire` and skipping the reachability walk entirely during auto-maintenance. Patches 1/4 and 2/4 optimize the reftable backend to skip writing new tables when nothing actually changes (dry-runs or no-op expirations), and patch 4/4 corrects a copy-paste typo in the maintenance documentation. Benchmarks on git.git show the full series dropping auto-maintenance wall time from 853 ms to 18.6 ms with memory usage down about 31 percent.

Review has been largely positive. Karthik Nayak reviewed the reftable changes substantively, confirming the technical approach while asking about a stale "future improvement" comment that Patrick clarified refers to a finer-grained optimization than what the patches implement. Toon Claes has engaged across the series: he approved patch 3/4's tradeoff of undercounting unreachable entries as acceptable given the performance gain, lightheartedly corrected the cover letter's description of patch 4/4 as the root-cause fix (it is only a doc change), and praised the benchmarks. Two minor nits remain open: Toon's question about inconsistent hash-size constants (`rawsz` versus `GIT_MAX_RAWSZ`) in `reftable_be_reflog_expire`, which he himself judged not worth a reroll, and his query about possible wording in patch 3/4's commit message; Patrick has not yet responded to either. No Reviewed-by or Acked-by trailers have been recorded yet, and the series remains under review with no indication of integration into `next`, `seen`, or `master`.
