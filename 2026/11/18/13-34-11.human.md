This is a substantive review reply from Patrick Steinhardt on the fourth patch of Elia Pinto's v2 series fixing 32-bit object-size truncation. Rather than commenting on the mechanical type change itself, he zeroes in on a behavioral consequence: when `git fsck` passes only a truncated size prefix to `parse_object_buffer()` and `fsck_object()`, he asks whether those functions actually detect that the object is incomplete and report an error, or whether they silently treat the prefix as the whole object. If the latter, he notes, that is itself a bug -- large objects could pass fsck while only a fraction of their content is actually verified.

The question lands squarely on the gap that patch 4/4 explicitly leaves open: the patch converts the callback signatures to `size_t`, but `parse_object_buffer()` and `fsck_object()` still accept `unsigned long` internally, so on 64-bit Windows with objects >= 4 GiB, fsck continues to inspect only a prefix until a follow-up series converts those functions as well. Patrick is probing whether that interim state is safe (errors are raised) or dangerous (silent partial verification).

No resolution is offered; the comment reads as an open question for the author to answer, alongside his earlier sequencing concerns about patches 1/4 and 2/4. The series remains under review with no merge status indicated.
