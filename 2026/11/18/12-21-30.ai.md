This is a substantive review of the first patch (1/5) in Elia Pinto's v2 series on loose object handling, written by Patrick Steinhardt. The patch fixes `unpack_loose_rest()` in `object-file.c` to validate that the inflated stream length matches the header-declared size, preventing both uninitialized memory exposure and silent data truncation.

Patrick engages directly with the mechanics of the bug: he acknowledges the uninitialized-heap case is "fishy" and asks for clarification on Elia's description of the opposite case (header claims fewer bytes than the stream contains), questioning why the zlib stream would have already ended in that scenario. He agrees with the core approach of comparing `total_out` against the expected size and rejecting mismatches as corruption rather than papering over them, noting this mirrors the existing validation in `unpack_compressed_entry()`. He raises two code-level suggestions: replacing the `strlen(3p)` call on the known-bounded header buffer with `memchr(buffer, '\0', size)` (which he notes could be a separate patch), and making the "corrupt loose object" error message more specific when the size mismatch is the cause rather than a generic stream-end failure. The review is substantive rather than surface-level, probing the edge-case logic of the streaming inflation path and proposing concrete alternatives, though it does not identify any blocking flaw in the patch's approach.
