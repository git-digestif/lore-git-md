This is a documentation follow-up from the author, not a new patch. Julia Evans replies to her own thread with personal notes on the `gittutorial-branches` patch after stepping away for a week, offering context that reviewers might find useful while evaluating the beginner-focused tutorial.

She flags four pedagogical pressure points she noticed during user testing. First, recruiting test readers proved harder than expected despite interest in the topic, and she suggests a table of contents might help readers gauge the commitment upfront. Second, the tutorial attempts to show collaboration workflows without explaining remotes or upstream branches, which she acknowledges leaves a gap; she floats the idea of a future `git help remotes` guide to fill it. Third, she notes that some test readers with years of Git experience still wanted a branches tutorial, and those readers were confused by `git switch` when they expected `git checkout` -- suggesting the tutorial might mention both commands. Fourth, she defends the choice to use `git log --graph` ASCII art for diagrams despite some readers struggling with it, arguing there is no better existing alternative and no prior art for SVG in the HTML docs.

The email also includes a personal note that her funding ends November 30, though she commits to seeing the patch through regardless. There are no new technical objections or requests for changes; this reads as the author doing her own quality pass and sharing observations that might help reviewers understand the tutorial's design trade-offs. The patch remains under review with no indication of movement toward an integration branch.
