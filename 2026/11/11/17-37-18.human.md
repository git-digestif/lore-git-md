This is a maintainer's response to Phillip Wood's critique of the v13 cover letter for Harald Nordgren's six-patch series introducing `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` for matching-inspired fetch mode. Junio acknowledges Phillip's point as fair and provides a concise, three-point summary of the first three patches' purpose, effectively filling the documentation gap Phillip identified. He describes the core design clearly: the series extends `--refmap` to work without explicit refspecs, refactors a helper for reuse, and implements dynamic branch enumeration so `git fetch remote` fetches only branches tracked by local branches. Junio expresses no strong opinion on the ergonomic improvements in patches 4 and 5 (the `--limited-fetch` flag and shallow-repository default) and is uninterested in patch 6 (the `@{push}` resolution fix), but notes the series is blocking another topic and should move forward. The email signals that the series is ready to graduate once the override semantics edge case is resolved and the cover letter is improved, but does not itself address either issue. It is a low-key endorsement of the design and a nudge to wrap up loose ends.
