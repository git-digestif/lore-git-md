This is a follow-up in the now-merged `git rerere` subcommand modernization series, focusing on a latent robustness issue in the flag-handling logic. The series itself—a 12-patch refactoring that replaced `git rerere`'s custom subcommand parsing with Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism—was merged to `master` on 2025-06-25 and is complete.

The discussion here centers on the three-state system formed by the `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` and `RERERE_NOAUTOUPDATE` flags. Junio’s earlier email clarified that these flags are not a binary toggle but a way to override the configured `rerere.autoUpdate` default: (0) follow the default, (1) force `--autoupdate`, or (2) force `--no-autoupdate`. The CLI parser (`parse_opt_tertiary()`) prevents both flags from being set simultaneously, but the consuming code does not explicitly validate this. Patrick points out that in `rerere_cache_setup()`, `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` silently takes precedence if both flags are set, which is not documented and could mask bugs. Both agree this is a latent safety concern, though not a regression—it predates the series and is now more visible due to the refactoring.

The tone is exploratory rather than urgent. No patch is proposed here, and the issue is framed as a low-priority correctness nit. The thread remains open on how (or whether) to address it, but the series itself is already merged and no further revisions are expected. For a busy reader, this is a minor follow-up about internal robustness, not a blocker or a user-facing change.
