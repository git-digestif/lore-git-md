This is a process critique from Phillip Wood, a key reviewer, on the v13 cover letter for Harald Nordgren’s six-patch series introducing `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap`. The series is otherwise technically complete, under final review for merging to `master`, and addresses a long-standing performance issue in shallow repositories by dynamically fetching only branches tracked by local branches at the remote.

Phillip’s feedback is blunt but constructive: the cover letter is too terse. The diffstat (593 insertions, 33 deletions across 12 files) is substantial, and the one-line summary—“Avoid fetching every branch of a new remote in a shallow repo”—fails to orient reviewers. It omits the design rationale (decoupling “what to fetch” from “where to store it”), the trade-offs (forced-update placement, precedence rules), the alternatives considered (overloading `remote.<name>.refmap`), and the current status (finalized design, one remaining edge case on override semantics). Phillip makes it clear that a detailed cover letter is not optional; it is a prerequisite for thorough review, especially for a series this size.

The critique is not a technical objection—Phillip has already endorsed the v13 design and helped resolve the major edge cases. It is a call for the author to invest the same care in the cover letter that went into the code and tests. Without that context, reviewers must reverse-engineer the series from the patches, which is inefficient and risks missing subtleties. The thread has converged on override semantics for command-line refspecs, and the series is otherwise ready to graduate, but this feedback suggests that the final hurdle is not technical but communicative.
