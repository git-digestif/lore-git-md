This is the fourth and final patch in Justin Tobler’s four-patch series to make Git’s object database (ODB) transactions create their temporary directory eagerly—at transaction start rather than lazily on first write. The series is part of the ongoing ODB abstraction effort led by Patrick Steinhardt, and this patch removes the now-unused lazy-creation logic, making the change fully effective.

Patrick Steinhardt’s review is substantive and positive. He acknowledges the correctness improvement and the simplification of the code, calling the new logic “quite a bit simpler” and praising the removal of the “leaking abstraction” in `odb/source-loose.c`. He also raises two minor points: whether the `"incoming"` prefix for `git-receive-pack` transactions could be unified with the `"transaction"` prefix (though he concedes this may be out of scope), and that the error message “failed to start ODB transaction” is user-unfriendly—suggesting “object” instead of “ODB” would be clearer. Neither point blocks the patch, and the feedback is framed as nitpicks rather than objections.

The patch itself is a clean, well-scoped correctness fix that ensures all transactional object writes go through the temporary directory, eliminating a subtle inconsistency. The commit message includes a micro-benchmark showing a 7 % slowdown for a synthetic `git add` workload, but notes this is atypical and the overhead is marginal in practice. With Patrick’s review in hand and no open questions, this patch looks ready to proceed.
