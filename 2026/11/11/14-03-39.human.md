This is a **clarification in the ongoing `git history --verbose` RFC discussion**. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Phillip Wood’s request for precision about what "format" means when he says the diff display must be generalizable across all `git history` subcommands.

Patrick confirms that he is primarily concerned with the **output format**—how the diff (or other change summary) is presented in the editor template—rather than just the option name. He lists four future use cases the format must accommodate: splitting commits, reordering commits, editing commit messages, and updating multiple references. He suggests that `git range-diff`’s format might serve as a useful model, implying that the editor template should be able to show structural changes to the commit sequence, not just textual diffs of individual commits.

The clarification sharpens the design constraint: the mechanism introduced for `reword` must be extensible enough to handle these more complex operations without requiring a separate template for each subcommand. This raises the technical bar for the feature and may influence whether reviewers see the current implementation as sufficient or as needing a more flexible foundation. No new positions on opt-in vs. opt-out or flag naming are introduced; the focus remains on the long-term viability of the diff-display machinery.
