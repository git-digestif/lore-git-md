This is a refactoring series targeting `git rerere`'s subcommand handling, now in its fourth and final version (v4). The series modernizes the command's internal structure by replacing its custom subcommand-parsing logic with Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism, aligning it with other multi-subcommand built-ins like `git notes` and `git maintenance`. The changes are purely internal: no new functionality, no behavior changes, and no on-disk format changes.

The series consists of 12 patches, all authored by Patrick Steinhardt, and is now complete. The key update in v4 is the addition of a `BUG()` check in `rerere_cache_setup()` to catch the invalid state where both `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` and `RERERE_NOAUTOUPDATE` flags are set simultaneously. This addresses the latent safety concern identified post-merge in v3, where the consuming code silently prioritized `RERERE_AUTOUPDATE` over `RERERE_NOAUTOUPDATE` without validation. The fix is minimal and targeted, preserving the existing behavior while adding robustness. The documentation for the flags has also been polished further to clarify their purpose and the three-state system they form (follow default, force autoupdate, force no-autoupdate).

Files touched include `builtin/rerere.c` (the bulk of the changes), `rerere.h`, `Documentation/git-rerere.adoc`, and callers in `apply.c`, `builtin/am.c`, `builtin/commit.c`, `builtin/merge.c`, `builtin/stash.c`, `rerere.c`, and `sequencer.c`. The series systematically renames functions (e.g., `repo_rerere()` to `rerere()`), converts flags to enums, drops `the_repository` usage, and turns each subcommand ("forget", "clear", "status", "remaining", "diff", "gc") into a proper subcommand with its own usage string. The final patch switches the command-line parser to `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()`, completing the modernization.

The series is well-scoped and mechanical, with clear commit messages and thorough test coverage. The `BUG()` addition in v4 is a direct response to feedback and closes the only open technical concern from the prior version. The changes are low-risk and high-consistency, making this a straightforward refactoring that should interest reviewers familiar with Git’s command-line parsing or the rerere subsystem. The series is self-contained and does not depend on any other ongoing efforts beyond the already-merged `ps/rerere-remove-global-state` topic.
