This is a substantive review of a test-infrastructure patch, in which Patrick Steinhardt engages with the lock mechanism and approves the overall approach.

Patrick Steinhardt reviews Kaartic Sivaraam's patch that serializes `EXPENSIVE` tests via a directory-based lock in `t/test-lib.sh`. The review is approving but substantive: he traces a race condition in the stale-lock detection (two waiters may race to read and delete the `pid` file), confirms the author's mitigation via the `test -n "$owner"` check, and questions whether writing the "waiting for another EXPENSIVE test to finish" message to file descriptor 3 (stderr/test output) could break TAP format compliance. He also asks whether the `expensive_lock_waited_` sentinel should be declared `local`. On wording he suggests "tests" instead of "test", "may" instead of "can", and cautions against naming `prove --jobs` specifically since the Makefile and Meson also run tests concurrently. He explicitly considers and rejects splitting the prerequisite into `EXPENSIVE_MEM` versus `EXPENSIVE_DISK` as unnecessary complexity, then closes with approval ("I'm fine with the approach").

Key details for downstream sessions: the lock lives at `$TEST_RESULTS_DIR/expensive.lock` and uses `mkdir` for atomic acquisition with a `pid` file for stale-lock recovery; `acquire_expensive_lock_()` and `release_expensive_lock_()` are called from `test_run_()` around tests whose prereq list contains `EXPENSIVE`, with release also wired into `die()`'s EXIT trap; non-`EXPENSIVE` tests continue to run in parallel. The patch also updates `t/README`. No integration branch is mentioned; the topic remains under review with maintainer-level approval signaled.
