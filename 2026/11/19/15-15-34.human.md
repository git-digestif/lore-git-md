This is a review reply providing historical context for Junio C Hamano's documentation patch. Kristoffer Haugsbakk dug into the origins of the "clever tricks" rule and found that it dates back to the very first version of `Documentation/CodingGuidelines` in commit 6d0618a8 from November 2007. Notably, he uncovered that Junio himself had objected to the `!!` prohibition in that original 2007 thread, arguing at the time that the `!!var` idiom was already established in the codebase.

The reply also clarifies why the rule originally singled out arithmetic constructs: Andreas Ericsson, who helped shape the guideline back then, was specifically concerned about expressions like `!!a + !!b + !!c`, where it is unclear why the operands can exceed 1 when only a boolean result matters. This distinction supports Junio's current argument that simple `!!value` normalization is standard and uncontroversial, while genuinely exotic arithmetic uses of `!!` remain rare. The historical evidence strengthens the case for removing the clause, showing the prohibition was contested from the start.
