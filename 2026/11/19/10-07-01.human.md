This is a **substantive review** of a test-infrastructure patch. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Kaartic Sivaraam's proposal to serialize `EXPENSIVE` tests and gives it his approval, while offering a few minor wording suggestions and technical observations.

Patrick confirms the problem is real and urgent, noting that Git's own CI pipelines are "currently a mess" because of concurrent memory-hungry tests causing timeouts, so he is keen to see this resolved. On the mechanics, he engages substantively with the stale-lock detection: he notes that two racing processes could delete the lock directory between the `cat` and the `kill -0` check, but acknowledges the patch handles this correctly by verifying a PID was actually read first. He asks whether `expensive_lock_waited_` should be declared as a local variable, and confirms that writing status messages to file descriptor 3 (stderr) is safe and does not break TAP output.

On wording, he suggests changing "tests" to "test" in a comment, "can" to "may" regarding memory needs, and advises against singling out `prove --jobs` since Git also runs tests concurrently via the plain Makefile and Meson. He also considers -- and dismisses -- the idea of splitting the lock into separate `EXPENSIVE_MEM` and `EXPENSIVE_DISK` categories, concluding the added complexity is not worth it. Overall he is "fine with the approach" and closes with cautious optimism about returning to green pipelines.
