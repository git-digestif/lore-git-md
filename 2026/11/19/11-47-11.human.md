This is patch 10 of 13 in Patrick Steinhardt's v5 series that modernizes `git rerere`'s subcommand handling, and it is a refactoring patch that extracts the `gc` subcommand into its own dedicated handler, `cmd_rerere_gc()`. The change moves the hidden `--skip-locked` option out of the main `cmd_rerere()` parser and into the subcommand's own `parse_options()` call, and it switches the main parser to `PARSE_OPT_KEEP_UNKNOWN_OPT` so that subcommand-specific flags are left for the subcommand to consume.

The patch introduces one intentional, user-visible behavior change: previously `git rerere --skip-locked gc` was accepted, but now the flag is only recognized after the `gc` subcommand name. The commit message justifies this as safe because `--skip-locked` was only recently introduced in commit 5f6b6aa3ab4 (2026-10-13) and has not yet shipped in any release, so almost no users will be affected. The old test that asserted `--skip-locked` before the subcommand fails with a specific error message is removed accordingly.

The patch is well-motivated and clearly written, with the trade-off explicitly documented rather than hidden. It is one step in the broader series goal of replacing the hand-rolled subcommand dispatch with the standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism, and it builds on the earlier preparatory patches that extracted `forget`, `clear`, `status`, `remaining`, and `diff` into their own handlers.
