This is **patch 1/4 of a v3 bugfix series** fixing silent 32-bit integer truncation in large-file handling, specifically targeting the `hashwrite()` function whose `uint32_t` byte-count parameter silently truncates buffers larger than 4 GiB, producing corrupt packfiles that `git fsck` rejects with "offset beyond end of packfile".

The patch leaves `hashwrite()`'s signature unchanged and instead modifies its three callers that can pass buffers exceeding 4 GiB to loop and call `hashwrite()` in chunks of at most `UINT32_MAX` bytes: `store_object()` in `builtin/fast-import.c`, and `copy_pack_data()` plus `write_no_reuse_object()` in `builtin/pack-objects.c`. This caller-side chunking approach was explicitly requested by Junio C Hamano and brian m. carlson during v2 review (both credited with Helped-by) because widening `hashwrite()` would have required updating the Rust FFI declaration in `src/csum_file.rs`.

No committed test accompanies the patch because reproduction requires an object of 4 GiB or more; the author instead verified the fix by simulating 16-bit truncation. The commit message lists the three concrete trigger paths: `write_no_reuse_object()` when `core.bigFileThreshold` is raised above 4 GiB, `copy_pack_data()` when `core.packedGitWindowSize` is set that high, and `store_object()` in fast-import under similar big-file thresholds.
