This is maintainer feedback on an ongoing bugfix series for a crash in `git apply --check`. Junio C Hamano is responding to Tanga Joris's proposal to reject empty `diff --git` lines (those with nothing after the command) as corrupt input, which would simplify the three-patch v3 series by making the first patch unnecessary.

Junio first challenges the assumption that modern Git never produces empty `diff --git` headers. He notes that while pure renames or mode changes lack `---`/`+++` lines, they still carry extended headers like `rename from` or `old mode`, so a truly bare `diff --git` line followed immediately by another section would be unusual. He asks whether Git ever actually generated such output, suggesting the "anymore" claim needs verification.

On the substance, Junio draws important distinctions about parser strictness. He agrees that malformed `diff --git` lines should be diagnosed as corrupt rather than silently ignored, endorsing Tanga's proposal to make `find_header()` die immediately upon encountering such a line. However, he cautions against tightening rules on two other categories: non-patch material between sections (which allows email commentary around patches) and third-party diffs that begin with `diff` but not `diff --git`. Those should remain handled by their existing, separate code paths without new restrictions.

The email captures convergence on a balanced approach: strict validation of Git's own `diff --git` format, while preserving permissiveness for surrounding email text and foreign diff formats. Tanga had asked for confirmation before sending v4, and Junio's reply provides that guidance while flagging the historical question about empty headers that should be resolved before finalizing the series.
