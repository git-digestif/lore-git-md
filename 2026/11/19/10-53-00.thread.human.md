Okhuomon Ajayi's documentation patch seeks to codify long-standing informal conventions about email formatting in Git contributions: wrapping text to a maximum of 72 columns so it remains readable in 80-column terminals after quoting, and separating paragraphs -- including blocks of quoted text -- with blank lines. The patch touches three files -- Documentation/CodingGuidelines, Documentation/ReviewingGuidelines.adoc, and Documentation/SubmittingPatches -- and is currently at version 3, which incorporated Andy Koppe's suggestion to specify "a maximum of 72 columns, matching the commit message convention."

Junio C Hamano has now provided a substantive review of v3. While he praised the patch for clearly stating the status quo, he offered several wording refinements: he suggested fixing the commit message's imperative mood (changing "Leave a blank line" to "Tell readers to leave..."), adding the rationale that these documents currently lack paragraph-break guidance for readability, and replacing the term "patch message" -- which he notes does not appear elsewhere in the documentation and could mislead readers into wrapping diff lines -- with broader phrasing like "E-mailed text intended for human-to-human communication" or specific terms like "commit message" and "cover letter." He also questioned whether explicitly stating "before and after it" for quoted text blocks was necessary, though he conceded some readers might benefit from the explicit guidance.
