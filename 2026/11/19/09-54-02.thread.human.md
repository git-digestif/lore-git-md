The thread began with a feature request from Jordan Kadish asking Git to support branch-aware `.gitignore` rules, so that files like a local `settings.py` could be ignored on specific branches but tracked on others. Kadish described a workplace workflow where such files are passed around manually because they intentionally differ from `main`, and proposed both whitelist and blacklist modes for branch-conditional ignoring.

Hsieh Chin Fan replied with a substantive alternative that avoids changing the ignore engine entirely: use `includeIf "onbranch:..."` directives in `.git/config` to conditionally load a branch-specific config file that sets `core.excludesFile` to a dedicated, untracked ignore file. This achieves the desired behavior -- `settings.py` ignored on `feature/x` but visible on `main` -- without embedding branch-conditional rules in the tracked `.gitignore`, which would otherwise propagate through merges and become hard to reason about. The approach also sidesteps performance concerns since the branch check happens once at config-load time rather than per-file during ignore matching.

The discussion remains conceptual with no patch or syntax proposal on the table. The thread now centers on whether branch-conditional exclusion belongs in core ignore handling or in configuration-based mechanisms, with Hsieh's reply steering the conversation toward the latter.
