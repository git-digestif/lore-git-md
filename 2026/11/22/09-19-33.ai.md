This is a review response in an ongoing bugfix series, specifically Dirk Gouders acknowledging Andy Koppe's feedback on the `[PATCH v4 0/5]` utf8 bounds-handling series. The email confirms two concrete changes to the series as posted: Gouders agrees with Koppe's recommendation to drop the `display_mode_esc_sequence_len_all()` helper (patch 5) entirely, calling it "only noise," and he acknowledges the build blocker Koppe identified in patch 4/5 where the variable `skip` was left undeclared due to a merge-conflict resolution mistake without subsequent test-compilation. Gouders states the compilation issue "should be fixed, now," implying an updated iteration is forthcoming.

The series targets `utf8_strnwidth()` and `utf8_width()` in `utf8.c` to ensure they respect their length arguments and avoid reading past buffer ends, including the mmapped-region SIGBUS case, while patch 1 (authored by Junio C Hamano) introduces an end-pointer variant of `display_mode_esc_sequence_len(const char *start, const char *end)` that scans ANSI SGR sequences without dereferencing at or beyond `end`. Koppe's review had suggested narrowing the series scope to three items: the `utf8_strnwidth()` comment fix, the optional end pointer for `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` with caller updates in `pretty.c` and `strbuf.c`, and the end guard plus bounded `utf8_width()` call inside `utf8_strnwidth()`. By dropping patch 5, Gouders accepts this narrowed scope, removing the greedy consecutive-sequence consumer that would have replaced manual while loops in `utf8_strnwidth()`, `strbuf_add_wrapped_text()`, and `strbuf_utf8_replace()`.

The thread remains under review with no version yet merged or queued in any integration branch. Koppe's substantive review also flagged that forming an end pointer as `start + SIZE_MAX` would be undefined behavior, reinforcing that all call sites must pass valid bounded end pointers rather than attempting unbounded scanning via pointer arithmetic overflow. The undeclared `skip` variable in patch 4/5 was the immediate build failure preventing compilation, now reportedly resolved in Gouders's working copy.
