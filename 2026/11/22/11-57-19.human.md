This is a feature patch, specifically the second of seven in Harald Nordgren's v15 series on fetch behavior in shallow repositories. The patch removes a longstanding limitation of `git fetch --refmap`: previously the option died with an error unless refspecs were also given on the command line, making it impossible to fetch what `remote.<name>.fetch` normally selects but store the results elsewhere for a single invocation. Now `--refmap` works against the configured refspecs, letting the configuration decide what to fetch while the refmap decides where to store it.

Within the broader v15 series, this implements the second half of the "override semantics" that Junio C Hamano and Phillip Wood confirmed during review: while patch 1/7 ensures that command-line refspecs with explicit destinations (`src:dst`) ignore the refmap entirely, this patch handles the complementary case where no command-line refspecs are present. It introduces a `drop_destinations()` helper in `builtin/fetch.c` that strips the configured destinations from the ref map before applying the user-supplied refmap via the existing `map_refs_without_dst()` mechanism, so refs land at the refmap locations rather than their usual remote-tracking branches.

The change touches `builtin/fetch.c`, `Documentation/fetch-options.adoc`, and `t/t5510-fetch.sh`. The updated test verifies that `git fetch --refmap="refs/heads/*:refs/remotes/none/*" origin` fetches the same refs as usual but stores them under `refs/remotes/none/` instead of `refs/remotes/origin/`, leaving the original remote-tracking refs unchanged. This complements the new `remote.<name>.fetchRefmap` config introduced elsewhere in the series, giving users both persistent and one-off ways to redirect fetched refs.
