This is a substantive performance review of the second patch in Harald Nordgren's six-patch series on rebased push branch handling. Aniket Gupta identifies a significant slowdown introduced by the `--cherry-mark` flag in `stat_outside_upstream()` within `remote.c`: when `status.compareBranches` includes `@{push}` and the compared branches have diverged, computing patch-ids for every commit on both sides makes `git status` roughly 5-8x slower (measured at 25ms to 150ms with 3000 diverged commits, and 60ms to 500ms with 10000). He suggests a two-phase optimization where the code first counts diverged commits without `--cherry-mark`, and only invokes the expensive patch-id comparison when those counts are small enough to justify the cost. This targets the three-way `git rev-list --cherry-mark` invocation (`branch...push ^upstream`) that the patch uses to detect clean rebases, and represents a concrete, measurable concern that likely needs resolution before the series can advance.
