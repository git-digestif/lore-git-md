This is a substantive review of the second patch in Harald Nordgren's six-patch series on clean-rebase detection for `git status` and `git push`.

Aniket Gupta flags a performance regression introduced by the new `--cherry-mark` flag in `stat_outside_upstream()`: when branches have diverged, `git status` now computes patch-ids for every commit on both sides, which he measures as a significant slowdown (from 25ms to 150ms with 3,000 commits on each side, and 60ms to 500ms with 10,000). He suggests a two-pass approach -- first counting commits without `--cherry-mark`, then running the patch-id comparison only when the divergence counts are small enough to make that cheap. The observation is precise, backed by benchmark numbers, and proposes a concrete mitigation, making it a useful piece of feedback for the series.
