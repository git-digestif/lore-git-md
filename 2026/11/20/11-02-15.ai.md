This is a documentation patch -- specifically version 3 of a standalone proposal from Okhuomon Ajayi to codify line-length and paragraph conventions for emailed contributions to the Git mailing list. The patch updates three contributor-facing documents (`Documentation/CodingGuidelines`, `Documentation/ReviewingGuidelines.adoc`, and `Documentation/SubmittingPatches`) with identical guidance: wrap emailed prose to a maximum of 72 columns so it remains readable on an 80-column terminal after several levels of quoting, leave blank lines between paragraphs, and treat a block of quoted text as a paragraph that should be surrounded by blank lines.

The substantive change from v2 is the wording of the column limit. Where v2 said "near 70 columns", v3 now states "to a maximum of 72 columns, matching the commit message convention", incorporating Andy Koppe's suggestion to align with the existing commit-message wrapping rule and remove ambiguity about whether the figure is a hard cap or an approximation. The inter-paragraph blank-line rule, including the clarification that quoted text blocks count as paragraphs, remains unchanged from v2 and directly addresses the scope concern Junio C Hamano raised when reviewing v1.

The patch is purely additive, adding nineteen lines across the three files with no code or behavior changes, and is based on commit 7fb6055e678f800320d8ab8cd8f201d9493d1ba0. The guidance is placed in contextually appropriate locations: near the general rules in CodingGuidelines, alongside quoting advice in ReviewingGuidelines, and next to the existing MUA word-wrap warning in SubmittingPatches. No integration-branch placement has been announced; the thread remains open pending further review.

For future sessions summarizing this thread: watch whether the "maximum of 72 columns" phrasing is accepted verbatim or triggers further debate about precision versus flexibility, and whether the rule that quoted text constitutes a paragraph draws additional scrutiny.
