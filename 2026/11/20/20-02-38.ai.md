This is a substantive maintainer review of the third patch in Harald Nordgren's v9 series, which introduces the core `--delete-integrated` machinery for detecting rebase- and squash-merged branches. Junio C Hamano engages with the CLI design and testing rather than just code mechanics, questioning several semantic choices while acknowledging the patch is largely sound.

Junio begins with a subject-line nit, suggesting "rebased or squash-merged branches" rather than the current "rebase and squash merged branches" for grammatical consistency. He then raises a design question about whether the existing `branch.<name>.deleteMerged` config variable should govern `--delete-integrated` behavior, noting that while understandable given the feature's evolution, it may not be intuitive for users; he floats the idea of a more future-proof `branch.<name>.deleteDone` variable that could cover any future "done" detection method, though he frames this as a question rather than a blocking objection.

The review identifies specific test-coverage gaps: he asks whether `--dry-run` combined with `--delete-integrated` has test coverage, whether opting out via `branch.<name>.deleteMerged=false` is tested with the new flag, and whether `--quiet` behavior is exercised in `t/t3200-branch.sh`. He also pushes back on the design decision to make `--dry-run` require either `--delete-merged` or `--delete-integrated`, arguing that `git branch delete --dry-run master` should behave like the real command and report that the currently checked-out branch cannot be deleted, rather than erroring out for lacking a delete-mode flag. He approves the new test ensuring `--delete-merged` and `--delete-integrated` cannot be combined.

This feedback arrives as the series remains under review with no integration status announced; while patch 3/4 was previously considered uncontroversial after earlier rounds addressed variable naming, Junio's current comments focus on edge-case semantics and test completeness rather than the core detection algorithm.
