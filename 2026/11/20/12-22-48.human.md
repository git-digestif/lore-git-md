This is a cover letter for the first half of a refactoring series, part of the ongoing ODB (object database) abstraction effort. Patrick Steinhardt has split the original twelve-patch v1 into two parts and submits here the first seven patches, which focus on cleaning up and simplifying the existing interfaces for writing commit graphs. The series now also includes a new leading commit that fixes verification of trailer hashes against the chain file, addressing a validation gap that Junio had raised during review of v1.

The patches touch `commit-graph.c` and `commit-graph.h` primarily, along with `builtin/commit-graph.c` and tests in `t/t5324-split-commit-graph.sh`. They introduce small but meaningful changes such as switching to standard error-code returns, simplifying how commit-graph chains are linked and read, exposing a validation function, and deriving layer information directly from the chain rather than passing it separately. The author notes that the split between the two parts is somewhat arbitrary, but the first half contains simplifications that stand on their own, while the second half (not included here) begins splitting functionality in ways that are less sensible as standalone commits.

The series is built on `ps/commit-graph-write-refactor` and is under review at v2, with no merge status reported. It lays groundwork for making commit-graph storage pluggable across different ODB backends, though this particular half stops short of introducing the backend hooks themselves -- those presumably arrive in part two.
