This is a reply in Harald Nordgren's six-patch series adding clean-rebase detection to `git status` and `git push`, specifically responding to a performance concern raised about the second patch. Aniket Gupta had measured a 5-8x slowdown in `git status` when branches diverge, caused by the `--cherry-mark` flag forcing patch-id computation for every commit on both sides. Gupta proposed a two-phase optimization: first count diverged commits without `--cherry-mark`, and only run the expensive patch-id comparison when those counts are small enough to justify the cost.

Harald Nordgren acknowledges the performance problem is "bad" but expresses hesitation about the two-phase approach. His concern centers on user experience consistency: if the clean-rebase detection silently disappears for large divergences, users may be confused about why `git status` sometimes reports "rebased cleanly" and sometimes does not, without an obvious explanation. He is weighing whether the performance gain justifies potentially inconsistent behavior, or whether the check should remain uniform regardless of divergence size.

The exchange highlights a classic tension in Git's UI design between responsiveness and predictable behavior. No resolution is reached in this email; Harald leaves the question open, suggesting the series may need to either accept the performance cost, find a cheaper way to detect clean rebases, or implement the two-phase approach with clear documentation of when the check activates.
