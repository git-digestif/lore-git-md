This is a short corrective reply in the review of the patch series fixing bounds handling in `utf8_strnwidth()` and `utf8_width()`. Andy Koppe walks back his earlier suggestion that callers could pass `SIZE_MAX` for unbounded scanning, noting that deriving an end pointer via `s + SIZE_MAX` would be undefined behavior; callers therefore must supply a valid length. He adds that he cannot identify use cases lacking a known remaining length where calling `strlen()` would be unacceptable, so the simpler two-argument API for `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` still looks adequate.

The exchange follows Junio C Hamano's endorsement of Andy's argument against adding a `size_t *remainder` out-parameter to the ANSI escape scanning function. Andy's correction is substantive in that it flags a concrete pointer-arithmetic hazard, but it does not reopen the broader design question -- it simply acknowledges the constraint while maintaining that the end-pointer approach without a remainder parameter serves the actual call sites.

No new patches or merge status are involved; the series remains under review.
