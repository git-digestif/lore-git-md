This is a substantive review from Junio C Hamano on version 3 of Okhuomon Ajayi's documentation patch proposing to codify 72-column wrapping and paragraph conventions for emailed contributions.

Junio engages with the commit message itself rather than just the diff, praising the clear statement of the status quo but noting that the "why" is more important than the "what" in proposed log messages. He identifies a grammatical issue in the proposed wording: "Leave a blank line" uses the wrong imperative mood for a commit message (which should instruct what to tell readers, not what the imaginary editor is adding), suggesting instead "Tell readers to leave a blank line between paragraphs." He also suggests adding the rationale that these documents currently lack guidance on paragraph breaks for readability, and notes that line-length limits and paragraph breaks are interdependent conventions, contrasting this with the LLM habit of single long lines per paragraph.

On the actual documentation text, Junio corrects the terminology: the phrase "patch message" is not used elsewhere in Documentation/ and risks misleading readers into wrapping diff lines, so he proposes alternatives like "log messages", "commit message", and "cover letter", suggesting the broader phrase "E-mailed text intended for human-to-human communication" to cover review comments and responses. Regarding the ReviewingGuidelines.adoc hunk about quoted text blocks counting as paragraphs, he questions whether explicitly stating "before and after it" is necessary but concedes some readers may need that guidance. The review closes with "Thanks", indicating the feedback is constructive polish rather than a rejection, and the thread remains open pending Okhuomon's response to these wording suggestions.
