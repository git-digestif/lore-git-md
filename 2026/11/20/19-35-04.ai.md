This is a review discussion reply in Harald Nordgren's six-patch series enhancing `git status` and `git push` for rebased push branches. Harald Nordgren is responding to Aniket Gupta's substantive performance review of patch 2/6, which measured that the `--cherry-mark` flag in `stat_outside_upstream()` causes `git status` to slow down roughly 5-8x when `status.compareBranches` includes `@{push}` and branches have diverged (25ms to 150ms with 3000 commits on each side, 60ms to 500ms with 10000).

Harald acknowledges the regression is serious ("Hmm, that's bad") and engages with Gupta's proposed two-phase optimization: first counting diverged commits without `--cherry-mark`, then only invoking the expensive patch-id comparison when those counts are small enough to justify the cost. However, he raises a user-experience concern about this approach, noting that users may find it confusing if the clean-rebase detection check suddenly does not appear in some cases, creating inconsistent behavior they cannot easily predict.

The exchange leaves the performance issue unresolved. No decision has been made on whether to implement the two-phase optimization, how to threshold the counts that would trigger the patch-id pass, or how to communicate to users why the "rebased cleanly" message might be absent in high-divergence scenarios. The thread remains open on how to balance the computational cost of `stat_outside_upstream()` against the consistency of the new status output.
