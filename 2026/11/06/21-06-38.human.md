This is a follow-up in the now-closed feature request for a `--type` filter in `git ls-files`. The thread concludes with the original author, Alejandro Colomar, accepting the maintainer’s rejection and implementing a local workaround instead.

Alejandro reports that after studying the code, he decided against pursuing a native `--type` (or `--objectmode`) flag in `git ls-files`. Instead, he wrote a small shell script, `git-find`, that wraps `git ls-files --format='%(objectmode) %(path)'` with a `grep -v '^120000'` to filter out symlinks and a `sed` to strip the mode. The script is simple, GPL-licensed, and lives in his local `PATH`; he plans to add a `-z` flag later if needed. This solution keeps the filtering logic outside Git while preserving the ergonomics he originally wanted.

The exchange with Junio remains cordial and instructional. Alejandro explains why he prefers his `grep | sed` pipeline over Junio’s single-`sed` example: it is easier to read and the performance overhead is negligible compared to the downstream tools that consume the filenames. He also notes that `--format=%(objectmode)` is simpler to parse than `git ls-files -s` because it omits extra fields. On the cosmetic point of pipeline readability, Alejandro politely disagrees with Junio’s preference for ending lines with `|`, stating that leading pipes make it clearer when a new pipeline begins.

The thread ends with mutual thanks and no loose ends. The proposal is effectively withdrawn; no patch will be sent.
