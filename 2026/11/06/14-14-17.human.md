This is a substantive review of the final patch in Miklos Vajna’s six-part series fixing and optimizing `git log --follow` for merges. Patrick Steinhardt identifies a regression: the series, as currently written, breaks `--follow` when tracking a file that was *copied* (not renamed) on one side of a merge and edited on the other side. The test case he provides—a copy of `F` to `G` on master, an edit to `F` on a side branch, and a merge that keeps `G`—fails with the patch series but passes on `master`. This is a clear correctness issue that must be resolved before the series can proceed. The review is precise, includes a reproduction case, and directly contradicts the commit message’s claim that the fix covers all cases where the merge base lacks the path.
