This thread began as a simple refactoring patch by Outreachy contributor Joy Gundu, replacing manual zero/NULL assignments in `repo_config_values_init()` with a single `memset()` call. Patrick Steinhardt reviewed it favorably, calling it "obviously correct" and a "good improvement," and the patch appeared ready for integration.

However, Junio C Hamano has now raised a stylistic objection, rejecting the `memset()` approach in favor of C99 compound literals (e.g., `(struct foo){ .member = value }`). This shifts the discussion from a straightforward cleanup to a broader question about Git's coding style. Junio cites a 2024 update to the `CodingGuidelines` that endorses compound literals as the preferred way to initialize structs, arguing that `memset()` is not idiomatic for this purpose—even when the goal is zero-initialization. The patch may now need reworking to align with this preference, though its functional correctness remains unchallenged. The outcome could set a precedent for similar refactoring efforts. No other participants have weighed in on this new point of contention.
