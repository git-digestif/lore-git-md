This is the fifth iteration of a documentation cleanup series that aligns Git’s terminology with the 2024 redefinition of "pseudoref" (now limited to `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD`) and "root ref" (refs outside the `refs/` hierarchy, such as `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`). The series updates the glossary, man pages, code comments, and tests to replace outdated terminology and clarify distinctions between pseudorefs and root refs. All prior review feedback has been incorporated, and the series is uncontroversial and ready for merging.

Andy Koppe responds to Julia Evans’s suggestion to improve the glossary’s "pseudoref" entry by adding a concrete example of `FETCH_HEAD`’s file format. While Andy agrees the current phrasing is hard to parse, he declines to include the example, arguing it would clutter the glossary. Instead, he adopts Junio C Hamano’s and Patrick Steinhardt’s wording refinements, emphasizing that pseudorefs can store multiple object IDs and metadata, and that commands like `git log` use the first object ID when multiple entries exist. The revised entry also clarifies that pseudorefs are not handled by the ref backend and contrasts them with root refs.

The updated glossary entry is now more precise and readable, explicitly listing the two pseudorefs (`FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD`), their storage location (`.git` directory), supported operations (`git show`, `git log`), and limitations (`git-update-ref`, `git-for-each-ref`). It also notes that `MERGE_HEAD` is written by `git-pull` (not just `git-merge`) and that pseudorefs carry extra metadata. The changes are purely editorial and do not alter Git’s behavior.

Two minor editorial suggestions from Patrick Steinhardt remain unresolved: dropping the word "also" from the "root refs" entry in `gitdatamodel.adoc` and adding glossary cross-references. These are surface-level and do not block merging.
