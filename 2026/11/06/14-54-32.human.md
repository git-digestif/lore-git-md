This is a review of the second patch in Kristofer Karlsson’s two-patch series, which modernizes the `t5608-clone-2gb.sh` test to ensure it actually creates the large packs it claims to test. Patrick Steinhardt’s review is substantive and constructive, engaging with both the intent and the mechanics of the patch.

The core of the patch adds assertions to verify that the packs involved in the test are genuinely larger than 2 GB or 4 GB, addressing a long-standing issue where the test setup silently stopped producing the expected large packs after a 2016 change to `fast-import`. The patch also switches from `git clone` to `git fetch` in the unpack-objects variant to ensure the `fetch.unpackLimit` setting is respected, which is necessary for the test to exercise the intended code path.

Patrick’s feedback is largely positive, calling the approach “makes sense” and noting the performance improvement from keeping the fast-import pack. He raises one technical question: whether the fact that `fetch.unpackLimit` does not apply to `git clone` is intentional or a bug. This is a reasonable point of curiosity, as the behavior might surprise users who expect the setting to apply uniformly. He also suggests a minor stylistic tweak (folding an assertion into the test setup), but considers it not worth a reroll.

Overall, the review signals that the patch is on the right track and unlikely to face major objections. The question about `fetch.unpackLimit` is the only open point, and it may not require changes to the patch itself—just clarification or a follow-up discussion. The series as a whole remains a well-motivated, focused fix for a real problem, and this patch in particular makes the test more reliable and self-documenting.
