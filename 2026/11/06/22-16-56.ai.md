This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in the v3 series updating Git’s glossary to align "pseudoref" vs "root ref" terminology. Junio C Hamano’s feedback targets the `per-worktree ref` entry, proposing two clarifications that go beyond surface-level wording:

1. **Future-proofing the list of per-worktree refs**: Junio suggests explicitly stating that the list of root refs (e.g., `HEAD`, `ORIG_HEAD`) is exhaustive and that new per-worktree refs should be created under `refs/worktree/` rather than as standalone root refs. This would add a normative note to the glossary, reinforcing the project’s intent to avoid extending the set of irregular root refs. The suggestion is motivated by the 2024 terminology change (commit 993d57eded), which narrowed the definition of "pseudoref" and introduced "root ref" to formalize the distinction.

2. **Clarifying pseudorefs’ status**: Junio proposes prefixing the sentence about pseudorefs with "Even though they are not refs," to avoid implying that pseudorefs are a subset of per-worktree refs. This aligns with the glossary’s updated definition of pseudorefs (which emphasizes their incompatibility with the ref backend) and prevents confusion with the itemized list of *actual* refs (root refs, `refs/worktree/`, etc.).

The feedback is **not blocking**—the patch is otherwise ready for merging—but it addresses potential ambiguities in the glossary’s phrasing. Andy Koppe has not yet responded, but incorporating these suggestions would require only minor edits to the AsciiDoc list. The rest of the patch (e.g., the itemized structure, cross-references) remains uncontroversial and well-received. This is a typical example of Junio’s attention to precision in documentation, particularly in areas where terminology changes have historically caused confusion.
