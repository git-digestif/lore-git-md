This is the review of the penultimate patch in Patrick Steinhardt’s 12-patch series refactoring `git rerere` to use Git’s standard `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` mechanism. The series is a clean, uncontroversial refactoring that brings `git rerere` in line with other multi-subcommand built-ins like `git notes` and `git maintenance`.

Karthik Nayak’s review here is **surface-level**. He confirms the patch’s mechanical correctness and raises minor stylistic points: whether the fallback subcommand logic could be inverted to reduce nesting, and whether a comment explaining the dummy "perform" placeholder would improve clarity. Neither suggestion questions the patch’s substance or behavior. The review explicitly acknowledges that the subcommand parsing now matches expectations (`PARSE_OPT_SUBCOMMAND_OPTIONAL` ensures backward compatibility) and that the `argv` copying logic is sound.

The patch itself replaces the hand-rolled `if (!strcmp(...))` ladder with an `OPT_SUBCOMMAND()` table, introduces `cmd_rerere_perform()` as the fallback subcommand, and removes the misleading global `--[no-]rerere-autoupdate` flag (which was previously accepted but ignored for all subcommands except the default). The change is purely internal, with no CLI, config, or on-disk behavior changes. The only remaining patch in the series (12/12) will add per-subcommand usage strings to the man page and code.

This patch is ready for integration; no substantive objections remain.
