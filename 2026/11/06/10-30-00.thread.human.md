This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s seven-patch series to speed up Git’s GitLab CI pipelines, now in its second version after initial review. The series targets bottlenecks in test execution, SDK installation, and build configuration, delivering measurable improvements across macOS, Fedora, and Windows runners—though Windows remains the slowest platform despite the gains.

Patrick’s v2 keeps the core optimizations from v1—such as cutting SDK extraction time from 3 minutes to 10 seconds, halving Fedora build times, and reducing test runtimes by 20–90%—while addressing feedback. Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has already signaled no technical objections, calling the patches “interesting” and “fully made sense,” which suggests the series is likely headed for Junio’s `next` branch unless new concerns arise. Torsten Bögershausen’s review of the first version was largely surface-level, but his latest reply to patch 5/7 introduces a substantive alternative: precomputing all expected output files upfront rather than caching them lazily during test execution. This change would eliminate runtime `tr` calls entirely, potentially offering even greater speedups at the cost of slightly more complex test setup. The ball is now in Patrick’s court to decide whether to adopt Torsten’s approach or stick with the current incremental improvement. No other reviewers have raised objections, and the series remains under active discussion.
