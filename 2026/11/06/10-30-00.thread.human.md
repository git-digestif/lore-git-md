This thread is about a seven-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt aimed at speeding up Git's GitLab CI pipelines. The series targets specific bottlenecks in test execution, SDK installation, and build configuration, with measurable improvements demonstrated across macOS, Fedora, and Windows runners. So far, the series is under review in its initial version (v1), with no prior iterations or merge status reported.

The latest update focuses on the fourth patch, which optimizes the `t0027-auto-crlf.sh` test script. This test exercises Git's CRLF conversion logic and was a known bottleneck on Windows, previously taking over 10 minutes to run. The patch replaces a loop that spawned multiple processes with a single `rm` and `git checkout` command, reducing runtime by about 20% across all platforms and saving roughly 2.5 minutes on Windows. This change is purely mechanical and confined to the test script, with no broader impact on the codebase.

Other patches in the series have already delivered significant speedups: Git for Windows SDK installation now takes 10 seconds instead of 3 minutes, artifact handling is twice as fast, and Fedora build times have been halved. While Windows runners remain a critical path, the series overall represents a practical improvement to CI infrastructure. No reviews or objections have been raised yet, so the discussion is still in its early stages.
