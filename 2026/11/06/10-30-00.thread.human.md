This thread is about a patch series from Patrick Steinhardt aimed at speeding up Git's GitLab CI pipelines. The series, now in its second version, tackles bottlenecks in test execution, SDK installation, and build configuration, with measurable improvements across macOS, Fedora, and Windows runners.

The latest patch (3/7) focuses on the `fedora-breaking-changes-meson` job, which was running twice as long as other Linux jobs due to unoptimized Meson builds. By switching from `debug` (`-O0`) to `debugoptimized` (`-O2`), the job's runtime drops from 35 minutes to 16 minutes. This change particularly benefits SHA256 hashing, which becomes six times faster with `-O2`. Other Linux and macOS jobs see minor speedups, while Windows builds remain neutral overall. The patch is uncontroversial and aligns Meson's build flags with Git's traditional Makefile defaults.

Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, has already reviewed the series and stated he has no technical objections, calling the patches "interesting" and "fully made sense." The series is likely to be queued for `next` unless other reviewers raise substantive issues. Key improvements from the series include cutting SDK installation time from 3 minutes to 10 seconds, halving artifact handling time, and reducing test runtimes by 20-25% in several cases. Windows runners remain the critical path, but the series still delivers a ~10-minute improvement there. No major open questions remain, and the series is positioned as low-risk with clear before/after metrics.
