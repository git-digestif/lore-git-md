The proposed wording frames ejection as a routine part of the review process rather than a negative judgment, addressing Phillip’s concern about demotivating contributors. This aligns with the broader policy shift toward stricter intake criteria for `seen` (requiring trusted reviewer endorsement) while preserving the branch’s role as a low-friction staging area.

The update is procedural rather than technical, with no code or tooling changes. Its impact will depend on how contributors perceive the messaging—whether it successfully conveys that ejection is a neutral, temporary state rather than a rejection. The policy remains active, with topics still expiring after four weeks without positive feedback, but the new wording may make the process feel less punitive.

This tweak doesn’t resolve the core tension between reviewer convenience and contributor expectations, but it softens the edges of the ejection approach. Future discussion might explore how to operationalize "renewed interest" (e.g., whether a new review is required) or where to document this guidance beyond the "What's cooking" reports.
