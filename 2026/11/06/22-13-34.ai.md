This is a documentation review reply in the ongoing v3 series to align Git’s glossary and documentation with the 2024 "pseudoref" vs "root ref" terminology change. The email is from Junio C Hamano, the maintainer, responding to Patch 1/5 (the glossary update).

Junio’s review is substantive but narrowly focused on two editorial points in the glossary’s "pseudoref" entry. He suggests removing the explicit contrast between pseudorefs and root refs in two places, arguing that pseudorefs are already established as *not* being refs at all, so contrasting them with root refs (which *are* refs) is redundant and potentially confusing. Specifically, he proposes dropping the phrase "In contrast to <<def_root_ref,root refs>>," from the sentence about pseudorefs not being handled by the ref backend, and removing the note that pseudorefs are not considered root refs even though they follow the naming rules. His rationale is that these contrasts risk implying pseudorefs might be a subset of refs, which they are not.

The rest of the patch—including the new "root ref" entry, the exhaustive list of irregular root refs, and the rewritten "pseudoref" definition—receives unqualified approval. Junio also notes a tangential but related technical issue: Git currently allows arbitrary files outside `refs/` (e.g., `.git/out/mister`) to be treated as refs by the revision parser, which he suggests should be tightened in a future patch. This observation is marked as #leftoverbits and does not affect the current series.

The review is positive and the patch is effectively ready to merge once the two suggested editorial tweaks are addressed. No new technical concerns are raised, and the discussion remains confined to clarity of exposition rather than correctness or scope.
