This is a **substantive review** of the second patch (`[PATCH v2 2/5]`) in Okhuomon Ajayi's five-patch series removing `the_repository` from the notes subsystem.

Junio C Hamano examines the mechanical conversion of five read-path functions in `notes.c` -- `note_tree_find()`, `add_non_note()`, `load_subtree()`, `remove_note()`, and `format_note()` -- from reading `the_repository` and `the_hash_algo` to using `t->repo` and `t->repo->hash_algo`. He draws a distinction between functions that are safe to convert and those that may not be. Functions like `remove_note()` and `prune_notes()` already accept NULL `t` parameters by falling back to `default_notes_tree` and assert `t->initialized` before use, so accessing `t->repo` in them is safe. However, he identifies a potential NULL-dereference risk in `note_tree_find()`, which calls `note_tree_search()`; that helper currently does not dereference `t` at all, meaning callers can safely pass NULL today. Converting `note_tree_find()` to access `t->repo` without a NULL guard or initialization check would introduce a crash where none existed before, unless the existing code already dereferences `t` unconditionally.

The review asks the author to verify whether each converted function already assumes a non-NULL, initialized `t` before proceeding with the mechanical replacement. This is a substantive correctness concern rather than a style nit, as it touches on the safety invariants of the notes API. The thread remains under active review with no merge status; the author is awaiting Patrick Steinhardt's confirmation on the scoping of the remaining `USE_THE_REPOSITORY_VARIABLE` guard, and Junio has separately flagged a similar NULL-safety issue in `copy_note()` in patch 3/5.
