This is the final patch (5/5) in Johannes Schindelin's v3 series of Coverity-inspired integer-overflow fixes, targeting the date-parsing code in `date.c`. The patch hardens `approxidate_alpha()` against signed overflow when multiplying a parsed number by a time-unit length (e.g., "4000 weeks") by performing the arithmetic in `uintmax_t`, checking with `unsigned_mult_overflows()` and `date_overflows()` before narrowing to `time_t`, and returning `NULL` on failure. `approxidate_digit()` now rejects any numeric quantity exceeding `INT_MAX` before conversion, and `approxidate_str()` propagates those failures by setting an error flag and returning zero, so commands like `git reflog expire` report "invalid timestamp" instead of invoking undefined behavior.

The commit message is clear and directly addresses the Coverity finding, explaining why checking the converted `time_t` is too late (signed overflow is undefined). Phillip Wood, who helped simplify earlier iterations, approved this version as much simpler than its predecessors. The patch adds two test cases to `t/t0006-date.sh`: one verifying that a large-but-valid quantity ("4000 weeks ago") parses correctly on 64-bit `time_t` platforms, and another confirming that an absurdly large number ("18446744073709551615 seconds ago") is rejected with an error rather than crashing or wrapping.

Within the broader five-patch series, this complements the wrapper I/O changes (patches 3 and 4) and the `http.c`/`archive-tar.c` fixes (patches 1 and 2). The series remains under review; Junio C Hamano previously approved the earlier patches and the v2 direction, and this final piece appears ready for his final look before queuing.
