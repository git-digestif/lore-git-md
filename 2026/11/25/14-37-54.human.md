This is a substantive review of Erik Cervin-Edin's v4 patch series that extends `git commit --fixup` to accept all message-source options. Phillip Wood approves the structural refactor in the first patch, noting it cleans up the message-handling logic in `prepare_to_commit()`, but raises one semantic concern about the third patch. With plain `--fixup`, the command never opens an editor, so combining it with `-c` makes `-c` behave identically to `-C` rather than opening the editor for editing as `-c` normally does. Phillip would prefer the meaning of `-c` not change in this combination, though he offers to accept the current behavior as a known leftover if fixing it proves difficult.

The series itself enables `-m`, `-F`, `-c`, and `-C` across all `--fixup` variants (plain, `amend:`, and `reword:`), addressing a real usability gap for non-interactive workflows. The v4 iteration restructures the series so the `--fixup` subject line is written up front, mirroring `--squash`, which removes awkward guards from the message-source arms. All prior feedback from earlier rounds has been addressed, including test hygiene around editor invocation.

Phillip's review is substantive rather than surface-level: he identifies a specific behavioral edge case where `-c` under plain `--fixup` loses its editor-opening semantics. He does not report testing the patches himself, but his concern is about intended behavior rather than implementation bugs. With this one caveat noted, he signals the series is otherwise acceptable and ready to merge.
