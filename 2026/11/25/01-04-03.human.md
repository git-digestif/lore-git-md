This is a design-discussion reply within the `[PATCH v10 0/5]` thread, not a patch itself. Junio C Hamano is responding to Harald Nordgren's suggestion that future error messages for unsupported `git branch --dry-run` operations (creation and renaming) should avoid the word "yet" so as not to promise eventual support.

Junio pushes back on that framing by distinguishing between experimental commands and core tooling. He notes that while refusing to promise future support might be reasonable for something like `git history`, `git branch` is part of everyday workflows for virtually every user, so a stance of promising as little as possible may not align with end-user expectations. The exchange sits squarely in the open question of `--dry-run` scope: patch 3/5 of the v10 series already allows `--dry-run` to work with `-d`, `-D`, `--delete-merged`, and `--delete-integrated`, but creation (`git branch --dry-run <new> <start>`) and renaming (`git branch --dry-run -m <old> <new>`) remain generically rejected.

Harald had proposed deferring those remaining operations to a separate follow-up series tagged `#leftoverbits`, leaving the v10 validation in `builtin/branch.c` unchanged. Junio's reply does not settle the question but highlights the tension between keeping this series focused and meeting user expectations for a foundational command. The thread remains under review with no integration status announced.
