This is a substantive review reply providing performance measurements within Karthik Nayak's 20-patch v2 test-modernization series that splits the monolithic `t/t9300-fast-import.sh` into smaller self-contained files. Tanga Joris responds directly to the previously unresolved question from Kaartic Sivaraam about how much extra wall-clock overhead results from creating and tearing down 16 additional repositories via `git init repo` in patch 2/20's "series B" tests.

Tanga Joris reports concrete timing data from a 4-core machine: running all fast-import test scripts sequentially increased from 17.8 seconds to 21.1 seconds after the split, while running with `prove -j4` increased from 11.0 seconds to 11.9 seconds. The measurements show that the anticipated parallelism gains from splitting tests into separate files do not quite offset the per-test repository setup cost at `-j4`, though the author concludes the readability improvements remain worth the modest time increase.

This email resolves the open performance question that Kaartic Sivaraam raised on patch 2/20, replacing Karthik Nayak's earlier unmeasured argument with empirical data. For future thread context: the overhead is quantified as roughly 3.3 seconds sequential and 0.9 seconds parallel on the tested hardware, suggesting the trade-off is small in absolute terms but measurable.
