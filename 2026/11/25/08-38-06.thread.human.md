Julian Maurin sent a single-patch bugfix fixing `git push --no-thin` being silently ignored for smart HTTP remotes, so a thin pack could still be sent despite the flag. Two causes were identified: `transport-helper.c` still listed `TRANS_OPT_THIN` in its `unsupported_options[]` (a leftover from 2009 when `--no-thin` affected no transport), and `remote-curl.c` unconditionally passed `--thin` to `send-pack`. The fix removes the exclusion, handles `TRANS_OPT_THIN` in `remote-curl.c`'s `set_option()` to toggle `options.thin`, documents the new `option thin` capability in `Documentation/gitremote-helpers.adoc`, and adds a `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT`-based test in `t/t5541-http-push-smart.sh`. The motivating production case is pushing from a shallow treeless partial clone (`--filter=tree:0`) over HTTPS, where a thin push triggered 37 lazy fetches for missing trees versus zero with `--no-thin`. Backward compatibility holds because older helpers reply "unsupported", which push already ignores.
