This is a bugfix for `git push --no-thin` being silently ignored when pushing to a smart HTTP remote. Julian Maurin reports that while the native transports have honored `--no-thin` since 2013, pushes routed through remote-curl always sent a thin pack regardless of the flag. The cause is twofold: `transport-helper.c` lists "thin" among the options it refuses to forward to remote helpers, and `remote-curl.c` unconditionally invokes `git send-pack` with `--thin`. The patch removes "thin" from that unsupported list and teaches remote-curl's `set_option` to handle a new `option thin` capability, so helpers that do not know the option simply reply "unsupported" and the push proceeds as before.

The motivation is a concrete production pain point: pushing from a shallow, treeless partial clone (`--filter=tree:0`) over HTTPS triggers a lazy fetch for every missing tree during a thin push -- potentially hundreds of round trips -- whereas `--no-thin` avoids that work entirely but had no effect over HTTP. The commit message includes reproduction numbers showing 37 lazy fetches with the default behavior versus zero once the flag is actually respected. The patch adds a test in `t/t5541-http-push-smart.sh` that uses `GIT_TRACE2_EVENT` to confirm `git pack-objects` receives or omits `--thin` depending on the push flag, and documents the new option in `gitremote-helpers`.

The change is small and surgical -- 29 insertions across four files -- with a commit message that traces why "thin" was originally excluded from helper forwarding in 2009 and why that rationale no longer holds. It looks like a well-motivated, thoroughly tested fix that should be straightforward to review.
