This thread is about Patrick Steinhardt’s ten-patch series refactoring Git’s commit-graph writing internals to consolidate scattered flags into a unified `struct commit_graph_write_options`, move verification into the pluggable ODB backend, and modernize function naming. The goal is to prepare the codebase for pluggable object database backends as part of Patrick’s broader ODB abstraction effort. The series also fixes a latent bug in `verify_commit_graph()` and aligns the API with Git’s modern naming conventions.

The series is currently under review in its first version, with no merge status reported. Junio C Hamano and Derrick Stolee have both marked it as "LGTM," though their feedback has been surface-level, focusing on readability and mechanical correctness rather than edge cases or hands-on validation. Kristofer Karlsson reviewed patch 6, suggesting a stylistic improvement (a `switch` statement in `builtin/fetch.c`) and endorsing the patch’s unification of call sites, but his feedback also lacked substantive critique. Patrick Steinhardt declined the stylistic change and floated a design idea for a future series—using a tagged union to clarify write modes—but did not pursue it here. The most recent exchange is a procedural note from Derrick Stolee about email reply behavior, which doesn’t affect the series’ technical status.

No user-visible changes are introduced; the work is purely internal interface hygiene. The series touches `commit-graph.c`, `commit-graph.h`, and several call sites, introducing new symbols like `struct commit_graph_write_options` and modernized function names (e.g., `commit_graph_parse()`). The on-disk format remains unchanged, and no new test files were added. The main open question is whether the unification of writer functions via the `COMMIT_GRAPH_WRITE_REACHABLE` flag might introduce edge cases, but this hasn’t been addressed in reviews yet. The series stands as a clean, preparatory step for the ODB abstraction effort.
