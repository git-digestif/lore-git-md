This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression thread. Linus Torvalds clarifies the root cause of the commit-date skew that triggered the bug: GitLab’s Marge-bot merge tool uses `--keep-committer` (via `--keep-committers`) when rebasing merge requests, which preserves the original committer date rather than updating it to the merge time. This creates multi-day "clock skew" in the Linux kernel tree, where commits merged later appear older than their parents. Linus expresses strong disapproval of the GitLab workflow and the flag itself, calling it "evil" and "a complete shit-show," and states he will refuse to pull such rebased branches in the future.

The email does not propose or modify any code, nor does it directly address the algorithmic debate between his generation-number-based fix and Kristofer Karlsson’s alternative prototype. Instead, it provides context for why the regression occurred and underscores the real-world impact on the kernel’s version reporting. This background may influence the project’s tolerance for edge cases: if the skew is caused by a deliberate (if misguided) workflow choice rather than random clock drift, the fix may need to handle extreme cases more robustly. The tone suggests Linus views the skew as a self-inflicted wound by GitLab, but the patch itself must still work correctly regardless of the cause. The thread remains queued in `next` with the design debate unresolved.
