This is a follow-up in the ongoing review of Elia Pinto’s three-patch bugfix series that addresses integer truncation in Git’s large-file handling. The series targets three subsystems—packfile generation, diffstat line counting, and rename detection—by widening function parameters from 32-bit types to `size_t`.

Phillip Wood corrects his own earlier misreading: the patch does *not* change the return type of `count_lines()` (it remains `int`), but the widened `size` parameter still creates an overflow risk. Even with the fix, a file with more than `INT_MAX` lines (about 2 billion) will cause the line counter to wrap, producing incorrect diffstat results. This undermines the patch’s goal and leaves a latent bug that could affect files smaller than 2 GiB if they contain enough lines.

The broader concern Phillip raises is that the series may rely too heavily on mechanical type widening without sufficient manual analysis of edge cases. His feedback implies the patch needs rework—either widening the return type and line counter to `size_t` or documenting why line counts above `INT_MAX` are impossible in practice. No alternative solution is proposed, but the critique is substantive and focused on correctness, not style. The series remains under review, with the second patch now clearly needing revision.
