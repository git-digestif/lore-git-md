This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing discussion about how to fix Git’s quarantine mechanism unnecessarily loading refs from its own object directory during connectivity checks. The thread is now focused on Jeff King’s proposed minimal fix, which Kristofer Karlsson acknowledges as a cleaner alternative to his earlier v2 patch.

Kristofer agrees that Peff’s approach—comparing the alternate’s path directly against the repository’s commondir in `refs_from_alternate_cb()`—is functionally equivalent to his v1 patch but avoids the "hacky" framing and is more localized. He notes that the quarantine parent path only matters in rare cases where the object directory isn’t under the commondir, making Peff’s solution sufficient for the immediate problem. However, he also points out that the commit message in v2 overstated the safety of dropping alternate refs, since `rev-list --alternate-refs` is a public option and its usage isn’t limited to connectivity checks. He plans to reword that part to avoid implying assumptions about how the option is used.

The discussion now hinges on whether to adopt Peff’s minimal fix, wait for Justin Tobler’s long-term transaction-layer solution, or simply add a regression test for now. Kristofer is deferring a v3 until the broader design direction is settled, with three clear options on the table. The patch remains in `next` as v2, but Peff’s alternative is gaining traction for its simplicity and alignment with Git’s ethos of small, self-contained changes. No technical objections remain, and the choice is now a matter of timing and preference.
