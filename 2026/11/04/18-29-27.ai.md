This is a follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread, specifically Linus Torvalds’ response to Junio C Hamano’s comment about clock skew. The email clarifies the root cause of the non-monotonic commit dates in the Linux kernel: **GitLab’s Marge-bot merge logic**, which uses `--keep-committers` (a flag that preserves the original committer date during rebase), artificially skewing commit timestamps by up to three days. Linus expresses strong disapproval of the flag and the GitLab workflow, calling it "evil" and "a complete shit-show," but acknowledges that the damage is already done and the fix is still needed.

The email does not propose or modify any code, nor does it engage with Kristofer Karlsson’s alternative algorithm. Instead, it provides **new context** for the regression: the skewed dates are not accidental clock drift but an intentional (if misguided) workflow choice. This reinforces the importance of the fix’s robustness, as the problem is systemic in the kernel’s merge process and unlikely to disappear soon. The tone is emphatic but the substance is diagnostic, not prescriptive — the thread’s technical direction (Linus’s generation-number fix vs. Kristofer’s prototype) remains unchanged, but the motivation for a durable solution is now clearer.
