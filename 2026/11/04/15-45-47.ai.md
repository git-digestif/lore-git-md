This is a follow-up to Phillip Wood’s **substantive review** of the second patch in Elia Pinto’s three-patch bugfix series addressing integer truncation in large-file handling. The series aims to fix silent truncation in `hashwrite()` (packfile generation), `count_lines()` (`git diff -B --stat`), and `hash_chars()` (rename detection).

Phillip corrects his earlier misreading: the patch does **not** change the return type of `count_lines()` (it remains `int`), but the widened `size` parameter (`size_t`) still creates an overflow risk. The line counter variable (`count`) is also still `int`, so files with more than `INT_MAX` lines (≈2 billion) will cause the counter to wrap, producing incorrect diffstat results even for files smaller than 2 GiB. This undermines the patch’s goal and confirms the series’ broader issue: a mechanical approach (changing parameter types without analyzing downstream effects) can introduce new bugs.

The review reiterates concern about the series’ reliance on automated checks over manual code analysis. No alternative solution is proposed, but the feedback implies the patch needs rework—either widening the return type and counter variable or documenting why line counts above `INT_MAX` are impossible in practice. The discussion remains focused on correctness, not style or trivialities. No other reviewers have weighed in yet on this or the other two patches.
