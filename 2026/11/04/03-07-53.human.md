This is a substantive review from Jeff King (Peff) on the second patch of Farid Zakaria’s two-patch series fixing a race condition in the Git credential-cache daemon. The series targets a real-world bug where concurrent `git credential-cache store` commands can cause one daemon to unlink another’s socket, leading to lost credentials and orphaned daemons.

Peff’s review is entirely positive. He confirms the core fix—using `unix_ss_create()` to safely handle concurrent socket creation—is correct and aligns with the simple-ipc server patterns already used elsewhere in Git. He explains why the original design avoided locking (fear of stale lockfiles) but agrees the brief lock held by `unix_ss_create()` is a worthwhile tradeoff to avoid races. His only minor suggestion is to make the "ok" response and early exit slightly more explicit with a comment or `exit(0)`, but he is satisfied either way.

The review also clarifies the test coverage: the new test in `t/t0301-credential-cache.sh` exercises the race condition by explicitly starting two daemons and verifying the second one defers to the first. Peff notes this case would likely have failed before the patch, confirming the fix’s necessity.

No objections or edge cases are raised; the review depth is substantive, engaging with both the design rationale and the code’s behavior. The patch looks ready for v2, pending incorporation of Junio’s architectural concern about mixing `unix_ss_*` and `tempfile` APIs (which Peff does not address here but which the author will need to resolve). For a busy reader, this is a clean, well-motivated fix that closes a subtle but impactful bug.
