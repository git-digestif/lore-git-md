This is a **test suite / platform compatibility follow-up** in the ongoing thread about fixing `t5702-protocol-v2.sh` for GNU coreutils 9.12+ quoting changes. Jeff King (Peff) replies to Collin Funk’s explanation of the upstream coreutils behavior change, offering a brief retrospective on the trade-offs involved.

Peff acknowledges that while the coreutils change created short-term disruption for Git’s test suite, it is ultimately a positive step for security and robustness, particularly in preventing environment variable injection attacks. He notes that Git’s test suite had another `env`-based check that was already a source of mild concern due to potential injection risks, so the coreutils improvement aligns with Git’s own security goals. The conversation then touches on the complexity of balancing usability, security, and compatibility: Collin’s suggestion to quote only when output goes to a terminal would avoid breaking scripts, but Peff points out this would sacrifice injection protection in non-interactive contexts. The discussion also references the `--quoting-style` option (not yet in a coreutils release) as a potential future solution, though Peff notes it introduces its own portability concerns.

The email closes on a constructive note: Peff thanks Collin for the context and reiterates that Git’s revised test logic (using explicit shell variable expansion instead of `env` output) is an improvement regardless of coreutils behavior. No new action is requested or proposed; this is a low-stakes exchange that reinforces the consensus around the patch’s correctness and the value of the upstream coreutils change.
