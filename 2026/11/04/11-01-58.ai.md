This is a follow-up proposal in the `git describe` regression fix thread. Kristofer Karlsson suggests an alternative algorithm that could make the `--exact` opt-out flag largely unnecessary by delivering near-exact commit distances without relying on commit generation numbers, at roughly the same performance cost as the current implementation.

Kristofer’s prototype avoids the eager depth-increment flaw in the current algorithm by tracking two counters per tag: the total number of commits visited, and the number of commits carrying the tag’s bit. The depth is computed as the difference between these counters, and commits are re-enqueued when they gain a bit late, ensuring the depth is accurate regardless of visit order. To determine when to stop the walk, the algorithm uses an epsilon-based heuristic (a week in testing) for commits outside the commit-graph, which proved sufficient for exact results on linux.git. The approach also works well with partial commit-graphs, as commits outside the graph are visited first and rely on the epsilon, while commits inside the graph can stop early once depths are final.

The prototype produces the correct distance for the regression case (2a500a04d6f9 → v7.3-19059) with and without a commit-graph, matches `rev-list --count` on the last merge window, and passes the existing test failures in `t6120-describe.sh` under both `GIT_TEST_COMMIT_GRAPH` settings. The implementation adds about 120 lines and removes 60 in `builtin/describe.c`.

Kristofer asks whether this approach is worth pursuing, either as a replacement for or an addition to Linus Torvalds’ generation-number-based patch. The thread has already converged on an opt-out `--exact` flag with a warning mechanism, but Kristofer’s proposal could obviate the need for the flag by delivering near-exact results without the performance cost of `ensure_generations_valid()`. The key trade-off is between the simplicity of Linus’s one-line fix and the robustness of Kristofer’s algorithm, which handles partial commit-graphs and skewed dates more gracefully. The thread’s participants (Linus, Junio, Patrick Steinhardt, Derrick Stolee) have not yet responded to this proposal.
