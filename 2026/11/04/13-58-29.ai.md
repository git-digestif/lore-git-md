This is a **substantive review** of the second patch in Elia Pinto’s three-patch bugfix series addressing integer truncation in large-file handling. Phillip Wood raises a critical flaw in the patch’s logic: while the `size` parameter is correctly widened to `size_t`, the function’s return type (`int`) and the line counter variable remain unchanged. This creates a new overflow risk—if a file contains more lines than `INT_MAX` (approximately 2 billion), the counter will wrap, producing incorrect results even for files smaller than 2 GiB. The review highlights that the patch’s mechanical approach (changing only the parameter type) fails to address the broader implications of the change, particularly the mismatch between the widened input size and the still-narrow return type. Phillip’s concern extends to the series as a whole, suggesting the patches may rely too heavily on automated checks without sufficient manual analysis of edge cases. No alternative solution is proposed, but the feedback implies the patch needs rework to either widen the return type or document why line counts above `INT_MAX` are impossible in practice. The review is technical and specific, focusing on correctness rather than style or trivialities.
