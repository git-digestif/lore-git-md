This is a follow-up proposal in the `git describe` regression fix thread. Kristofer Karlsson, a Spotify engineer, suggests an alternative algorithm that could make the `--exact` flag largely unnecessary by achieving near-exact commit distance calculations without relying on commit generation numbers, at roughly the same performance cost as the current implementation.

Kristofer’s approach reworks the commit walk to track two counters per tag: the total number of commits visited, and the number of commits carrying the tag’s bit. The depth is computed as the difference between these counters, allowing the walk to correct itself when a commit gains a tag’s bit late via a different path. This eliminates the eager depth increment that causes miscounts in the current implementation. To determine when to stop the walk, he uses a heuristic based on commit date skew: the walk stops when no commit is dated more than a week before one of its ancestors. This heuristic proved exact for all tested commits in linux.git, including those near skewed timestamps, and works seamlessly with partial commit graphs.

The prototype handles the regression case (2a500a04d6f9) correctly both with and without a commit graph, matches `rev-list --count` on recent commits, and passes the existing test suite. It adds about 120 lines to `builtin/describe.c` while removing 60, indicating a non-trivial but focused change. Kristofer frames this as a potential replacement for or complement to Linus Torvalds’ patch, which currently relies on commit generation numbers and introduces the `--exact` flag as a user-facing mitigation.

The proposal is well-timed: the thread had just converged on Patrick Steinhardt’s opt-out `--exact` design (defaulting to on with a warning), and Junio C Hamano had signaled intent to integrate the patch. Kristofer’s alternative could obviate the need for the `--exact` flag and its associated performance trade-offs, but it introduces a heuristic that may not be universally reliable. The thread now faces a choice between two technically sound but philosophically different approaches: Linus’s deterministic fix with a user-facing escape hatch, or Kristofer’s heuristic-based fix that aims for near-exact results without requiring commit graph updates. The discussion is likely to focus on the trade-offs between determinism, performance, and implementation complexity.
