This is a **substantive review follow-up** from Jeff King (Peff) on Aaron Plattner’s bugfix patch `[PATCH v2] http: preserve WWW-Authenticate headers on redirect`. Peff confirms the patch is fundamentally sound and addresses a real issue—Git’s HTTP layer losing `WWW-Authenticate` headers during redirects—but flags two minor areas for improvement: the code could be slightly clearer, and the commit message should explicitly explain why the change is safe.

The review is notable for its depth. Peff engages with the security model behind `wwwauth_headers`, confirming that the patch does not introduce risks (headers are already scoped to the redirect target) but suggesting the commit message should document this invariant to prevent future misuse. He also questions the necessity of the new `credential_update_url()` helper introduced in v2, arguing that the v1’s inline approach might be simpler and that the helper’s reusability is speculative. This feedback is framed as a design preference rather than a blocker, and Peff explicitly states the patch is "OK as-is."

A key procedural insight: Peff attributes the lack of earlier review to the niche nature of the `wwwauth_headers` feature, which is not widely understood even among HTTP subsystem contributors. He cc’d Matthew Cheetham, the original author of the `wwwauth_headers` logic, but notes Cheetham’s limited recent activity, implying this may not yield additional feedback. The review itself is likely to unblock the patch, as Peff’s endorsement carries significant weight, but the open question about the helper’s design may prompt a v3 or a follow-up discussion.

The patch remains in `seen` (proposed updates) and is still at risk of being dropped if no further action is taken, but Peff’s review substantially improves its chances of progressing. For readers tracking this thread, the next steps will likely hinge on whether Aaron Plattner incorporates Peff’s suggestions or whether Junio decides the current version is sufficient to move forward.
