This is the first patch in a two-part bugfix series addressing 4 GiB file size truncation on 64-bit Windows (and other platforms where `long` is 32 bits but `size_t` is 64 bits). It focuses on the `grep` subsystem, converting `struct grep_source.size` and related internal buffer size variables from `unsigned long` to `size_t`. This change ensures `git grep` correctly searches files larger than 4 GiB in the working tree, with `--no-index`, and in blobs, eliminating silent truncation that previously caused matches beyond the 4 GiB boundary to be missed.

The patch touches `grep.c` and `grep.h`, updating function signatures and variable types in the core matching engine (`grep_source_1`, `end_of_line`, `look_ahead`, `grep_source_init_buf`, and `grep_buffer`). It also removes the `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` call in `grep_source_load_oid()`, allowing grepping of blobs ≥4 GiB to succeed instead of aborting. The commit message confirms the fix was verified by simulating truncation at 16 bits, though no test is included due to the impracticality of generating 4 GiB inputs.

This is a targeted, mechanical change with no behavior regression on platforms where `long` and `size_t` are the same size. The patch is self-contained and merges cleanly with current `master`. The remaining `-Wshorten-64-to-32` warnings in the `grep` codebase are either unreachable or in xdiff internals, where input sizes are already capped at 1023 MiB. The series' second patch will address similar truncation issues in the `diff` subsystem.
