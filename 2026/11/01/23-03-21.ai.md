This is the third patch in a five-part documentation series (v2) that aligns Git’s terminology around "pseudorefs" and "root refs" with the 2024 redefinition. The patch updates six man pages—`git-update-ref(1)`, `git-worktree(1)`, `git-ls-remote(1)`, `for-each-ref-options(7)`, `rev-list-options(7)`, and `config/core.adoc`—to stop using "pseudoref" for refs outside `refs/` (now called "root refs") and to correct related inaccuracies.

The changes are mechanical but precise: "pseudoref" is replaced with "root ref" where appropriate, with HEAD and ORIG_HEAD given as examples. `git-worktree(1)` now explicitly states that both root refs and pseudorefs are per-worktree, and drops the outdated claim that they live directly under `$GIT_DIR` (which doesn’t hold for reftable). `git-ls-remote(1)` clarifies that `--refs` omits refs outside `refs/` and fixes a typo ("symbolics" → "symbolic refs"). `git-update-ref(1)` removes the incorrect mention of ORIG_HEAD from its reflog documentation (only HEAD gets a reflog by default) and corrects the scope of `core.logAllRefUpdates` to include root refs. `gitdatamodel.adoc` now distinguishes root refs and pseudorefs in its "References" section, addressing a prior gap in the pedagogical documentation.

The patch addresses most of Patrick Steinhardt’s feedback from v1: the ORIG_HEAD reflog documentation is now correct, and the `git-worktree(1)` description is clearer. However, two issues remain unaddressed: the phrasing of `core.logAllRefUpdates` in `git-update-ref(1)` is still ambiguous about whether "always" applies to all subsequent conditions, and the reflog storage explanation still references `$GIT_DIR/logs/<ref>`, which is outdated for reftable. These omissions are noted in the commit message but not resolved in this iteration.

No behavioral changes are introduced; the patch is purely about terminology consistency and accuracy. The updates are uncontroversial and appear ready for merging, pending resolution of the remaining documentation nits. The series as a whole is a follow-up to the 2024 terminology change and should help users and contributors avoid confusion from inconsistent documentation.
