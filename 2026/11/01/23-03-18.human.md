This is a documentation and terminology cleanup series that aligns Git’s glossary, man pages, code comments, and tests with the 2024 decision to narrow "pseudoref" to mean only FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD, while calling other refs outside the "refs/" hierarchy (like HEAD and ORIG_HEAD) "root refs." The v2 update addresses feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt by refining the glossary definitions, converting prose lists of per-worktree refs into itemized AsciiDoc lists for better readability, and adding root refs and pseudorefs to the data-model guide (`gitdatamodel.adoc`).

The series touches 22 files, including the glossary, six man pages, built-in commands (`for-each-ref`), refs internals (`refs.h`, `revision.c`), and test scripts. Changes are mechanical but thorough: replacing outdated uses of "pseudoref" with "root ref," correcting minor inaccuracies (e.g., reflog behavior in `git-update-ref(1)`), and ensuring the glossary’s new "root ref" entry cross-references related terms. The only deliberate omission is the bash-completion helper `__git_pseudoref_exists()`, whose name is left unchanged due to ambiguity about what term would cover both root refs and pseudorefs.

Feedback on v1 was generally positive, with Junio calling the series "a pleasant read" and Patrick approving the structural split between "ref," "root ref," and "pseudoref" entries. The v2 changes—particularly the itemized lists and tighter commit messages—address the remaining editorial suggestions. No behavioral changes are introduced, and the series appears uncontroversial. If you care about Git’s documentation consistency or work with ref internals, this is worth a quick skim; otherwise, it’s safe to ignore unless you’re maintaining downstream tooling that interacts with Git’s ref storage.
