This is a documentation patch, the first in a five-part series that aligns Git’s glossary and documentation with the 2024 terminology change that narrowed "pseudoref" to FETCH_HEAD and MERGE_HEAD while introducing "root ref" for refs outside the "refs/" hierarchy (e.g., HEAD, ORIG_HEAD). The series is under active review; this patch adds the missing "root ref" glossary entry and clarifies the "pseudoref" entry to reflect the current definition.

The patch moves the naming rules and list of irregular root refs from the "ref" entry to a new "root ref" entry, explicitly states that pseudorefs are not root refs, and cross-references `gitrevisions(7)` for descriptions of commonly used root refs and pseudorefs. The "pseudoref" entry is reworded to no longer call them refs, adds `git-for-each-ref(1)` as an example of how they are not proper refs, and explains that using a pseudoref containing multiple object IDs yields the first one. The changes are mechanical and align the glossary with the 2024 terminology change, addressing feedback from Junio C Hamano and Patrick Steinhardt on the v1 series.

This patch is uncontroversial and well-motivated, focusing on terminology consistency rather than behavioral changes. The glossary updates are clear and should help users and contributors avoid confusion from outdated documentation. No functional regressions or new features are introduced. The series as a whole looks like a solid cleanup effort that deserves attention from anyone working with Git’s ref system.
