Elia Pinto has submitted a two-patch series fixing silent truncation of files 4GiB or larger on platforms where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit, most notably 64-bit Windows. Patch 1/2 switches file-size variables from `unsigned long` to `size_t` in `git archive`'s tar and zip backends (`archive-tar.c`, `archive-zip.c`, `archive.h`), fixing truncated tar headers and corrupted zip output. Patch 2/2 makes the analogous change in `git apply` (`apply.c`, covering `create_file`, `create_one_file`, `try_create_file`, and `add_index_file`). The series is at v1 and under review on `master`, with no technical objections raised so far.

The one substantive decision so far came from Patrick Steinhardt, who ruled that patch 2/2 should be held back entirely until a separate in-flight series changes `odb_write_object()`'s length parameter to `size_t`, rather than working around the remaining gap with a loud cast. That gap matters: even with the patch, `git apply --index` and `git am` would still silently truncate the blob written to the object database on 32-bit `long` platforms, because `add_index_file()` passes the size to `odb_write_object()` whose parameter is still `unsigned long`. Elia Pinto has now acknowledged this in a brief reply and confirmed he will resend a rebased series with patch 2/2 once the dependency lands, leaving patch 1/2 to proceed on its own. The thread is otherwise quiet, with no new tests or review feedback yet; the author also noted that the existing `t5004` zip test, currently disabled on 32-bit `long` platforms, could eventually be enabled on 64-bit Windows by relaxing its guard.
