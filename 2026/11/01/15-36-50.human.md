This is the second patch in a three-part bugfix series addressing integer truncation in Git’s large-file handling. The series targets silent data corruption or miscounts when processing files or buffers larger than 2–4 GiB.

This patch fixes a line-counting error in `count_lines()` (used by `git diff -B --stat` and `emit_rewrite_diff()`) by changing its size parameter from `int` to `size_t`. The current `int` type truncates or wraps around for files ≥2 GiB, causing the diffstat to report incorrect line counts—verified by simulating 16-bit truncation, which produced zero changed lines for a large file. The change is minimal (one line in `diff.c`) and does not affect memory safety, only output accuracy.

The fix is well-motivated and clearly explained, though the lack of test coverage is understandable given the impracticality of generating multi-gigabyte test files. The patch is part of a broader effort to eliminate 32-bit truncation risks across Git’s I/O and diff subsystems, with the other two patches addressing `hashwrite()` (packfile generation) and `hash_chars()` (rename detection).
