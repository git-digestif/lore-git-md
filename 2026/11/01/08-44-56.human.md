This is the first patch in a two-part bugfix series addressing silent 4 GiB file-size truncation on 64-bit Windows (and other platforms where `long` is 32 bits but `size_t` is 64 bits). The patch fixes `git grep` by changing the internal buffer-size tracking from `unsigned long` to `size_t`, ensuring it correctly searches files larger than 4 GiB in the working tree, with `--no-index`, and in blobs.

The change is mechanical: every function that touches `grep_source.size` or passes buffer sizes through the grep engine (`grep_source_1`, `end_of_line`, `look_ahead`, `grep_buffer`) is updated to use `size_t`. The patch also removes the `cast_size_t_to_ulong()` call in `grep_source_load_oid()`, allowing grepping of blobs ≥ 4 GiB instead of aborting. No tests are included because generating 4 GiB inputs is impractical; the commit message confirms the fix was verified by simulating truncation at 16 bits.

The patch is clean, self-contained, and merges without conflicts. It directly addresses the truncation issue without introducing new behavior or dependencies. If you care about large-file correctness on Windows, this is worth reviewing.
