This thread is about a two-patch bugfix series from Farid Zakaria that fixes a race condition in Git’s credential-cache daemon, where concurrent `git credential-cache store` commands could cause one daemon to unlink another’s socket, leading to lost credentials and orphaned processes. The issue primarily affects users with large repositories during parallel submodule fetches over HTTPS.

The series is currently under review, with v1 posted and v2 expected. Patrick Steinhardt has provided feedback on both patches: for the first, he identified a pre-existing permission-handling issue and suggested a preparatory commit, while for the second, he focused on improving commit-message clarity and code comments. The core fix—using `unix_ss_create()` to safely handle concurrent socket creation—has not been contested, and the new behavior (ignoring `EEXIST` from `mkdir()` and printing "ok" when a second daemon detects an existing socket) is uncontroversial. Tests in `t/t0301-credential-cache.sh` cover both the `mkdir()` race and socket hijacking scenarios.

Farid’s patches address distinct parts of the race window, and Patrick’s reviews have been substantive, engaging with behavior and identifying latent issues. The only remaining open questions are minor: edge-case handling for socket cleanup, platform coverage, timeout interactions, and permission correctness. No maintainer decision has been recorded yet, but the series is on track for v2 incorporating the feedback.
