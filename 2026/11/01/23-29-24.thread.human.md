This thread is fixing a race condition in Git’s credential-cache daemon that can cause credential loss during parallel HTTPS fetches, particularly in large repositories with submodules. The issue arises when multiple `git credential-cache store` commands start daemons concurrently: one daemon may unlink another’s socket, leaving orphaned processes and inaccessible passwords.

Farid Zakaria’s two-patch series (now preparing v2) addresses this by making the daemon ignore `EEXIST` from `mkdir()` (patch 1) and switch to `unix_ss_create()` for socket handling (patch 2). Reviews from Patrick Steinhardt, Junio C Hamano, and Jeff King were substantive, focusing on correctness, security, and API consistency. The only unresolved architectural concern is Junio’s objection to mixing `unix_ss_*` and `tempfile` APIs in patch 2; Farid plans to drop `tempfile` registration in v2 to align with `fsmonitor--daemon.c` precedent. Minor improvements—like clarifying the "ok" response in the protocol and fixing commit message phrasing—will also be included.

The series is under active review, with v2 expected soon. The core fixes are uncontroversial, but the API consistency question remains open. Testing covers both the `mkdir()` and socket-hijacking races, though the latter may not have been reliably reproducible pre-patch. No regressions or edge cases have been raised beyond the architectural concern.
