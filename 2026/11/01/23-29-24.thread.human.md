This thread is about fixing a race condition in Git’s credential-cache daemon where concurrent `git credential-cache store` commands can cause one daemon to unlink another’s socket, leading to lost credentials and orphaned processes. The issue particularly affects users with large repositories during parallel submodule fetches over HTTPS. Farid Zakaria posted a v2 patch series addressing this, along with a pre-existing permission-handling issue in socket directory creation.

The series is under review, with patches 2 and 3 (the core fixes) confirmed as correct in their logic by Jeff King and Patrick Steinhardt. However, Junio C Hamano has raised an architectural concern about patch 3’s mixing of `unix_ss_*` and `tempfile` APIs, arguing it could lead to unsafe socket cleanup. Farid initially suggested the race might only occur during Git upgrades, but Junio rejected this, emphasizing the issue is persistent in real-world setups with daemon-respawning processes. Farid’s latest reply frames his contribution as a best-effort fix but does not engage with the technical concern, leaving the series blocked. Meanwhile, Patrick Steinhardt flagged a misleading commit message in patch 2, which claims the directory is created with mode `0700` to avoid a race, though the patch only adds an `EEXIST` check. The preparatory commit (patch 1) fixing the permission issue is uncontroversial and ready to merge.

The thread’s key sticking point is Junio’s architectural objection, which Farid has not addressed in his latest reply. Until this is resolved—either through justification or a revised implementation—the series is unlikely to progress. The tone has grown somewhat tense, with Junio expressing frustration over Farid’s dismissal of the concern as optional, while Farid appears reluctant to revisit the design. The next steps will likely require either a v3 patch or maintainer intervention to break the impasse.
