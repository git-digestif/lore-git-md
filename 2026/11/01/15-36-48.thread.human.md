This thread is Elia Pinto's v2 series fixing silent integer truncation in Git's large-file handling: widening 32-bit size and line-count types to `size_t`/`uintmax_t` across packfile writing (`hashwrite()`), the complete-rewrite diff path, diffstat totals and `scale_linear()`, and rename detection (`hash_chars()`). The series is under review on master at 50fe5cc63f; v2 rewrote patch 2/4 to address Phillip Wood's critique of v1 and added a new patch 4/4 for diffstat overflow. The only prior review beyond Wood's was a surface-level "looks good" from Torsten Boegershausen on patch 4/4.

Junio C Hamano has now substantively reviewed patch 1/4 and raised two points that keep the series open. First, a concrete oversight: the Rust FFI bindings in `src/csum_file.rs` still declare `hashwrite()` with a `u32` length parameter and must be updated to match the C signature change. Second, and more fundamental, he questions whether widening the parameter to `size_t` is the right API design at all, asking instead whether callers holding a buffer over 4 GiB should loop `hashwrite()` in chunks of at most 4 GiB. No resolution has been reached, so it remains undecided whether the series keeps the `size_t` widening or switches to a chunked-call approach.

Still awaiting substantive review are patches 1/4 (now with Junio's two open questions), 3/4 (`hash_chars()` rename detection), and 4/4 (diffstat totals and `scale_linear()`). The series carries EXPENSIVE tests in `t/t4022-diff-rewrite.sh` and a cheap `scale_linear()` test in `t/t4052-stat-output.sh`, with the remaining failure modes verified only via 16-bit truncation simulation.
