This thread concerns Elia Pinto's v2 series of four patches fixing silent integer truncation in Git when handling files larger than 2--4 GiB, covering packfile generation, complete-rewrite diff paths, diffstat accumulation, and rename detection. The series is under review with no merge status reported. v2 addresses Phillip Wood's earlier critique of the complete-rewrite path by propagating `size_t` through the relevant functions and adds a fourth patch for diffstat and `scale_linear()` overflows, but several substantive concerns from maintainer Junio C Hamano remain open across patches 1/4, 3/4, and 4/4.

The most active discussion centers on patch 1/4, which widens `hashwrite()`'s byte-count parameter from `uint32_t` to `size_t`. Junio questioned whether callers holding contiguous buffers over 4 GiB should instead loop calling `hashwrite()` in smaller chunks rather than changing the API contract, and noted that the Rust FFI bindings in `src/csum_file.rs` still declare the parameter as `u32`. brian m. carlson has now weighed in agreeing with Junio that the chunked-call approach is the right design, though he adds that if the series retains the `size_t` widening instead, the Rust bindings must be updated to match. The author has not yet responded to indicate whether patch 1/4 will be reworked to enforce chunking at call sites or whether the widening will stand with corresponding Rust updates.

Junio has also raised unresolved technical concerns on the other patches: for patch 3/4 he questioned whether the `cnt` member of `struct spanhash` should be widened to `size_t` to prevent wrap-around after four billion hash collisions, and for patch 4/4 he asked what happens when the `uintmax_t` diffstat accumulator itself eventually overflows and why the `graph_width` capping logic was not documented as a behavioral change in the commit message. Phillip Wood's feedback on patch 2/4 has been addressed but awaits his re-review, while Torsten Bögershausen offered only surface-level approval of patch 4/4. The series remains in active review with multiple open design and edge-case questions awaiting the author's response.
