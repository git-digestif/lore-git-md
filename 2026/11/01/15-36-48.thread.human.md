Elia Pinto's v2 series fixes silent integer truncation in Git's large-file paths -- widening types in packfile hashing (`hashwrite()`), the complete-rewrite diff path, diffstat accumulation and `scale_linear()`, and rename detection (`hash_chars()`). The series is under review on master at 50fe5cc63f, with v2 adding a fourth patch for diffstat overflow after Phillip Wood's feedback on `count_lines()` was addressed in patch 2/4. So far, no patch has been merged; Junio C Hamano has raised substantive open questions on patches 1/4 (Rust FFI bindings in `src/csum_file.rs` still use `u32`, and whether callers should loop in chunks instead of widening the parameter) and 4/4 (undocumented `graph_width` behavioral change and eventual `uintmax_t` overflow), while Torsten Bögershausen gave a surface-level "looks good" on 4/4.

The newest email extends Junio's review to patch 3/4, where he probes the `struct spanhash` data structure itself: the `cnt` member is still `uint` and could theoretically wrap to zero after roughly four billion hash collisions, making a populated bucket look empty. He asks whether `cnt` should be widened to `size_t` alongside `sz`, or whether it can safely stay 32-bit, while noting `hashval` can remain `uint`. No resolution yet.

Still open across the series: the `hashwrite()` API design question and the Rust binding update on 1/4; the two commit-message gaps on 4/4; and now the `spanhash.cnt` widening question on 3/4, which until this email had no substantive review at all. Patch 2/4 appears settled after Phillip Wood's earlier critique was incorporated, but the series as a whole remains in review with several unanswered maintainer questions for the author to address.
