This thread covers Patrick Steinhardt's seven-patch v2 part 1 series refactoring Git's commit-graph internals to prepare for the ODB abstraction effort. The series simplifies chain management, unifies error handling to the "0 means success" convention, and isolates path-dependent logic so it can eventually move into a pluggable backend. It remains under review, with no merge status reported; the original twelve-patch v1 was split to defer the actual `odb_source` callback hooks to a follow-up part 2.

The series has now concluded with patch 7/7, which removes redundant bookkeeping arrays from `struct write_commit_graph_context` -- including `base_graph_name`, `commit_graph_filenames_before`, `commit_graph_filenames_after`, and `commit_graph_hash_after` -- and instead derives all layer information directly from the commit-graph chain structure. Inside `write_commit_graph_file()`, local `string_list` snapshots (`chain_hashes` and `kept`) capture the hashes and filenames needed before the chain is closed, and the helper functions `mark_commit_graphs()` and `expire_commit_graphs()` now take chain pointers and the `kept` list as explicit parameters. One behavioral change accompanies the cleanup: `expire_commit_graphs()` now runs only after a successful write rather than unconditionally, which the author notes is arguably more correct. The patch nets a reduction of 33 lines in `commit-graph.c`.

Junio C Hamano provided substantive review on v1 that Patrick addressed in v2, particularly the trailer-hash validation that makes the `oid` field readable and the shift to depth-based counters in the merge strategy. With part 1 complete, the remaining work -- introducing pluggable `read_commit_graph` and `write_commit_graph` callbacks in `struct odb_source` and moving path-derivation logic into `odb/source-files.c` -- is deferred to part 2, which has not yet been posted.
