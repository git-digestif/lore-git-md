This thread is a 12-patch series from Patrick Steinhardt that introduces pluggable commit graphs as part of the broader ODB (object database) abstraction effort. The goal is to move commit-graph storage logic into the ODB backend layer, allowing alternative ODB implementations to control where and how commit graphs are read and written. The series is now complete with the final patch, which makes all path-derivation functions private to the "files" ODB backend and exposes backend-specific entry points for reading and writing commit graphs.

The first eleven patches were infrastructure-only refactors, isolating path-dependent logic, simplifying chain management, and preparing for backend-specific control. The final patch removes the last external caller of path-derivation logic (in `git-commit-graph verify`) by introducing new backend-specific functions, ensuring that the built-in `commit-graph` command can operate on a specific object directory without knowing exact paths. The series touches core files like `commit-graph.c`, `odb.c`, and `odb/source-files.c`, introducing new symbols such as `read_commit_graph` and `write_commit_graph` hooks in the ODB backend interface. No user-facing behavior changes or on-disk format changes were introduced, and performance impact is neutral.

The series is under review and is feature-complete for its goal, though Patrick notes that future backends may require further abstraction of the commit-graph data structure. Open questions remain about the flexibility of the design for backends that might avoid the commit-graph data structure entirely, the performance implications of moving path logic into the backend, and the clarity of the new abstractions. The series depends on `ps/commit-graph-write-refactor` and is part of the larger ODB abstraction effort driven by Patrick Steinhardt, Justin Tobler, and others.
