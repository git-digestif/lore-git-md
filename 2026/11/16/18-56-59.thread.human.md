This thread concerns a two-patch series from Dirk Gouders fixing bounds handling in `utf8_strnwidth()` in `utf8.c`, which currently ignores its `len` argument when processing valid UTF-8 and can read past the intended boundary, plus a documentation correction for the function's return value. The series is at v1 and still under review; no one has proposed merging it.

Junio C Hamano has driven the substantive technical discussion. He argued that the real problem is a missing guard after the ANSI-escape-skipping loop rather than a flaw in `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` itself, and he raised a memory-safety edge case: if the string points to the end of an mmapped region, reading past `len` could raise `SIGBUS` before any check is reached. Dirk Gouders conceded these points, framed the issue as a contract mismatch where `utf8_strnwidth()` accepts a `len` boundary but does not enforce it in its callees, and said he is preparing a v2 addressing both the guard placement and the mmapped-region risk.

In the latest email, Junio sent a concrete illustrative patch that threads a `size_t len` parameter through `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` -- adding `end <= p` guards throughout the escape-sequence parser in `utf8.c`, changing the signature in `utf8.h`, and updating the four call sites (`format_and_pad_commit()` in `pretty.c`, plus `utf8_strnwidth()`, `strbuf_add_wrapped_text()`, and `strbuf_utf8_replace()` in `utf8.c`) to pass the remaining byte count. He explicitly noted this does not replace the separate post-loop guard inside `utf8_strnwidth()` that Gouders proposed, and the patch is marked as not compile-tested. The thread now awaits Gouders's v2 respin incorporating both fixes.
