This is a follow-up in the ongoing discussion about the loose object database (ODB) cache invalidation bugfix series. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Jeff King’s (Peff) concerns about removing the `QUICK` flag, acknowledging the performance trade-offs but emphasizing correctness as the higher priority.

Patrick agrees that the `QUICK` flag cannot be removed outright without further investigation, citing Peff’s benchmark data showing a **1320% slowdown** in the `p5550.4: fetch` test when the flag is omitted. However, he shifts the focus to correctness, questioning whether the tag-following logic (the specific use case in Peff’s benchmark) actually behaves correctly in all scenarios, particularly with concurrent repository maintenance and promisor remotes. If the answer is "no," he argues, the performance gain becomes irrelevant. This frames the `QUICK` flag as a potential footgun that may need rethinking, even if immediate removal isn’t feasible.

The email doesn’t propose a concrete path forward but signals that Patrick sees this as a topic worth revisiting soon. For now, the immediate bugfix (patch 2/2) remains on track for `next`, with the broader `QUICK` flag issues documented via a `NEEDSWORK` comment in `odb.h`. The tone is pragmatic, balancing the need for performance with the risks of incorrect behavior. Future discussion is likely to center on auditing `QUICK` users, validating their correctness, and measuring the real-world impact of removing the flag.
