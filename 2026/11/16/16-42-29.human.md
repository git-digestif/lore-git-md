This is a review of Jasper Mayone’s `[PATCH v5 4/5]` introducing the `%(currentworktree)` atom for `git for-each-ref`. The review comes from Phillip Wood, who originally suggested the feature, and it is substantive but narrowly focused on design and implementation choices.

Phillip now questions whether the atom should exist at all. He points out that `%(HEAD)` already indicates whether a ref is checked out in the current worktree, and suggests extending `%(HEAD)` to cover rebasing/bisecting branches instead of adding a new atom. If the atom is kept, he prefers a boolean format (printing `*` like `%(HEAD)`) rather than returning the worktree path, and proposes renaming it to `%(currentworktreepath)` for consistency with `%(worktreepath)` if the path is retained. He also raises two implementation concerns: (1) the atom should agree with `%(worktreepath)` on which worktree to report when a branch is checked out in multiple worktrees, and (2) using a hashmap to store a single entry is inefficient and unnecessary.

The review is thoughtful and constructive, but it arrives late in the series lifecycle (v5) and after the thread summary already recorded Jasper’s concession on patches 4/5 and 5/5. Phillip’s feedback does not address the broader consensus that the redundancy in `git branch` output (which this atom was meant to clean up) is not a significant enough problem to justify the changes, so it is unlikely to revive the dropped patches. However, if the series were to be revisited, his suggestions would be the starting point for any redesign.
