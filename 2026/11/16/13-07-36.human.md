This is a major refactoring patch series from Patrick Steinhardt, introducing **pluggable commit graphs** as part of the ongoing ODB (object database) abstraction effort. The series consists of 12 patches and targets the commit-graph subsystem, moving logic for reading and writing commit graphs into the "files" ODB backend to allow future backends to control their own storage.

The goal is to give ODB backends full control over where commit graphs are stored, rather than hardcoding paths in the core commit-graph code. This is a stepping stone toward deeper abstraction—Patrick notes that a more ambitious approach could abstract the commit-graph data structure itself, but this series focuses on the storage layer. The changes are mechanical but extensive, touching `commit-graph.c`, `commit-graph.h`, and introducing new ODB source files (`odb/source-files.c`, `odb/source.h`).

Key changes include:
- Moving path-derivation logic for commit graphs into the "files" backend.
- Splitting and refactoring `write_commit_graph_file()` to make it more modular.
- Introducing a common exit path for commit-graph writing and marking incomplete chains.
- Adding ODB hooks to make commit-graph operations pluggable.

The series is built on top of recent work (`ps/commit-graph-write-refactor`) and is part of Patrick’s broader ODB abstraction effort, which has been a multi-year project. This looks like a well-structured, incremental step—no behavior changes are intended, but the refactoring is substantial enough to warrant close review, especially around edge cases in commit-graph chain handling.

If you care about ODB abstraction or the future of pluggable backends, this series is worth following. The cover letter is clear, and the patch breakdown suggests careful decomposition. Expect discussion about whether this is the right abstraction level and how it interacts with other ODB-related work.
