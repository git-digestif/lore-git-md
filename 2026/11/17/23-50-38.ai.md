This is a review reply in the ongoing discussion of patch 1/4 of Elia Pinto's v2 series on integer truncation in large-file handling. brian m. carlsson agrees with Junio C Hamano's suggestion that callers holding contiguous memory regions larger than 4 GiB should loop calling `hashwrite()` in chunks of 4 GiB or less rather than widening the function's parameter from `uint32_t` to `size_t`. He explicitly states that the chunked-call approach would be the right thing to do, signaling support for changing the caller behavior instead of the API contract.

He adds a conditional caveat: if the series does not adopt the chunked approach and instead keeps the `size_t` widening, then the Rust FFI bindings in `src/csum_file.rs` must be updated accordingly to match the new C signature, since they currently still declare the length parameter as `u32`. This leaves the design question technically unresolved -- the thread now has two reviewers leaning toward the chunked-call approach, but the author has not yet responded to say whether patch 1/4 will be reworked to enforce chunking at call sites or whether the `size_t` widening will be retained with corresponding Rust updates.
