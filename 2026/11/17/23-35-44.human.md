This is a review reply in the ongoing discussion of patch 2/4 of Justin Tobler's four-patch series on eagerly creating the temporary object directory at ODB transaction start. Justin is responding to Patrick Steinhardt's questions about the new `flush_batched_fsync()` helper and the conditional logic that skips the hardware flush when batched fsync is not enabled.

Justin confirms that the single hardware flush at transaction commit is only necessary as a durability barrier when objects have been written with batched (writeout-only) fsync; otherwise the flush is redundant or pointless. He agrees with Patrick that the optimization is not just about performance but also about avoiding semantically incorrect behavior, since issuing a flush when no writeout requests are pending serves no purpose. He also confirms Patrick's suspicion that batched fsync currently applies only to loose objects and not to packfiles, and he commits to documenting this implicit assumption in the commit message of the next version.

The exchange is collaborative and narrows down the remaining issues on this patch to a commit-message clarification rather than a code change. It sits within the broader series context where Karthik Nayak has separately challenged the fundamental motivation for eager temporary-directory creation, a question Justin has not yet addressed in this thread.
