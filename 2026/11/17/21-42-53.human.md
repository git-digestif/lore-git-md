This is a follow-up ping on a two-patch bugfix series targeting the tree-walk subsystem. Elia Pinto is nudging the list for feedback on his proposal to fix silent truncation of trees larger than 4 GiB, where 32-bit size fields in `struct tree_desc` and `struct tree` cause the walker to ignore entries beyond the boundary. The series widens these fields to `size_t`, and the author notes it still applies cleanly to current `master` with no intervening changes to the touched files.

The email asks two specific questions of potential reviewers: first, whether the issue is worth addressing at all given that such enormous trees can only be created deliberately, and second, whether widening the types is the correct approach or whether oversized trees should instead be rejected outright in `init_tree_desc_gently()`. The author mentions he is happy to drop the series if there is no interest.

The underlying problem is real but edge-case: when a truncation lands between tree entries, the remainder of the tree silently vanishes from `git ls-tree` output and, more concerning, from `fsck_tree()` validation, meaning security-sensitive paths like `.gitmodules` could go unchecked. The series is part of a broader effort by the same author to eliminate similar size-truncation issues across Git, and the lack of test coverage for the actual 4 GiB case is acknowledged as impractical rather than overlooked.
