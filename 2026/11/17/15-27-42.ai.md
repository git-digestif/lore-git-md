This is a substantive review discussion within Adrian Ratiu's three-patch `compatObjectFormat` interoperability bugfix series, specifically a reply to Patrick Steinhardt's feedback on patch 3/3 (copying unknown commit headers unchanged). Adrian acknowledges cosmetic commit-message fixes (capitalizing "object ID", `git-hash-object(1)` references) for the next iteration, then reports two edge-case bugs he found in the v1 implementation: a `"tree "` line with a trailing space but no value slips through the `(bufptr + 5) < eol` check and gets copied rather than rejected, and a `"mergetag "` line with an empty first line followed by the tag in continuation lines is also copied, leaving its embedded object ID unconverted while `git commit --amend` refuses to convert it -- reintroducing the very inconsistency the patch aims to fix.

To resolve this in v2, Adrian proposes changing the three header-length checks from `<` to `<=` so that the converter classifies headers by the key before the first space, matching the behavior of `read_commit_extra_header_lines()`. Under this scheme, `"tree "`, `"parent "`, and `"mergetag "` with empty or malformed values are rejected as before the series, while bare `"tree"`, `"parent"`, or `"mergetag"` without a trailing space are treated as unknown headers and copied verbatim -- exactly what `git commit --amend` already does with them. He commits to adding a test for the empty-mergetag case.

On Patrick's suggestion to add generic format validation (requiring `key<space>value` structure) inside the converter, Adrian pushes back: such a check would reject headers that `read_commit_extra_header_lines()` and the `--amend` path accept (e.g., a bare `"x-foo"` line), causing `git commit --amend` to record compat IDs that cannot be read back. He argues that if stricter validation is needed, `fsck` is the appropriate place (noting it currently ignores headers after `committer`), but treats that as a separate topic. He also agrees with Patrick that neither rejecting all unknown headers nor maintaining an allowlist is viable, and acknowledges the `x-obj-` prefixed-header proposal as a worthwhile future direction but out of scope for keeping this series focused.

The thread remains under review with no merge decision yet. Patch 3's core direction -- unifying `convert_commit_object()` and `convert_commit_extra_headers()` to copy unknown headers -- has Patrick's endorsement, but the implementation details around malformed headers are still being refined. The immediate next step is Adrian's v2, which will incorporate the `<=` classification fix, the new test, and the cosmetic cleanups, while leaving the broader questions of fsck-based header validation and standardized object-ID header prefixes (`x-obj-*`) as noted future work.
