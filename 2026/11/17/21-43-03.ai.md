This is a review reply from Junio C Hamano on his own v2 bugfix patch that adds bounds-checked ANSI escape sequence scanning to `utf8.c`. The underlying patch introduces `display_mode_esc_sequence_len_counted(const char *s, size_t len)` in `utf8.c` (declared in `utf8.h`), which scans exactly `len` bytes without reading past the buffer, while retaining the original `display_mode_esc_sequence_len()` as a wrapper for NUL-terminated strings; three internal callers in `utf8.c` (`utf8_strnwidth`, `strbuf_add_wrapped_text`, `strbuf_utf8_replace`) are switched to the counted variant.

In this email, Junio responds to two suggestions from Dirk Gouders. He pushes back on the proposed early-return optimization `if (len < 3) return 0;`, stating he has no benchmarks and finding the performance merit dubious since callers consume long strings one UTF-8 character at a time and short strings incur negligible cost without the check. He agrees to incorporate Dirk's clar unit test suite, asking "Should I just steal what you sent out?" and concluding with "Thanks." The proposed tests would live in a new file `t/unit-tests/u-utf8-ansi.c` (registered via `CLAR_TEST_SUITES += u-utf8-ansi` in the `Makefile` and added to `clar_test_suites` in `t/meson.build`) and assert that `display_mode_esc_sequence_len_counted()` returns 0 for `"\033[m"` with `len=2`, 3 for `len=3`, 5 for `"\033[31m"`, and 7 for `"\033[0;31m"`.

The thread remains under review with no integration branch status mentioned; the test addition appears accepted while the early-return optimization was not adopted in this reply.
