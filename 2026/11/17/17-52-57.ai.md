This is a substantive review of patch 1/4 of Elia Pinto's v2 series fixing integer truncation in large-file handling. Junio C Hamano raises two points about the change that widens `hashwrite()`'s count parameter from `uint32_t` to `size_t` in `csum-file.c` and `csum-file.h`.

First, he notes a concrete oversight: the Rust FFI bindings in `src/csum_file.rs` still declare `hashwrite()` as taking `u32` for the length parameter, so those declarations need to be updated to match the C signature change to `size_t`.

Second, he questions the API design choice itself. He asks whether a caller holding a contiguous memory region exceeding 4 GiB should instead loop calling `hashwrite()` in chunks of 4 GiB or less, rather than widening the parameter type -- suggesting that changing the API contract may not be the right approach compared to constraining callers to chunk their writes. No resolution or agreement is reached yet; this leaves open whether the series will keep the `size_t` widening or switch to a chunked-call approach.
