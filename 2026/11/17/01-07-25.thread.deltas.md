# Thread deltas

## 2026/11/17

- [2026/11/17/01-07-25 by wheakerd <wheakerd@gmail.com>] wheakerd reported that "git push <name>" recurses when a remote group lists a remote of its own name: with remotes.origin set to "origin member", the command spawns thousands of nested "git push" processes (5,945 "Pushing to origin" lines and up to 5,898 concurrent "git" processes in a 12-second run), pushes to the other member but never to the remote named origin, and exits with status 1.
- [2026/11/17/01-07-25 by wheakerd <wheakerd@gmail.com>] wheakerd traced the recursion to push_multiple() in builtin/push.c running "git push <member>" as a child for each group member while add_remote_or_group() in remote.c resolves a name as a group first when remotes.<name> exists, so the same-named child expands the group again; the same code was present on master as of 2026-11-15 and on next and seen.
- [2026/11/17/01-07-25 by wheakerd <wheakerd@gmail.com>] wheakerd noted that "git push <group>" is new in 2.55.0, t/t5566-push-group.sh has no case for a group containing its own name, and git-push(1) does not mention one.
- [2026/11/17/01-07-25 by wheakerd <wheakerd@gmail.com>] wheakerd cited an older fetch-side report of the same cycle (Alex Riesen, 2010-11-17 and 2013-12-28) in which Jeff King sketched a fix (expand groups in one process, tell child processes not to expand again, and detect cycles), but no patch was found in that thread and fetch_multiple() in builtin/fetch.c on master still passes no such option to its children.
