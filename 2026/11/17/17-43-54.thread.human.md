This thread covers Christian Couder's three-patch series fixing latent truncation bugs on 64-bit Windows and other LLP64 platforms, where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit and several APIs silently narrowed large values at their boundaries. The series widens the path from `ll_merge()` through to the object database so that `size_t` flows end to end: patch 1 changes `write_buf_to_worktree()` in `notes-merge.c` to take `size_t`, patch 2 widens the length parameters of `odb_write_object()`, `write_loose_object()`, and related ODB write functions, and patch 3 removes the now-redundant `size > LONG_MAX` guard in `xdl_do_merge()` in `xdiff/xmerge.c`, along with corresponding test updates in `t/t4300-merge-tree.sh`. The combined effect eliminates silent truncation for merge results or objects of 4 GiB or larger on those platforms.

Review has been substantive and positive. Patrick Steinhardt called patch 1 obviously correct and traced the data flow for patch 2, confirming every downstream consumer already accepts `size_t`; Tanga Joris provided concrete test evidence from two 64-bit Windows environments showing the EXPENSIVE big-merge tests in `t/t4300-merge-tree.sh` now pass with the series applied, and audited all remaining callers of `ll_merge()` to confirm the deleted check in patch 3 protects nothing, ending with "Looks good to me." Couder's latest email simply thanks Joris for the tests and review, closing the loop on the third patch.

Nothing has been merged or queued in any integration branch; the series remains under review. The main open item is that Couder has committed to rebasing this series on top of Spiros Gitter's overlapping patch series, so a v2 is expected, and full validation on 64-bit Windows with `GIT_TEST_LONG=1` remains the gold standard.
