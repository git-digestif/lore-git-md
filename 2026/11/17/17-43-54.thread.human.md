Christian Couder has posted a three-patch series addressing 64-bit Windows (LLP64) compatibility, where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit, fixing latent truncation bugs that could silently corrupt objects or merge results of 4 GiB or more. The series widens the data path so `size_t` flows unbroken from `ll_merge()` through to the object database: patch 1 changes `write_buf_to_worktree()` in `notes-merge.c`, patch 2 widens the length parameters of `odb_write_object()` and related ODB write functions, and patch 3 removes a now-redundant `LONG_MAX` guard in `xdl_do_merge()` in the xdiff merge path.

Patrick Steinhardt reviewed patches 1 and 2 substantively, calling the first "obviously correct" and tracing the downstream data flow for the second to confirm all consumers already accept `size_t`; he raised no objections. Tanga Joris provided concrete test evidence on 64-bit Windows with `GIT_TEST_LONG=1`, showing that the two big-merge tests in `t/t4300-merge-tree.sh` now pass with the series applied, and audited all remaining `ll_merge()` callers to confirm the deleted check protected nothing, concluding "Looks good to me." The review loop on the series is now closed satisfactorily.

The series remains under review with no integration-branch status. Couder has acknowledged overlap with Spiros Gitter's separate patch series and committed to rebasing on top of it, so a v2 is expected; full validation on 64-bit Windows with sufficient memory for a 4 GiB+ merge remains the outstanding gold standard.
