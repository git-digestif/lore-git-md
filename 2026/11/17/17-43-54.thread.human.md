Christian Couder has posted a three-patch series fixing latent 64-bit truncation bugs on LLP64 platforms such as 64-bit Windows, where `long` is 32-bit but `size_t` is 64-bit. The series widens the path from `ll_merge()` down into the object database so that `size_t` flows unbroken end to end: patch 1 changes `write_buf_to_worktree()` in `notes-merge.c` to take `size_t`, patch 2 widens the length parameters of `odb_write_object()` and its wrappers across the ODB layer, and patch 3 removes a now-redundant `LONG_MAX` guard in `xdl_do_merge()` while relaxing the EXPENSIVE big-merge tests in `t/t4300-merge-tree.sh` to require only a 64-bit `size_t`. The series builds on recently merged xdiff work and is not yet queued in any integration branch.

Patrick Steinhardt reviewed the first two patches substantively, calling patch 1 obviously correct and tracing the data flow for patch 2 to confirm every downstream consumer already accepts `size_t`; he raised no objections but flagged overlap with a separate series from Spiros Gitter, and Couder has committed to rebasing onto it, so a v2 is expected. The new email adds the first real test evidence for patch 3: Tanga Joris ran `t/t4300-merge-tree.sh` with `GIT_TEST_LONG=1` on two actual 64-bit Windows environments, showing that the two EXPENSIVE big-merge tests now pass with the series applied where they previously skipped, and audited all remaining `ll_merge()` callers to confirm the deleted guard protects nothing. He closes with "Looks good to me."

Still open: no review has been recorded from anyone beyond Steinhardt and Joris, the promised rebase onto Gitter's series has not yet appeared, and the LLP64-specific paths still cannot be exercised by Linux or macOS CI, so Windows-only testing like Joris's remains the only real coverage.
