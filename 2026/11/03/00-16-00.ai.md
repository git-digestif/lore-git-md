This is a **maintainer clarification** in the `--matched-only` feature thread for `git range-diff`. Junio responds to Matt Hunter’s surface-level workflow question by explaining why the proposed alternative—using the three-revision form of `git range-diff`—does not actually solve the problem Harald described.

The key technical point is that Harald’s use case involves comparing two ranges that share no common base (e.g., a topic branch before and after rebasing onto an updated `origin/master`). In such cases, there is no single `<base>` revision that can be used to construct the two ranges `<base>..<rev1>` and `<base>..<rev2>` for the three-revision form. Junio’s clarification underscores that the `--matched-only` feature is not redundant: it addresses a real gap in the current workflow where users want to filter range-diff output to show only commits present in both ranges, regardless of whether those ranges share a common base.

This exchange does not alter the patch’s technical readiness—it remains queued in `seen` and at risk of being dropped—but it strengthens the feature’s conceptual justification. The thread’s core question—whether the feature is a meaningful addition—is now framed more clearly: the three-revision form cannot replace `--matched-only` in the rebasing scenario, so the feature’s necessity is reaffirmed. The ball is back in the court of potential reviewers to validate the patch’s fit for the `range-diff` workflow.
