This is a substantive review of Kristofer Karlsson’s patch to make `git rev-list --connectivity-check` ignore replacement objects. Patrick Steinhardt raises two points that could affect the patch’s readiness.

First, he questions whether the test change in `t5413`—which now expects rejection when a replacement masks a missing tree—was originally written to accommodate a real-world edge case. If that test was added to paper over a specific workflow (e.g., a downstream tool that relies on replacements to hide missing objects), flipping it could break existing setups. The thread context suggests no one has yet dug into the test’s original intent, so this remains an open risk.

Second, Steinhardt notes that the patch also removes `OBJECT_INFO_LOOKUP_REPLACE` from `tree-verify.c`, a helper used outside the connectivity check. He asks whether the author has audited every other call site that might still expect replacements to be honored. The thread summary does not mention such an audit, so this change could introduce regressions elsewhere. The review is otherwise supportive of the core idea—aligning `rev-list` with `fsck` and friends—but these two loose ends need resolution before the patch can land.
