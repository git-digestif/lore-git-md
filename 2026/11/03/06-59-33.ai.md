This is a **meta-discussion reply** in the ongoing thread about Windows 64-to-32-bit truncation fixes, specifically addressing the project's norms for AI-assisted contributions. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Junio C Hamano's exploratory questions about how to evaluate submissions that involve AI tooling.

Patrick clarifies that his objection is not to the use of AI itself but to the *perception* that a human has not meaningfully engaged with the submission. He explains that AI-generated prose often has a distinct, uncanny style that feels overly formal or bloated, and that this is his primary signal for whether the author truly understands their own changes. For code, he acknowledges that AI can imitate Git's style so well that it is often indistinguishable from human-written code, leaving commit messages and mailing-list prose as the only reliable indicators of human involvement. He takes a pragmatic stance: if the author can demonstrate understanding by explaining the change coherently, he is satisfied, even if the code itself might be AI-generated. However, if the author cannot answer follow-up questions or if their explanations still read as AI-generated, he will question the submission's legitimacy.

The exchange does not resolve the immediate dispute over Elia Pinto's commit message, but it establishes Patrick's criteria for acceptable AI-assisted contributions: human authorship must be evident in the prose, and the author must be able to engage substantively in discussion. This sets a precedent for how the project may handle similar cases in the future.
