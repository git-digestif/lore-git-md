This is a **substantive review** of the first patch in Andy Koppe’s five-patch v2 series to align Git’s documentation with the 2024 terminology change distinguishing "pseudorefs" (FETCH_HEAD, MERGE_HEAD) from "root refs" (HEAD, ORIG_HEAD, etc.). The series is uncontroversial and appears ready for merging, but Patrick Steinhardt’s reply proposes a significant refinement to the glossary’s "pseudoref" entry.

Patrick’s suggestion shifts the focus from storage differences to **semantic differences**: pseudorefs can record multiple object IDs (e.g., FETCH_HEAD’s multi-line format) and carry metadata (e.g., branch names, "not-for-merge" flags), while root refs behave like normal refs except for their location outside `refs/`. His proposed wording explicitly contrasts pseudorefs with root refs, clarifies that pseudorefs are not handled by the ref backend, and retains the operational examples (supported/unsupported commands). This aligns with Junio C Hamano’s earlier clarification in the thread, which emphasized the on-disk format distinction and FETCH_HEAD’s metadata-rich structure.

The review is substantive because it engages with the *meaning* of the terminology rather than just phrasing. Patrick’s alternative definition would make the glossary more precise about why pseudorefs are treated differently, addressing a gap in the current patch’s explanation. While the series has already addressed all prior feedback, this new suggestion could further improve clarity without requiring behavioral changes. The patch remains uncontroversial, but adopting Patrick’s wording would strengthen the glossary’s accuracy.
