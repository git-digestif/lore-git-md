This is a substantive review of a bug in the `git diff --word-diff` implementation. Phillip Wood confirms the issue reported by Jean Abou Samra and explains its root cause: the word-diff logic splits hunks at context lines (unchanged lines) before computing word-level differences, which breaks when a blank line is introduced. In this case, the blank line is treated as context, causing the first paragraph to appear entirely deleted and leading to incorrect word matching in the diff output. Phillip notes this is a known limitation and suggests a fix—feeding entire hunks from the line-level diff into the word-diff logic instead of splitting them at context lines.

The review is technically detailed, referencing the diff engine’s behavior and even linking to a local patch that modifies the patience algorithm to avoid falling back to Myers diff when no context lines are found. This patch produces a more sensible word-diff for the reported case, though it’s not proposed as a formal solution.
