This is a bug report about `git diff --word-diff` producing incorrect output when comparing files that differ by both textual changes and whitespace additions. The author, Jean Abou Samra, provides a minimal reproduction case: three files where `file2.txt` differs from `file1.txt` by a few word substitutions, and `file3.txt` differs from `file2.txt` only by the addition of a blank line. While `git diff --word-diff` correctly shows the changes between `file1.txt` and `file2.txt`, and between `file2.txt` and `file3.txt`, it produces a confusing and incorrect diff when comparing `file1.txt` and `file3.txt`—matching words out of context and omitting large unchanged sections.

The report is clear and well-structured, with exact commands, expected output, and actual output. The attached archive contains the three files needed to reproduce the issue. The author notes that the problem occurs regardless of the `--diff-algorithm` setting, suggesting it is not a heuristic edge case but likely a deeper issue in the word-diff logic. This appears to be a genuine bug in Git's word-level diffing algorithm, possibly related to how it handles line boundaries or whitespace when computing word-level similarity. The report is credible and actionable, making it worth attention from maintainers of the diff subsystem.
