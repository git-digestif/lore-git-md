This is a substantive follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread, focusing on the trade-off between correctness and performance when commit graphs are stale or missing. Patrick Steinhardt responds to Linus Torvalds' proposal for a `--exact` flag by raising a key usability concern: users may not realize `git describe` can produce incorrect results by default, making an opt-in flag ineffective. Instead, he suggests flipping the default to *always* use accurate generation numbers (via `ensure_generations_valid()`), with an opt-out `--no-exact` flag for performance-sensitive cases. To improve discoverability, he proposes emitting a warning when the fallback to incorrect behavior occurs due to a missing commit graph.

The email advances the thread by challenging the assumption that users will proactively opt into correctness. Patrick frames the issue as a timing-dependent inconsistency (results varying based on commit-graph freshness) being more confusing than the original bug, which at least produced consistent (if wrong) results. His alternative—defaulting to accuracy with a warning—aims to make the behavior both correct and transparent, though it risks performance regressions for users who don’t need exact results. The proposal is well-motivated but likely to be controversial, as it shifts the burden from users to explicitly request correctness to users explicitly requesting speed.

This is a high-stakes design discussion with implications for `git describe`'s usability and performance. The thread has already seen alignment on the core fix (prioritizing generation numbers over commit dates) and proposals to amortize costs via commit-graph write-back, but Patrick’s suggestion reframes the debate around defaults and discoverability. Expect further discussion on whether warnings are sufficient, whether the default should be flipped, and how to balance performance with user expectations. The Linux kernel’s reliance on `git describe` adds urgency, as the regression directly impacted its versioning workflow.
