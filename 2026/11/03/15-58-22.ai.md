This is a brief follow-up in the ongoing `git describe` regression fix thread, now focused on the user-facing design of the proposed `--exact` flag. Linus Torvalds responds to Patrick Steinhardt’s counterproposal to make `--exact` the default (opt-out) rather than opt-in, agreeing that the approach is "sane." His reply explicitly endorses the opt-out model, noting that it preserves a way for users to avoid the performance cost of `ensure_generations_valid()` in edge cases like read-only or legacy repositories.

The email is short but significant: it removes the last substantive objection to Patrick’s opt-out proposal, leaving the design axis settled in favor of correctness-by-default with a warning mechanism. The only remaining open question is whether the warning should be rate-limited or configurable, but Linus does not address that here. The thread is now effectively converged on a systemic resolution: the original one-line fix will be augmented by an opt-out `--exact` flag (defaulting to on) and a warning when the commit graph is unavailable, with the commit-graph write-back mechanism proposed by Junio and Derrick Stolee as a complementary performance optimization.

Key technical details for future reference:
- **Flag name**: `--exact` (default: on, opt-out via `--no-exact`).
- **Warning trigger**: Emitted when the commit graph is unavailable and the command falls back to commit dates.
- **Edge case**: Read-only or legacy repositories can opt out of the performance cost.
- **Subsystems**: `git describe` command, commit-graph generation numbers, warning mechanism.
- **Related work**: Commit-graph write-back (proposed but not yet implemented) to amortize the cost of `ensure_generations_valid()`.
