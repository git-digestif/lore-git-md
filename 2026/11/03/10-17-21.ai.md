This is a **substantive review** of Kristofer Karlsson’s patch to make `git rev-list --connectivity-check` ignore replacement objects. Patrick Steinhardt raises two technical concerns that could affect the patch’s correctness and scope.

First, he questions whether the test change in `t5413`—which now expects the connectivity check to reject a commit whose original tree is incomplete but whose replacement is complete—was originally written to accommodate a real-world edge case. If so, the new behavior might break existing workflows that rely on replacement objects masking missing objects during fetches or pushes. This is not a rejection of the patch’s design (which the thread has converged on) but a request for historical context: was the test’s original expectation intentional, or was it simply an oversight?

Second, he points out that the patch’s removal of `OBJECT_INFO_LOOKUP_REPLACE` from `tree-verify.c` affects more than just the connectivity check. The `tree-verify.c` helper is used by other code paths (e.g., `git fsck`, `git index-pack`), and blindly dropping the flag could unintentionally change their behavior. He asks whether the author audited all callers of `odb_read_object_info_extended()` to ensure consistency—specifically, whether other sites that verify objects should also ignore replacements, or if this change should be scoped more narrowly to the connectivity check.
