This is a substantive review in the ongoing bugfix thread about `git rev-list`'s connectivity check and replacement objects. Patrick Steinhardt weighs in on the unresolved design question: whether the connectivity check should ignore replacements (current patch) or verify both original *and* replacement objects (Junio's alternative).

Patrick's response is measured and pragmatic. He acknowledges Kristofer's point that replacement refs are stored as regular refs (`refs/replace/OLD -> NEW`), so their reachable objects should already be covered by ordinary connectivity checks during fetch or push. However, he raises a subtle edge case: what if the replacement ref was written *locally* rather than fetched or pushed? In that scenario, the objects referenced by the replacement might not have been validated by a prior connectivity check. Patrick ultimately concludes that this edge case is not the connectivity check's responsibility—users who create local replacement refs are expected to ensure the new objects are valid, just as they would for any other ref. This aligns with Git's existing assumption that locally existing refs are fully connected during push operations.

The review does not propose code changes or challenge the implementation itself. Instead, it reinforces the current patch's design by framing the connectivity check's scope narrowly: its job is to validate the objects being transferred, not to police the integrity of locally created refs. This perspective effectively closes the loop on Junio's conceptual question, suggesting that the current approach (ignoring replacements) is sufficient and correct. The thread now appears to have converged on the original patch's design, pending any further objections.

Key technical details for follow-up: the patch touches `builtin/rev-list.c` (adding `--connectivity-check`), `tree-verify.c` (dropping `OBJECT_INFO_LOOKUP_REPLACE`), and `t/t5413-rev-list-connectivity.sh` (updated test coverage). The unresolved documentation inconsistency noted by Karthik Nayak—where `git-replace.adoc` does not reflect that `rev-list --connectivity-check` ignores replacements—remains the only loose end. No new behavioral alternatives are on the table, and the patch is now effectively ready for integration unless Junio or others raise further concerns.
