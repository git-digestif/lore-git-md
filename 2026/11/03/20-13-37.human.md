This is a review comment on Kevin Lyles’ bug report about the interaction between the pre-commit hook and `git commit <pathspec>`.

Kristoffer Haugsbakk questions the legitimacy of the reported issue by challenging the intended use of the pre-commit hook. He points to the `githooks` documentation, which frames the pre-commit hook primarily as a tool for validation and checking, not for modifying the index or worktree. His comment suggests that Kevin’s use case—using the hook to mutate file contents—may not be a supported or reasonable pattern, implying the observed behavior might not be a bug but rather an unsupported edge case.

The review is surface-level, focusing on the design intent of the hook rather than the technical mechanics of the pathspec behavior. It does not engage with the inconsistency Kevin described (where hook-induced changes are discarded only when a pathspec is provided) or propose an alternative approach. The tone is matter-of-fact, but the implication is that the report may not warrant a fix if the hook’s purpose is narrowly interpreted. This could steer the discussion toward whether the hook’s documentation should be clarified or whether the behavior should be adjusted to accommodate mutation use cases.
