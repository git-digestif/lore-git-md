This is a documentation-focused follow-up in the ongoing effort to clarify Git’s terminology around "root refs" and "pseudorefs." Julia Evans proposes a more concrete and example-driven rewrite of the glossary’s "pseudoref" entry, replacing the current abstract description of `FETCH_HEAD`’s metadata with a direct example of its file format. Her suggestion aims to make the entry more accessible to users who may not be familiar with the on-disk representation of pseudorefs, while preserving the technical accuracy of the series’ broader goal: distinguishing pseudorefs (like `FETCH_HEAD` and `MERGE_HEAD`) from root refs (like `HEAD` and `ORIG_HEAD`).

The proposal is narrowly scoped but substantive for documentation quality. It does not challenge the series’ direction or introduce new technical claims, but it directly addresses a readability gap in the glossary. The current phrasing ("Each object ID is annotated with metadata...") is technically correct but opaque to users who haven’t inspected `.git/FETCH_HEAD`; Julia’s example-driven alternative would make the format immediately clear. This aligns with the series’ pedagogical goals, particularly the incorporation of her earlier `gitdatamodel.adoc` proposal, which also prioritizes clarity for users learning Git’s data model.

No controversy is evident, and the change would likely be uncontroversial if adopted. The proposal is framed as a suggestion rather than a demand, and it builds on the existing glossary structure (e.g., retaining the list of supported operations and the distinction from root refs). Andy Koppe or Junio may choose to incorporate it into a future revision of the series, but it does not block the current v2 patches, which remain ready for merging once the four outstanding issues (including the commit message mismatch in Patch 2/5) are resolved.
