This is a bug report about surprising behavior in the interaction between the pre-commit hook and `git commit <pathspec>`.

Kevin Lyles describes a scenario where `git commit <file>` behaves differently depending on whether other files are already staged and whether a pathspec is provided. When committing a specific file, the pre-commit hook’s index modifications are lost, but the worktree changes persist. If other files are staged or no pathspec is given, the hook’s changes are preserved in both index and worktree. The commit message’s claim that files are staged "on top of what have been staged before" appears to be violated in the pathspec case.

The example pre-commit script appends text to staged files in both index and worktree, making the discrepancy easy to reproduce. Kevin notes this isn’t his real hook but a minimal demonstration; his actual use case modifies files to ensure updated content is committed while minimizing spurious diffs. He’s open to alternatives but needs something that scales to large repositories.

The question of intent is left open: is this inconsistency a bug or an undocumented edge case? No prior discussion or patch is referenced, so this appears to be a fresh observation rather than a follow-up. The report is clear and includes a reproducible recipe, suggesting it’s worth attention from maintainers familiar with the hook and commit internals.
