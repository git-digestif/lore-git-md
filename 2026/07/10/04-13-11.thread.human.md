This thread is about a bugfix for `git rebase --autosquash` when `fixup!` or `squash!` commits cancel out their target, leaving an empty commit. The patch, now at version 5, has been developed by Farid Zakaria to respect the `--empty` option (`drop`, `keep`, or `stop`) instead of halting the rebase with an error. The fix touches the sequencer subsystem, adding logic to detect when applying a fixup empties the target commit and ensuring the rebase behaves as configured.

Phillip Wood has been the primary reviewer, providing detailed feedback across all iterations. His latest review of `v5` is thorough, focusing on clarity, correctness, and edge cases. While most of his suggestions are "nice to have" improvements—such as better comments, error handling, and redundant check removal—he flags one correctness issue in `commit_staged_changes()` that must be addressed. The patch is otherwise ready for merging once it’s rebased onto `master` and the requested changes are incorporated. No new design concerns have emerged, though the unrelated discussion about a potential `revert!` autosquash directive remains open.

The patch has evolved significantly since its first version, incorporating all substantive feedback. It now includes comprehensive test coverage for all `--empty` modes and edge cases, ensuring the fix works correctly for mid-chain fixups, root commits, and conflict resolution. Farid’s motivation comes from a real-world workflow at Meta, where this bug disrupted automated patch management. The series is self-contained and has no dependencies, though it may pave the way for future enhancements like the `revert!` directive.
