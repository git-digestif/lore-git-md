## 2026/08/27

- [2026/08/27/05-10-53 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King distinguished banned-die.h from universal bans like banned.h: the former targets runtime effects in specific code paths rather than source-level unfitness, but banning die() only catches direct calls, not transitive ones through other functions, creating a whack-a-mole problem; he concluded this is probably the best practical approach for die() specifically (since callers are not prepared for it to return under a runtime flag), but would not want the strategy extended to other areas where runtime support is usually better.
- [2026/08/27/05-23-18 by Jeff King <peff@peff.net>] Jeff King cautioned that banning the listed allocation functions is the tip of the iceberg, since all of strbuf would also need to be off-limits (it calls malloc directly and bails if snprintf() returns -1) and other indirect calls may hide elsewhere (e.g., json-writer.c); he suggested that truly avoiding allocations in trace2 would probably require a ground-up no-dependency rewrite.
